Daniel 11

A Prophetic Overview · 536–164 B.C. Four centuries of history, foretold in precise detail nearly 400 years before it came to pass.

Prophecy written before the history

Why this chapter astonishes

Daniel 11 bridges the silent centuries between the close of the Old Testament and the time of Christ, tracing the Persian Empire, Greece, and the wars of the North and South.

~400years of silence between the last Old Testament prophet and the time of Christ
~536 B.C.when Daniel received it, in the third year of Cyrus
~370years foretold, from the Persian kings down to Antiochus IV (536 to 164 B.C.)

The conclusion: only an omniscient God could dictate such precise historical realities hundreds of years before they occurred.

The King of the North and the King of the South

King of the North

The Seleucid Empire: Syria and Mesopotamia. Capital: Antioch.

King of the South

The Ptolemaic Empire: Egypt. Capital: Alexandria.

Between them lay Israel, “the glorious land.” Daniel 11 traces the long tug-of-war of these two dynasties, with Israel caught in the middle.

Act I · Daniel 11:1-4

Persia and the Rise of Greece

From the kings of Persia to a mighty Greek king whose empire is shattered the moment it peaks.

✦ ✦ ✦
Act I · ~530–465 B.C.

Three kings of Persia, and a rich fourth

Also I in the first year of Darius the Mede, even I, stood to confirm and to strengthen him. And now will I shew thee the truth. Behold, there shall stand up yet three kings in Persia; and the fourth shall be far richer than they all: and by his strength through his riches he shall stir up all against the realm of Grecia.Daniel 11:1-2

History. After Cyrus came Cambyses II, Smerdis and Darius I. The fourth, Xerxes I, was far richer than all of them, and he hurled his armies against Greece in the campaign of Thermopylae and Salamis (480 B.C.).

Act I · ~336–323 B.C.

Alexander’s empire, split four ways

And a mighty king shall stand up, that shall rule with great dominion, and do according to his will. And when he shall stand up, his kingdom shall be broken, and shall be divided toward the four winds of heaven; and not to his posterity, nor according to his dominion which he ruled: for his kingdom shall be plucked up, even for others beside those.Daniel 11:3-4

History. Alexander the Great conquered widely and died young in 323 B.C. His empire was divided among four of his generals, toward the four winds of heaven.

Ptolemy, the South

Egypt. His line drives Daniel 11.

Seleucus, the North

Syria and Mesopotamia. His line drives Daniel 11.

Lysimachus

Asia Minor and Thrace.

Cassander

Macedonia and Greece.

Act II · Daniel 11:5-20

The Wars of North and South

Political marriages, broken alliances, and armies surging back and forth across the glorious land.

✦ ✦ ✦
Act II · ~252 B.C.

The first alliance, and its betrayal

And the king of the south shall be strong, and one of his princes; and he shall be strong above him, and have dominion; his dominion shall be a great dominion. And in the end of years they shall join themselves together; for the king’s daughter of the south shall come to the king of the north to make an agreement: but she shall not retain the power of the arm; neither shall he stand, nor his arm: but she shall be given up, and they that brought her, and he that begat her, and he that strengthened her in these times.Daniel 11:5-6

History. Berenice, daughter of Ptolemy II of Egypt, marries Antiochus II of Syria to seal a peace. After her father’s death the arrangement fails, and she is abandoned and later murdered.

Act II · ~246–241 B.C.

Berenice avenged

But out of a branch of her roots shall one stand up in his estate, which shall come with an army, and shall enter into the fortress of the king of the north, and shall deal against them, and shall prevail: And shall also carry captives into Egypt their gods, with their princes, and with their precious vessels of silver and of gold; and he shall continue more years than the king of the north.Daniel 11:7-8

History. Her brother Ptolemy III invades the North, defeats Seleucus II, and returns to Egypt with captives and treasures, including idols.

Act II · ~225–219 B.C.

The sons strike back

So the king of the south shall come into his kingdom, and shall return into his own land. But his sons shall be stirred up, and shall assemble a multitude of great forces: and one shall certainly come, and overflow, and pass through: then shall he return, and be stirred up, even to his fortress.Daniel 11:9-10

History. The sons of Seleucus II, Seleucus III and Antiochus III the Great, assemble great forces to retaliate against Egypt.

Act II · ~217 B.C.

A prideful victory

And the king of the south shall be moved with choler, and shall come forth and fight with him, even with the king of the north: and he shall set forth a great multitude; but the multitude shall be given into his hand. And when he hath taken away the multitude, his heart shall be lifted up; and he shall cast down many ten thousands: but he shall not be strengthened by it.Daniel 11:11-12

History. Ptolemy IV defeats Antiochus III at Raphia, but his proud victory does not strengthen him.

Act II · ~200 B.C.

The North returns in force

For the king of the north shall return, and shall set forth a multitude greater than the former, and shall certainly come after certain years with a great army and with much riches. And in those times there shall many stand up against the king of the south: also the robbers of thy people shall exalt themselves to establish the vision; but they shall fall. So the king of the north shall come, and cast up a mount, and take the most fenced cities: and the arms of the south shall not withstand, neither his chosen people, neither shall there be any strength to withstand.Daniel 11:13-15

History. Antiochus III returns with a larger army, and Egypt’s enemies join him against Ptolemy V. He captures fortified cities, including Sidon. Some Jews who tried to help him establish the vision fell short.

Act II · ~198–192 B.C.

Into the glorious land

But he that cometh against him shall do according to his own will, and none shall stand before him: and he shall stand in the glorious land, which by his hand shall be consumed. He shall also set his face to enter with the strength of his whole kingdom, and upright ones with him; thus shall he do: and he shall give him the daughter of women, corrupting her: but she shall not stand on his side, neither be for him.Daniel 11:16-17

History. Antiochus III defeats Ptolemy V and takes Israel, then gives him his daughter Cleopatra I in marriage. She sides with Egypt, and his plan fails.

Act II · ~190–175 B.C.

Stopped by Rome, and gone

After this shall he turn his face unto the isles, and shall take many: but a prince for his own behalf shall cause the reproach offered by him to cease; without his own reproach he shall cause it to turn upon him. Then he shall turn his face toward the fort of his own land: but he shall stumble and fall, and not be found. Then shall stand up in his estate a raiser of taxes in the glory of the kingdom: but within few days he shall be destroyed, neither in anger, nor in battle.Daniel 11:18-20

History. Antiochus III is beaten by Rome at Magnesia (Lucius Scipio) and dies in 187 B.C. His successor, Seleucus IV, taxes heavily and is dead within a few years, not in battle and not in open anger.

Act III · Daniel 11:21-35

Antiochus IV Epiphanes

A vile person who seizes power by flattery, desecrates the temple, and foreshadows the Antichrist to come.

✦ ✦ ✦

A prophecy with two fulfillments

Daniel 11 is fulfilled in two layers. Antiochus IV fulfills it first, in the second century before Christ. The same words reach their final fulfillment in the Antichrist at the time of the end, and the end is what matters most.

First fulfillment

Antiochus IV Epiphanes, 175–164 B.C. His flatteries, his war on the covenant and the desecration of the temple are history, and the Maccabees lived through them.

Final fulfillment

The Antichrist, at the time of the end. Jesus speaks of the abomination of desolation as still to come, long after Antiochus.

Antiochus is the pattern and the Antichrist is the fulfillment. Watch how the verses fit the first, and then reach past it to the last.

Act III · ~175–171 B.C.

A vile person rises

And in his estate shall stand up a vile person, to whom they shall not give the honour of the kingdom: but he shall come in peaceably, and obtain the kingdom by flatteries. And with the arms of a flood shall they be overflown from before him, and shall be broken; yea, also the prince of the covenant.Daniel 11:21-22

History. Antiochus IV seizes power by flattery, overwhelms his opponents, and has the high priest Onias III killed, the “prince of the covenant.”

Act III · ~170 B.C.

Strong through deceit

And after the league made with him he shall work deceitfully: for he shall come up, and shall become strong with a small people. He shall enter peaceably even upon the fattest places of the province; and he shall do that which his fathers have not done, nor his fathers’ fathers; he shall scatter among them the prey, and spoil, and riches: yea, and he shall forecast his devices against the strong holds, even for a time.Daniel 11:23-24

History. He makes deceptive alliances, grows strong with a small following, and lavishes plunder on them as he moves against strongholds.

Act III · ~170–169 B.C.

War against the South

And he shall stir up his power and his courage against the king of the south with a great army; and the king of the south shall be stirred up to battle with a very great and mighty army; but he shall not stand: for they shall forecast devices against him. Yea, they that feed of the portion of his meat shall destroy him, and his army shall overflow: and many shall fall down slain.Daniel 11:25-26

History. Antiochus IV defeats Ptolemy VI in battle, helped by betrayal within Egypt’s own court.

Act III · ~169 B.C.

Lies at one table

And both these kings’ hearts shall be to do mischief, and they shall speak lies at one table; but it shall not prosper: for yet the end shall be at the time appointed. Then shall he return into his land with great riches; and his heart shall be against the holy covenant; and he shall do exploits, and return to his own land.Daniel 11:27-28

History. Antiochus and Ptolemy VI talk peace and lie to one another, and neither plan succeeds. Antiochus returns home with great riches, plundering the temple on the way, with his heart set against Israel’s religion.

Act III · ~168 B.C.

Turned back by Rome’s ships

At the time appointed he shall return, and come toward the south; but it shall not be as the former, or as the latter. For the ships of Chittim shall come against him: therefore he shall be grieved, and return, and have indignation against the holy covenant: so shall he do; he shall even return, and have intelligence with them that forsake the holy covenant.Daniel 11:29-30

History. His second campaign in Egypt is halted when the Roman envoy Popilius Laenas, with Rome’s ships behind him, orders him out. Furious, he turns on Israel and favors the Jews who forsook the covenant.

Act III · ~167 B.C.

The abomination of desolation

And arms shall stand on his part, and they shall pollute the sanctuary of strength, and shall take away the daily sacrifice, and they shall place the abomination that maketh desolate.Daniel 11:31

History. Antiochus’ forces and apostate Jews defile the temple, stop the daily sacrifice, and set up an altar to Zeus on the altar of the Lord, the “abomination that maketh desolate.” Jesus speaks of it again as still to come.

Act III · ~167–164 B.C.

The people who know their God

And such as do wickedly against the covenant shall he corrupt by flatteries: but the people that do know their God shall be strong, and do exploits. And they that understand among the people shall instruct many: yet they shall fall by the sword, and by flame, by captivity, and by spoil, many days. Now when they shall fall, they shall be holpen with a little help: but many shall cleave to them with flatteries. And some of them of understanding shall fall, to try them, and to purge, and to make them white, even to the time of the end: because it is yet for a time appointed.Daniel 11:32-35

History. Faithful Jews resist and teach others, the Maccabean revolt follows, and many are persecuted and martyred, refined for the time of the end.

Act IV · Daniel 11:36-45

The Willful King and the End

He magnifies himself above all, and comes to his end with none to help him.

✦ ✦ ✦
Act IV · Daniel 11:36-37

He magnifies himself above all

And the king shall do according to his will; and he shall exalt himself, and magnify himself above every god, and shall speak marvellous things against the God of gods, and shall prosper till the indignation be accomplished: for that that is determined shall be done. Neither shall he regard the God of his fathers, nor the desire of women, nor regard any god: for he shall magnify himself above all.Daniel 11:36-37

First fulfillment. Antiochus IV took the title Epiphanes, “God manifest,” and exalted himself above the gods of his fathers.

Final fulfillment. The same words describe the man of sin who exalts himself above all that is called God.

Act IV · Daniel 11:38-39

The god of forces

But in his estate shall he honour the God of forces: and a god whom his fathers knew not shall he honour with gold, and silver, and with precious stones, and pleasant things. Thus shall he do in the most strong holds with a strange god, whom he shall acknowledge and increase with glory: and he shall cause them to rule over many, and shall divide the land for gain.Daniel 11:38-39

First fulfillment. Antiochus honored a god his fathers did not know, read as Zeus Olympios, and rewarded his followers with gold and silver and precious stones, dividing the land among them for gain.

Final fulfillment. A world ruler who honors a god of power and rewards those who follow him.

Act IV · Daniel 11:40-43

The time of the end

And at the time of the end shall the king of the south push at him: and the king of the north shall come against him like a whirlwind, with chariots, and with horsemen, and with many ships; and he shall enter into the countries, and shall overflow and pass over. He shall enter also into the glorious land, and many countries shall be overthrown: but these shall escape out of his hand, even Edom, and Moab, and the chief of the children of Ammon. He shall stretch forth his hand also upon the countries: and the land of Egypt shall not escape. But he shall have power over the treasures of gold and of silver, and over all the precious things of Egypt: and the Libyans and the Ethiopians shall be at his steps.Daniel 11:40-43

First fulfillment. Only in part. Rome turned Antiochus back from Egypt in 168 B.C., and nothing in the record has him ruling Egypt, Libya and Ethiopia.

Final fulfillment. These verses reach to the time of the end, as the chapter itself says, when the king of the north comes like a whirlwind.

Act IV · Daniel 11:44-45

He comes to his end

But tidings out of the east and out of the north shall trouble him: therefore he shall go forth with great fury to destroy, and utterly to make away many. And he shall plant the tabernacles of his palace between the seas in the glorious holy mountain; yet he shall come to his end, and none shall help him.Daniel 11:44-45

First fulfillment. Antiochus died in Persia in 164 B.C., not in the holy mountain.

Final fulfillment. The palace planted between the seas in the glorious holy mountain, and an end with none to help him, belong to the Antichrist, and Daniel 12 goes on at once to the time of trouble.

History foretold, and yet to come

First fulfillment

Detailed history from the Persian kings to Antiochus’ desecration of the temple, fulfilled between 536 and 164 B.C.

Final fulfillment

The end is what matters most: a future Antichrist, the abomination of desolation, and persecution, as in Daniel 12.

Undeniable

The precision of biblical prophecy gives us faith in God’s word, and undeniable proof of its truth.
