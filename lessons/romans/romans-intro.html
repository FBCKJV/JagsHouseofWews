Who is Paul & Why Does He Matter?

The author of Romans is one of the most pivotal figures in history. His life was defined by a dramatic transformation, from a zealous persecutor of the church to its most influential apostle.

Saul of Tarsus: The Persecutor

A Pharisee from the Tribe of Benjamin. Zealously persecuted the early church in Jerusalem. Was present at the martyrdom of Stephen (Acts 7).

The Conversion: The Damascus Road

Occurred around AD 35. A great light from heaven and the voice of Jesus. Christ’s words: “Saul, Saul, why persecutest thou me?”

Paul’s Ministry & Legacy

Following his conversion, Paul spent years in preparation before embarking on a ministry that would establish the theological foundations of Christianity and spread the gospel across the Roman Empire.

Understanding the Form and Format of the Epistle

Romans: A Logical Argument

In the epistle, or letter, to the Romans, Paul is using a dialectical format called a Diatribe.

✦ ✦ ✦

Dialectic: Discovering Truth Through Logical Exchange

In an argument presentation, dialectic is a method of reasoning that seeks to discover truth through the exchange and logical reconciliation of opposing ideas.

It is a process of critical analysis and dialogue (or even a single structured presentation) that aims for a higher, more comprehensive understanding rather than simply “winning” a debate.

Diatribe: A Vigorous, Conversational Moral Discourse

A diatribe (Greek diatribē, meaning “pastime” or “discourse”) was a form of popular moral discourse or lecture among Greek and Roman philosophers.

It is an oral or written presentation that uses a vigorous, conversational style to explore ethical questions and find truth.

The Diatribe: Dialogical Approach and Moral Exhortation

Dialogical Approach

It often employed a dialogue format with an imaginary or hypothetical opponent (interlocutor). It used a question-and-answer structure, rhetorical questions, and responses to anticipated objections. This was an extension of the Socratic method, designed to guide students or listeners to a better understanding of a philosophical point.

Moral Exhortation

The primary goal was not simply debate, but moral instruction and persuasion. It aimed to expose contradictions in the audience’s presuppositions and help them overcome false and biased ideas they held.

The Diatribe: Accessible Style and a Single Theme

Accessible Style

The language used is often colloquial, direct, and sometimes informal. It utilized colorful, everyday imagery, anecdotes, and historical examples to make complex philosophical ideas accessible to a general audience.

Focus on a Single Theme

These discourses typically centered on a single ethical or philosophical theme. Paul’s overall theme is the election of grace, the understanding that the Gentile believers in Christ were as much God’s chosen people and equal part of the Kingdom as Jewish believers, and the implications of salvation by grace through faith for the believer’s life.

Inferential and Explanatory Words

The Connective Tissue

Words like “For,” “Because,” “Wherefore,” and “Therefore” are not prepositions; they are logical connectors or inferential markers. They are essential transitional phrases that structure the debate, allowing the reader to trace Paul’s logic from premises to conclusions.

1. Explanatory (Causal) Markers: “For” & “Because”

These introduce the reason or cause behind a previous statement. They tell the reader, “Here is the evidence that supports what I just said.”

2. Inferential (Conclusive) Markers: “Wherefore” & “Therefore”

These introduce the consequence or conclusion drawn from the preceding facts. They tell the reader, “Based on everything I just proved, this is the logical outcome.”

The Connective Tissue: Markers at a Glance

Marker TypeKey Words
Explanatory (Premise)For (gar), Because
Inferential (Conclusion)Therefore (dio, oun, ara), Wherefore

Function in the argument. Explanatory markers introduce the reason, evidence, or bedrock doctrine that justifies the preceding claim. Inferential markers introduce the logical outcome or major implication of the premises just established.

Paul’s Logical, “If/Then” Structure

Paul’s diatribe uses a logical, “if/then” structure.

He teaches the bedrock doctrines of faith as the building blocks for the main argument.

IF
Everyone (Jew & Gentile) is under sin.
THEN
Salvation is by grace through faith.
THEREFORE
Gentile believers are equal in the Kingdom.
HOW THEN
Should believers live?
