Romans 11

Dispensation

Biblical Stewardship • Household Management of the Gospel

✦ ✦ ✦

The Greek word Oikonomia

The word "Dispensation" in the Greek, Oikonomia literally comes from οἶκος (oikos = "house") + νόμος (nomos = "law" or "management/rule"), so it fundamentally means "household management," "administration," or "stewardship."

A steward or house manager is entrusted to oversee, protect, and faithfully distribute what belongs to the master. In this case, Paul compares his role to being entrusted with the gospel to faithfully preach and deliver it to others.

1 Corinthians 9:17

A dispensation of the gospel

For if I do this thing willingly, I have a reward: but if against my will, a dispensation of the gospel is committed unto me.1 Corinthians 9:17
Ephesians 3:2

The dispensation of the grace of God

If ye have heard of the dispensation of the grace of God which is given me to you-ward:Ephesians 3:2
Colossians 1:25

The dispensation of God

Whereof I am made a minister, according to the dispensation of God which is given to me for you, to fulfil the word of God;Colossians 1:25
Ephesians 1:10

The fulness of times

That in the dispensation of the fulness of times he might gather together in one all things in Christ, both which are in heaven, and which are on earth; even in him:Ephesians 1:10

Paul's use of the term

In the three passages where Paul applies "dispensation" to himself (1 Cor 9:17, Eph 3:2, Col 1:25), he is saying he has been entrusted/stewarded with the gospel and the grace of God to faithfully preach and administer it to others.

In Ephesians 1:10, the term refers to God's own stewardship/dispensation over the entire fullness of times, gathering all things together in Christ at the end.

One continuous gospel

We all have a dispensation of the gospel, to keep the faith.

One continuous gospel in Christ from Adam through today.

God, who at sundry times and in divers manners spake in time past unto the fathers by the prophets, Hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son, whom he hath appointed heir of all things, by whom also he made the worlds;Hebrews 1:1-2

The difference over time was revelation of mysteries, given by the word of God through the prophets, the preaching of Christ, and the inspired New Testament authors, which gives us full revelation.

No "Church age"

The Old Testament replaced by the New, a physical nation with a spiritual one.

The Bible does not say anywhere that there is a "Church age," or various "church ages" (Revelation 1-3).

The "Church" is the called-out assembly of believers at the resurrection or individual independent congregations gathering at their local NT church.

View 1

Mild Dispensationalism

✦ ✦ ✦
Mild Dispensationalism

Mild Dispensationalism: what it teaches

Teaches

Dispensations as stewardships or "administrations" (from the Greek oikonomia), different ways God interacts with and tests humanity in various eras of history. Salvation is always by grace through faith alone in the promised Christ (), looking forward to the cross (though some teach the understanding of a Messiah wasn't always clear, "through a glass darkly").

Common eras taught

Innocence (Garden of Eden), Conscience (after fall to flood), Human Government (Noah onward), Promise (Abraham to Moses), Law (Moses to Christ), Grace/Church Age (Pentecost to rapture), Kingdom/Millennium (future 1,000-year reign).

End times

Emphasizes end-times prophecy and charts laying out the timing of events according to their theology. Holds to a future 7-year Tribulation, pre-tribulation rapture, and eventual Millennial kingdom; rejects post-millennial, amillennial, and preterist views. Sees future restoration of the physical nation of Israel during the Tribulation, where many (if not all) Jews will see the signs and wonders and turn to Christ. Uses as support.

Mild Dispensationalism

Mild Dispensationalism: strength and issue

Who generally holds this view

Most Independent Fundamental Baptists (IFB), many conservative evangelicals, Bible churches, teachers like Jack Hyles, John R. Rice.

Strength

Strongly affirms one way of salvation, grace through faith in Christ, in every era, whether OT saints looking forward to the promised Savior or NT believers looking back to the cross. Provides room for disagreement on exact timing of end-times events (e.g., pre-, mid-, or post-tribulation rapture details) to avoid division among believers, and acts as a practical compromise with the prevailing theology in modern evangelical circles.

Issue

Uses "dispensation" to refer to distinct ages/eras with different tests/rules of responsibility, which the Bible does not explicitly define this way. May unintentionally suggest that the "church" is separate from Israel, with corporate Israel receiving promises and inheritance that are promised to all believers as a whole (see ; , one body, one faith, no distinction in Christ).

View 2

Traditional Dispensationalism / Zionism

✦ ✦ ✦
Traditional Dispensationalism / Zionism

Traditional Dispensationalism: what it teaches

Teaches

God works in distinct dispensations/ages with different tests/rules of responsibility (e.g., innocence, law, grace). The Church is seen as a "parenthesis" in God's primary plan for Israel, an ultimate failure or incomplete entity necessitating the future restoration of physical/national Israel. Uses the 7 churches in Revelation 1–3 as prophetic of distinct "church ages" (e.g., Ephesus = apostolic, Laodicea = apostate modern Church).

Teaches, continued

Holds to a future literal 7-year Tribulation, pre-tribulation rapture of the Church, second coming, and restored national Israel in a literal millennial kingdom (with temple sacrifices as memorials). Ethnic Israel is still God's chosen people today, despite apostasy, with unconditional future promises to the nation as a whole. Pays lip-service to salvation always being by grace through faith, but often drifts into implying the OT dispensation required obedience to the law/works for blessing or acceptance.

Traditional Dispensationalism / Zionism

Traditional Dispensationalism: strength and issue

Who generally holds this view

Traditional dispensationalists, many Southern Baptists, Calvary Chapel, popular prophecy teachers (e.g., Tim LaHaye, John Walvoord, Hal Lindsey), and organizations like those associated with Scofield Bible notes.

Strength

Takes OT prophecies literally and emphasizes God's faithfulness to His promises. Strongly promotes evangelism and end-times awareness.

Issue

Pays lip-service to salvation always being by grace through faith, but often drifts into implying the OT dispensation required works of the law for acceptance/blessing. Treats the "Church" as an ultimate failure necessitating the restoration of physical Israel. Uses the 7 churches in Revelation as distinct ages of the "Church" (not stated in text). Sees ethnic Israel currently as still God's chosen people despite apostasy, potentially downplaying the NT emphasis on faith alone and one body in Christ (; ).

View 3

Replacement Theology

✦ ✦ ✦
Replacement Theology

Replacement Theology: what it teaches

Teaches

The Church directly replaces Israel because of Israel's rejection of Christ. Israel has forfeited its promises and role as God's chosen nation; the Church inherits them spiritually. The New Covenant replaces the Old (). OT promises (land, kingdom, blessings) are transferred to or fulfilled in the Church. No future distinct restoration or role for ethnic/national Israel as a nation. Any salvation for Jews is individual entry into the Church. Strongly affirms the biblical pattern of God choosing the lesser over the greater, the younger over the older, the new over the old (e.g., Seth over Cain, Isaac over Ishmael, Jacob over Esau, Judah over Reuben, David over Saul, Esther over Vashti, publicans/harlots over Pharisees, etc.).

Note on Historic Premillennialism

Some who hold a similar view of replacement/fulfillment and a future literal kingdom for all believers (Jew and Gentile together) call it "historic premillennialism," future millennium after Christ's return, no pre-trib rapture, no separate Israel program.

Replacement Theology

Replacement Theology: strength and issue

Who generally holds this view

Many Independent Fundamental Baptists (especially NIFB and some who embrace the "replacement" label), conservative evangelicals who reject Zionism, post-trib/pre-wrath teachers, and others who emphasize the NT's fulfillment language while rejecting dispensational future-national-Israel programs.

Strength

Faithfully highlights the biblical pattern of replacement/fulfillment (lesser over greater, new over old) seen throughout Scripture. Rejects Zionism and any separate future program for national Israel. Holds to post-trib/pre-wrath timing and a millennial kingdom for all believers (Jew and Gentile together). Emphasizes faith in Christ alone and warns against relying on physical descent or works.

Issue

Can create too sharp a break with OT believers (while still acknowledging them as part of the Church), and in defending against critics sometimes over-emphasizes discontinuity and misses the full continuity of one vine/assembly by faith from Adam to now (; ).

View 4

Biblical Fundamentalism

✦ ✦ ✦
Biblical Fundamentalism

Biblical Fundamentalism: what it teaches

Teaches

One continuous vine/root in Christ (): natural (Jewish) and wild (Gentile) branches one congregation/assembly in resurrection. All saved by calling on the Lord (; ), knowing Redeemer liveth (), esteeming Christ's riches. Gospel preached, witnessed by Moses/prophets (; ), testified by apostles (), unbroken to believers today. Various covenants/revelations, but salvation always grace through faith, no different gospels. New Covenant ends Old (); physical nation to spiritual (). No separate peoples/programs: unbelief cuts off, faith grafts in, mercy to whosoever believes.

Key Scriptures

Rom 11:16–24 (one olive tree); Gal 3:28–29 (one in Christ); Eph 4:4–6 (one body); Heb 11:39–40 (OT perfected with us); Rev 7:9–17 (all nations redeemed).

Biblical Fundamentalism

Biblical Fundamentalism: strength and issue

Who generally holds this view

Bible-believing Christians rejecting covenant over-spiritualization and dispensational insertions, prioritizing plain text (many IFB, independent Baptists).

Strength

Lets Scripture speak plainly, one gospel by grace through faith from Genesis to Revelation, one people (Israel of God by faith), no inserted programs or multiple gospels.

Issue

None, aligns with NT teaching and avoids extremes.

View 5

Supersessionism

✦ ✦ ✦
Supersessionism

Supersessionism

Teaches

Church supersedes/replaces Israel in God's plan due to unbelief. OT promises fulfilled spiritually in Church. No distinct future for ethnic Israel; salvation for Jews is individual conversion into Church. Often amillennial; no literal future kingdom.

Who generally holds this view

Roman Catholic Church, Eastern Orthodox, many mainline Protestants (Methodist, Episcopal), some early Church Fathers.

Strength

Affirms fulfillment in Christ and unity by faith.

Issue

May imply God abandoned Israel (contrary to ). Over-spiritualizes NT eschatology, potentially downplaying literal future resurrection/reign for all believers ().

View 6

Mid-Acts Dispensationalism

✦ ✦ ✦
Mid-Acts Dispensationalism

Mid-Acts Dispensationalism

Teaches

Church begins mid-Acts (Acts 9/13) with Paul, not Pentecost. Early Acts under "Kingdom gospel" for Jews (baptism, signs). Paul's gospel distinct for Gentiles. Twists "rightly dividing" () to claim two gospels. Adds works subtly to both. False notion Paul's gospel different from Abraham's (), Jesus'/Apostles' (; ). Rejects baptism/Lord's Supper for Church today.

Who generally holds this view

Mid-Acts "grace movement," Berean Bible Society, teachers like Cornelius Stam, Charles Baker. Less common in mainstream IFB.

Strength

Emphasizes grace alone and Paul's apostleship.

Issue

Multiple gospels accursed (). Adds works subtly. Creates unbiblical divisions, diminishing unity from Pentecost ().

View 7

Hyper-Dispensationalism

✦ ✦ ✦
Hyper-Dispensationalism

Hyper-Dispensationalism

Teaches

Extreme Mid-Acts: Church begins post-Acts 28 or Paul's prison epistles. Rejects most NT for Church (Gospels, early Acts for Israel). Multiple gospels (Peter's, Paul's, others). Rejects baptism/Lord's Supper today. Some claim KJV advanced revelation corrects originals (Ruckman). Outlandish claims (millennium for physical Jews only, temple sacrifices necessary, OT saved by works+faith, Antichrist giant black alien in spaceship, believers transformed into 33-yo males, new Adams/Eves for alien planets).

Who generally holds this view

Extreme KJV-only/hyper groups, Ruckman followers, Gene Kim/Bibleline Ministries, fringe online "grace" circles. Rejected by mainstream IFB/evangelicals.

Strength

Attempts to emphasize Paul's ministry (severely distorted).

Issue

Multiple gospels accursed (). False distinctions from Abraham/Jesus/Apostles. Adds works/extreme divisions. Even further off rails: cultic/racial views (Christian Identity, Black Hebrew Israelites, etc.) contradict gospel to all nations (; ).
