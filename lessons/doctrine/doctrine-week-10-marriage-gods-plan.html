I.

In the Beginning

God, having created all things — light, day and night, the heavens and the earth, dividing the waters from the land, the creatures and their habitations — made man from the dust of the earth. Genesis 1 gives us the overview of creation; Genesis 2 slows down to detail the creation of man and woman.

From the beginning, having created man in Their image — a picture of Christ, through whom all things were made — God created woman from man as a help meet: a helper for all things he might do. Because of this, a man leaves his father and mother and cleaves unto his wife, that they be one flesh. This fulfills the command of the Lord: be fruitful and multiply. Replenish the earth, meaning to fill it. Subdue the earth, exercising dominion and stewardship of creation. This was the purpose of mankind, in communion with the Lord.

And God saw His creation, that it was good, and rested from His works. But it wasn't to last — Genesis 3.
II.

God's Design for Marriage

One man, one woman, one flesh — a lifelong covenant with a purpose bigger than the two people in it.

III.

God's View of Divorce

The LORD hates putting away — but Scripture also shows how He deals with the hardness of men's hearts.

Jesus' Full Answer

What God Hath Joined Together

The Pharisees came tempting Jesus, asking whether it was lawful to put away a wife "for every cause." Jesus took them back past Moses, past the Law, all the way to Eden — to what marriage was always meant to be.

"Wherefore they are no more twain, but one flesh. What therefore God hath joined together, let not man put asunder."

— Jesus, Matthew 19:6

He saith unto them, Moses because of the hardness of your hearts suffered you to put away your wives: but from the beginning it was not so. And I say unto you, Whosoever shall put away his wife, except it be for fornication, and shall marry another, committeth adultery.

IV.

Adultery Under the Law

The Law demanded death for both parties, without exception.

Grace That Upholds the Law

The Woman Taken in Adultery

The scribes and Pharisees brought a woman caught in the very act, testing Jesus: Moses commanded she be stoned — what sayest thou? Jesus stooped down and wrote on the ground.

"He that is without sin among you, let him first cast a stone at her... Neither do I condemn thee: go, and sin no more."

— Jesus, John 8:7, 11

The Law demanded death for both parties — yet only the woman was brought. Jesus did not abolish the Law; He exposed the accusers' own guilt. His verdict was not cheap grace — "go, and sin no more" — but mercy that upheld the standard while extending redemption. Grace does not erase the Law; it fulfills it.

V.

The Marriage Supper of the Lamb

Every earthly marriage points forward to this — Christ the Bridegroom, and His Church, the bride, prepared and adorned.
