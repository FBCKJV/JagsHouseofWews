In the beginning

God, having created all things (light, day and night, the heavens and the earth, dividing the waters from the land, the creatures and their habitations) made man from the dust of the earth. Genesis 1 gives us the overview of creation; Genesis 2 slows down to detail the creation of man and woman.

An help meet for him

An help meet for him

Bone of my bones, flesh of my flesh

Woman was taken out of Man, and so a man leaves father and mother to become one flesh with his wife.

The purpose of mankind

From the beginning, having created man in Their image, a picture of Christ, through whom all things were made, God created woman from man as a help meet: a helper for all things he might do. Because of this, a man leaves his father and mother and cleaves unto his wife, that they be one flesh.

This fulfills the command of the Lord: be fruitful and multiply. Replenish the earth, meaning to fill it. Subdue the earth, exercising dominion and stewardship of creation. This was the purpose of mankind, in communion with the Lord.

And God saw His creation, that it was good, and rested from His works. But it wasn’t to last: Genesis 3.

Without sin

She said, No man, Lord. And Jesus said unto her, Neither do I condemn thee: go, and sin no more.John 8:11

The standard upheld

The Law demanded death for both parties, yet only the woman was brought. Jesus did not abolish the Law; He exposed the accusers’ own guilt. His verdict was not cheap grace, “go, and sin no more,” but mercy that upheld the standard while extending redemption. Grace does not erase the Law; it fulfills it.

Marriage: from Eden to eternity

The Bible opens with a wedding: one man, one woman, one flesh in the garden of God. The Bible closes with a wedding: the Lamb and His bride, the redeemed of all ages, clothed in white, gathered at the marriage supper of the Lamb.

Every earthly marriage is a shadow of that eternal union. Christ is the Bridegroom. The Church is His bride. He gave Himself for her, He is coming for her, and He will present her to Himself: holy, without blemish, in fine linen, clean and white.

This is a great mystery: but I speak concerning Christ and the church.Ephesians 5:32
