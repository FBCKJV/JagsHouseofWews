What wine means

Juice of the vine, fresh or fermented

Wine in the Bible means all juices, fresh or fermented. Most places in the Bible it means freshly squeezed or preserved fruit juice. Some places it means fermented.

“They didn't know how to preserve juice until Louis Pasteur!” That's an ignorant lie. They did, with multiple methods, including sulphur, boiling, wax capped bottles.

New wine, fresh juice, put into an old, dried out and worn cask, as it begins to ferment will give off gasses, causing the bottle to be ruined and the wine to spill out ().

What wine means

Wines on the lees well refined

And in this mountain shall the Lord of hosts make unto all people a feast of fat things, a feast of wines on the lees, of fat things full of marrow, of wines on the lees well refined.Isaiah 25:6

The Hebrew word shemer refers to the dregs or lees, and it generally is used in a negative context: Moab hath settled on his lees (). The refining, the filtering, is the difference. Wines filtered from their lees before fermentation, and frequently refined, were significantly weaker and the alcohol content negligible.

Wine is rendered feeble in strength when it is frequently filtered. The strength or spirit thus being excluded, the wine neither inflames the brain nor infests the mind and passions, and is much more pleasant to drink.Plutarch (46–120 A.D.)

Keeping the juice sweet

Sweet juice all year

In biblical and ancient usage, “wine” (yayin, oinos, vinum, mustum) commonly referred to the juice of the grape in any state: freshly squeezed, preserved unfermented, or fermented.

  • Fresh, sweet grape juice was regarded as a gift and was regularly available year-round, because ancient people possessed reliable methods to prevent or minimize fermentation.
  • Alcoholic fermentation was not inevitable or universal. Multiple practical techniques kept juice sweet and non-intoxicating, or only weakly alcoholic when diluted.
  • Drunkenness is a sin. Jesus did not produce or serve intoxicating wine that would make people drunk.
Keeping the juice sweet

How they kept it from fermenting

MethodHow it was done
SulfurBurning sulfur inside jars, so the fumes kill wild yeasts before the vessel is sealed.
Boiling downFresh must boiled to a half, a third or less, a thick concentrate (defrutum, sapa) that resists fermentation, later reconstituted with water.
Sealing and coldJars coated and stopped with pitch or beeswax, sunk in a cold pond or wet sand: sweet a year or longer (“semper mustum”).
Removing the skinsYeast lives primarily on the skins. Filtering them out before fermentation can begin.
DilutionAny lightly fermented wine mixed with water, essentially non-alcoholic.
Cool storageCool caves, underground rooms or buried vessels, to slow or prevent fermentation.

Not obscure experiments: described in the agricultural treatises of Cato and others.

Keeping the juice sweet

What the ancient writers said

Cato the Elder, On Agriculture 120

Explicitly instructs how to keep grape juice sweet the entire year: put the must in an amphora, seal the stopper with pitch, and sink it in a pond. After thirty days it is removed and remains sweet for a full year.

Josephus, Antiquities 2

Distinguishes fresh, sweet juice from ordinary wine. In his retelling of the chief butler's dream, he uses gleukos, the standard Greek term for fresh, sweet, unfermented grape juice, for the juice squeezed from the clusters into Pharaoh's cup, rather than the more general term oinos.

And Pharaoh’s cup was in my hand: and I took the grapes, and pressed them into Pharaoh’s cup, and I gave the cup into Pharaoh’s hand.Genesis 40:11

Not a nineteenth-century invention

The lie that juice wasn't invented until the 1800s by Welch's is a foolish cope.

The custom of the time of Christ was to abstain from consuming fermented wine. Jews were expected to not drink alcohol. Especially in the Passover, where all leaven, including the yeasts that ferment juice, were forbidden.

Wisdom's wine is mingled: the fruit of the vine mixed with water, spiced with myrrh, sweetened with honey ( · ). And at Cana, thou hast kept the good wine until now (): the fruit of the true vine, under a better testament.

Jesus didn't get people inebriated at a wedding. He didn't use booze as a memorial of His blood given in a new testament.

Not for kings, nor for priests

It is not for kings

It is not for kings, O Lemuel, it is not for kings to drink wine; nor for princes strong drink: Lest they drink, and forget the law, and pervert the judgment of any of the afflicted. Give strong drink unto him that is ready to perish, and wine unto those that be of heavy hearts. Let him drink, and forget his poverty, and remember his misery no more.Proverbs 31:4-7

This should be the end of the matter for saved believers. We are princes and heirs, co-heirs with Christ. It is not for us to drink wine or strong drink.
