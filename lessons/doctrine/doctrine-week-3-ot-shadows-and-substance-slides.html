The Purpose of Old Testament Practices
& Salvation by Faith

Shadow & Substance — how the Tabernacle, the sacrifices, and the Law all pointed to the one true Savior, while righteousness before God has always come by faith alone.

Imagine a shadow on the ground — it shows the shape of a person but isn't the person. Old Testament practices like the Tabernacle and sacrifices were shadows pointing to Jesus, the real thing.

I.

Introduction

The Tabernacle, Temple, sacrifices, washings, and dietary laws were God's instructions to Israel — but they never saved a soul. Salvation has always been by faith, just as it is for us today in Christ.

II.

Old Testament Practices: Sanctifying the Flesh

Temporary, outward acts that set Israel apart — they cleansed the body, not the soul.

A. What Were These Practices?

Tabernacle & Temple — mobile, then permanent structures where God's presence dwelt. They marked Israel as God's people, but did not save their souls. The ritual restored ceremonial cleanliness for approaching God's presence — it did not justify the soul, which required faith.

Sacrifices — animal offerings, like on the Day of Atonement, cleaned the outside but couldn't fix the heart forever. Their yearly repetition underscores a temporary, external effect.

Washings & Dietary Laws — ceremonial cleansings and food rules kept Israel distinct, addressing sources of ritual uncleanness. These were about physical purity and obedience, not eternal salvation.

B. Why Did God Require Them?

To make Israel holy — set apart — among pagan nations, and to point to a greater sacrifice: Jesus. These rituals were a "picture" of the spiritual cleansing and atonement Christ would accomplish.

C. Limitation of These Practices

They couldn't permanently remove sin or save the soul — a temporary fix, like a bandage, not a cure.

These rituals were like a picture book pointing to Jesus, the real Savior.
III.

Salvation by Faith: The Eternal Truth

Salvation has always been by faith, not works — Christ fulfills and surpasses every Old Testament ritual.

A. Faith in the Old Testament

Before the Law, salvation was by faith. While the sacrificial system dealt with ceremonial cleanliness, righteousness before God was consistently tied to faith in the Lord, not the performance of rituals.

B. Christ Fulfills the Law

Jesus' sacrifice replaces and perfects all Old Testament rituals — accomplishing both sanctification and the forgiveness of sins, resolving what the OT system never fully could.

C. Salvation by Faith Today

We are saved by trusting in Christ, not by doing rituals or works.

Old Testament rituals were like a map leading to Jesus, the destination.
IV.

The New Heaven and New Earth: No Rituals Needed

The temporary nature of Old Testament practices gives way to the eternal reality in Christ.

A. God's Presence Replaces the Temple

B. Eternal Sanctification

Christ's work means we're fully sanctified by faith, not by repeated washings or sacrifices. The Old Testament pointed to this future, where God's presence is enough.

The Tabernacle was like a tent for God's presence; now, Jesus Himself is our home.
V.

Application

Key takeaways for young believers.

Fleshly Sanctification

Sacrifices and washings restored ritual purity to allow access to the tabernacle. They addressed external states but could not cleanse the conscience or remove sin's guilt.

Spiritual Sanctification

Righteousness before God came through faith. Sacrifices were never a substitute for faith, but a means to maintain covenantal relationship in the physical sphere.

Christ as Fulfillment

Christ's sacrifice transcends the OT system, purifying both flesh and spirit. His blood cleanses the conscience and grants eternal redemption — what the rituals could only symbolize.

Practical Application: Encourage trust in Christ's finished work, not in personal efforts to earn salvation. Read Hebrews 9–10 at home to see how Jesus fulfills the Law.

You're saved by faith, and Jesus has already made you holy in God's eyes. Rest in that!
VI.

Conclusion

The Tabernacle, Temple, sacrifices, washings, and dietary laws were God's way to teach Israel about holiness and point to Jesus. Salvation has always been by faith, and Christ's sacrifice makes us holy forever — as we'll see in Heaven, with no need for a Temple.
