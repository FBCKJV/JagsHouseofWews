Repentance Is a Change of Mind

Repentance means a change of heart, to believe something that we hadn’t before.

The doctrine

Repent and turn to God

But shewed first unto them of Damascus, and at Jerusalem, and throughout all the coasts of Judaea, and then to the Gentiles, that they should repent and turn to God, and do works meet for repentance.Acts 26:20

We must turn from false beliefs regarding righteousness, and turn to believing God’s report, obeying the Gospel, by trusting in Jesus Christ alone as the sole propitiation for our sins. This is what the Bible says repent means.

It is turning your trust from false beliefs, your own works, false gods and idols, and towards God. And once saved doing works suitable and proper for such a turning.

Metanoia

Metanoia, the Greek word translated in our King James Bible as “repentance,” is a changing of the mind.

“Meta” comes from the root for “after” or “beyond,” while “noeo” is “to think.” So metanoeo means to reconsider or to rethink something. At its root, repentance is about changing one’s mind.

That’s why in scripture, again and again, repentance and belief are used in tandem to express conversion, by believing on Jesus and receiving the gift of eternal life promised by God.

Repentance is a changing of mind or turning from one thing to another.

God repented

And God saw their works, that they turned from their evil way; and God repented of the evil, that he had said that he would do unto them; and he did it not.Jonah 3:10

Here we get the biblical definition of repent, God changing His mind, AND the acknowledgement that “turning from their evil ways” ie “Repenting of sin” = works.

So in one verse you have evidence that repenting of sin is a work, and that repentance does not always mean of sin, because God does it.

No one would say God repented of his sin because God is sinless. The Bible defines itself.

Acts 11

Repentance unto life

When they heard these things, they held their peace, and glorified God, saying, Then hath God also to the Gentiles granted repentance unto life.Acts 11:18
And the hand of the Lord was with them: and a great number believed, and turned unto the Lord.Acts 11:21

Note that verse 18 and 21, both quoted above, state similar things. 18 using “repentance,” 21 “turned unto.”

They have identical meaning. There is no implied “...from sin.” It is Biblical repentance, turning to the Lord.

Matthew 21

That ye might believe him

For John came unto you in the way of righteousness, and ye believed him not: but the publicans and the harlots believed him: and ye, when ye had seen it, repented not afterward, that ye might believe him.Matthew 21:32

A certain man with two sons, orders them to work his vineyard. One refuses, but changes his mind and goes to work.

We see again, Biblical repentance is a turning towards belief in what God tells us is His will. Faith in His word.

Behave, or believe?

From that time Jesus began to preach, and to say, Repent: for the kingdom of heaven is at hand.Matthew 4:17

“Behave”

Would He be telling Jews to clean up their acts …

“Believe”

… or to believe they need salvation, and that that salvation, promised by God, was at hand?

John the Baptist is saying “Reconsider what you know and believe me.” He is not saying “get your life together and stop sinning, the Kingdom of Heaven is here.”

We go to the doctor to be healed

And Jesus answering said unto them, They that are whole need not a physician; but they that are sick. I came not to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance.Luke 5:31-32

When Jesus says to repent, He isn’t talking about cleaning up your life, and trying to live more in line with the law. That’s the false religion of the Pharisee that He repeatedly condemns.

He calls sinners to repentance, towards God. The remission of sins that is the result of repentance unto faith. Then the healing of the body. Then the turning from sin.

We go to the doctor to be healed. We don’t get healed to see the doctor.

I changed my mind

I didn’t repent of sin to be saved because I’ve never thought sin was good or right. I’ve never changed my mind about that, I’ve always known it’s wrong.

But thank God and praise Jesus, I changed my mind about what I needed to do to be saved, and believed on the blood of the Lamb that washes away sins, and the gift of eternal life. That’s real repentance in order to be born again.

What people most often repent of are the false beliefs they have about God. About His judgment. About their own righteousness or how they can escape punishment for their sins.

“Oh I thought idols would save me.
“I thought the law would save me.
“I thought maybe hell wasn’t real.
“I didn’t know my works weren’t enough.

Turning from sin is a work

The false gospel

If you are trusting in your ability to turn over a new leaf, to be reformed from sin, even your subjective “willingness” to “repent of sin”, or anything else that you might do in order to be saved, other than believe, you’re perishing.

The Bible

The phrase “repent of sin” doesn’t appear in scripture, nor does any similar implied doctrine regarding salvation. What we see over and over is belief, faith, that saved.

Everyone turns from sin at various points in their lives. None of that saves. Only faith in the risen Christ and belief in the promise of God that Jesus is the sole propitiation for all sin can save us.

The verses in context

It’s easy to understand, by looking at verses commonly cited as supporting the false damnable heresy of repentance of sin for salvation, to see precisely what is being repented of in each.

  • Nation turning from sin. Not about salvation.
  • Directed at the nation of Israel which had put their faith in idols.
  • They are as sinful as those they are judging; repent of that misunderstanding.
  • Repent of being ignorant of who Christ was that they and their leaders had killed.
  • Repent of the ignorance God had winked at: worshipping false gods.
  • The unbelieving didn’t repent of their unbelief, to be saved physically from the wrath.
  • Already saved church members, turn from being slack in doing the first works.
Acts 2

Repent, and be baptized

Therefore let all the house of Israel know assuredly, that God hath made that same Jesus, whom ye have crucified, both Lord and Christ. Now when they heard this, they were pricked in their heart, and said unto Peter and to the rest of the apostles, Men and brethren, what shall we do? Then Peter said unto them, Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and ye shall receive the gift of the Holy Ghost.Acts 2:36-38

Note the order of things. Peter gives them the Gospel. They ask what they now must do. Peter tells them repent, or change their mind. Not “of sin” but of what they, in ignorance and unbelief did, participating in His death. That they believe. Then they should be baptized in Jesus’ name.

They that received the good news and believed were then baptized. (Again note the order of operations.)

Judas repented

Judas repented himself

Then Judas, which had betrayed him, when he saw that he was condemned, repented himself, and brought again the thirty pieces of silver to the chief priests and elders, Saying, I have sinned in that I have betrayed the innocent blood. And they said, What is that to us? see thou to that. And he cast down the pieces of silver in the temple, and departed, and went and hanged himself.Matthew 27:3-5

Repenting of a wrong done doesn’t equate with believing on Christ for salvation.

In modern translations, they remove the word “repented” here, and replace it with “felt remorse,” because the idea that Judas repented would conflict with the false teaching of turning from sin to be saved, rather than faith in Christ.

Bad doctrine begets bad doctrine and confusion.

Godly sorrow and saving repentance

It is the conflating of regret/godly sorrow/understanding of our sinful nature and the wages of death earned by it, with the actual change of mind, turning to faith in God, in Christ for our salvation, upon which bad doctrine and damnable heresies are founded.

Recognizing we are sinners

Before we repent and believe, we have to first recognize we are sinners in need of salvation, damned without Christ. This is not the same as repentance, although it could be the reason for repenting.

Godly sorrow

These may work us to the change of heart and towards faith, but they aren’t requirements for salvation, which is to believe on Jesus Christ, that He was raised from the dead.

Luke 15

When he came to himself

And when he came to himself, he said, How many hired servants of my father’s have bread enough and to spare, and I perish with hunger! I will arise and go to my father, and will say unto him, Father, I have sinned against heaven, and before thee, And am no more worthy to be called thy son: make me as one of thy hired servants. And he arose, and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.Luke 15:17-20

It was that turning towards the Father that shows repentance. Not his sorrow and regret. Not his willingness to work, or wanting to make his father his lord.

That is biblical repentance. It’s all God asks of us. That we turn to Him and allow Him to clothe us in righteousness that we did not earn or expect or deserve.

Believers who repented of a sin

Simon

Repent therefore of this thy wickedness, and pray God, if perhaps the thought of thine heart may be forgiven thee.Acts 8:22

Simon was already saved. He believed on Jesus already. He did something wicked, but his repentance isn’t for righteousness but as a believer who has sinned.

The Corinthians

Now I rejoice, not that ye were made sorry, but that ye sorrowed to repentance: for ye were made sorry after a godly manner, that ye might receive damage by us in nothing.2 Corinthians 7:9

Paul isn’t telling the Corinthians that their turning from the sin he pointed out among them is how they were saved, for they were already.

More saved than others?

Resentment

The promotion of the idea that we must repent of our sins to be saved feeds that resentment, and can make a repentant heart grow bitter over time.

A caste

By placing the emphasis on what was turned from, rather than the testimony of what is turned to, it creates an almost inevitable form of a caste system within a church. Of respecters of persons, who in hypocrisy see themselves as more saved than others.

The answer

We should not glory in the fact that those more sinful than we were saved. We should glory in God that he would save any of us. We were all equally damned. We all had to turn to believe the same thing to gain life.

There are many who are raised Christian, in godly homes, and can’t even tell you when they believed.

After salvation

That discernment, if the new man created at salvation is fed, leads to a change in attitude towards sin. It becomes less desirable.

“Real repentance WILL mean you turn from sin”

Saying “real repentance WILL mean you turn from sin, and WILL walk in the Spirit,” is false. We must pick up our cross daily. It is a choice to engage in sanctification.

Out of love for God

Having been saved, we still should turn from the sin that we did while perishing under the law. But this again isn’t repenting of sin to be saved. It is turning from sin out of love for God.

It is being born again, quickened in spirit, that leads us from sin.

Summary

  1. A change of mindRepentance is a changing of mind or turning from one thing to another.
  2. The Bible defines itselfHere we get the biblical definition of repent, God changing His mind.
  3. Turned unto the LordThey have identical meaning. There is no implied “...from sin.”
  4. What we repent ofWhat people most often repent of are the false beliefs they have about God.
  5. Sorrow may lead thereNot of sin, though godly sorrow for sin may lead us there. It was their faith that saves them.

If you have placed your trust in anything else besides the perfect work of Christ Jesus, this is what you must repent of.
