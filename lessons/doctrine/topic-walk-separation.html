Separated unto God

The walk, part two: separation from the world, and the liberty we have in Christ.

God is a God of separation

God is a God of separation. The Bible has example after example of division, separating one thing from another.

Day from night. The creatures after their own kind. The descendants of Seth, the sons of God that called upon the name of the Lord, and the children of men who did not. Noah and his perfect generations from fallen man. Abraham out of Ur. Jacob and Esau. Out of Egypt and back in the land. The tares from the wheat. Sheep from goats.

But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people; that ye should shew forth the praises of him who hath called you out of darkness into his marvellous light:1 Peter 2:9

Separated unto His gospel

The Christian life is one of separation. Of leaving behind the things of this world, and the wickedness men do, separated unto His gospel, and proclaiming His truth.

Wherefore come out from among them, and be ye separate, saith the Lord, and touch not the unclean thing; and I will receive you,2 Corinthians 6:17

This doesn't mean that if you had or have struggles with sin that you're done, defiled and unusable. It means the more we purify ourselves, the more we walk in the Spirit, the more useful we are as a vessel in His holy service.

Come out from among them

Be ye not unequally yoked

Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers: for what fellowship hath righteousness with unrighteousness? and what communion hath light with darkness? And what concord hath Christ with Belial? or what part hath he that believeth with an infidel? And what agreement hath the temple of God with idols? for ye are the temple of the living God; as God hath said, I will dwell in them, and walk in them; and I will be their God, and they shall be my people.2 Corinthians 6:14-16

Many apply this to marriage and being with an unbelieving spouse, but it's far more broad than that. So many Christians, in desperation and fear, join forces and ally themselves with unbelievers.

How can one set of unbelieving, Christ-hating, worldly materialists help save us from another? They can't. All they can do is lead us to a place that brings offense and blame to our ministry.

Come out from among them

Jehoshaphat yoked with Ahab

How often in our Christian life do we find ourselves in a similar situation? Where maybe we seek peace, or not to offend, or a fruitful partnership with someone who is unsaved, and wicked.

Jehoshaphat, wearing his kingly robes at Ahab's suggestion, becomes the target of the Syrians. If not for calling out to the Lord, he would have been slain then and there. The wicked taking advantage of mistaken loyalty to save their own skin is one of the biggest risks of being allied with them.

And Jehu the son of Hanani the seer went out to meet him, and said to king Jehoshaphat, Shouldest thou help the ungodly, and love them that hate the Lord? therefore is wrath upon thee from before the Lord.2 Chronicles 19:2
Come out from among them

Not partakers of other men's sins

Abram refuses spoils from the King of Sodom, not wanting anything from that wicked man. He did what he did because it was right. Staying out of the war, not getting entangled in commitments with the wicked, but defending brethren and having faith in God to destroy our enemies ().

The Bible says that even by his presence and holding the coats of those stoning Stephen to death, Saul was “consenting unto his death” (). Giving even passive consent to sin is a sin.

Lay hands suddenly on no man, neither be partaker of other men’s sins: keep thyself pure.1 Timothy 5:22

Righteous Lot

Pitching his tent toward Sodom

Righteous Lot, saved by faith while in the camp of Abram, who pitched his tent towards Sodom, representing the saved man who is taken in by the world rather than living a life of separation.

Worldly. Carnal. Vexed in the spirit by the filthy conversation around him, but mired in it. So much so his daughters are betrothed to men of the city. His wife, unable to leave sin behind, looks back despite the warning.

And delivered just Lot, vexed with the filthy conversation of the wicked: (For that righteous man dwelling among them, in seeing and hearing, vexed his righteous soul from day to day with their unlawful deeds;)2 Peter 2:7-8

As everyone and everything around us falls into wickedness, we cling to the worldly and the profane, despite knowing better. Despite having the Spirit within us.

Righteous Lot

Lusting after Egypt

They and the mixed multitudes among them begin to lust. To look back to Egypt as better than their miraculous salvation. To miss the life they had, and resent the heavenly bread which God provided.

The separation and sanctified life of the Christian can often feel like this. Working, reading scripture, praying, church... a routine that sees little variance, and if we aren't careful, can become stale and boring.

When I first read through Exodus and Numbers, I would be angry at the Israelites. Now, as a more mature Christian, I see how my own walk with God has likely angered Him, as I have gone after lusts of the world. Be hungry for the manna.

Righteous Lot

The high places left standing

A little leaven leavens the whole lump. God promises to remove the sin and temptation in our hearts, and yet we allow these temptations to remain among us.

The Jebusites were never fully driven out, and acted as a canker (). The kings of Judah would break down the house of Baal, but they never turn to the high places. We should seek to have a heart like David's, and strive to remove the high places in our hearts.

That which is holy doesn't make that which is not holy clean, and that which is unclean makes even that which is holy unclean. A little dirt in water makes all the water dirty ().

The spirit of the command

Joshua's silence

Joshua not only conveyed the word of God, but cautioned the people to be silent, lest they feel the need to shout at another time, and in doing so disobey the word of God ().

This is a key point of separation. Taking not just the letter of the commandments and statutes God has provided us, but honoring the Spirit of it and not leaving ourselves open to temptation. Some professing Christians call this “legalism.” But Joshua was safeguarding them against temptation.

The spirit of the command

All things are not expedient

All things are lawful unto me, but all things are not expedient: all things are lawful for me, but I will not be brought under the power of any.1 Corinthians 6:12

We are at liberty in Christ. But not all things edify. Not all things bring a good report of the gospel to those outside the faith. Not all things serve to keep us from backsliding into sin.

Sobriety. Modest and gender appropriate dress. Separation from heathen practices, such as cuttings of the flesh for the dead and the marks of the pagans. These are examples of being careful in applying the spirit of the commandments of God. To be a good witness. To avoid our own temptation.

The spirit of the command

Constant vigilance

The priests are instructed to change their clothes to even approach the holy chambers (). Spiritually, as a holy priesthood, we separate ourselves unto the Lord. To keep our inner chambers, our heart and mind, pure of the profane.

Nehemiah is gone a short time and the city of Jerusalem has descended into sin. People forget the bondage of their unregenerate life. They quickly forget chastisements. It takes constant vigilance, preaching and hard leadership to maintain even a bare minimum of standards. But it is a good work.

In the world, not of it

Bloom where the Lord has planted you

Oh that I had in the wilderness a lodging place of wayfaring men; that I might leave my people, and go from them! for they be all adulterers, an assembly of treacherous men.Jeremiah 9:2

This same impulse is given to Christians today. To flee the world, to get to a redoubt in the wilderness. But, like the prophets, and like Christ Himself, we go to Jerusalem. We can't be a light to others if we are hidden in the woods.

And so we are in the world, not of it. We are supposed to bloom where the good Lord has planted us.

In the world, not of it

The world hates an evil report

Joseph brings his father an evil report of his brothers, and they hate him for it ( · ). Much the same way today the world hates a faithful Christian for the reminder of their sin we bring.

For every one that doeth evil hateth the light, neither cometh to the light, lest his deeds should be reproved.John 3:20

In our Christian life, we may experience initial scoffing by our friends, family and neighbors. People who doubt our conversion because they know us. The old man. As Nehemiah did, keep building the wall while the mockers scoff ().

In the world, not of it

Blessed with separation

Almost all of the provocations meant to anger us happen in the realms they control, which are wicked and worldly.

Most of what they “ruin” isn't something we should be consuming or engaged in to begin with. By turning you off or excluding you from the things they control, they are essentially blessing you with separation. God is using these things to bring you out of Sodom.

By subjecting you to their provocations, rather than anger, be glad that you now have reason to leave behind their sin, and better serve God, as a separate people, a holy nation.

In the world, not of it

When family despises your walk

Michal looks upon David, gladly worshipping the Lord, and despises him in her heart (). In a like manner, we may experience this as saved believers, where a family member resents our going to church, reading the Bible, cleaning up our life.

For ye were sometimes darkness, but now are ye light in the Lord: walk as children of light:Ephesians 5:8

Walking in the light, where the sins of the dark are made manifest. Making the most of our short time here, in these evil days.

Liberty and the weaker brother

Receive the weak in faith

Him that is weak in the faith receive ye, but not to doubtful disputations. For one believeth that he may eat all things: another, who is weak, eateth herbs. Let not him that eateth despise him that eateth not; and let not him which eateth not judge him that eateth: for God hath received him.Romans 14:1-3

Romans 14 is addressing brothers in Christ, weak in the faith they may be, who add as sin things not inherently sinful, to their own judgement in service to God. And it's twisted by some who take the liberty found in Christ as a license to sin.

A person weak in the faith may hold themselves to a higher standard than the Bible calls for, that we being more stable in the faith may see as silly. Paul is warning not to use the occasion to argue with them.

Liberty and the weaker brother

Not at the expense of others

But take heed lest by any means this liberty of yours become a stumblingblock to them that are weak.1 Corinthians 8:9

Knowing it's nothing but meat, and the pagan practice silly, we could in good conscience eat the sacrifice and give all blessing and honor to God. But if that thing done in our knowledge were to cause the weaker man to sin or not come to the faith of the one true God, we shouldn't do it.

When we act agreeable to others to win them to the gospel, that is not the same as accepting wickedness in our presence. Being mature enough in the word to understand the distinction is one of the goals of consuming the word daily.

Liberty and the weaker brother

Who art thou that judgest?

Who art thou that judgest another man’s servant? to his own master he standeth or falleth. Yea, he shall be holden up: for God is able to make him stand. One man esteemeth one day above another: another esteemeth every day alike. Let every man be fully persuaded in his own mind.Romans 14:4-5

And by the same token, those weaker in the faith should learn not to judge another's walk, if it is keeping with the spirit of the law, but maybe not to your liking.

Those who don't strive as you strive need your encouragement. Not your scorn, lest we think better of ourselves than we ought. Save admonishment for actual clear doctrines, not your opinions.

Liberty and the weaker brother

Meat, drink and holy days

Let no man therefore judge you in meat, or in drink, or in respect of an holyday, or of the new moon, or of the sabbath days:Colossians 2:16

The vain philosophy of men, as well as the old testament strictures, have no power over we who are saved by faith in Christ. Voluntary humility, asceticism, the denial of self for the purpose of self-righteousness: Touch not; taste not; handle not.

Jeremiah 10 is about graven images decked with silver and gold, not decorating your house at Christmas. No one can be tricked into sin. If the heart is blessing the day in Christ, then it is of Christ.

Liberty and the weaker brother

From glory to glory

Now the Lord is that Spirit: and where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is liberty. But we all, with open face beholding as in a glass the glory of the Lord, are changed into the same image from glory to glory, even as by the Spirit of the Lord.2 Corinthians 3:17-18

We have the Spirit of the Lord so we are at liberty. We aren't judged by the law. But even so, in liberty, and beholding the glory of God towards us, by walking in that Spirit, we see our own selves transformed by His glory. We are changed by it if we choose to be.
