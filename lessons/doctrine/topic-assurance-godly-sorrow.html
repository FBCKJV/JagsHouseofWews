Godly Sorrow Worketh Repentance

The sorrow of the world, held by Cain, and the godly sorrow, expressed by David.

✦ ✦ ✦

The general rule

For godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation not to be repented of: but the sorrow of the world worketh death.2 Corinthians 7:10

Note in verse 10, Paul is giving us a general rule, that godly sorrow, that is sincerely being sorry at a wrong you've done, leads many to the repentance of belief that is salvation, while the sorrow of this world to death.

A change of mind

Repent means to change your mind, to accept new information as true and have a change of heart, a conversion.

What people most often repent of are the false beliefs they have about God. About His judgment. About their own righteousness or how they can escape punishment for their sins.

  • “Oh I thought idols would save me.”
  • “I thought the law would save me.”
  • “I thought maybe hell wasn't real.”
  • “I didn't know my works weren't enough.”
Matthew 3

Repent ye

And saying, Repent ye: for the kingdom of heaven is at hand.Matthew 3:2

Not this

He is not saying “get your life together and stop sinning, the Kingdom of Heaven is here.” False teachers have perverted “repent” into penance.

But this

Here, John the Baptist is saying “Reconsider what you know and believe me.” They were baptized in the water confessing their sins, because they believed him, and were saved.

Cain and David

Cain

And Cain said unto the Lord, My punishment is greater than I can bear.Genesis 4:13

There was no remorse for what he had done, just sorrow at the consequence.

David

There is no soundness in my flesh because of thine anger; neither is there any rest in my bones because of my sin.Psalm 38:3

David's sorrow is godly, he cannot bear his own iniquity and hates his sin that brought him low, rather than resenting the punishment he'd received.

The burden Cain couldn't bear was the punishment, but he sorrowed not unto repentance. He never changed his mind about what he had done.

The sorrow of the world

The sorrow of the world

Being sorry you were caught, sorry you're in trouble, sorry you're paying a price for it, sorry that God has chastised you, this is worldly sorrow, and in it is death, because it isn't genuine, it's selfish.

Godly sorrow

Godly sorrow, truly understanding the harm done to others or to God by our transgressions, can lead to repentance towards salvation, a change of mind that cannot be repented of.

Faith in Christ, that cannot be lost once gained. Once saved, always saved.

Those who had already believed

For though I made you sorry with a letter, I do not repent, though I did repent: for I perceive that the same epistle hath made you sorry, though it were but for a season. Now I rejoice, not that ye were made sorry, but that ye sorrowed to repentance: for ye were made sorry after a godly manner, that ye might receive damage by us in nothing.2 Corinthians 7:8-9

Is there a biblical example of repentance from sin? Yes. But it's not regarding getting saved (not directly), but of those who had already believed getting right.

So Paul isn't telling the Corinthians that their turning from the sin he pointed out among them is how they were saved, for they were already, but makes this comment as a part of a larger point, regarding their acceptance of his rebuke.

Sorrow is not itself repentance

This sincere sorrow for sin is not itself repentance, but rather, repentance is the work of that sorrow.

Amen, this is the case of many, brought to repent unto Christ for the forgiveness of sins, and relief from the godly sorrow and guilt weighing their conscience, lifted up by the Holy Spirit and the surety of the promise.

But again, that's not the only thing that worketh repentance. Our caring and meekness, our ready answer for our hope, our chaste conversation, our example, the light of Jesus Christ shining through us, bring many to repentance as well.

As many receive it in tears, many still receive the truth in joy.

What carefulness it wrought

For behold this selfsame thing, that ye sorrowed after a godly sort, what carefulness it wrought in you, yea, what clearing of yourselves, yea, what indignation, yea, what fear, yea, what vehement desire, yea, what zeal, yea, what revenge! In all things ye have approved yourselves to be clear in this matter.2 Corinthians 7:11

This is a helpful list, both for those of us seeking to get right with God regarding sin in our life, and how we should act, and for judging rightly the sincerity, be it godly or earthly sorrow, of those who have wronged us and are seeking forgiveness and claim to be making amends.

The fruits of godly sorrow

  • Carefulness. An understanding of what it is you've done, how it affects others, the ripple effects and implications for the harm you've caused, and the things that lead you to the sin. Not giving occasion to the flesh.
  • Clearing. To make yourself as blameless as possible going forward, in careful consideration.
  • Indignation. You should be indignant at what you've done. Outraged at yourself.
  • Fear. The fear of the chastisement from God, and the natural repercussions of their actions.
  • Vehement desire, zeal, revenge. Making an honest retribution for the harm caused, not begrudgingly, but with zeal.

Now note again, these all are the fruits of godly sorrow, just as repentance is the coming to understanding, in this case through that sorrow. They are not the same thing.

The peaceable fruit

Now no chastening for the present seemeth to be joyous, but grievous: nevertheless afterward it yieldeth the peaceable fruit of righteousness unto them which are exercised thereby.Hebrews 12:11

If that new man isn't fed, and they continue walking in the flesh, they are still saved by the knowledge of the truth, but that discernment is dulled and God may have to chasten the person to work godly sorrow in their heart.

He came to himself

And when he came to himself, he said, How many hired servants of my father’s have bread enough and to spare, and I perish with hunger! I will arise and go to my father, and will say unto him, Father, I have sinned against heaven, and before thee,Luke 15:17-18

When the son came to himself, is the moment of repentance.

It was that turning towards the Father that shows repentance. Not his sorrow and regret. Not his willingness to work, or wanting to make his father his lord.

He doesn't want you to be sorry, or need you to be sorrowful, the Lord just wants you to admit it's wrong and stop it.

John 8

Go, and sin no more

She said, No man, Lord. And Jesus said unto her, Neither do I condemn thee: go, and sin no more.John 8:11

Their real sin was their own adultery against God. The hypocrisy wasn't so much in their judgement of the woman, but in their unfaithfulness to the Lord.

And then Jesus, having shown them their own adulterous hearts, tells the woman to go and sin no more. Which spiritually, in that context of whoredoms against God, is a call to repentance towards Christ. (not a call to sinless perfection and works salvation)
