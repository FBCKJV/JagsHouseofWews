The “Rightly Dividing” Framework

This document presents a framework for reconciling apparent contradictions between Old Testament prophecies and their New Testament fulfillments.

The core principle is that many Old Testament prophecies were conditional, dependent on Israel’s obedience and repentance. When these conditions were unmet, God’s plan shifted, and the New Testament presents a reconfigured, often spiritual, fulfillment of those promises.

This framework views the Old Testament prophecies as “what could have been” and the New Testament narratives as “what will be” under the new covenant, explaining the differences in timing, scope, and detail between the two testaments.

From Conditional to Unconditional

The shift from Old Testament’s conditional, national promises to the New Testament’s unconditional, spiritual realities is best understood through a historical timeline of Israel’s progressive rejection of the “kingdom offer.”

The framework also distinguishes between individual judgment by faith and national judgment by works, emphasizing God’s longsuffering and the free will He offers between life and death.

God’s foreknowledge allows Him to offer conditional promises while respecting human choices, adapting His plan and redirecting blessings to a “new nation” of believers when conditions are not met.

A Biblical Timeline of Conditional Promises, Choices, and Changed Outcomes

This section traces a biblically consecutive timeline of conditional promises and their outcomes, from Genesis through Revelation.

✦ ✦ ✦
Genesis

The Foundation of Winnowing

Promise
God promises a great nation and land to Abraham. God also promised that Abraham’s seed, singular, which is Christ Jesus, would be the light of nations. The promise of spiritual salvation is unconditional, only faith is required. But the physical promise and the land’s immediate possession requires obedience.
Choice
Cain chooses disobedience by offering an improper sacrifice and murdering Abel, leading to his exile and a shift of the godly line to Seth. Abraham’s lack of faith also delays the fulfillment of God’s promise.
Outcome
God’s plan adapts, winnowing the unfaithful while preserving the promised line.
Exodus and Leviticus

National Promises and Priesthood

Promise
God promises to deliver Israel from Egypt and give them Canaan, on the condition of covenantal obedience. The priesthood is also conditionally offered based on faithfulness.
Choice
Pharaoh’s defiance leads to judgment on Egypt. Israel’s murmuring and Moses’ disobedience in striking the rock forfeit his entry into Canaan. Israel’s idolatry with the golden calf leads to the Levites being chosen for the priesthood due to their loyalty.
Outcome
The land promise is delayed. The priesthood shifts from the firstborn to the Levites.
Numbers and Deuteronomy

Forfeiture of the Promised Land

Promise
God reaffirms the land promise, contingent on Israel’s trust and obedience. God outlines blessings for obedience and curses for disobedience.
Choice
The Kadeshbarnea rebellion, spurred by the spies’ unbelief, causes God to declare that the rebellious generation will not see the promised land. Korah’s rebellion also leads to his destruction.
Outcome
The land promise is delayed for forty years, and that generation dies in the wilderness. The promise persists for future generations, but national disobedience ultimately leads to exile.
Joshua to 1 Kings

Fulfillment, Disobedience, and Forfeited Dynasties

Promise
The land promise is fulfilled under Joshua, but continued possession requires obedience. God promises Saul a lasting dynasty, contingent on his obedience. God also promises Jeroboam a sure dynasty like David’s, on the condition of obedience.
Choice
Achan’s sin brings defeat at Ai, and Israel’s failure to fully expel the Canaanites leads to future idolatry. Saul disobeys God’s commands, leading to his rejection as king and the forfeiture of his lineage in the Messiah’s line. Jeroboam’s idolatry leads to the destruction of his house.
Outcome
The fulfilled land promise under Joshua is annulled by the curses God laid out for their disobedience. The Messianic lineage shifts to David from Saul. The promise to Jeroboam’s line is gone, because he rejected the faith of his fathers for a religion of idols that he invented, leading to the destruction of the 10 northern tribes.
The Prophets

Reprobate Silver and Conditional Eschatology

Promise
God promises judgment on nations and conditional restoration for Israel. He promises restoration if Judah repents. Ezekiel 40-48 promises a restored temple and land, contingent on Israel’s repentance and shame for their iniquities. Zechariah promises a kingdom “if ye will diligently obey” ().
Choice
Babylon’s pride leads to its destruction. Judah’s persistent idolatry renders them “reprobate silver” (), and they are rejected. Israel’s failure to repent forfeits Ezekiel’s vision, rendering it “what could have been.” Their failure to obey Zechariah’s commands forfeits the promises.
Outcome
God’s mercy offers a remnant a return from exile. The promise of a restored kingdom and temple is forfeited, and the blessings redirect to the “new nation” of believers.
The Prophets

Hope

There is always an offer to repentance. The natural branches can be grafted back in.

Old Testament believers are joint-heirs with Christ and will see their Kingdom in the Millennium, ruling and reigning from Jerusalem with Jesus, just as the believing Gentiles, the wild branches grafted into the Vine will rule with Him throughout the nations of men.

The promise to Abraham and to David will be fulfilled.

The Kingdom Offer Timeline

The Road to Final Rejection

1
The Opening of the Kingdom Offer. John the Baptist and Jesus both preached, “Repent ye: for the kingdom of heaven is at hand” (; ). This was a literal offer to Israel, confirmed by Jesus’ miracles and a message exclusively to “the lost sheep of the house of Israel” ().
2
The Rise of National Rejection. The Pharisees committed the “blasphemy against the Holy Ghost” () by attributing Jesus’ power to Satan. Jesus’ subsequent teaching in parables symbolized the concealment of truth from those with hardened hearts.
3
The Official Rejection. The ultimate rejection occurred at the Cross, where the nation’s leaders and people rejected Jesus as their King and chose a murderer instead.
4
The Final Offer and “The Last Straw.” After the cross, God extended one final chance, with Peter promising that Jesus would return if the nation repented. The definitive “last straw” was the martyrdom of Stephen in . His sermon exposed their complete rejection, and their act of stoning him confirmed their “no.”
The “What Will Be”

New Testament Fulfillment and Resolution

After Israel’s final rejection, the kingdom offer to the nation was withdrawn, and the establishment of the earthly kingdom was delayed until Christ’s Second Coming.

God’s plan shifted, and the prophecies were fulfilled in a reconfigured, spiritual way under the new covenant. The focus shifted to a universal, spiritual kingdom for all believers.

Reconfigured fulfillment

Gog and Magog, and the Temple

Ezekiel’s Gog and Magog vs. Revelation’s Gog and Magog

Ezekiel’s conditional prophecy, tied to a national victory for Israel, is reconfigured in as a global, spiritual conflict for the “camp of the saints” that occurs after the millennial reign of Christ.

Ezekiel’s Temple vs. The Spiritual Temple

The conditional promise of Ezekiel’s physical temple is reconfigured in the New Testament. The church is now the temple of the Holy Spirit, and Christ is the cornerstone. The New Jerusalem, which descends from heaven, has no temple, for “the Lord God Almighty and the Lamb are the temple of it” (). This spiritual reality reflects the shift from a conditional promise to a universal, spiritual kingdom.

Reconfigured fulfillment

Zechariah, Isaiah and Joel

Zechariah’s Messianic Kingdom vs. Revelation’s Millennial Kingdom

Zechariah’s physical, earthly kingdom is fulfilled as a spiritual reign with Christ and the saints. Revelation’s New Jerusalem descends from heaven with no physical temple, fulfilling Zechariah’s conditional prophecy in a spiritual manner necessitated by Israel’s rejection.

Isaiah’s Peace vs. Revelation’s Tribulation

Isaiah’s vision of immediate peace is fulfilled in Revelation 6-19 as an intense period of suffering before final peace is established. This shows the suffering that must precede Christ’s spiritual kingdom due to Israel’s failure to bring about immediate peace.

Joel’s Day of the Lord vs. Acts & Revelation

The initial part of Joel’s prophecy, the outpouring of the Spirit on “all flesh” (), was fulfilled in Acts 2, marking the beginning of the new covenant. The latter part of the prophecy, the “Day of the Lord,” is reinterpreted in Revelation as a global judgment on the unrepentant world.

Conclusion

The prophecies are not contradictions but different pictures reflecting a conditional covenant.

Israel’s failure to meet the conditions of the Old Testament covenant, the death, burial and resurrection of Jesus Christ was the blood of a new covenant, the death of the Testator ushering in the New Testament, all culminating in the definitive rejection of the Messiah by the Jews at Stephen’s martyrdom.

This new covenant shifts the focus from a conditional, national, and physical fulfillment to an unconditional, universal, and spiritual one. The New Testament, therefore, presents the ultimate reality of God’s plan, while the Old Testament prophecies reveal the path that could have been.

The fulfilled land promise and redirected blessings to the “new nation” of believers affirm God’s sovereignty and His desire for all to choose life, by believing on Jesus as the Christ, the Son of God, promised by the Father from the foundation of the world.
