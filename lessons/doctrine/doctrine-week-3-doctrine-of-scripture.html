The Doctrine of Scripture

Inspiration · Preservation · Inerrancy — and why all doctrine must come from clear Scripture

All scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness: That the man of God may be perfect, thoroughly furnished unto all good works.

2 Timothy 3:16–17
Inspiration Preservation Inerrancy Clear Scripture Dr. Hyles
I.

Inspiration of Scripture

Scripture is "God-breathed," authored by God through human writers moved by the Holy Spirit, ensuring its divine origin and authority.

II.

Preservation of Scripture

God ensures His Word remains intact and available across generations — never lost or corrupted beyond recovery.

III.

Inerrancy of Scripture

Scripture is without error in all it affirms — it is God's truth, perfectly reliable in every detail.

Implications: Man does not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God. We need — and have — an every-word Bible, sufficient to make us perfect (complete) and thoroughly furnished (everything we need). It is unchanging, settled in Heaven before creation — the rock upon which we build our Christian life. All unsaved men will be judged by His Word.

Faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God. Those who hear the Shepherd's voice and respond to it are responding to these inspired, preserved, and inerrant words — trusting in them. A testament is a testimony: a promise based on the truthfulness of the one making it. Under God's law, at least two witnesses were needed; God gives us three — plus four Gospels, plus hundreds of fulfilled prophecies, "that we may know He is the Lord."

IV.

All Doctrine Must Come From Clear Scripture

Not from traditions handed down verbally, which could change. Not by a council or group of men deciding what is true. Not by "dark sayings" and parables — these can enhance understanding and give context to clear doctrines, but they should never be the sole source of one.

Context matters. When someone posts a verse to claim a doctrine is true, don't just accept their word — check the context and compare it with other Scripture to see if it contradicts clear doctrine. God is not the author of confusion; search the Scriptures to see if it is so. If you are presented with a contradiction, it is not up to you to square it for the person who introduced it — it is on them to.

Watch for verses lifted out of context to build a doctrine:

"Faith without works is dead" — James 2 "Baptism now saves!" — 2 Peter 2 "Make Jesus Lord of your life" "Repent of your sin to be saved"

From the Pulpit — Dr. Jack Hyles on Inspiration

The words of God were given to us by God and not by holy men. They were given to us by God through holy men, using them to get the words to us. Sometimes that pen didn't understand all he wrote... They were just pens; the Penman was doing the writing! God's voice gave His Word through them, not to them.

— Dr. Jack Hyles

If I did not believe that every word in the Bible had been preserved from the time it was given through the mouth of men as God, the Penman, wrote with the pen of humanity, I would walk out of this pulpit, and I wouldn't waste my life preaching. If there's a word in the Bible that is wrong, no one knows which one it is, so it could be any of them. I believe that we have God's preserved words for us in the King James Bible.

How Did We Get God's Words?

  1. The words of God were always in Heaven.
  2. Man must have every word to live.
  3. God had to find a way to get every word to man.
  4. God chose men and made them holy — "set apart" to be God's pens.
  5. God gave His words through those chosen men. "I will not use your brain; I will use your mouth." God's breath across the vocal cords of human beings gave us His words.
  6. God has preserved His words for us in the King James Bible.
