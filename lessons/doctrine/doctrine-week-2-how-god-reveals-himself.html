How God Reveals Himself to Man

General Revelation vs. Specific Revelation — how God speaks through nature, His actions, and His Word

The heavens declare the glory of God; and the firmament sheweth his handywork. Day unto day uttereth speech, and night unto night sheweth knowledge. There is no speech nor language, where their voice is not heard.

Psalm 19:1–4
General & Specific Creation Wrath Mercy The Word
I.

General & Specific Revelation

Have you ever looked at a sunset, or read a Bible verse, and felt God's presence? God's revelation comes in two forms — one available to all, one given through His Word.

General Revelation — Available to All

God revealing Himself through creation and the human conscience, His law written on men's hearts. Universal, non-verbal — it points to God's existence but not to the specifics of salvation. All creation is a parable for us to know God.

What about that guy on that deserted island? What about people who have never heard of Jesus?

Specific Revelation — Through the Word

God revealing Himself through direct means — His Word, the prophets, and Jesus Christ. Detailed and verbal, it reveals God's plan for salvation, His commandments, and prophecy for times past and times to come. Specific Revelation builds on General Revelation, giving clarity so that we might know God's will, collectively and individually.

II.

Revelation Through Creation

Creation — sky, stars, nature, animals — declares God's glory, power, and wisdom, leaving mankind without excuse.

A bird's migration shows God's design. The miracle of birth, the intricacy of DNA — all of nature is a parable pointing back to its Maker.

III.

Revelation Through Wrath

God's wrath is not arbitrary — it is a response to sin, revealing His holiness, His justice, and His moral standard.

Seeing wrath fall on the wicked — even natural consequences of sin — teaches unbelievers that there is a just Creator. Even the secular idea of "karma" points to a moral order only a just God could establish. God judges nations, not only individuals, by their works.

How does knowing God judges fairly affect our view of Him?
Why is it important to teach about God's wrath alongside His love?
IV.

Revelation Through Mercy

Where wrath reveals God's justice, mercy reveals His love — slow to anger, rich in compassion toward the undeserving.

When we show others the mercy and grace God has shown us, it reveals His nature to them. They hear our praise for answered prayer, or see a changed life, and come to a better understanding of His longsuffering — and He receives the glory for loving His people.

Leader: share a brief story of God's mercy — personal, or biblical, such as the Prodigal Son.
V.

Revelation Through the Word

God spoke through the prophets in ages past; now He has spoken through His Son, and given us Scripture, breathed out by His Spirit.

Scripture is "God-breathed," written by men carried along by the Holy Ghost — the prophets were the pen, God was the Author. This is what secures Scripture's trustworthiness as God's Specific Revelation.

Why is it comforting to know Scripture comes from God, and not just human ideas?
How has a Bible verse, or a story about Jesus, helped you know God better?
