A Trail of Blood

A brief history of the Baptists, tracing the continuous lineage of churches that held to core principles of believer’s baptism and biblical authority, often in the face of persecution.

Chronology of a Continuous Faith

33–100 AD: Apostolic Era

The church founded by Jesus Christ, practicing believer's baptism and local church autonomy, spreads through apostolic preaching.

100–313 AD: Post-Apostolic Persecutions

Groups like the Montanists face Roman persecution and opposition from the growing pre-Catholic hierarchy for their adherence to biblical purity.

313–600 AD: Rise of Catholicism

Constantine legalizes Christianity, but persecutes groups like the Donatists who reject infant baptism and church hierarchy. The Catholic Church restricts Scripture to Latin, suppressing vernacular translations.

600–1200 AD: Medieval Dissenters

Groups like the Paulicians and Bogomils maintain believer’s baptism and Scripture-based worship, enduring Catholic crusades and executions. The Church condemns and burns non-Latin Scriptures.

1200–1500 AD: Pre-Reformation Movements

The Waldensians and Albigensians preach biblical simplicity and translate the Bible. The Albigensian Crusade kills hundreds of thousands, and the Waldensians face massacres for rejecting clerical authority.

1500–1600 AD: Reformation and Anabaptists

Anabaptists revive believer’s baptism and face persecution from both Catholics and Protestants. Key events include the executions of Felix Manz and George Blaurock, and the Münster siege. Tyndale's Bible translations are burned.

1600–1700 AD: Baptist Emergence

Early Baptists like John Smyth form congregations, enduring persecution. They face Anglican and Puritan restrictions in England and the American colonies, with leaders like John Clarke and Obadiah Holmes being whipped and imprisoned.

1700–1900 AD: Baptist Growth

Baptists continue to face fines and imprisonment in the American colonies. The Great Awakening and the Revolutionary War lead to the adoption of Baptist principles of religious liberty in the First Amendment, allowing the church to grow.

1900 AD–Present: Modern Baptists

Baptists preserve the apostolic faith through local churches, upholding the King James Bible and the "Old Paths" and "Landmarks" that define their distinctives today.
