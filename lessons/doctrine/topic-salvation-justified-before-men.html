Justified Before Men

We are justified before God by our faith and justified before men by our works.

The doctrine

No contradiction, only context

By comparing scripture with scripture we see that there is no contradiction, only context.

Before God

For if Abraham were justified by works, he hath whereof to glory; but not before God. For what saith the scripture? Abraham believed God, and it was counted unto him for righteousness.Romans 4:2-3

Before men

Yea, a man may say, Thou hast faith, and I have works: shew me thy faith without thy works, and I will shew thee my faith by my works.James 2:18

Paul is writing of justification before God in grace, while James is writing of justification before man, by doing good works in faith.

The Lord looketh on the heart

But the Lord said unto Samuel, Look not on his countenance, or on the height of his stature; because I have refused him: for the Lord seeth not as man seeth; for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the Lord looketh on the heart.1 Samuel 16:7

This is an important doctrine, that reinforces the seeming dichotomy of and that so many struggle with. That we are justified before God by our faith and justified before men by our works. Our appearance. That men are respecters of persons.

Written to the brethren

It is written to the brethren, the author assumes it is to those already saved, to exhort and edify, not to get them to believe.

Not to save them spiritually, but to those already saved, become more complete in their Christian life.

We do not receive doctrine from rhetorical questions or “a man may say…” reasoning, stripped of context. We get them from clear statements that do not contradict others.

Dead faith

What doth it profit, my brethren, though a man say he hath faith, and have not works? can faith save him? If a brother or sister be naked, and destitute of daily food, And one of you say unto them, Depart in peace, be ye warmed and filled; notwithstanding ye give them not those things which are needful to the body; what doth it profit? Even so faith, if it hath not works, is dead, being alone.James 2:14-17

If someone is needy and you basically just say “thoughts and prayers!” it doesn’t profit them anything. It’s useless. Dead. It doesn’t do anyone any good.

Dead faith doesn’t mean it doesn’t exist, but that there’s no life in it. Nothing to indicate it, outwardly. It’s alone, profiting no one else. A dead body exists, but it just lays there.

Doers of the word

The example of this rhetorical question says he has faith. This is important.

The same is the faith of a Christian who doesn’t exercise it to help the fatherless and widows and the poor. See the theme? Understand the larger context?

We aren’t judged by our works and sin, as the debtors are. We are free in Christ. James has already told us that we earn crowns of life for our deeds. But salvation is a gift, unmerited. Grace-mixing isn’t how any of it works.

A man may say

Yea, a man may say, Thou hast faith, and I have works: shew me thy faith without thy works, and I will shew thee my faith by my works. Thou believest that there is one God; thou doest well: the devils also believe, and tremble. But wilt thou know, O vain man, that faith without works is dead?James 2:18-20

A man may say this. What kind of man? Saved? Unsaved? A respecter of persons? Someone who could reasonably or unfairly doubt your profession of faith by your lack of visible works?

It is not directly stated doctrine. A man may say all manner of things. For example, we know devils are not capable of coming to saving belief in Christ. Yet here it says, in this hypothetical man’s argument, that they believe and tremble!

Was not Abraham justified by works?

The rhetorical question

Was not Abraham our father justified by works, when he had offered Isaac his son upon the altar?James 2:21

Not before God

For if Abraham were justified by works, he hath whereof to glory; but not before God. For what saith the scripture? Abraham believed God, and it was counted unto him for righteousness.Romans 4:2-3

The rhetorical question asked by a rhetorical man, “Was not Abraham justified by works?” is answered by the holy Spirit in and . Not before God, or he would have to glory. The boasting wouldn’t be excluded. The law of grace would become one of debt.

Justified before God, by faith alone

Abraham was saved spiritually, justified before God, by his faith alone.

And he believed in the Lord; and he counted it to him for righteousness.Genesis 15:6

Decades before his circumcision. Decades before bringing Isaac to the altar on the same mountain in Jerusalem where Jesus would later be crucified.

Justified before men, by works

Was not Abraham our father justified by works, when he had offered Isaac his son upon the altar? Seest thou how faith wrought with his works, and by works was faith made perfect? And the scripture was fulfilled which saith, Abraham believed God, and it was imputed unto him for righteousness: and he was called the Friend of God.James 2:21-23

But to be justified before man, that outward sign of his faith, made his faith more perfect. There was a visible tangible act of faith, works that a man may say that they’ve been shown his faith.

A man may say that Abraham’s faith was shown in his works and thus he was justified to others. But we know that scripture says it was faith that justified Abraham to God.

The people justified God

And all the people that heard him, and the publicans, justified God, being baptized with the baptism of John. But the Pharisees and lawyers rejected the counsel of God against themselves, being not baptized of him.Luke 7:29-30

Justified is also used in a legal sense, meaning vindication. To show that they believed the report, accepted the witness, agreed with the argument, they were baptized, justifying God.

Baptism itself doesn’t save. It is a manifestation of the faith, the substance of things not seen, that can be seen of men.

Saved by faith, shown by works

We are saved by faith alone. We share that faith by how we live. And by being mindful of that, we get others saved.

We may be justified by the blood of Christ before God, but if we hide that faith under a bushel, or we bury that which we’ve been trusted rather than gain an increase for the Lord, our faith is fruitless. Dead.

The works God brings us to aren’t of ourselves. They’re almost always a blessing for others.

The snare of James 2

Ye are they which justify yourselves before men

And he said unto them, Ye are they which justify yourselves before men; but God knoweth your hearts: for that which is highly esteemed among men is abomination in the sight of God.Luke 16:15

Works for justification

Those that twist it to make works their means of justification before God, damn themselves and put Christ to an open shame, for their dead works are without faith.

The answer

If you are trusting in your own works to get righteous or to maintain the righteousness given by God in Christ, you are damning yourself and being made debtor to the whole of the law which no one can keep perfectly. There are none righteous. Our only righteousness is imputed of Christ.

Repent, and obey the Gospel.
