The Importance of the King James Bible

A Timeless Legacy — how God preserved His Word for the English-speaking world

In 1604, King James I of England commissioned renowned scholars to create a unified English Bible, birthing the KJV in 1611. This masterpiece shaped Christianity worldwide, becoming the spiritual cornerstone for the Pilgrims, fueling Baptist growth in America, and empowering missionaries to spread the Gospel across every continent.

I.

A Timeless Legacy

Fun facts about the King James, or Authorized, Version.

Martyred for the Word

William Tyndale — A Bible Translator Executed for Heresy

William Tyndale was a prominent figure during the Protestant Reformation who was burned at the stake in 1536 — executed for "heresy" by the Roman Catholic Church for translating the Bible into English and promoting its accessibility to the common people.

Why Was Translating the Bible Considered "Heretical"?

Challenging Church Authority: The Catholic Church held absolute authority on religious issues and guarded its power. Translations of the Bible into vernacular languages like English threatened that authority.

Providing the Bible in English allowed literate individuals to read and interpret Scripture for themselves — often arriving at conclusions that differed from unbiblical Catholic doctrine.

"Lord, open the King of England's eyes."

— William Tyndale, before his execution

This prayer was partially answered when King Henry VIII later allowed the English Bible to be available in every parish church.

II.

Translation, Text & Impact

The King James Version was created by a team of 54 scholars, including the greatest linguists of the time, from various backgrounds — ensuring a more comprehensive and accurate translation.

The King James Version has profoundly influenced literature, language, and art — but its powerful, divinely inspired and preserved nature, as the complete Word of God without error in English, the "lingua franca" of this age, has shaped nations, influenced kings and presidents, converted millions, fed the spirit of believers, and served as the rock of Christianity since 1611.

IV.

Precision in Translation

Why the exact words matter — the KJV's careful rendering guards doctrine that looser translations can blur.

"Are Saved" — the Greek Behind Eternal Security

The Greek σῳζομένοις (sōzomenois) is a present passive participle meaning "to those saved." The KJV renders it "are saved" — a settled, present reality accomplished by God. Modern versions often render it "are being saved," an ongoing-process wording that can imply salvation is incomplete or losable.

Thee, Thou, Ye, You — the KJV's Built-In Precision

Modern English collapsed singular and plural "you" into one word. The KJV never did — its pronouns tell you at a glance whether God (or a speaker) is addressing one person or a whole group.
