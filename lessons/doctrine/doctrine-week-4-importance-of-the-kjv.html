The Importance of the King James Bible

A Timeless Legacy: how God preserved His Word for the English-speaking world

✦ ✦ ✦

In 1604, King James I of England commissioned renowned scholars to create a unified English Bible, birthing the KJV in 1611. This masterpiece shaped Christianity worldwide, becoming the spiritual cornerstone for the Pilgrims, fueling Baptist growth in America, and empowering missionaries to spread the Gospel across every continent.

A Timeless Legacy (1 of 2)

Fun facts about the King James, or Authorized, Version.

47 Scholars, One Epic Team

1604 Commission. 54 revisers were initially approved, but 47 brilliant minds crafted the KJV.

Six Predecessors Paved the Way

Earlier English Bibles. Built on Wycliffe’s (1384), Tyndale’s (1525), Coverdale’s (1535), Matthew’s (1537), Great (1539), and Bishops’ (1568) Bibles, the KJV refined them all.

Seven Years of Sweat

1604 to 1611. Scholars toiled to translate from Hebrew, Greek, and Aramaic, ensuring accuracy and poetic flair.

Six Companies, Divide and Conquer

Westminster · Oxford · Cambridge. Split into six groups, two each in Westminster, Oxford, and Cambridge, they tackled different Bible sections for precision.

A Timeless Legacy (2 of 2)

Tyndale’s Ghost Lives On

84% NT · 76% OT. About 84% of the KJV’s New Testament and 76% of its Old Testament echo William Tyndale’s groundbreaking work.

The “He Bible,” 1611

First edition. The first 1611 edition, dubbed the “He Bible,” is estimated at 1,500 to 2,000 copies; the exact number is not known.

No Calvinist Notes Allowed

Royal Approval. King James banned the Geneva Bible’s Puritan notes, ensuring the KJV was a neutral, royal-approved text.

Martyred for the Word

William Tyndale: A Bible Translator Executed for Heresy

William Tyndale was a prominent figure during the Protestant Reformation who was burned at the stake in 1536, executed for “heresy” by the Roman Catholic Church for translating the Bible into English and promoting its accessibility to the common people.

William Tyndale

Why Was Translating the Bible Considered “Heretical”?

Challenging Church Authority: The Catholic Church held absolute authority on religious issues and guarded its power. Translations of the Bible into vernacular languages like English threatened that authority.

Providing the Bible in English allowed literate individuals to read and interpret Scripture for themselves, often arriving at conclusions that differed from unbiblical Catholic doctrine.

“Lord, open the King of England’s eyes.”William Tyndale, before his execution

This prayer was partially answered when King Henry VIII later allowed the English Bible to be available in every parish church.

Translation, Text & Impact

The King James Version was created by a team of 47 scholars (54 were originally appointed), including the greatest linguists of the time, from various backgrounds, ensuring a more comprehensive and accurate translation.

Collaboration

Textual Sources. Translators of the KJV utilized earlier translations and the original preserved manuscripts: the Masoretic Text for the Old Testament, and the Antioch manuscripts, called “The Received Text,” or Textus Receptus, for the New Testament, striving for accuracy while adapting the language to be accessible and beautiful to the English-speaking public.

Literary Style

A Literary Masterpiece. The KJV is renowned for its poetic and rhythmic style, making it not only a religious text but also a literary masterpiece that has influenced English literature significantly.

Cultural Impact

400 Years of Influence. The King James Version has left an indelible mark on Christianity, used for 400 years to win souls on every continent and country, shaping the culture, language, and literature of the world, and the expressions and idioms that remain in common use even today.

Its Powerful, Preserved Nature

The King James Version has profoundly influenced literature, language, and art, but its powerful, divinely inspired and preserved nature, as the complete Word of God without error in English, the “lingua franca” of this age, has shaped nations, influenced kings and presidents, converted millions, fed the spirit of believers, and served as the rock of Christianity since 1611.

Lesser-Known KJV Facts (1 of 2)

Oxford & Cambridge Tweak It

1762 & 1769 Revisions. Revised editions in 1769 (Oxford) and 1762 (Cambridge) standardized spelling, punctuation, and italics for clarity.

Apocrypha? Not Inspired!

1611 Edition. The 1611 KJV included the Apocrypha but clearly marked it as non-inspired, separating it from canonical Scripture.

Sinners’ Bible Blunder

1631 Misprint. A 1631 misprint, dubbed the “Sinners’ Bible,” omitted “not” from , reading “Thou shalt commit adultery”!

Lesser-Known KJV Facts (2 of 2)

Printer’s Pricey Mistake

Robert Barker, 1631. The “Sinners’ Bible” led to a £300 fine for printer Robert Barker, big money in 1631!

Best-Selling Book Ever

Global Reach. The KJV holds the title as the world’s top-selling book, with billions of copies distributed globally.

No Copyright (Outside England)

Crown Copyright. Free to print worldwide, except in England, where the Crown holds rights, fueling its global spread.

“Are Saved”: the Greek Behind Eternal Security

Why the exact words matter: the KJV’s careful rendering guards doctrine that looser translations can blur.

The Greek σῳζομένοις (sōzomenois) is a present passive participle meaning “to those saved.” The KJV renders it “are saved”, a settled, present reality accomplished by God. Modern versions often render it “are being saved,” an ongoing-process wording that can imply salvation is incomplete or losable.

The Power of God to Them That Are Saved

The Power of God to Them That Are Saved

For the preaching of the cross is to them that perish foolishness; but unto us which are saved it is the power of God.1 Corinthians 1:18

Meaning: The word of the cross is the power of God precisely to those who stand in the state of having been saved. God’s effective, completed work secures the believer.

If Ye Keep in Memory

By which also ye are saved, if ye keep in memory what I preached unto you, unless ye have believed in vain.1 Corinthians 15:2

Meaning: The “if” clause is not a condition for retaining salvation but the evidence of genuine faith. True believers hold fast because they are already saved.

Kings, shepherds, prophets, fishermen. Written by over 40 diverse authors, inspired by the Holy Spirit.
