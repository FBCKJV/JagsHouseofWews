What a church is

Church is a called out assembly of believers. The people of God are required to come when called together. This was true in the OT and it is true in the New.

“A church is not a place it's a people!” So true, but the people need a place to gather, and having one fixed location and time makes that possible.

For where two or three are gathered together in my name, there am I in the midst of them. Wonderful promise of the Lord. This presupposes you are actually gathering.

A line of believers stretching back to Adam

I am a saved Christian. Born again by the faith that was shared to me by another saved Christian, who in turn believed and was converted by yet another saved Christian. A line of succession going back to the beginning of the world.

A line of believers stretching back to Adam. To Abel. To Seth and Enos. The same faith of Abraham who believed the Lord and it was counted unto him for righteousness. The same faith that makes up the record of the “Hall of faith,” in Hebrews chapter 11.

From Abel to today

Before Abraham, before the law

The idea of the worship of God originating with Abraham, and only his seed after the flesh, is incorrect. There were many following the flood and the dispersion of nations out from Babel, that called upon the name of the Lord for salvation.

We see the greatest man on Earth at this moment, according to God, Job, who feared Him, eschewing evil, offering sacrifices to sanctify himself and his family. Without the law. Without the temple. Without a priesthood.

Salvation has always been by grace alone through faith in the Lord, calling upon his name. And that salvation was always offered to all people.

The church in the wilderness

The Tabernacle of the congregation

And Moses took the tabernacle, and pitched it without the camp, afar off from the camp, and called it the Tabernacle of the congregation. And it came to pass, that every one which sought the Lord went out unto the tabernacle of the congregation, which was without the camp.Exodus 33:7

The Tabernacle without the camp, the church in the wilderness. This is the representation of the local church, the gathering of the congregation of those seeking the Lord. It represents both spiritual separation, and the physical assembly of believers that is our community church.

This is the pattern God showed Moses, and the pattern He gives us throughout the NT in the epistles. Local, independent churches that have fellowship in the Lord.

Every Levite given his charge

Numbers 4 sees God lay out the calling to each family of the Levites for ministering to the Tabernacle. And each man of each clan given a role, specific to them. With the whole given to the purpose of God.

Just as today, we who are saved are given different ways to be ministers of the faith and to edify the church, carrying that which is holy forth, into the world. Whatever you are called to contribute, do with cheer that the church may grow, and move out into the wilderness bringing others to Christ.

Without the camp

The unclean were put without the camp that their uncleanliness doesn't affect others. Physically they are instructions to keep diseases from spreading. Spiritually, all are a picture of sin, and the exile similar to church discipline, the removal of leaven that can spoil a whole lump.

Even today, in our churches, there are those that give a bad report, who sow discord among the brethren. We are called to a different spirit, like Caleb. Don't be a burden or a stumbling block for others in church. Be a blessing. Seek to build.

Entering into the house of God

As His custom was

And he came to Nazareth, where he had been brought up: and, as his custom was, he went into the synagogue on the sabbath day, and stood up for to read.Luke 4:16

In these times, synagogues were the local churches of those who worshipped God. And Luke tells us that going to these houses of worship was Jesus' custom. It was His routine.

I am sure Jesus Christ Himself sat through boring sermons, by dull preachers who were wrong on doctrine, in congregations of people who were sinners and hypocrites or holier than thous. But it was Jesus' custom. And so, to begin His work, Jesus went to church. We should too.

Local, independent churches

Local, independent, Jesus-led

Biblical churches planted by the Apostles were local, independent, Jesus-led, via a pastor aka a bishop.

Elders of the church sent evangelists out to start new independent churches, and members were sent out as the priesthood of believers to win souls locally. They had one Gospel, the doctrine came directly from scripture and had no hidden or extra-biblical offices, rites or oral tradition.

Yes, churches is plural. That's the NT Christian perspective on a church. A fellowship of like-minded churches. Not a universal church on earth.

Local, independent churches

No top-down denominations

There is no biblical basis for top down denominational structure of a church.

Large institutions of men where power is consolidated always will drift into apostasy, and when it does it takes down huge swaths of people. An independent church might falter or pick up a strange doctrine, but the people within can leave and often just go across town to another independent Bible believing church.

Pastors and deacons

Two offices

The only two biblical church offices; Bishop and Deacon. Bishop being the pastor, or head of a local independent NT church, and deacon being an assistive role, aiding the pastor in the administration of the church.

Wherefore, brethren, look ye out among you seven men of honest report, full of the Holy Ghost and wisdom, whom we may appoint over this business. But we will give ourselves continually to prayer, and to the ministry of the word.Acts 6:3-4

They should be Christ-led, pastor in charge, deacons serving the pastor, as the Bible lays out.

The qualifications of a bishop

A bishop then must be blameless, the husband of one wife, vigilant, sober, of good behaviour, given to hospitality, apt to teach; Not given to wine, no striker, not greedy of filthy lucre; but patient, not a brawler, not covetous; One that ruleth well his own house, having his children in subjection with all gravity; (For if a man know not how to rule his own house, how shall he take care of the church of God?) Not a novice, lest being lifted up with pride he fall into the condemnation of the devil. Moreover he must have a good report of them which are without; lest he fall into reproach and the snare of the devil.1 Timothy 3:2-7

Not that he, his wife or kids must be perfect, but that the pastors and deacons be in control at home.

Telegram isn't church. Discord isn't church. I am not a pastor. Unless someone leads an actual assembly of believers, in real life, they are not a pastor.

Pastors and deacons

A royal priesthood

But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people; that ye should shew forth the praises of him who hath called you out of darkness into his marvellous light:1 Peter 2:9

Every saved believer is of the priesthood of Christ. Giving the gospel that others become part of that holy priesthood of believers. There is one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus ().

A saved woman is called to give the gospel and save souls and use their gifts to the edification of the church, just as a man is. But in church, under the leadership of a man of God, the pastor, they are to learn in silence. It isn't the role but the goal.

Pastors and deacons

The labourer is worthy of his reward

Let the elders that rule well be counted worthy of double honour, especially they who labour in the word and doctrine. For the scripture saith, Thou shalt not muzzle the ox that treadeth out the corn. And, The labourer is worthy of his reward.1 Timothy 5:17-18

It's a pretty clear line between being paid for the ministry with enough to live on, and taking advantage of the position to become rich from it. Tithes and supporting a pastor are a good thing. Pastors are to not take advantage of their flock.

And accusations against an elder should be handled with respect, requiring witnesses, but publicly rebuked if they don't repent. By not being respecters of persons in these matters, an active lively church can avoid these pitfalls.

One body, two ordinances

Baptized into one body

For by one Spirit are we all baptized into one body, whether we be Jews or Gentiles, whether we be bond or free; and have been all made to drink into one Spirit.1 Corinthians 12:13

By the Spirit of God that has baptized us into the body of Christ, a spiritual baptism, we become partakers of the gifts given every man, according to the purpose of God. Christ as the head.

These passages are not about the gifts, but about the hearts of Christians regarding them. That we all work as one body, under the head that is Christ.

One body, two ordinances

A little leaven

Your glorying is not good. Know ye not that a little leaven leaveneth the whole lump? Purge out therefore the old leaven, that ye may be a new lump, as ye are unleavened. For even Christ our passover is sacrificed for us:1 Corinthians 5:6-7

Jesus taught of church discipline in Matthew 18, first alone, then with witness, then before the entire church if the person won't take the reproach. Far too many churches today, even doctrinally sound ones full of believers, fail at this simple task.

And when a brother or sister in Christ comes into your congregation, their past bondage should not be held against them. Their sins are as forgiven as yours. Thou shalt not oppress him.

One body, two ordinances

Believer's baptism

There are two new ordinances made to which we should adhere. Baptism and the Lord's supper. Just as the ordinances of the OT held no actual power to forgive sin or earn grace, but were a picture of the Christ to come, so too the ordinances we are told to keep are an image of Christ.

In baptism, the believer is immersed in water, dead, buried and risen in the manner reminiscent of Jesus. Baptism not being the putting away of sin, but the answer of a good conscience by those who have called upon the name of the Lord.

The like figure whereunto even baptism doth also now save us (not the putting away of the filth of the flesh, but the answer of a good conscience toward God,) by the resurrection of Jesus Christ:1 Peter 3:21
One body, two ordinances

This do in remembrance of me

And he took bread, and gave thanks, and brake it, and gave unto them, saying, This is my body which is given for you: this do in remembrance of me.Luke 22:19

The Lord's supper is a continuation of Passover, which pictured Jesus. Jesus is that lamb, who takes away the sins of the world. The bread now, represents His body. The cup poured out, His blood. It is a memorial and a picture of Christ.

Done within the congregation of believers, using the breaking of unleavened bread and the pouring out of the unfermented juice of the vine, done in memory of Jesus. It is not a means of obtaining grace or salvation.

Assembling together

Not forsaking the assembling

Let us hold fast the profession of our faith without wavering; (for he is faithful that promised;) And let us consider one another to provoke unto love and to good works: Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as the manner of some is; but exhorting one another: and so much the more, as ye see the day approaching.Hebrews 10:23-25

This is an appeal to go to church. Note, not for salvation, not just because it's sin not to, not because you can lose your salvation if you don't. But like all good works, to the benefit of others and a reflection of the love God has shown us in our salvation.

This appeal is explicitly about what we should do because things are getting worse, not an excuse not to.

Assembling together

White-knuckle Christianity

For many Christians, they are living what can only be described as “white-knuckle Christianity.”

They are trying to get right without any guidance. Without accountability. Without a church. Without real life brethren to hold them accountable. Without guidance from older and more mature Christians that know them personally.

They are easily taken in by people who seem to have more knowledge. People that they don't know personally. And they tend to be carried away by strange doctrines.

Assembling together

Many hands make light work

All hands on deck is how we should approach the local church. If you have a talent or skill or even time, you should pitch in.

It is where the works of God are done. Edifying others, praying as a group, singing Psalms and hymns, giving one to another, learning under a pastor, and most importantly, soul winning and evangelizing of some kind, even if it's just invitations and tracts.

Assembling together

Bring them to church

Every saved believer has a difficult time preaching to their loved ones, and getting friends and relatives saved.

But Jesus said unto them, A prophet is not without honour, but in his own country, and among his own kin, and in his own house.Mark 6:4

If the Lord Jesus Christ had a hard time reaching those who knew Him best, it is natural that we do too. But church-goers? Where the word is preached? There is a solution: Inviting them to services. Christmas, Easter, potluck, perhaps your own baptism, or just because.
