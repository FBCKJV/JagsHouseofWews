Shadows & Substance

Old Testament Practices and Salvation by Faith

✦ ✦ ✦

Use the arrow keys or swipe to move through the study. Tap any gold reference to read the verse.

The question

There is a misunderstanding among many about the Old Testament’s commands for building a temple to offer blood sacrifice to God. People ask, how does this line up with the understanding that we are saved by faith in the sacrifice of Jesus alone in the New Testament? Does this mean works were needed to be saved in the Old Testament?

God commanded Moses to build the Tabernacle.

And let them make me a sanctuary; that I may dwell among them.Exodus 25:8

God also commanded Moses that the people would bring sacrifices and offerings, culminating each year in the Day of Atonement, a statute for Israel to keep continually.

And this shall be an everlasting statute unto you, to make an atonement for the children of Israel for all their sins once a year. And he did as the Lord commanded Moses.Leviticus 16:34

A shadow on the ground

Imagine a shadow on the ground. It shows the shape of a person but is not the person. The Tabernacle and its sacrifices were shadows cast by Jesus, the real thing standing just out of sight.

Key Point: The Tabernacle, Temple, sacrifices, washings, and dietary laws were God’s instructions to Israel, but they never saved a single soul. Salvation has always been by faith, exactly as it is for us today in Christ.

The Tabernacle & Temple

A dwelling place, first mobile, then permanent, where God’s presence would meet with His people.

The Tabernacle

“Let them make me a sanctuary; that I may dwell among them.”

The Temple

Later made permanent: the Ark, the mercy seat, the glory-cloud filling the house of the Lord.

These were special places marking Israel as God’s own nation, but they did not save souls. The ritual restored ceremonial cleanliness, allowing a person to approach God’s presence without defiling the sanctuary. It was never about justifying the soul, which required faith.

Sacrifices, washings & dietary laws

Sacrifices

Animal offerings, like on the Day of Atonement, covered sin temporarily. Blood cleaned the outside; it could not fix the heart forever. Their yearly repetition underscores a temporary, external effect. Ritual purity to maintain access to the tabernacle, not a transformation of the heart.

Washings & dietary laws

Ceremonial cleansings and food rules kept Israel distinct and addressed ritual uncleanness. These were about physical purity and obedience, not eternal salvation.

Carnal ordinances

“Which stood only in meats and drinks, and divers washings, and carnal ordinances, imposed on them until the time of reformation.” These were “carnal” (fleshly) rules, temporary until Christ came.

Purifying the flesh, not the soul

For if the blood of bulls and of goats, and the ashes of an heifer sprinkling the unclean, sanctifieth to the purifying of the flesh:Hebrews 9:13

These rituals purified the body, not the soul.

Why God required them

To make Israel holy, set apart among pagan nations, and to point ahead to a greater sacrifice: Jesus, who would truly save. These rituals were a “picture” of the spiritual cleansing and atonement Christ would accomplish.

These rituals were like a picture book pointing to Jesus, the real Savior.

Where they fell short

Limitation: They could never permanently remove sin. Sacrifices offered “year by year continually” could never make the worshipper perfect.

For the law having a shadow of good things to come, and not the very image of the things, can never with those sacrifices which they offered year by year continually make the comers thereunto perfect. For then would they not have ceased to be offered? because that the worshippers once purged should have had no more conscience of sins. But in those sacrifices there is a remembrance again made of sins every year. For it is not possible that the blood of bulls and of goats should take away sins.Hebrews 10:1-4

Animal sacrifices were a temporary fix, like a bandage, not a cure.

The Day of Atonement

The slain goat

A sin offering whose blood was shed for the sins of the nation, a picture of Christ’s atoning blood.

The High Priest

Carried the blood of the slain goat into the Holy of Holies within the Tabernacle, sprinkling it upon the Mercy Seat, foreshadowing Christ our great High Priest.

The scapegoat

Bore Israel’s sins into the wilderness, symbolizing guilt removed.

Jesus is the scapegoat, the Lamb slain, and the High Priest.

More shadows of Christ

The Passover lamb

And the blood shall be to you for a token upon the houses where ye are: and when I see the blood, I will pass over you, and the plague shall not be upon you to destroy you, when I smite the land of Egypt.Exodus 12:13

The temporary, external protection of the Passover lamb prefigures Christ’s blood, which provides eternal spiritual redemption.

The scapegoat

And when he hath made an end of reconciling the holy place, and the tabernacle of the congregation, and the altar, he shall bring the live goat: And Aaron shall lay both his hands upon the head of the live goat, and confess over him all the iniquities of the children of Israel, and all their transgressions in all their sins, putting them upon the head of the goat, and shall send him away by the hand of a fit man into the wilderness: And the goat shall bear upon him all their iniquities unto a land not inhabited: and he shall let go the goat in the wilderness.Leviticus 16:20-22

A ritual act pointing to Christ, who “bare our sins in his own body” , but symbolic and external, requiring annual repetition, unlike Christ’s once-for-all sacrifice.

Abraham believed God

Before any Tabernacle or Law existed, Abraham was already saved by faith. Before the Law, salvation was by faith. The sacrificial system dealt with ceremonial cleanliness, but righteousness before God was always tied to faith, not ritual.

And he believed in the Lord; and he counted it to him for righteousness.Genesis 15:6

Abraham was saved by believing God’s promise, not by sacrifices or rules, and long before circumcision was given at 99.

Faith in the Old Testament

The Old Testament itself critiques leaning on sacrifice for spiritual standing. God sought faith and obedience over the blood of bulls and goats.

Counted for righteousness

And he believed in the Lord; and he counted it to him for righteousness.Genesis 15:6

The just shall live by faith

Behold, his soul which is lifted up is not upright in him: but the just shall live by his faith.Habakkuk 2:4

A broken and contrite heart

For thou desirest not sacrifice; else would I give it: thou delightest not in burnt offering. The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit: a broken and a contrite heart, O God, thou wilt not despise.Psalm 51:16-17

Cease to do evil

To what purpose is the multitude of your sacrifices unto me? saith the Lord: I am full of the burnt offerings of rams, and the fat of fed beasts; and I delight not in the blood of bullocks, or of lambs, or of he goats.Isaiah 1:11
Wash you, make you clean; put away the evil of your doings from before mine eyes; cease to do evil; Learn to do well; seek judgment, relieve the oppressed, judge the fatherless, plead for the widow.Isaiah 1:16-17

Christ fulfills the law

Jesus’ death was the one-time, perfect sacrifice that saves the soul forever, accomplishing both sanctification and forgiveness, resolving what the Old Testament system never could.

Wisdom, righteousness, sanctification, redemption

Jesus doesn’t just cover sin. He makes us holy in God’s eyes.

I will remember their sins no more

The covenant that makes it so.

Saved by faith today

Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ:Romans 5:1

We are saved by trusting Christ, not by rituals or works. Faith gives peace with God that no sacrifice could fully give.

Old Testament rituals were like a map leading to Jesus, the destination.

No temple needed

In the New Jerusalem, no physical Temple is needed. God and the Lamb are the temple. We do not need rituals or buildings, because Jesus’ sacrifice makes us holy forever.

And I saw no temple therein: for the Lord God Almighty and the Lamb are the temple of it.Revelation 21:22

Christ’s work means we are fully sanctified by faith, not by repeated washings or sacrifices. The Old Testament pointed to this future, where God’s presence is enough.

We are His temple

The Tabernacle was like a tent for God’s presence; now, Jesus Himself is our home.

Key takeaways

Fleshly sanctification

Sacrifices and washings restored ritual purity to allow access to the tabernacle. They addressed external states but could not cleanse the conscience or remove sin’s guilt.

Spiritual sanctification

Righteousness before God came through faith. Sacrifices were never a substitute for faith, but a means to maintain covenantal relationship in the physical sphere.

Christ as fulfillment

Christ’s sacrifice transcends the Old Testament system, purifying both flesh and spirit. His blood cleanses the conscience and grants eternal redemption, what the rituals could only symbolize.

Practical application: Encourage trust in Christ’s finished work, not in personal efforts to earn salvation. Read and at home to see how Jesus fulfills the Law.

Conclusion

The Tabernacle, Temple, sacrifices, washings, and dietary laws were God’s way to teach Israel about holiness and point to Jesus. Salvation has always been by faith, and Christ’s sacrifice makes us holy forever, as we will see in Heaven, with no need for a Temple.

  • Old Testament practices addressed outward holiness, not the saving of the soul.
  • Salvation is by faith alone, as it was for Abraham, and is for us in Christ.
  • Jesus’ sacrifice makes us holy forever, replacing every ritual.
But of him are ye in Christ Jesus, who of God is made unto us wisdom, and righteousness, and sanctification, and redemption:1 Corinthians 1:30

No sacrifice but Jesus. You are saved by faith, and Jesus has already made you holy in God’s eyes. Rest in that!
