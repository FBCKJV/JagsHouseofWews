The lesson

I The Author and His Seer

God speaks in the first person

1The mighty God, even the LORD, hath spoken, and called the earth from the rising of the sun unto the going down thereof.

Psalm 50 is a song written in the voice of God Himself, His own perspective spoken directly to man. It is instruction in doctrine and in prophecy, given by His word, in the first person.

The psalm opens by introducing its Author: the mighty God. All scripture is given by inspiration of God . It is God-breathed and God-spoken. But this psalm is set apart as the word of God delivered as a message of judgment for His people.

I The Author and His Seer

Asaph, the seer

God spoke these words through a psalmist other than David. The superscription titles it “A Psalm of Asaph,” the first of the twelve Asaphite psalms in the Bible.

Asaph was a prominent Levite, appointed by King David as the chief musician and worship leader before the Ark of the Covenant when it was brought to Jerusalem . He was more than a song director. Scripture credits him as a prophet, and calls him a seer.

Prophesying

May refer to preaching and declaring the word of God.

Seeing

The specific term for a visionary given glimpses of the future by God: prophetic understanding of things that are not, but will be.

I The Author and His Seer

The Word who called the earth

The psalm first establishes the primacy of God the Creator. By His Word the earth and the heavens were made.

When the Bible credits creation to God, remember that this includes Jesus. We read the Old Testament revelation with a New Testament understanding. The nature of the Godhead is revealed in the creation, and it matters for this psalm: the God who called the earth is the God who will come to judge it.

II Our God Shall Come

Out of Zion God hath shined

2Out of Zion, the perfection of beauty, God hath shined.

Zion is the place God chose to put His name.

His presence in the Tabernacle and later the Temple, God dwelling with His people through their worship, is an echo of the true coming of the Lord in the flesh, and of His return on His day to judge the nations upon that holy mountain.

TabernacleGod among His people in worship
IncarnationGod come in the flesh
ReturnGod come to judge on His holy mountain

II Our God Shall Come

A fire shall devour before Him

3Our God shall come, and shall not keep silence: a fire shall devour before him, and it shall be very tempestuous round about him.

The Second Coming, the return of Christ, is the day we look forward to with gladness. He will come with power, a consuming fire proceeding from Him, and the sword of His word from His mouth shall slay the wicked.

The future judgment of the world was a key part of Israel’s understanding, though they saw it through a glass, darkly . With the full revelation of the completed Bible, we see clearly what they only glimpsed in faith.

II Our God Shall Come

Gather my saints together

4He shall call to the heavens from above, and to the earth, that he may judge his people. 5Gather my saints together unto me; those that have made a covenant with me by sacrifice.

He comes as Judge, the Son calling up to heaven and down to earth, with a shout, with the trump: the call to judgment of His people, before His wrath is poured out on the world.

The gathering of the elect, those sanctified by the Spirit in Christ, is not a “new doctrine,” as some would claim. The Rapture was a key part of prophecy for the Lord’s people, sung by Asaph the seer.

II Our God Shall Come

A covenant made by sacrifice

6And the heavens shall declare his righteousness: for God is judge himself. Selah.

The covenant Moses made between the people and God was sealed with blood that represented the blood of Christ, and with it the promise of a better covenant made by a better sacrifice: the sacrifice of the Son.

The heavens declare the righteousness of Christ Jesus, and His righteousness is upon all His people.

The AtonementHimself
The MediatorHimself

The saints are secure in His righteousness. But now God speaks to Israel in judgment, beginning with His own people.

III Not Thy Sacrifices

Hear, O my people

7Hear, O my people, and I will speak; O Israel, and I will testify against thee: I am God, even thy God. 8I will not reprove thee for thy sacrifices or thy burnt offerings, to have been continually before me.

The psalm turns from the future prophecy to the present call.

The sacrifices had been kept. Israel maintained the Levitical ordinances on which God had based His promises, the constant offerings that preserved ritual cleanliness and kept His presence among them. Those offerings stayed His hand from reproving them harshly. He is not asking for more sacrifice. No additional bulls or goats need shed their blood.

III Not Thy Sacrifices

The better sacrifice

Under the old covenant the blood of bulls and goats was offered continually by the Levites, each one signifying the Sacrifice to come. We have that better sacrifice, once and for all: a covenant made by the blood of Jesus, the burnt offering for our sin.

III Not Thy Sacrifices

The cattle upon a thousand hills

10For every beast of the forest is mine, and the cattle upon a thousand hills. … 12If I were hungry, I would not tell thee: for the world is mine, and the fulness thereof. 13Will I eat the flesh of bulls, or drink the blood of goats?

Every beast is His. If He were hungry, wanting the savour of the fat and the sustenance of meat, He would have His pick and His fill. He does not need them.

Israel’s sacrifices were a picture, and a necessity of the covenant, but they did not please Him in and of themselves.

III Not Thy Sacrifices

Mercy, and not sacrifice

The prophet

“For I desired mercy, and not sacrifice; and the knowledge of God more than burnt offerings.”

The Lord Jesus

“But if ye had known what this meaneth, I will have mercy, and not sacrifice, ye would not have condemned the guiltless.”

The same sentiment, spoken by Hosea and repeated by Christ. What God was after in Psalm 50 is what He is still after.

IV The Offering God Seeks

Offer unto God thanksgiving

14Offer unto God thanksgiving; and pay thy vows unto the most High:

God makes it plain that the offering is thanksgiving.

Their vows were to be paid to keep their part in the covenant as His nation: tithes and firstfruits as acts of faith. Unlike the offerings to other gods, it was not a meal for Him to consume. It was thanksgiving, offered in gratitude for all He had supplied, and to keep themselves unspotted as His people.

God desires mercy toward others: right judgment, defending the weak, and hating the workers of iniquity.

These are not the repeated acts of priests, but matters of the heart, which God judges and which He seeks from us.

IV The Offering God Seeks

Call upon me in the day of trouble

15And call upon me in the day of trouble: I will deliver thee, and thou shalt glorify me.

Having established His ways, God gives the gospel: good news to all men in all ages, the dispensation of grace.

Calling upon the name of the Lord for salvation, that we may glorify Him before all men. It is the promise of salvation and the heart of the Great Commission, joined to the truth that when we are in Him we magnify His light, so that men see our works of righteousness, mercy and thanksgiving, and glorify God.

This is our purpose as believers: to make known the judgment of God and to be an example of His mercy, praising His name in thanksgiving, all openly before men, that He may be glorified.

V The Indictment

The charge of formalism

16But unto the wicked God saith, What hast thou to do to declare my statutes, or that thou shouldest take my covenant in thy mouth? 17Seeing thou hatest instruction, and casteth my words behind thee.

Having summoned the court and stated His position, God begins the indictment. Having shown that He has no need of bulls, goats or fowls, He lays the charge of formalism against His covenant people.

The religious practices are being kept, the bare minimum of service to maintain the covenant. The Lord is not accusing them of failing there, but in the weightier matters, just as Jesus accused the Pharisees.

V The Indictment

A light to all nations

God gave Israel promises and practices to keep them as His people, but they were failing to declare His statutes and proclaim His law.

The whole point of Israel was to be a light to all men, not to selfishly reap the benefits while doing nothing to teach the nations by example. They squandered their blessings by not fulfilling that role.

V The Indictment

Near with their lips, far in their heart

God accuses the wicked among His people of missing the entire point, the same charge Jesus would bring against the religious leaders of the Jews.

V The Indictment

The counts against them

God’s accusations are sharp.

Consent

“When thou sawest a thief, then thou consentedst with him.” They went along with the thief.

Partaking

“…and hast been partaker with adulterers.” Both the literal engagement with the world, and the spiritual adultery of general ecumenicalism.

Deceit

“Thou givest thy mouth to evil, and thy tongue frameth deceit.” They speak evil things, and lie.

Slander

“Thou sittest and speakest against thy brother.” They not only fail to love their neighbour, they lie against their own brother.

V The Indictment

“And I kept silence”

21These things hast thou done, and I kept silence; thou thoughtest that I was altogether such an one as thyself: but I will reprove thee, and set them in order before thine eyes.

God is longsuffering and merciful, not willing that any should perish . He gave them space and time to reconsider their ways.

Instead they considered themselves holy as God is holy. They thought that by their religious practice, keeping the letter of the law, they could ignore the purpose and spirit of the law. It is the same indictment as Matthew 23, and it could be said of so many supposed Christians today.

VI The Case Against Us

The leaven of the Pharisees

For Christians today, the indictment is the same leaven of the Pharisees: hypocrisy.

Just as the Pharisees had the Old Testament sacrifices, we have the once-and-for-all sacrifice of Christ and the total remission of sins.

VI The Case Against Us

No more sacrifice

As God told Israel He was not seeking to add sacrifices to those they already offered, He is not asking us for additional acts of atonement. He remembers our sins no more.

For those in Christ

“Now where remission of these is, there is no more offering for sin.”

For wilful sin

“…there remaineth no more sacrifice for sins, but a certain fearful looking for of judgment…”

Like Israel in Psalm 50, we are covered by the blood. We are His people. But are we bound by formalism and the outward appearance of being clean, while failing at the good work He saved us for, the work that gives Him glory?

VI The Case Against Us

Taking His covenant in our mouth

If we are not declaring His righteousness and glory in thanksgiving, and showing mercy and judgment to others, loving our neighbour, how are we taking His covenant in our mouths?

How are we telling the world we are Christians when we are not acting like it? Who does it benefit?

VI The Case Against Us

This is not legalism

God is not saying that we must do works to be saved, or to be His. We are not justified before God by our works, but before men. Yet God does have works for us to do.

Before God

Justified by faith, through the blood of Christ alone.

Before men

Shown to be His by the good works He ordained for us.

VI The Case Against Us

The buried talent

The indictment against His people is that while we have availed ourselves of His covenant and promises, we have squandered the talents of silver He has given us.

Five talents

Traded, and gained five more. “Well done, thou good and faithful servant.”

Two talents

Gained two more. “Well done, good and faithful servant.”

One talent

Afraid, went and digged in the earth, and hid it. “Thou wicked and slothful servant.”

To whom much is given, much is required . There are professing Christians who have taken the offer and buried it. They never took it in as their own, nurtured it, or used it to increase God’s kingdom.

Again, this is not works-based salvation. Had the servant kept his one talent in his own care, he would not have been cast out. Instead he buried it and went through the motions.

VI The Case Against Us

Not forsaking the assembling

Hebrews 10 sets the command to gather between its two statements on sacrifice: no more need of sacrifice for those in Christ, and no more sacrifice for wilful sin.

We are commanded not to forsake the assembly, and its purpose is to love one another: edifying, building each other up, so much the more as the day approaches.

Yet some treat church as a checkbox for their righteousness, proving themselves holy by attendance alone. They wash the outside of the cup while within they are whited tombs full of dead men’s bones. To them religion is rituals and obligations that benefit only themselves, sacraments as their sacrifice rather than the blood of Christ.

VII Consider This

Ye that forget God

22Now consider this, ye that forget God, lest I tear you in pieces, and there be none to deliver.

Christians in name only go through the motions and never once consider God.

God goes out of His way in this psalm to stipulate that His righteous people are protected under His care. His case is with the false professors of His name, who think the formalism of their worship is enough to spare them from His wrath. They forget God, and He will tear them in pieces. They bury their talent out of fear, while never truly fearing God.

VII Consider This

Whoso offereth praise

23Whoso offereth praise glorifieth me: and to him that ordereth his conversation aright will I shew the salvation of God.

But God, ever gracious, ends on a note of promise.

Our purpose as His people is to praise His name, that He be glorified among men. We are the salt of the earth, preserving the world against judgment, and He encourages us to continue in praise and thanksgiving.

Salt can lose its savour. A lukewarm church can have its candlestick removed, and be of no further use to God.

VII Consider This

Stay salty

God’s case against Israel is God’s case against all who call themselves by His name and fail to live to proclaim His glory. Let us then keep our savour:

  • Do all things with mercy and judgment
  • Forgive those who trespass against us
  • Consider the poor, the widow and the fatherless
  • Exhort one another, so much the more as the day approaches
  • Offer praise and thanksgiving, that God be glorified among men

So that at the coming of Christ, when He brings fiery judgment upon the world, we will be found acceptable: “Well done, thou good and faithful servant.”
