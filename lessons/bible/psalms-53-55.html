Psalm 53 · The fool

The nature of the atheist

The fool hath said in his heart, There is no God. Corrupt are they, and have done abominable iniquity: there is none that doeth good.Psalm 53:1

Psalm 53 opens with one of the clearest and concise views of the nature of the atheist.

While it is, in context, about the man who denies God to do evil, lacking fear in Him who he cannot see, and so, unchecked and without fear proceeds to work iniquity, that is ultimately the driving force behind so-called "intellectual atheism" as well. The danger of this delusion leading to reprobation is very real.

Psalm 53 · The reprobate mind

Given over

The brute beast of man, ignoring the evidence of God; His wrath revealed from Heaven against the wicked, His mercy upon the faithful, the very creation that proclaims His power and glory, will engage in all manner of evil, justified within his desperately wicked heart, until God gives him up to his vile affections. Searing his conscience, giving him over to a reprobate mind, and he is filled with all unrighteousness.

Taken to the logical end, the fool who began by denying the existence of God, will ultimately become rejected utterly if they continue in their error.

Cross references • Psalm 53:1

Without excuse

Because that which may be known of God is manifest in them; for God hath shewed it unto them. For the invisible things of him from the creation of the world are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made, even his eternal power and Godhead; so that they are without excuse: Because that, when they knew God, they glorified him not as God, neither were thankful; but became vain in their imaginations, and their foolish heart was darkened. Professing themselves to be wise, they became fools, And changed the glory of the uncorruptible God into an image made like to corruptible man, and to birds, and fourfooted beasts, and creeping things. Wherefore God also gave them up to uncleanness through the lusts of their own hearts, to dishonour their own bodies between themselves:Romans 1:19-24
Cross references • Psalm 53:1–3

There is none righteous

But the general meaning here in Psalm 53 can apply to all men who don’t know or understand the truth. Well before their being made twice-dead. And it is a warning that can still prick at the heart of the lost, while there is yet time.

It is Paul’s paraphrase of this Psalm that we use when we give the gospel to people;

As it is written, There is none righteous, no, not one: There is none that understandeth, there is none that seeketh after God. They are all gone out of the way, they are together become unprofitable; there is none that doeth good, no, not one.Romans 3:10-12
Psalm 53 · Every one of them is gone back

None that doeth good

God looked down from heaven upon the children of men, to see if there were any that did understand, that did seek God. Every one of them is gone back: they are altogether become filthy; there is none that doeth good, no, not one.Psalm 53:2-3

This truth, that we are all sinners, is often sufficient to bring a person to at least hear the salvation plan. But first they have to acknowledge that God is.

Usually, if a person admits to the reality of God and does not try to excuse away His existence, getting them to admit they are a sinner is easy. They won’t disagree with the scripture when it says Every one of them is gone back: they are altogether become filthy; there is none that doeth good, no, not one.

Application • The atheist

The law written in their hearts

But the fool who denies God will balk at that characterization. The atheist will claim that they do not need fear of God to "do good." That they keep the commandment to love their neighbors, and reject that they are sinners.

Of course this is delusional. But even on the surface level, they are acknowledging a truth of the God who they reject existing, a morality that comes explicitly from Him, from the law written in our hearts, by the rebuke of the Spirit those who aren’t reprobate, feel.

For when the Gentiles, which have not the law, do by nature the things contained in the law, these, having not the law, are a law unto themselves: Which shew the work of the law written in their hearts, their conscience also bearing witness, and their thoughts the mean while accusing or else excusing one another;)Romans 2:14-15
Application • The atheist

Fish who deny they are wet

They are fish who deny they are wet.

And their judgment against others, accusing and excusing the sins of other men, betrays their own heart that denies the truth, by accepting God’s work in them, and His judgement.

Therefore thou art inexcusable, O man, whosoever thou art that judgest: for wherein thou judgest another, thou condemnest thyself; for thou that judgest doest the same things. But we are sure that the judgment of God is according to truth against them which commit such things. And thinkest thou this, O man, that judgest them which do such things, and doest the same, that thou shalt escape the judgment of God?Romans 2:1-3
Application • The atheist

Before it is too late

This internal conflict between their wicked hearts and the conscience of the Spirit will often bring the fool, the "intellectual atheist," out of it. The cognitive dissonance will trigger repentance for many. There are great Christians who were once seduced by the foolishness of their heart into denying His truth. Professing themselves to be wise, they became fools, but in their foolishness, God’s word preached unto them triggers a change of heart, and an acceptance of the reality of God, before it is "too late, reprobate."

But for many, they seek to deny the conflict in their hearts, and they seek to cauterize their conscience, and not wishing to retain God in their knowledge, they prefer to believe a lie.

Psalm 53 · The workers of iniquity

In great fear, where no fear was

Have the workers of iniquity no knowledge? who eat up my people as they eat bread: they have not called upon God. There were they in great fear, where no fear was: for God hath scattered the bones of him that encampeth against thee: thou hast put them to shame, because God hath despised them.Psalm 53:4-5

The reprobate lives in constant fear. They know the truth somewhere in themselves, but have built up strong walls against it, hardening their hearts and ultimately God hardens it completely. They live in constant fear, trapped in a fight or flight mode. And it leads them to do evermore wicked things, as God scatters their bones.

Cross references • Psalm 53:4–5

Haters of God

Being filled with all unrighteousness, fornication, wickedness, covetousness, maliciousness; full of envy, murder, debate, deceit, malignity; whisperers, Backbiters, haters of God, despiteful, proud, boasters, inventors of evil things, disobedient to parents, Without understanding, covenantbreakers, without natural affection, implacable, unmerciful: Who knowing the judgment of God, that they which commit such things are worthy of death, not only do the same, but have pleasure in them that do them.Romans 1:29-32

God hath despised them.

Application • The reprobate

Wise as serpents

We may lament, as David does here, wondering Have the workers of iniquity no knowledge? Do they not understand how God is already judging them? We often cannot comprehend the unsaved person’s refusal to see the truth, let alone one given up and given over to a reprobate mind. Don’t they see the sudden destruction they are headed for?

But our inability to think like they do shouldn’t keep us from understanding they exist. We are to be wise as serpents, Jesus said. And so, like righteous Lot, we are vexed by their filthy conversation.

Psalm 53 · The salvation of Israel

Even so, come, Lord Jesus

Oh that the salvation of Israel were come out of Zion! When God bringeth back the captivity of his people, Jacob shall rejoice, and Israel shall be glad.Psalm 53:6

The prayer of David that the deliverer come, the day of the Lord, the judgment of God’s wrath upon a wicked world, is the same prayer we have today for the coming again of Christ. Thy Kingdom come. Even so, come, Lord Jesus.

He will deal with the tares and the goats and all the fools who have denied God in their hearts. Come quickly, Lord Jesus. Amen

Psalm 53 · A note

Psalm 53 and Psalm 14

It should be noted that Psalm 53 directly mirrors Psalm 14, the major difference is the Lord vs God in its language, while the content is close to identical.

We can draw application from this, that the LORD is God and vice versa. The foolish idea some propose that there are different Gods in the Old Testament, can be debunked strictly from this minor distinction within the same language and theme.

But we can also draw out the importance of understanding the heart and mind of those that not only reject God’s judgment against their wicked deeds, but their delusional heart that denies He even exists, in order to give them excuse for their evil.

Psalm 55 · Give ear to my prayer

Hide not thyself

Give ear to my prayer, O God; and hide not thyself from my supplication. Attend unto me, and hear me: I mourn in my complaint, and make a noise;Psalm 55:1-2

Psalm 55 is a Psalm of David, but it isn't given to us when or who it is about.

We can make a guess from the context that it may have been during the time of Absalom's rebellion, that perhaps the man David considered an equal, a guide and acquaintance, who gave the King sweet counsel, sage advice, and who accompanied David to the Tabernacle to praise God, was the royal advisor, Ahithophel. Ahithophel betrayed David, took up the seditious plans of the King's son, and after his betrayal, and having his advice ignored by Absalom, went home and hanged himself.

Cross references • Psalm 55:13

Ahithophel

And when Ahithophel saw that his counsel was not followed, he saddled his ass, and arose, and gat him home to his house, to his city, and put his household in order, and hanged himself, and died, and was buried in the sepulchre of his father.2 Samuel 17:23

Sadly, for David, the number of betrayals and those who met terrible ends for it, are many. While it is debatable whom this particular Psalm is about, what is clearly shown in its verse is the pain, the sting of betrayal, lies against his character, and the resulting death and destruction that came about by these evil works.

Cross references • Psalm 55:1

I will hide mine eyes

He opens, as he often does, petitioning God to hear his prayer. To not hide Himself from his supplication. We know the omnipotence and omniscience of God. His understanding is not bound by time or space. There is nothing He doesn't see or hear.

But we also know, at times and for His purpose, God turns a blind eye and a deaf ear to those under His judgement or caught in tribulation that serves a larger purpose.

God tells the people this, plainly;

And when ye spread forth your hands, I will hide mine eyes from you: yea, when ye make many prayers, I will not hear: your hands are full of blood.Isaiah 1:15
Cross references • Psalm 55:1

A cloud

And the people recognize its truth;

Thou hast covered thyself with a cloud, that our prayer should not pass through.Lamentations 3:44

It can happen. The judgment of the Lord may be upon someone. But it also might simply feel that way in the midst of trouble. We may feel God is absent, His hand removed from our lives, tossed in the turmoil of the moment.

It is wise to not assume to know the mind of God, while seeking His involvement in the situation you're in. Like a subject going before the throne, David's petition is formal and proper, and without presumption.

That shows a healthy fear of the Lord.

Psalm 55 · The voice of the enemy

They cast iniquity upon me

Because of the voice of the enemy, because of the oppression of the wicked: for they cast iniquity upon me, and in wrath they hate me. My heart is sore pained within me: and the terrors of death are fallen upon me. Fearfulness and trembling are come upon me, and horror hath overwhelmed me.Psalm 55:3-5

David lays out the complaint that has brought him to seek relief from the Lord. There are wicked men engaged in violence, and who cast the blame and cause for their actions on David, by means of slander, justifying their hatred of him and the evil they are doing, by falsely accusing him.

Psalm 55 · Horror hath overwhelmed me

Violence and strife in the city

Destroy, O Lord, and divide their tongues: for I have seen violence and strife in the city. Day and night they go about it upon the walls thereof: mischief also and sorrow are in the midst of it. Wickedness is in the midst thereof: deceit and guile depart not from her streets.Psalm 55:9-11

The horror of this has brought David to absolute despair. It has overwhelmed him in sorrow and grief.

Application • Betrayal

Readily applicable

For modern Christians, living normal lives, in the relative safety of America, the extremes of intrigue and war that afflict a King in ancient Israel could seem to be unrelatable. We may feel far removed in our comfort from the high stakes and bloody acts being described.

But spiritually, the truth given us in these moments is readily applicable.

We may have been lied about at work, seen a family member turn on us out of the blue, had false assumptions made about our character, and seen the wicked take opportunity to gain for themselves at our expense. Lost and damaged relationships, people we love hurt by it, and the devastating feelings of loss and misery that comes from it all.

Psalm 55 · Fly away

Wings like a dove

And I said, Oh that I had wings like a dove! for then would I fly away, and be at rest. Lo, then would I wander far off, and remain in the wilderness. Selah. I would hasten my escape from the windy storm and tempest.Psalm 55:6-8

We may, like David, wish we had wings to fly away from it all, we may imagine just fleeing it and finding peace in another place, in some other world.

Psalm 55 · It was not an enemy

Mine equal, my guide

For it was not an enemy that reproached me; then I could have borne it: neither was it he that hated me that did magnify himself against me; then I would have hid myself from him: But it was thou, a man mine equal, my guide, and mine acquaintance. We took sweet counsel together, and walked unto the house of God in company.Psalm 55:12-14

And then, in the midst of this sudden chaos caused by our enemies, a friend, a brother, a child, suddenly and without cause that you could have ever imagined, turns on you as well.

We may be overwhelmed, as David, especially looking at the fallout the betrayal causes. Guilt, hurt and loss, coupled with having people you trusted take up the cause of those against you, just when you need them the most.

Psalm 55 · Vengeance is the Lord's

Let death seize upon them

Let death seize upon them, and let them go down quick into hell: for wickedness is in their dwellings, and among them.Psalm 55:15

David uses this insult upon injury to seek the Lord's help. That hurt is just cause for God to step in and punish them. To not only remove the threat and bring peace, but fiery vengeance on the wicked and the betrayer.

Better imprecatory prayers and recognition that vengeance is the Lord's, than bitterness and depression, guilt and wrath.

Psalm 55 · The Lord shall save me

I will call upon God

As always, following the pattern of prayer David lays out, as we desire God to get involved in our lives, we leave judgment to Him as we plead our case, our feelings and broken-hearted appeals, we leave judgment and deliverance to a perfect and holy God.

And that should lead us to close our prayer in the blessed hope we have placed our trust in. The peace that passes understanding in Christ.

As for me, I will call upon God; and the Lord shall save me.Psalm 55:16
Psalm 55 · He shall hear my voice

Evening, and morning, and at noon

Evening, and morning, and at noon, will I pray, and cry aloud: and he shall hear my voice. He hath delivered my soul in peace from the battle that was against me: for there were many with me. God shall hear, and afflict them, even he that abideth of old. Selah. Because they have no changes, therefore they fear not God.Psalm 55:17-19

God shall hear us, afflict the enemies, rescue His children, those that have repented to the truth and fear only Him, who call upon His name.

Psalm 55 · The betrayer

Smoother than butter

He hath put forth his hands against such as be at peace with him: he hath broken his covenant. The words of his mouth were smoother than butter, but war was in his heart: his words were softer than oil, yet were they drawn swords.Psalm 55:20-21
But thou, O God, shalt bring them down into the pit of destruction: bloody and deceitful men shall not live out half their days; but I will trust in thee.Psalm 55:23

The betrayer, who with feigned words and flattering tongue conspired to do evil, will be destroyed. The covenantbreakers and the mischief-making men will get their rewards. They will be brought low, to the pit of the grave, to the fires of Hell. They will be taken swiftly, while our trust is in the Lord.

Psalm 55 · He shall sustain thee

Cast thy burden upon the Lord

Cast thy burden upon the Lord, and he shall sustain thee: he shall never suffer the righteous to be moved.Psalm 55:22

The same promise quoted by Peter in the New Testament, showing the continuity of God's word.

Humble yourselves therefore under the mighty hand of God, that he may exalt you in due time: Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you.1 Peter 5:6-7

What a comfort. What a blessing. Praise God for His faithfulness. Amen
