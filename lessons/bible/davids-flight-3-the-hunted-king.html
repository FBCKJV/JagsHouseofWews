Psalm 54

Save me, O God, by thy name, and judge me by thy strength. Hear my prayer, O God; give ear to the words of my mouth. For strangers are risen up against me, and oppressors seek after my soul: they have not set God before them. Selah. Behold, God is mine helper: the Lord is with them that uphold my soul. He shall reward evil unto mine enemies: cut them off in thy truth. I will freely sacrifice unto thee: I will praise thy name, O Lord; for it is good. For he hath delivered me out of all trouble: and mine eye hath seen his desire upon mine enemies.Psalm 54:1-7
1 Samuel 23 · Ziph

In the wilderness of Ziph

Psalm 54 is another prayerful song that was penned by David during his flight from Saul.

And David abode in the wilderness in strong holds, and remained in a mountain in the wilderness of Ziph. And Saul sought him every day, but God delivered him not into his hand. And David saw that Saul was come out to seek his life: and David was in the wilderness of Ziph in a wood.1 Samuel 23:14-15
1 Samuel 23 · Ziph

Jonathan strengthened his hand in God

Shortly after making his refuge in the woods of Ziph, Saul's son Jonathan comes to David and assures his beloved friend that his father will not find him.

And Jonathan Saul’s son arose, and went to David into the wood, and strengthened his hand in God. And he said unto him, Fear not: for the hand of Saul my father shall not find thee; and thou shalt be king over Israel, and I shall be next unto thee; and that also Saul my father knoweth. And they two made a covenant before the Lord: and David abode in the wood, and Jonathan went to his house.1 Samuel 23:16-18
1 Samuel 23 · Ziph

Doth not David hide himself with us?

Credited as a Psalm of David, when the Ziphims came and said to Saul, Doth not David hide himself with us? We can sense the urgency of David's prayer to God, with the plaintive Save me, and Hear my prayer.

Then came up the Ziphites to Saul to Gibeah, saying, Doth not David hide himself with us in strong holds in the wood, in the hill of Hachilah, which is on the south of Jeshimon? Now therefore, O king, come down according to all the desire of thy soul to come down; and our part shall be to deliver him into the king’s hand. And Saul said, Blessed be ye of the Lord; for ye have compassion on me.1 Samuel 23:19-21
Psalm 54 · Ziph

Behold, God is my helper

There are times in our life when the relative safety we have is suddenly threatened. When even strangers rise up against us, and we have unexpected enemies that we weren't even aware of...when in desperation we should turn to God.

Behold, God is my helper. This simple truth is reassuring in our time of trouble.

Psalm 54 · Ziph

Them that uphold my soul

And blessing on those who have our backs, who give us comfort and support, should be in our prayers as well. David says, the Lord is with them that uphold my soul.

In the Psalm, we see David praising those that help him, (mirroring King Saul's blessing upon the Ziphites for revealing David's hiding spot. The irony here is interesting. Especially given the context). The Ziphites and Saul pursue David, but he makes his escape to Maon.

Psalm 54 · Ziph

God delivers us from all trouble

This sudden threat rising against the future King, God's anointed, troubles him enough to bring him to call for God's mercy, to deliver him with His strength, to reward his enemies for the evil they sought to do to him. To bless those that stand with him.

What a model for believers to have, so when our fear rises, we have the assurance of this Psalm to guide us in prayer, along with the results; God delivers us from all trouble, He takes vengeance on our enemies for us. All that we may give praise to His holy name. Amen

God shall send forth his mercy and his truth. Psalm 57 · The cave

When he fled from Saul in the cave

Psalm 57 is yet another song written by David during his flight from King Saul, when he fled from Saul in the cave.

The psalmist cries out for mercy, twice pleading to God to bestow favor on him. Not grace, unmerited favor, but mercy because David has trusted in Him, making his refuge in the Lord.

Psalm 57 · The cave

Mercy and grace

Mercy is often said to be not receiving what you deserve, while grace is receiving what you don't deserve. God shows His mercy on us that we are not destroyed for our wickedness. Grace is then receiving eternal life and made partakers of the inheritance of Christ.

But asking for mercy can also mean seeking right judgment based on mitigating circumstances and motivation of the heart.

Psalm 57 · The cave

Mercy may be graceful

Grace cannot be earned and is given out of the love of the one granting it, be it man or God. Showing merciful favor, above and beyond what's expected given the facts of the matter, because the one granting it simply can.

Mercy may be graceful, but it may also be an understanding that other factors may be at play, that influence the judge in his judgement.

Psalm 57 · The cave

The mercy of the court

David confesses his trust in God to shield himself from the calamities that he is facing, and asks for this sake that God performs this ask, the same way David trusts the Lord for all things.

Note David isn't boasting. He isn't telling God that he deserves anything. He is simply reminding God, and himself, that his trust is entirely placed on Him. He is throwing himself at the mercy of the court. The mighty Judge.

Psalm 57 · The cave

The substance of things hoped for

He then gives the sure hope he has, having yet to be rescued or brought out of calamity, seeing the thing far off, that God shall send forth His mercy and truth.

Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen. For by it the elders obtained a good report.Hebrews 11:1-2
Psalm 57 · The cave

Saved by hope

The Bible tells us that hope isn't a wish, or a desire for an outcome, but the thing itself we are placing our trust in, sight unseen.

For we are saved by hope: but hope that is seen is not hope: for what a man seeth, why doth he yet hope for? But if we hope for that we see not, then do we with patience wait for it.Romans 8:24-25
Psalm 57 · The cave

Be thou exalted, O God

It is this good report of the faith of David, that we are taught the value of hope. David's constant faith resulting in deliverance gives witness to us the faithfulness of God.

Now David, in the cave at Engedi, camped with his mighty men among the mountain lions in their dens, calls out to the Lord and gives Him praise.

Be thou exalted, O God, above the heavens; let thy glory be above all the earth.Psalm 57:5
Psalm 57 · The cave

How can we not when we pray?

Even in desperation, in danger, pleading his case to the God he's trusted, David gives Him praise.

How can we not when we pray?

Psalm 57 · The cave

Come boldly unto the throne of grace

We have all been guilty of haphazard prayer. Of casting our cares upon the Lord in a manner that almost seems like we are taking for granted the audience we are given to the Almighty God.

Yes, in Christ, we may enter boldly;

For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin. Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.Hebrews 4:15-16
Psalm 57 · The cave

The High Priest

We need not an invitation or a human agent acting as priests, between the Lord and man. We have the High Priest and advocate with the Father, the holy Son of God.

Because our sins are remembered no more, washed clean in His blood, no other sacrifice is needed to purify ourselves before seeking an audience with God.

Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus,Hebrews 10:19
Psalm 57 · The cave

God understands

We can know He will hear our plea.

And sometimes that plea may be unintelligible to even ourselves, made in such sadness and heartache we can't find words enough to convey our fears. God understands.

Likewise the Spirit also helpeth our infirmities: for we know not what we should pray for as we ought: but the Spirit itself maketh intercession for us with groanings which cannot be uttered. And he that searcheth the hearts knoweth what is the mind of the Spirit, because he maketh intercession for the saints according to the will of God.Romans 8:26-27
Psalm 57 · The cave

Ye ask amiss

God makes allowances for our infirmities of the flesh. But, we may also pray amiss;

Ye ask, and receive not, because ye ask amiss, that ye may consume it upon your lusts.James 4:3
Psalm 57 · The cave

Self serving at best

We may cavalierly ask for the things we covet, offhand requests that are self serving at best, and attempts to mask sin with Godliness, at worst.

And we may come before the throne of grace to ask of Him that giveth generously, in an offhand way that fails to acknowledge Who He is.

Psalm 57 · The cave

The glory of God in view

David always keeps the glory of God in view during his prayers. Exalting His holy name. Humbling himself before the throne. The judgment seat that he has brought his troubles to, seeking mercy.

But he giveth more grace. Wherefore he saith, God resisteth the proud, but giveth grace unto the humble. Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you. Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you. Cleanse your hands, ye sinners; and purify your hearts, ye double minded. Be afflicted, and mourn, and weep: let your laughter be turned to mourning, and your joy to heaviness. Humble yourselves in the sight of the Lord, and he shall lift you up.James 4:6-10
Psalm 57 · The cave

When we pray, humble yourself

When we pray, humble yourself. When we are in comfort and under the hedge of protection, and blessed beyond measure, far from the dens of lions, with no man seeking our soul to destroy it, we often forget this aspect of prayer.

We may toss out quick and perfunctory words, asking God to bless this or that, or even to help others in need, but forgetting Who it is we have an audience with.

Psalm 57 · The cave

A man after His own heart

It may be easier in desperate times to remember our place, but it shouldn't be forgotten just because you're in His hand. Never take for granted the favor we have, the grace that's been shown, and the awesome power of God.

When we look at David, especially his behavior towards Saul during this time, especially this incident, and we get the context of his prayerful posture during this time of trial, and great faith towards his Redeemer to judge righteous judgment, and deliver him from evil, we can understand why God considered David a man after His own heart.

Psalm 57 · The cave

But we see Jesus

All these things are written for our admonition, here at the end of the world. David looking forward through the Tabernacle and the Temple, and the Holy Of Holies covered by a veil, has placed his trust in the continuing blessings of God.

But we see Jesus, we can know His righteous name. The veil is torn, the access has been granted, sanctified by the Spirit, a High Priest forever. We have a greater testimony in the Spirit and a more sure word of prophecy in the complete scripture.

Psalm 57 · The cave

And so we have no excuse

We can know the outcome of David's time in the cave, the way his humble prayer led to his conviction that if he obeyed God, and trusted in the Lord, things would turn out well.

Again and again through Psalms we see the same pattern play out. Throughout scripture we see miracles that move men to do great things for the Lord.

1 Samuel 24 · The cave

In the wilderness of Engedi

And so we have no excuse.

And it came to pass, when Saul was returned from following the Philistines, that it was told him, saying, Behold, David is in the wilderness of Engedi. Then Saul took three thousand chosen men out of all Israel, and went to seek David and his men upon the rocks of the wild goats. And he came to the sheepcotes by the way, where was a cave; and Saul went in to cover his feet: and David and his men remained in the sides of the cave.1 Samuel 24:1-3
1 Samuel 24 · The cave

Saul's skirt

And the men of David said unto him, Behold the day of which the Lord said unto thee, Behold, I will deliver thine enemy into thine hand, that thou mayest do to him as it shall seem good unto thee. Then David arose, and cut off the skirt of Saul’s robe privily. And it came to pass afterward, that David’s heart smote him, because he had cut off Saul’s skirt. And he said unto his men, The Lord forbid that I should do this thing unto my master, the Lord’s anointed, to stretch forth mine hand against him, seeing he is the anointed of the Lord.1 Samuel 24:4-6
1 Samuel 24 · The cave

Among lions

This is the context of Psalm 57. David is among lions, physically and metaphorically. The mighty men with him, armed and dangerous. Encouraging David to kill Saul.

Saul, who is irrationally envious of David, and seeking his life has prepared a net for the younger man's steps. The King has happened upon the caves, digging a pit to capture David, but Saul himself has fallen into it. The hunter is in the snare of the hunted.

1 Samuel 24 · The cave

David does the right thing

Defenseless. Caught with his pants down, quite literally. David has the opportunity to take matters into his own hands, to be his own avenger. To slay the King and let whatever may come of it unfold.

And instead, David trusts in the Lord and does the right thing.

1 Samuel 24 · The cave

Mine eye spared thee

He cuts the piece of robe, silently. Even this act of rebellion pricks his heart. He has done nothing wrong to deserve what's happening, he has trusted in God, blameless before Him. Even this slight move against the King makes David humble before God. His trust and meekness is rewarded.

Following Saul out of the cave, at a distance, he cries out to his oppressor;

Behold, this day thine eyes have seen how that the Lord had delivered thee to day into mine hand in the cave: and some bade me kill thee: but mine eye spared thee; and I said, I will not put forth mine hand against my lord; for he is the Lord’s anointed.1 Samuel 24:10
1 Samuel 24 · The cave

See the skirt of thy robe

He treats his enemy to a sign of peace. David makes known to Saul and his armies exactly the heart he has towards his King.

Moreover, my father, see, yea, see the skirt of thy robe in my hand: for in that I cut off the skirt of thy robe, and killed thee not, know thou and see that there is neither evil nor transgression in mine hand, and I have not sinned against thee; yet thou huntest my soul to take it.1 Samuel 24:11
1 Samuel 24 · The cave

The true Judge

David then places the entire matter before the true Judge;

The Lord judge between me and thee, and the Lord avenge me of thee: but mine hand shall not be upon thee.1 Samuel 24:12
1 Samuel 24 · The cave

And Saul wept

Reminding Saul of his exaggerated hatred for David, a humble flea, pleading his innocence, and appealing to God, David has negotiated the way of peace, and left it to the Lord to decide his fate. And Saul weeps.

The Lord therefore be judge, and judge between me and thee, and see, and plead my cause, and deliver me out of thine hand. And it came to pass, when David had made an end of speaking these words unto Saul, that Saul said, Is this thy voice, my son David? And Saul lifted up his voice, and wept. And he said to David, Thou art more righteous than I: for thou hast rewarded me good, whereas I have rewarded thee evil.1 Samuel 24:15-17
1 Samuel 24 · The cave

Thou shalt surely be king

Saul relents, admits (for the time being) that he was wrong. That David sparing his life shows the righteousness of the man before God.

For if a man find his enemy, will he let him go well away? wherefore the Lord reward thee good for that thou hast done unto me this day. And now, behold, I know well that thou shalt surely be king, and that the kingdom of Israel shall be established in thine hand.1 Samuel 24:19-20
1 Samuel 24 · The cave

He gives all credit to God

The King asks only mercy for his sons when David comes into his power, and then rides away. Defeated by the truth and the faith of David, who did the right thing, and left his fate to the Lord.

And for this, David didn't praise himself. He didn't pat himself on the back for having done the right thing. He gives all credit to God. He sings unto Him among the nations. He exalts God above the heavens and His glory above the earth.

Psalm 57 · The cave

We give all honor to God

We as Christians today may find ourselves on our knees in times of crisis, begging to God to save us. But how often does He deliver us and we then credit ourselves? How many times do we forget his tender mercies? How often do we turn in praise to the congregation, letting them know the power of God in your life?

We don't have to write an entire songbook like David, praising God's holy name. But we should take the opportunity whenever possible to let others know and to let God know we appreciate what has been done in our lives.

That even when we do the right thing and make that decision like David, we don't boast of ourselves. We give all honor to God. Amen
