The Bread of Life

A plain reading of John 6:26–71, as Christ Himself explains it

Swipe or use the arrows to move through the lesson. Tap any gold reference to read the verse.

One figure, explained twice

From the rebuke in verse 26 to Peter’s confession in verse 69, Jesus speaks in a single figure: bread, eating and drinking. He does not leave it unexplained. He interprets it Himself near the start and again near the end.

  1. The figureBread from heaven, eaten and drunk (6:27–58)
  2. The first key“he that cometh to me… he that believeth on me” (6:35)
  3. The last key“the words that I speak unto you, they are spirit, and they are life” (6:63)

Peter’s answer in 6:68 shows that the disciples who stayed understood Him.

I

The Reproach

John 6:26–29

The discourse begins with a rebuke. The crowd had crossed the sea after another meal, not after the One who gave it.

Christ takes the bread they wanted and turns it into a figure: meat that perisheth set against meat that endureth unto everlasting life (6:27). When they ask what work God requires (6:28), He answers in plain words before the figure is developed at all:

Everything after verse 29 is commentary on believing.

II

The True Bread

John 6:30–36

The crowd asks for a sign and points to the manna (6:30–31). Jesus answers that Moses never gave the true bread (6:32). The Father gives it, and the bread is a Person:

They still hear loaves: “Lord, evermore give us this bread” (6:34), just as the woman at the well asked for water so she would not have to draw again (John 4:15). So Jesus defines the figure in the same breath He uses it:

To eat
is to come to Him
To drink
is to believe on Him

Verse 36 names what the crowd lacked: “ye also have seen me, and believe not” (6:36).

IIThe True Bread

Never hunger, never thirst

A meal that must be repeated to keep you alive is the opposite of what Christ promises in 6:35. Whoever comes and believes is satisfied once, and for ever.

John uses the same figure with water, and he explains it for us.

  • John 4:13–14Well water leaves you thirsty again. The water Christ gives becomes a well within, springing up into everlasting life.
  • John 7:37–39“Come unto me, and drink.” John adds that He spoke of the Spirit, received by those who believe on Him.

The water is received by believing. The bread is received the same way.

III

Kept by the Son

John 6:37–40

Those who come are not received on probation. Christ states their security again and again.

Of all the Father has given Him, He will “lose nothing” (6:39). Every one that believeth “may have everlasting life” (6:40). And four times in the chapter He promises, “I will raise him up at the last day”:

6:396:406:446:54
  • John 10:28They shall never perish, and no man shall pluck them out of His hand.

The keeping rests on the Father’s will and the Son’s promise, not on the believer eating again.

IV

Taught of God

John 6:41–47

The Jews murmur because a man they know, the son of Joseph, claims to have come down from heaven (6:41–42). Jesus tells them who comes to Him:

He immediately explains that drawing by quoting the prophet:

The Father draws through His word. Those who had truly heard and learned from the Father in the scriptures recognized His Son and came readily.

  • Isaiah 54:13The promise Jesus quotes: all thy children shall be taught of the LORD.
  • John 5:46–47Believing Moses leads to believing Christ. Rejecting Moses’ writings means rejecting His words.
  • John 12:32Once lifted up on the cross, He draws all men unto Him.
IVTaught of God

Believing is eating

Before the figure turns to flesh and blood, Jesus sets the plain truth and the figure side by side:

One verse says believe and have everlasting life. The next says, I am that bread. They belong together, and the rest of the discourse is read in their light.

V

Flesh and Blood

John 6:48–58

The fathers ate manna and died (6:49). This bread is eaten and the eater lives for ever (6:50). Then Jesus adds a new element to the same figure:

“Will give” is future. His flesh would be given for the life of the world at the cross. Naming flesh and blood apart (6:53) describes a body given in death.

The figure now points to His sacrifice, received by faith.

VFlesh and Blood

The same misunderstanding

Throughout John’s gospel, Jesus speaks in a figure and unbelief answers it literally.

  • John 3:4Nicodemus hears “born again” and asks about entering the womb a second time.
  • John 4:11The woman hears “living water” and points out how deep the well is.
  • John 6:52The Jews hear “my flesh” and ask how this man can give it to eat.

Each time, the literal reading belongs to the one who has not yet understood.

VFlesh and Blood

Born twice

Jesus teaches Nicodemus the same way. He states the figure (John 3:3), the hearer takes it literally (John 3:4), and Jesus says it again two ways, one verse after the other.

John 3:5The figure
John 3:6Said again
born of water
That which is born of the flesh is flesh
and of the Spirit
that which is born of the Spirit is spirit

A man is born once of water, in the flesh. He must be born a second time, of the Spirit (John 3:7). Jesus then gives a second figure, the wind, and applies it in the same verse (John 3:8).

Before the conversation ends, the brass serpent becomes a call to believe (John 3:14–16).

The figure is followed by its plain sense, and the plain sense is believing.

VFlesh and Blood

Said, then said plainly

John records the pattern throughout his gospel: a figure, a misunderstanding, then the meaning given outright.

  • John 2:19–21“Destroy this temple.” The Jews think of the building. John explains He spoke of the temple of His body.
  • John 4:32–34“I have meat to eat that ye know not of.” The disciples wonder who brought Him food. His meat is to do the Father’s will.
  • John 10:6–9John calls the sheepfold a parable they did not understand, so Jesus speaks again: “I am the door.”
  • John 11:11–14“Lazarus sleepeth.” The disciples think of rest, so Jesus tells them plainly, Lazarus is dead.
  • John 12:32–33“Lifted up,” and John adds that it signified what death He should die.
  • John 16:25 John 16:29Jesus says He has spoken in proverbs. The disciples answer that now He speaks plainly.

Jesus Himself called His sayings proverbs. Reading John 6 as a figure follows His own description of how He taught.

To eat His flesh and drink His blood is to believe on the Son.

VFlesh and Blood

The text marks the figure

The discourse itself shows that this language is figurative.

  • John 6:56“dwelleth in me, and I in him.” A mutual indwelling is spiritual by nature.
  • John 6:57“he that eateth me.” Eating a whole Person can only be a figure.
  • Leviticus 17:10–11The Law forbade eating blood, because the life is in the blood given on the altar for atonement. The Lord would not command His hearers to break His own law.

Those who took offense heard Him literally and missed what He meant.

VI

Spirit and Life

John 6:59–65

In the synagogue at Capernaum, even many disciples object: “This is an hard saying; who can hear it?” (6:60). Jesus answers in two steps.

First, they would see the Son of man ascend up where He was before (6:62). His flesh was going back to heaven. Second, He gives the interpretation of everything He has said:

Then He names the real problem. It was not a hard teaching but unbelief:

No man comes unless it is given him of the Father (6:65), and from that time many went back (6:66).

  • 1 John 2:19They went out from us because they were never of us.
VII

The Words of Eternal Life

John 6:66–71

Jesus turns to the twelve: “Will ye also go away?” (6:67). Peter answers:

Jesus, 6:63“the words that I speak unto you, they are spirit, and they are life”

Peter, 6:68“thou hast the words of eternal life”

Peter speaks of words and belief, not of flesh. He understood the figure.

VIIThe Words of Eternal Life

Near the bread, never fed

Jesus chose twelve, and one of them was a devil (6:70). John names him: Judas Iscariot, who should betray Him (6:71).

Verse 64 had already said Jesus knew from the beginning “who they were that believed not, and who should betray him.” Judas held the office, heard every word and saw every miracle, yet he never believed.

Being near Christ is not the same as having come to Him.

Believe, nine times

The word runs through the whole discourse, from the opening question to Peter’s confession. Tap a mark to read the verse.

The line runs from verse 26 to verse 71.

  • 6:29The work of God is to believe.
  • 6:30The crowd asks for a sign so they may believe.
  • 6:35He that believeth shall never thirst.
  • 6:36Ye have seen me, and believe not.
  • 6:40Every one that believeth may have everlasting life.
  • 6:47He that believeth hath everlasting life.
  • 6:64Some of you believe not, twice over.
  • 6:69We believe and are sure.

The discourse opens with believing, turns on believing, explains departure as not believing, and closes with believing. The eating language sits inside that frame.

The plain reading

  • Christ is the true Bread come down from heaven.
  • To come to Him and believe on Him is to eat and drink, and never hunger or thirst again.
  • Those taught of the Father through His word came to Him, and none who come are cast out or lost.
  • The flesh and blood point to His death for the life of the world, received by faith.
  • His words are spirit and life, and Peter confessed them as the words of eternal life.
