Godly sorrow

✦ ✦ ✦
Psalm 38 · Thine arrows

Rebuke me not in thy wrath

O Lord, rebuke me not in thy wrath: neither chasten me in thy hot displeasure. For thine arrows stick fast in me, and thy hand presseth me sore.Psalm 38:1-2

It is a fearful thing to fall into the hands of the Living God. The Lord's chastening can be fierce, and our wish should be to acknowledge our wrongdoing, and fear God.

Recognizing the predicaments we may find ourselves in are our own making, and calling upon the Lord to help is the only way out of whatever we have fallen into. Whatever snare we are caught in. Whatever arrows the Lord sends our way as a wake-up call, there is no relief except confession and a plea for mercy from the longsuffering God of our salvation.

Cross references • Psalm 38

Greater than I can bear

In Genesis 4, we find Cain being cursed with punishment from the Lord for the first murder, the blood of Cain's brother, righteous Abel, and he says to the Lord;

And Cain said unto the Lord, My punishment is greater than I can bear.Genesis 4:13

There was no remorse for what he had done, just sorrow at the consequence. But David paints the picture of a repentant sinner, recognizing that the punishment must be just, because it comes from God.

Psalm 38 · Because of my sin

No rest in my bones

There is no soundness in my flesh because of thine anger; neither is there any rest in my bones because of my sin.Psalm 38:3

Where Cain went about his day, and lied without a qualm when asked of God about Abel; Am I my brother's keeper?

In contrast, David, as a saved believer, has become unable to rest because of the guilt his sin carries. It is his iniquities that are the burden he cannot bear.

Psalm 38 · Mine iniquities

Too heavy for me

For mine iniquities are gone over mine head: as an heavy burden they are too heavy for me.Psalm 38:4

Faced with his sin and the consequences, it is David's sin that worries him more.

We should keep this perspective. To not go the way of Cain. Notice too, that rather than seeking healing and relief from the punishment by confession and having a contrite heart, Cain's concern is for his own life. God leaves a mark on Cain to ensure he lives and suffers for it.

Cross references • Psalm 38:4

Abhor that which is evil

But we who are His are commanded to hate iniquity. Even our own. Especially our own.

Let love be without dissimulation. Abhor that which is evil; cleave to that which is good.Romans 12:9
The fear of the Lord is to hate evil: pride, and arrogancy, and the evil way, and the froward mouth, do I hate.Proverbs 8:13
Psalm 38 · My foolishness

My wounds stink

My wounds stink and are corrupt because of my foolishness. I am troubled; I am bowed down greatly; I go mourning all the day long.Psalm 38:5-6

David sees the wounds caused by his sin as festering and infected, stinking. Literal or metaphorical, we all see the damage of sin in our lives. The harm it's caused.

We may not be spared the consequences, but we need not continue in the chastening that comes with unconfessed sin.

Psalm 38 · The disquietness of my heart

Feeble and sore broken

For my loins are filled with a loathsome disease: and there is no soundness in my flesh. I am feeble and sore broken: I have roared by reason of the disquietness of my heart.Psalm 38:7-8

The loathsome disease that rots our flesh, that makes us feeble and sore broken. The disquietude of our hearts. This may be spiritual, it may be in our soul and how we feel, it may be the effect of sin in our bodies, literal and tangible.

Sin manifests in our hearts and in our bodies, and brings men to conviction when we fear God.

Psalm 38 · Abandoned

My friends stand aloof

Lord, all my desire is before thee; and my groaning is not hid from thee. My heart panteth, my strength faileth me: as for the light of mine eyes, it also is gone from me. My lovers and my friends stand aloof from my sore; and my kinsmen stand afar off. They also that seek after my life lay snares for me: and they that seek my hurt speak mischievous things, and imagine deceits all the day long.Psalm 38:9-12

David, who in Psalm 32 sang of the blessedness of the man whose sins are forgiven, covered and not imputed against them, is now singing of the wounds, failing strength, bitter loneliness as a result of his actions. Abandoned and betrayed.

Psalm 38 · Betrayed

As a deaf man

But I, as a deaf man, heard not; and I was as a dumb man that openeth not his mouth. Thus I was as a man that heareth not, and in whose mouth are no reproofs.Psalm 38:13-14

In his sin, he has made friends with those that seek His life. He was deaf to their intentions. He did not speak up against them. Now he's between the reproof of God and the mischief of the wicked, and has no recourse but to seek the Lord.

Psalm 38 · Thou wilt hear

In thee, O Lord, do I hope

For in thee, O Lord, do I hope: thou wilt hear, O Lord my God.Psalm 38:15

No matter how desperate our situation, no matter how foolish or wicked we've been, no matter the cause for our misery; be it the chastening of the Lord or the betrayal of those we've given breadth to do evil, the mercies of our Father, seeking His face and His grace, are always there for those who have trusted in Him. He has promised. God Who cannot lie. Almighty God. So we know we can turn to Him.

Psalm 38 · I will be sorry for my sin

I will declare mine iniquity

For I said, Hear me, lest otherwise they should rejoice over me: when my foot slippeth, they magnify themselves against me. For I am ready to halt, and my sorrow is continually before me. For I will declare mine iniquity; I will be sorry for my sin.Psalm 38:16-18

Because He is our hope. Because evildoers may magnify themselves in our failures. Because we are willing to admit we are helpless without Him. To come up halt, stumbling and falling, unable to go on. Sorrow continually before us.

Because we declare our iniquity, admitting our wrongdoing, being sorry for what we have done.

For these reasons, we can cry out to the Lord with confidence to deliver us.

Psalm 38 · Mine adversaries

Evil for good

But mine enemies are lively, and they are strong: and they that hate me wrongfully are multiplied. They also that render evil for good are mine adversaries; because I follow the thing that good is.Psalm 38:19-20

Finally, David reminds God that many of his adversaries are wicked themselves, they render evil for good, persecuting David for doing things righteously, and hating him for it.

Crying out for God to make haste and plead his cause, asking Him to not leave us or forsake us, reminding Him of His promises, and really just acknowledging to the all-knowing Lord that we remember them and trust in those promises.

Psalm 38 · Make haste to help me

Forsake me not

This model prayer begins with a plea to God to not judge us too harshly, for Him to know we understand, we got the message. To confess our iniquity. To abhor it. To cry out the pain we are in, and seek relief. To admit our role in our misery, even if it is the evil of others taking advantage of our failures. And reminding ourselves by reminding God of His promises and our trust in Him.

Forsake me not, O Lord: O my God, be not far from me. Make haste to help me, O Lord my salvation.Psalm 38:21-22

Amen

Cross references • Psalm 38:18

Godly sorrow

Now I rejoice, not that ye were made sorry, but that ye sorrowed to repentance: for ye were made sorry after a godly manner, that ye might receive damage by us in nothing. For godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation not to be repented of: but the sorrow of the world worketh death. For behold this selfsame thing, that ye sorrowed after a godly sort, what carefulness it wrought in you, yea, what clearing of yourselves, yea, what indignation, yea, what fear, yea, what vehement desire, yea, what zeal, yea, what revenge! In all things ye have approved yourselves to be clear in this matter.2 Corinthians 7:9-11

This exchange perfectly encapsulates the fundamental difference between the sorrow of the world, held by Cain, and the godly sorrow, expressed by David.

Application • Godly sorrow

Cain and David

The burden Cain couldn't bear was the punishment, but he sorrowed not unto repentance. He never changed his mind about what he had done.

David's sorrow is godly, he cannot bear his own iniquity and hates his sin that brought him low, rather than resenting the punishment he'd received.

The difference between worldly sorrow that works death versus godly sorrow that works repentance to salvation, being delivered from the scourging, the ancillary consequences and from the scorners and those that plot mischief, taking advantage of our broken and contrite state.

The peaceable fruit of righteousness

Wherefore lift up the hands which hang down, and the feeble knees; And make straight paths for your feet, lest that which is lame be turned out of the way; but let it rather be healed.Hebrews 12:11-13

Psalm 39 · The vanity of life

To die is gain

Psalm 39 is sorrowful. It marks the hardship of being in the flesh, in a fallen world and the vanity of life, as a spiritual child of God.

Man's days are like a vapor. The difficulty of being vexed by the filthy conversation of the wicked, and by the weakness of our mortal bodies can cause us to question the Lord, what wait I for? Paul felt the same tug towards death as David, For to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain.

For to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain. But if I live in the flesh, this is the fruit of my labour: yet what I shall choose I wot not. For I am in a strait betwixt two, having a desire to depart, and to be with Christ; which is far better: Nevertheless to abide in the flesh is more needful for you.Philippians 1:21-24
Cross references • Psalm 39:5–6

Vanity and vexation of spirit

For those of us whose hope is in the Lord to be delivered, these sentiments can become powerful. Especially in our repeated sin, our lack of control at times. Feeling the stripes of God's chastening. The effects of time. The vanity of it all.

Solomon, David's son certainly felt this.

I have seen all the works that are done under the sun; and, behold, all is vanity and vexation of spirit.Ecclesiastes 1:14

Here in Psalm 39, David begins with confession, and reveals the frustration he has with himself.

Psalm 39 · My tongue

A bridle

I said, I will take heed to my ways, that I sin not with my tongue: I will keep my mouth with a bridle, while the wicked is before me.Psalm 39:1

David writes that he made it a point of emphasis, that he would not sin with his mouth, to take heed of what he said. Keeping his tongue as with a bridle on a mule or a horse.

James, the brother of Christ, writes of the importance of this in his epistle.

Cross references • Psalm 39:1

Able also to bridle the whole body

For in many things we offend all. If any man offend not in word, the same is a perfect man, and able also to bridle the whole body. Behold, we put bits in the horses’ mouths, that they may obey us; and we turn about their whole body. Behold also the ships, which though they be so great, and are driven of fierce winds, yet are they turned about with a very small helm, whithersoever the governor listeth. Even so the tongue is a little member, and boasteth great things. Behold, how great a matter a little fire kindleth! And the tongue is a fire, a world of iniquity: so is the tongue among our members, that it defileth the whole body, and setteth on fire the course of nature; and it is set on fire of hell.James 3:2-6
Cross references • Psalm 39:1

These things ought not so to be

For every kind of beasts, and of birds, and of serpents, and of things in the sea, is tamed, and hath been tamed of mankind: But the tongue can no man tame; it is an unruly evil, full of deadly poison. Therewith bless we God, even the Father; and therewith curse we men, which are made after the similitude of God. Out of the same mouth proceedeth blessing and cursing. My brethren, these things ought not so to be.James 3:7-10
Psalm 39 · I held my peace

My sorrow was stirred

I was dumb with silence, I held my peace, even from good; and my sorrow was stirred. My heart was hot within me, while I was musing the fire burned: then spake I with my tongue,Psalm 39:2-3

And it ought not be. But as David goes on to say, it can be difficult. He set out to do what was right, keeping silent even when it may have done good to speak, careful in his speech to not say the wrong thing, and in his silence, his sorrow was stirred.

Application • Silence

More harm than good

Mature Christians, especially parents with adult children, or pastors with a flock, should recognize this feeling. When we see others doing evil, or worse, people we love being led away towards it, and we know if we speak out, correct them, especially in anger, that we'd do more harm than good.

The situation where we aren't angry with the person, but we know what the Bible says. Where we gently suggest and correct, but withhold our own opinion because we know it will lead us to sin, and give the others cause to justify their own evil. Where we will do more harm than good.

My heart was hot within me, while I was musing the fire burned.

Psalm 39 · How frail I am

Make me to know mine end

Lord, make me to know mine end, and the measure of my days, what it is: that I may know how frail I am.Psalm 39:4

But David's first instinct is to open his mouth to the Lord, and in his sorrow and burning heart, he asks make me to know mine end, and the measure of my days, what it is: that I may know how frail I am. Asking, how much longer do I have to suffer this?

Psalm 39 · Altogether vanity

An handbreadth

Behold, thou hast made my days as an handbreadth; and mine age is as nothing before thee: verily every man at his best state is altogether vanity. Selah. Surely every man walketh in a vain shew: surely they are disquieted in vain: he heapeth up riches, and knoweth not who shall gather them.Psalm 39:5-6

Answering his own question, he notes the short lifespan of man, the handbreadth of years, especially compared to the eternal God. Verily every man at his best state is altogether vanity.

In the end, we know our life here is brief, and the pain we endure is nothing, especially compared to the glory to come and the eternity with the Lord.

For I reckon that the sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory which shall be revealed in us.Romans 8:18
Application • The measure of my days

Life is brutal and short

This perspective, the finite life vs everlasting, can be a comfort. But it also can be a sobering reminder. That when taken together, men's lives are vain. People experience needless worry, anxiety, and inner turmoil over fleeting things like the accumulations of wealth.

Life is brutal and short. Our own tongue betrays us. Those we see around us constantly striving for temporary gain, often at the expense of their spiritual good, only to die, taking nothing of it with them into eternity.

Psalm 39 · My hope is in thee

What wait I for?

And now, Lord, what wait I for? my hope is in thee. Deliver me from all my transgressions: make me not the reproach of the foolish. I was dumb, I opened not my mouth; because thou didst it.Psalm 39:7-9

For those of us with Heaven as our home, who knows that being absent from the body is to be present with the Lord, the futility of this world can be a sorrowful burden at times.

And the knowledge that the things that vex us, that the silence we take up is not only to keep us from sin, but recognizing that our complaints may well stem from how much we deserve what we get.

Psalm 39 · Rebukes

Remove thy stroke

Remove thy stroke away from me: I am consumed by the blow of thine hand. When thou with rebukes dost correct man for iniquity, thou makest his beauty to consume away like a moth: surely every man is vanity. Selah.Psalm 39:10-11

The strokes upon our back can be more than we can take. As children of God, being scourged, we can be consumed by His blows.

We grow old and weary from His correction. The wages of our sin is taxing, of the flesh and the heart.

Better to be silent, and contrite. But still, how long O Lord?

Psalm 39 · Hear my prayer

That I may recover strength

Hear my prayer, O Lord, and give ear unto my cry; hold not thy peace at my tears: for I am a stranger with thee, and a sojourner, as all my fathers were. O spare me, that I may recover strength, before I go hence, and be no more.Psalm 39:12-13

David's Psalms frequently call out in Godly sorrow, seeking restoration.

Restore unto me the joy of thy salvation; and uphold me with thy free spirit.Psalm 51:12

We may frequently ask God to bring us nearer to Thee. To spare me that I may recover strength, to better use the time we have left, not in vain pursuits, but in service to our Savior.

These are the prayers we can relate to, in our broken hearted times. Comfort we can count on during tribulations. Given for our example, and praise God for understanding our infirmities, but giving us hope. Amen.

Psalm 40 · I waited patiently

Out of the miry clay

I waited patiently for the Lord; and he inclined unto me, and heard my cry. He brought me up also out of an horrible pit, out of the miry clay, and set my feet upon a rock, and established my goings.Psalm 40:1-2

After the somber and broken-hearted appeal of Psalm 39, Psalm 40 gives us the rejoicing of those that trust in the Lord, and declares His power to all.

How wonderful is our patient waiting on the Lord, having called out His name for deliverance, to be rewarded by seeing Him turn towards us to save us in love!

He frees us from the miry pit of sin, He delivers us out of tribulation and temptations. He brings us from the shifting sands of doubt and fear, and sets us upon a rock. Our rock, Christ Jesus. The solid ground where He establishes our goings, and directs our paths. Praise Jesus for His great salvation!

Psalm 40 · Many shall see it

A new song in my mouth

And he hath put a new song in my mouth, even praise unto our God: many shall see it, and fear, and shall trust in the Lord. Blessed is that man that maketh the Lord his trust, and respecteth not the proud, nor such as turn aside to lies.Psalm 40:3-4

We who are saved have a new song in our heart, the gospel which we preach, the good news of eternal life and forgiveness in Christ, even praise unto our God: many shall see it, and fear, and shall trust in the LORD.

Is there any better news than this? Repenting from lies to trust in the Lord, and the blessings that we are given by His grace in exchange. Eternal life, His perfect word, His judgments. The Spirit of truth. The rejection of the proud. The deliverance from the evil of others. Made sons of God and joint-heirs with Jesus. Many are the wonderful works that our Lord God has done!

Psalm 40 · Thy wonderful works

More than can be numbered

Many, O Lord my God, are thy wonderful works which thou hast done, and thy thoughts which are to us-ward: they cannot be reckoned up in order unto thee: if I would declare and speak of them, they are more than can be numbered.Psalm 40:5

The tone of worship and blessings towards God in this prayer song of gratitude is as infectious as it is doctrinally rich.

The thoughts of the Lord towards us, that He even considered a wretch like me, the wonderful things He has for us cannot be numbered. There's not enough time or words in this life for us to declare all He has done for us.

All we can do is cry out, "Thank you!" And share this good news with others.

Psalm 40 · Sacrifice and offering

Mine ears hast thou opened

Sacrifice and offering thou didst not desire; mine ears hast thou opened: burnt offering and sin offering hast thou not required.Psalm 40:6

The blood of bulls and goats never took away sin. It may have cleansed the flesh to be in the presence of God in the temple made with hands, but to enter into His presence forever, in the Temple of Heaven, all He has ever required was faith in His promises.

So then faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.Romans 10:17
Cross references • Psalm 40:6

Hearing they hear not

Have they not heard? The word of God has gone out to the world and not returned void. But those that harden their hearts, and stiffen their necks and refuse to obey the gospel of God.

As Jesus and the prophets taught:

Therefore speak I to them in parables: because they seeing see not; and hearing they hear not, neither do they understand. And in them is fulfilled the prophecy of Esaias, which saith, By hearing ye shall hear, and shall not understand; and seeing ye shall see, and shall not perceive: For this people’s heart is waxed gross, and their ears are dull of hearing, and their eyes they have closed; lest at any time they should see with their eyes and hear with their ears, and should understand with their heart, and should be converted, and I should heal them. But blessed are your eyes, for they see: and your ears, for they hear.Matthew 13:13-16
Cross references • Psalm 40:6

Whosoever shall call

God has blessed us with the conviction of the Spirit, that even in the miry pit, we heard Him, and see with our eyes the mighty God, being converted and healed. Calling upon the name of the Lord.

For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.Romans 10:13
Psalm 40 · Thy law is within my heart

I delight to do thy will

Then said I, Lo, I come: in the volume of the book it is written of me, I delight to do thy will, O my God: yea, thy law is within my heart. I have preached righteousness in the great congregation: lo, I have not refrained my lips, O Lord, thou knowest. I have not hid thy righteousness within my heart; I have declared thy faithfulness and thy salvation: I have not concealed thy lovingkindness and thy truth from the great congregation.Psalm 40:7-10

Continuing in Psalm 40, rejoicing that God has given men ears to hear the truth, and the glory to God for His protection and comfort, we are called to spread this word, proclaiming it to all men.

We are delighted to do His will. His law written in our hearts.

Cross references • Psalm 40:8

After the inward man

For I delight in the law of God after the inward man:Romans 7:22

Even as our flesh may be prone to wander, the outward man waging war against the spirit, we can know His righteous paths, and preach them among the beloved believers. Declaring His faithfulness and the salvation of the Lord to all who will hear. Singing His praises, letting His light shine through us, magnifying His glory. Proclaiming His great Gospel.

Psalm 40 · Mine iniquities

Innumerable evils

Withhold not thou thy tender mercies from me, O Lord: let thy lovingkindness and thy truth continually preserve me. For innumerable evils have compassed me about: mine iniquities have taken hold upon me, so that I am not able to look up; they are more than the hairs of mine head: therefore my heart faileth me.Psalm 40:11-12

Having acknowledged all He has done for us, and encouraging ourselves and others to turn to Him for salvation through the blessing of the Lord, David's Psalm turns introspective. He confesses the sin that has beset him, and compasses him about with the repercussions of it.

We cannot acknowledge salvation without acknowledging what God is saving us from.

Cross references • Psalm 40:12

O wretched man that I am!

Just as David and Paul write of having the law written in their heart, and delighting in His word, they both lament the sinful flesh that requires a Savior.

O wretched man that I am! who shall deliver me from the body of this death? I thank God through Jesus Christ our Lord. So then with the mind I myself serve the law of God; but with the flesh the law of sin.Romans 7:24-25
Psalm 40 · Deliver me

Make haste to help me

Be pleased, O Lord, to deliver me: O Lord, make haste to help me. Let them be ashamed and confounded together that seek after my soul to destroy it; let them be driven backward and put to shame that wish me evil. Let them be desolate for a reward of their shame that say unto me, Aha, aha.Psalm 40:13-15

We ask that the Lord deliver us. That it is His will that be done. That those who are moved to scorn, mocking our trust in the Lord be made ashamed.

That God use us to shame those that wish to do us evil, to His glory.

Praise, thanksgiving, confession, deliverance, and destruction of those who hold the truth in unrighteousness. This is a model of prayer. That His will be done on earth as it is in Heaven.

Psalm 40 · The Lord be magnified

The Lord thinketh upon me

And in the end, the joy of His salvation overwhelming us with gratitude and praise, that such a holy and Almighty God takes heed of our prayers and notices us. Loving us so much, while we are yet sinners. Thinking of us. Providing for us. Delivering us.

Let all those that seek thee rejoice and be glad in thee: let such as love thy salvation say continually, The Lord be magnified. But I am poor and needy; yet the Lord thinketh upon me: thou art my help and my deliverer; make no tarrying, O my God.Psalm 40:16-17

Amen
