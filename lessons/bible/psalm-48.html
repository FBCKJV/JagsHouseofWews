Psalm 48 · Mount Zion

The city of the great King

Great is the Lord, and greatly to be praised in the city of our God, in the mountain of his holiness. Beautiful for situation, the joy of the whole earth, is mount Zion, on the sides of the north, the city of the great King.Psalm 48:1-2

Mount Zion, Jerusalem, is often referenced and praised as the place God chose to place His name. His holy city, the mountain providing natural protection on 3 sides, situated perfectly. A river running through the fertile valley. The setting where Abraham brought Isaac. Where God prepared Himself a sacrifice. Where Jesus was slain and rose again, and where He will reign in the Kingdom to come.

The name, Zion, means a “monument; raised up; sepulcher,” the place of the tomb, hewn out of rock, the site of Christ's burial. Chosen by God.

Beautiful for situation, a perfect location, the city of the great King on its north face.

Psalm 48 · The defense of Zion

Sennacherib

The defenses of this holy mountain were enough to dissuade kings and armies.

Sennacherib, of Assyria, the most powerful emperor of the ancient world, invaded Judah with a massive army of 185,000 soldiers.

He sent his top military commanders to the walls of Jerusalem, fiercely mocking King Hezekiah and blaspheming God, confidently expecting the city to fall just like every other nation before it.

Hezekiah called out to the Lord for protection of the city.

Psalm 48 · The holy land

The promise to Abraham

Psalm 48 broadly captures the grandeur and love of God towards His holy land. This love is seen long before Israel as a nation enters in, or even exists. It was in the beginning, when God promised a land to Abraham.

Now the Lord had said unto Abram, Get thee out of thy country, and from thy kindred, and from thy father’s house, unto a land that I will shew thee: And I will make of thee a great nation, and I will bless thee, and make thy name great; and thou shalt be a blessing: And I will bless them that bless thee, and curse him that curseth thee: and in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed.Genesis 12:1-3
Psalm 48 · The holy land

And to thy seed, which is Christ

The promise to Abraham was both for the land, and the Christ. Long before the exodus. Long before the wandering. Before Isaac was ever born, the land that would become Israel, the province of Judah, the city of Jerusalem, in the mountains of Zion, was set apart for God.

The blessings and inheritance made to Abraham, who believed God and it was counted for righteousness, was made to him and his seed. Jesus Christ.

Now to Abraham and his seed were the promises made. He saith not, And to seeds, as of many; but as of one, And to thy seed, which is Christ.Galatians 3:16

Zionism

Zionism is a modern political ideology that takes the promise to Abraham, and rather than give the inheritance and blessing to Christ, and all who are in Christ, gives it to unbelieving, apostate Jews.

For as many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ. There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither bond nor free, there is neither male nor female: for ye are all one in Christ Jesus. And if ye be Christ’s, then are ye Abraham’s seed, and heirs according to the promise.Galatians 3:27-29

Application • Zionism

Cannot disannul

Many Christians today will say "God keeps His promises! The Jews deserve this land!" They place political Zionism and the ethnic ultranationalism of the modern Jewish state as being part of an unconditional and irrevocable covenant with the people by heritage and genealogy.

But the Bible is clear, that the covenant made in the wilderness was explicitly conditional, that what was given to the people through Moses could not, by their failure, disannul the promise made to Abraham and his singular seed.

And this I say, that the covenant, that was confirmed before of God in Christ, the law, which was four hundred and thirty years after, cannot disannul, that it should make the promise of none effect. For if the inheritance be of the law, it is no more of promise: but God gave it to Abraham by promise.Galatians 3:17-18

Psalm 48 · God is known for a refuge

They hasted away

God is known in her palaces for a refuge. For, lo, the kings were assembled, they passed by together. They saw it, and so they marvelled; they were troubled, and hasted away. Fear took hold upon them there, and pain, as of a woman in travail.Psalm 48:3-6

This promise of God, to be a ready shelter for His people and to cause their enemies to marvel and fear His power, and flee, is demonstrated multiple times in scripture.

During the entry and conquest of the promised land, we see it happen to the 5 Kings and their armies.

Therefore the five kings of the Amorites, the king of Jerusalem, the king of Hebron, the king of Jarmuth, the king of Lachish, the king of Eglon, gathered themselves together, and went up, they and all their hosts, and encamped before Gibeon, and made war against it.Joshua 10:5
Cross references • Psalm 48:4–5

Fear them not

God promises Joshua that He will win them the victory.

And the Lord said unto Joshua, Fear them not: for I have delivered them into thine hand; there shall not a man of them stand before thee.Joshua 10:8

And God kept His promise.

And the Lord discomfited them before Israel, and slew them with a great slaughter at Gibeon, and chased them along the way that goeth up to Bethhoron, and smote them to Azekah, and unto Makkedah.Joshua 10:10
Cross references • Psalm 48:4–5

The strong hold of Zion

Ironically, despite the defeat of the King of Jerusalem, among all the others, Joshua was not able to conquer Jerusalem itself. This honor would fall on King David himself.

And the king and his men went to Jerusalem unto the Jebusites, the inhabitants of the land: which spake unto David, saying, Except thou take away the blind and the lame, thou shalt not come in hither: thinking, David cannot come in hither. Nevertheless David took the strong hold of Zion: the same is the city of David.2 Samuel 5:6-7

But this was not the only example of God confounding the enemies of the people, especially in the defense of Zion.
