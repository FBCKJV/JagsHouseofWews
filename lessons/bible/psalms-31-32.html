Psalm 31

The LORD God of truth • My times are in thy hand

✦ ✦ ✦
Cross references • Psalm 31:5

Father, into thy hands

And when Jesus had cried with a loud voice, he said, Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit: and having said thus, he gave up the ghost.Luke 23:46

David's Psalms of trust in the Lord God, to be delivered and never ashamed, redeemed in His righteousness, are models of prayer, and Jesus upon the cross quotes them (or rather, they quote Him). How much more should we?

Psalm 31 · In thee, O Lord

My rock and my fortress

In thee, O Lord, do I put my trust; let me never be ashamed: deliver me in thy righteousness. Bow down thine ear to me; deliver me speedily: be thou my strong rock, for an house of defence to save me. For thou art my rock and my fortress; therefore for thy name’s sake lead me, and guide me. Pull me out of the net that they have laid privily for me: for thou art my strength.Psalm 31:1-4

The cries to listen to our prayers and save us out of trouble, acknowledging that He is our rock and our fortress, our Savior, our ready help in time of tribulation, they echo through time and are as relevant to Christians today as they were to the King of Israel, and the congregation that sang them 3,000 years ago.

Psalm 31 · Into thine hand

O Lord God of truth

Into thine hand I commit my spirit: thou hast redeemed me, O Lord God of truth.Psalm 31:5

Trusting the God of truth to keep His word, having faith in His promises, is the foundation for our Christian life. Relying on His word to feed us with truth. Knowing the truth that sets us free.

And loving the truth puts us in a position where we hate those that lie about the Lord.

Psalm 31 · Lying vanities

I hate every false way

I have hated them that regard lying vanities: but I trust in the Lord.Psalm 31:6
Through thy precepts I get understanding: therefore I hate every false way.Psalm 119:104

This love of the truth and understanding of His word is a gift of the Spirit of God.

We are of God: he that knoweth God heareth us; he that is not of God heareth not us. Hereby know we the spirit of truth, and the spirit of error.1 John 4:6
Cross references • Psalm 31:6

Doctrines of devils

All things are made known to us by the Spirit, through Christ who is the way, the truth, the light.

And so we should be vexed by false prophets who teach damnable heresy.

Now the Spirit speaketh expressly, that in the latter times some shall depart from the faith, giving heed to seducing spirits, and doctrines of devils; Speaking lies in hypocrisy; having their conscience seared with a hot iron;1 Timothy 4:1-2

These false prophets are reprobates concerning the faith. Their lies make their followers twofold more the child of Hell than themselves. They are enemies of God, and we should hate them with a perfect hatred, while loving those misled by them enough to proclaim the truth.

Psalm 31 · Thy mercy

A large room

I will be glad and rejoice in thy mercy: for thou hast considered my trouble; thou hast known my soul in adversities; And hast not shut me up into the hand of the enemy: thou hast set my feet in a large room.Psalm 31:7-8

Continuing in Psalm 31, picking up in verse 9. The gladness and rejoicing in God's mercy and crying out to Him in the earlier stanzas, brings us the heart of the matter, the trouble in David's life, and reflected in our own struggles.

Psalm 31 · Have mercy upon me

I am in trouble

Have mercy upon me, O Lord, for I am in trouble: mine eye is consumed with grief, yea, my soul and my belly.Psalm 31:9

He says I am in trouble, consumed with grief and physically and spiritually affected by the tribulation in his life. This is something everyone alive can relate to. Anyone alive long enough to be broken hearted. Betrayed. Lied about and persecuted.

Psalm 31 · Grief

My life is spent with grief

For my life is spent with grief, and my years with sighing: my strength faileth because of mine iniquity, and my bones are consumed.Psalm 31:10

When we are going through it, it can feel as though all we ever have known is grief. We can feel all alone, when not only our enemies abandon us, but our neighbors and friends won't even look at us. So miserable and alone.

Psalm 31 · A broken vessel

Forgotten as a dead man

I was a reproach among all mine enemies, but especially among my neighbours, and a fear to mine acquaintance: they that did see me without fled from me. I am forgotten as a dead man out of mind: I am like a broken vessel. For I have heard the slander of many: fear was on every side: while they took counsel together against me, they devised to take away my life.Psalm 31:11-13

We may feel forgotten as a dead man, out of sight out of mind. Truly miserable. Feeling as though the whole world has conspired against us. Having nowhere to turn or anyone to trust.

Psalm 31 · Thou art my God

My times are in thy hand

But I trusted in thee, O Lord: I said, Thou art my God. My times are in thy hand: deliver me from the hand of mine enemies, and from them that persecute me. Make thy face to shine upon thy servant: save me for thy mercies’ sake.Psalm 31:14-16

But I trusted in thee, O LORD: I said, Thou art my God. What a beautiful reminder that we have a friend in Jesus. We have a Lord who is trustworthy and Who cares. Whose face shines on us, and saves us. Not because of how good we are, but despite our sin. For His mercies' sake. Because He so loved us.

Psalm 31 · Let me not be ashamed

Let the wicked be ashamed

Let me not be ashamed, O Lord; for I have called upon thee: let the wicked be ashamed, and let them be silent in the grave.Psalm 31:17

We plead, Let me not be ashamed, O LORD; for I have called upon thee, and we know that God, Who cannot lie, has promised us deliverance. Let the wicked be ashamed, and let them be silent in the grave. We will sing His praise forever, while they are tormented by unquenchable fire, where the worm dieth not.

Cross references • Psalm 31:17

Where their worm dieth not

And they shall go forth, and look upon the carcases of the men that have transgressed against me: for their worm shall not die, neither shall their fire be quenched; and they shall be an abhorring unto all flesh.Isaiah 66:24
And if thine eye offend thee, pluck it out: it is better for thee to enter into the kingdom of God with one eye, than having two eyes to be cast into hell fire: Where their worm dieth not, and the fire is not quenched. For every one shall be salted with fire, and every sacrifice shall be salted with salt.Mark 9:47-49
Psalm 31 · Put to silence

Lying lips

All those that have denied the truth of God shall be silenced. They shall be made to be ashamed. Let the wicked be ashamed, but we have trusted the Lord.

Let the lying lips be put to silence; which speak grievous things proudly and contemptuously against the righteous.Psalm 31:18
Psalm 31 · Thy goodness

Oh how great is thy goodness

Oh how great is thy goodness, which thou hast laid up for them that fear thee; which thou hast wrought for them that trust in thee before the sons of men!Psalm 31:19

The culmination of the Psalm is a tribute to the richness of God's mercy and fidelity in times of trouble, and the sure destruction of those that hate Him.

Psalm 31 · Thy marvellous kindness

The strife of tongues

Thou shalt hide them in the secret of thy presence from the pride of man: thou shalt keep them secretly in a pavilion from the strife of tongues. Blessed be the Lord: for he hath shewed me his marvellous kindness in a strong city. For I said in my haste, I am cut off from before thine eyes: nevertheless thou heardest the voice of my supplications when I cried unto thee.Psalm 31:20-22

God shall hide those that trust in Him from the wrath to come. Shield us from the wicked, guard us from the strife of their tongues, their slander and railing will come to nothing.

Bless the Lord for His marvelous kindness. For the protection He has granted those that trust in Him. Even when we feel alone and desperate. Even the great prophet, Elijah, fresh from a great work for God, and a spiritual highpoint, felt alone and in his haste thought he was cut off from before God's eyes.

Cross references • Psalm 31:22

I have reserved to myself

Lord, they have killed thy prophets, and digged down thine altars; and I am left alone, and they seek my life. But what saith the answer of God unto him? I have reserved to myself seven thousand men, who have not bowed the knee to the image of Baal.Romans 11:3-4

We might have moments of doubt, when God's perfect timing isn't lining up with our own. When we think we cannot go on. That there are Jezebels and her men after us. That we are all alone. But God has reserved His own. We will be preserved. We can comfort ourselves and others with this truth.

Psalm 31 · Be of good courage

O love the Lord, all ye his saints

O love the Lord, all ye his saints: for the Lord preserveth the faithful, and plentifully rewardeth the proud doer. Be of good courage, and he shall strengthen your heart, all ye that hope in the Lord.Psalm 31:23-24

Amen.

The blessedness of forgiveness

Psalm 32 · Blessed

Whose transgression is forgiven

Blessed is he whose transgression is forgiven, whose sin is covered. Blessed is the man unto whom the Lord imputeth not iniquity, and in whose spirit there is no guile.Psalm 32:1-2

Psalm 32 opens with a very familiar and important doctrine, the understanding of God's forgiveness for sin in the saved believer's life. This foundational fact is repeated by Paul in the letter to the Romans;

Cross references • Psalm 32:1–2

Righteousness without works

Even as David also describeth the blessedness of the man, unto whom God imputeth righteousness without works, Saying, Blessed are they whose iniquities are forgiven, and whose sins are covered. Blessed is the man to whom the Lord will not impute sin.Romans 4:6-8

The truth is that, in Christ, whether it was in David's day, or Paul's or our own, God forgives our past transgressions, covers our current sin, and will not hold our future sins and iniquity against us.

Psalm 32 · The new man

No guile

This positional standing of righteousness before God, despite our sins, through His imputed righteousness is made possible, through the Holy Spirit, by the renewal, the regeneration, the quickening of our spirit. The new man within us. Blessed is the man in whose spirit there is no guile.

We are given the Spirit of truth, that we may acknowledge our sin, knowing that we are forgiven of all sin, first, not by works but by faith.

The same good news today as in David's day. And this Psalm serves as an evangelical appeal.

Psalm 32 · Thy hand was heavy

When I kept silence

When I kept silence, my bones waxed old through my roaring all the day long. For day and night thy hand was heavy upon me: my moisture is turned into the drought of summer. Selah.Psalm 32:3-4

David writes of a man who kept his silence, who hadn't called upon the name of the Lord, who suffered in their sins, bones waxing old and dried up by their roaring in the daytime, the heaviness of their iniquity weighing on them at night.

Psalm 32 · Confession

I acknowledged my sin

I acknowledged my sin unto thee, and mine iniquity have I not hid. I said, I will confess my transgressions unto the Lord; and thou forgavest the iniquity of my sin. Selah.Psalm 32:5

But the honesty and humility of acknowledging sin, not hiding anything from God (as if we could), confessing our transgressions unto the Lord, and being forgiven, is a blessing. Roaring in the daytime and being kept up at night by our conscience, being convicted in the Spirit, gives way to the peace that passes understanding, through the forgiveness that is given to us by God's mercy and grace.

Psalm 32 · A time when thou mayest be found

While he may be found

For this shall every one that is godly pray unto thee in a time when thou mayest be found: surely in the floods of great waters they shall not come nigh unto him.Psalm 32:6

David reminds us that there is a time where God may be sought, before it is too late.

Seek ye the Lord while he may be found, call ye upon him while he is near:Isaiah 55:6

God's Spirit will not always strive with man. There comes a time when those that put off or reject the truth, not acknowledging that they are sinners, lying to God and themselves, are no longer able to come to Him. Where He no longer extends His hand, because they have hardened their hearts and will not seek Him, even as they face destruction.

Cross references • Psalm 32:6

To day if ye will hear his voice

Again, he limiteth a certain day, saying in David, To day, after so long a time; as it is said, To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts.Hebrews 4:7

We have the example of those in the days of Noah, who mocked him for his faith in preparing the ark, and whose disobedient spirits who Christ preached to in Hell, were destroyed by the flood, never coming nigh unto God. Just as those left to God's wrath to come will not repent, despite the horrors of His judgment.

Cross references • Psalm 32:6

Now is the day of salvation

There is a time, it is today. There is a day, and it is the present. If you haven't accepted the truth that we are sinners in need of salvation, and that it is offered as a free gift to all who believe, through the grace of Almighty God, now is the time.

(For he saith, I have heard thee in a time accepted, and in the day of salvation have I succoured thee: behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation.)2 Corinthians 6:2
Psalm 32 · Songs of deliverance

Thou art my hiding place

Those who have heard and responded to the truth, who in the appointed day prayed unto Him, can rest assured that we are saved.

Thou art my hiding place; thou shalt preserve me from trouble; thou shalt compass me about with songs of deliverance. Selah.Psalm 32:7

This is the hope we have. The same hope the congregations of the children of Israel sang of in the temple of Solomon, with the songs of his father echoing this blessed promise, centuries before the first coming of Christ Jesus.

Psalm 32 · I will guide thee

Not as the horse, or as the mule

I will instruct thee and teach thee in the way which thou shalt go: I will guide thee with mine eye. Be ye not as the horse, or as the mule, which have no understanding: whose mouth must be held in with bit and bridle, lest they come near unto thee.Psalm 32:8-9

And God responds to His children, in this inspired Psalm. Promising to teach and guide us in His ways. He asks we come willingly. That we have understanding to walk with Him in His ways, and not be like the horse or mule, needing a bit and bridle to pull us along. His yoke is light. The burden is easy.

Psalm 32 · He that trusteth in the Lord

Mercy shall compass him about

Many sorrows shall be to the wicked: but he that trusteth in the Lord, mercy shall compass him about.Psalm 32:10

The wicked may suffer their sorrows of their own making, but we who are forgiven by His mercy because we trust in the Lord, are preserved. What an amazing relief to know we are already forgiven. That we may come boldly before God to sing His praises, glorifying Him for His mercies' sake.

Psalm 32 · Rejoice, ye righteous

Shout for joy

Be glad in the Lord, and rejoice, ye righteous: and shout for joy, all ye that are upright in heart.Psalm 32:11

Amen!

Psalm 32 · Transgression, sin and iniquity

Transgression

While it isn't entirely wrong to take the words transgression, sin and iniquity, and use them interchangeably, they do carry subtle differences in their meaning.

Transgression is the wilful rebellion against the truth of God and His commands. The transgression of Adam, for example, having one law to obey, and choosing of their own volition (even as Eve was deceived, Adam most assuredly knew better), deciding to be disobedient.

Psalm 32 · Transgression, sin and iniquity

Sin

Sin, which the Bible says is the transgression of the Law, also carries with it the idea of missing the mark, falling short.

For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God;Romans 3:23

We aren't able, in the flesh, to hit the mark, the target being the perfect holiness of God. Only Jesus Christ did.

For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.2 Corinthians 5:21
Psalm 32 · Transgression, sin and iniquity

Iniquity

Iniquity comes from the Hebrew word, Avon, meaning to twist, bend, or distort. The perverted and crooked nature of fallen man. The bent toward evil, the guilt that stays with us, and the perverted character that drives the outward actions. It is the guile or deceit mentioned in verse 2.

Our natural tendencies towards willful rebellion, to sin, and to lie to ourselves and others, including God, about the evil that we do. Excusing ourselves with lies.

Cross references • Psalm 32:1–2

Wounded for our transgressions

Taken together, the blessed assurance that our transgressions, sins and iniquities are eternally forgiven demonstrates the mercy and grace of our Lord towards His children, through the suffering of Jesus.

But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed. All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned every one to his own way; and the Lord hath laid on him the iniquity of us all.Isaiah 53:5-6
Psalm 32 · Justified in Christ

The ledger

He suffered these things for our iniquity and transgressions, that we may be purged. That His righteousness be imputed on our ledger, even as His blood blots away our sins.

Our transgressions are forgiven, our sin is covered, our iniquity will not be added to our account, because we are justified in Christ.

This accounting is a spiritual gift, applied to the ledger that in judgment, we are found not guilty by God.

Psalm 32 · Justified in Christ

Past, present and future

(Some call this Penal Substitutionary Atonement, the idea that Jesus paid the price of the debt we owe, so that we do not have to receive the wages of sin. Many reject this idea. Whatever you want to call it, it is undeniable that Christ suffered that those who trust on Him, won't)

On the cross, Jesus carried away our rebellion, covered our failures with His blood, and cleared the ledger of our crooked nature by imputing His own perfect righteousness to us. Past, present and future. That we may be made the righteousness of God.

Cross references • Leviticus 16

The day of atonement

And Aaron shall lay both his hands upon the head of the live goat, and confess over him all the iniquities of the children of Israel, and all their transgressions in all their sins, putting them upon the head of the goat, and shall send him away by the hand of a fit man into the wilderness: And the goat shall bear upon him all their iniquities unto a land not inhabited: and he shall let go the goat in the wilderness.Leviticus 16:21-22
For on that day shall the priest make an atonement for you, to cleanse you, that ye may be clean from all your sins before the Lord.Leviticus 16:30

Cross references • Leviticus 16

One sacrifice for all time

This concept of atonement by the sacrifice, the scapegoat and the blood, is established in Leviticus 16.

Rather than the picture of the imperfect sacrifice, the blood of bulls and goats which could never take away our sin, we have the flawless Lamb, Who is also the ultimate High Priest, and one sacrifice for all time.

By the which will we are sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all. And every priest standeth daily ministering and offering oftentimes the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins: But this man, after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God; From henceforth expecting till his enemies be made his footstool. For by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified.Hebrews 10:10-14
Psalm 32 · Covered

The work is finished

He had no sin of His own to sacrifice for. He did not enter a man-made temple, but Heaven itself. He did not offer the blood of animals, but His own flawless blood.

Because of what Jesus did as both the High Priest and the Sacrifice, the work is finished. The ledger is cleared permanently. God no longer imputes our iniquity, our sins are eternally covered, and our transgressions have been carried as far as the east is from the west. And God will remember them no more.

Cross references • Hebrews 10

Every priest standeth daily

And every priest standeth daily ministering and offering oftentimes the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins:Hebrews 10:11

In the physical Tabernacle and the Temple of Solomon, there were no chairs. A priest could never sit down because his job was never done. The blood of bulls and goats only provided a temporary cover for the remembrance of sins made again every year, meaning the priest had to stay on his feet, continuously working, because he could never truly take away sins.

Cross references • Hebrews 10

He sat down

But then we see Jesus, the author and most importantly, the finisher of our faith, sits down, on the right hand of the Father.

But this man, after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God; From henceforth expecting till his enemies be made his footstool. For by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified.Hebrews 10:12-14

The contrast of those who stood, continually ministering, versus Jesus, Who sat down forever, having perfected them that are sanctified, is a subtle but crucial detail. An interesting side note to the study, that we shouldn't overlook.
