A harrowing time, likely more than a decade where the future King lives as a fugitive, hunted, lied about and sought by wicked men in King Saul's service.

1 Samuel 19 · The window

Michal let David down through a window

Saul also sent messengers unto David’s house, to watch him, and to slay him in the morning: and Michal David’s wife told him, saying, If thou save not thy life to night, to morrow thou shalt be slain. So Michal let David down through a window: and he went, and fled, and escaped.1 Samuel 19:11-12

Psalm 59 was written as Saul, David's King and father in law sent men to David's house to capture and kill him, while Michal, David's wife and daughter of the King aided in his escape, being lowered from the window to flee.

And we see that David's faithfulness towards God as His deliverer was present from the beginning.

About 3 minutes in

Psalm 59 · The window

Without my fault

They run and prepare themselves without my fault: awake to help me, and behold.Psalm 59:4

He trusts God to deliver him from the bloody men coming round his home, barking like dogs, saying cruel lies. David is innocent of wrongdoing, yet they seek his life. Without fault, pursued unto death, he cries out for the Lord to awake and help him.

This is similar to the prayer of Jesus, the sinless Son of God, in the hours leading up to His arrest. He, like David, could have asked for deliverance, and a host of angels to come and save Him. But for the sake of the cross, Jesus allowed them to take Him.

However, the willingness of Christ to be obedient unto death doesn't change the fact of Almighty God being able to deliver in a time of persecution.

Psalm 59 · The window

The God of my mercy shall prevent me

The God of my mercy shall prevent me: God shall let me see my desire upon mine enemies.Psalm 59:10

When David writes, The God of my mercy shall prevent me, he isn't using the modern understanding of the word, to stop or hinder, rather the actual definition of the word; to "go before" or "anticipate." His faith is that the mercy of God would be there with him, providing that means of escape. Knowing that the Lord would not leave him ashamed.

The mercy of God has already mapped out the boundary of the trial, and the window exit would be the means God had already allowed.

And while Jesus rejected the hosts to save Him, David took the window, the way God made for his escape.

Psalm 59 · The window

Thy mercy in the morning

But I will sing of thy power; yea, I will sing aloud of thy mercy in the morning: for thou hast been my defence and refuge in the day of my trouble.Psalm 59:16

And, the night past, the fleeing David sings of the mercy of God in the morning, even as the dogs are barking around his door.

Just as David found deliverance in the morning, Christ’s voluntary obedience unto death led to the ultimate morning of the resurrection. Because He did not take the window, He broke the power of death entirely, ensuring that for every believer facing tribulation, hope maketh not ashamed.

But God uses all things for good.

1 Samuel 21 · Gath

He feigned himself mad

And David laid up these words in his heart, and was sore afraid of Achish the king of Gath. And he changed his behaviour before them, and feigned himself mad in their hands, and scrabbled on the doors of the gate, and let his spittle fall down upon his beard.1 Samuel 21:12-13

David has been delivered to the Kingdom of Gath by his flight. He stood at the gates, to seek an audience with King Achish. But, he notices the words of the guards, and the heightened senses sparked by fear cause him to sense potential trouble.

In this case fear helped David to react quickly. Rather than being taken captive and potentially turned over to Saul, David's feigned madness allows him to escape and continue his flight. His short-term fear protected him in a harrowing situation.

About 11 minutes in

Psalm 56 · Gath

What time I am afraid

What time I am afraid, I will trust in thee.Psalm 56:3

Psalm 56 was written commemorating the flight of David from Saul, when it seemed all Israel was against him and sought David's life. He prays for mercy from the Lord, in the midst of this tribulation, reminding himself that, when he is afraid, he will trust in God.

Being fearful is a sin. Having fear itself is not. We see David throughout the Psalms express fear. He is a mighty man of valor, great in battle, a giant-killer, a man of great faith, and yet he feels fear.

For some of us, What time I am afraid, I will trust in thee could be a life-verse.

Psalm 56 · Gath

Fear and being fearful

In God I will praise his word, in God I have put my trust; I will not fear what flesh can do unto me.Psalm 56:4

Fear is a natural response to the threats against us and those that we love. Fear is something God has given us in order to react to immediate stimuli and avoid danger. Fear saves lives.

But being fearful, the condition where we take that warning system, and become slaves to it, where we allow present troubles of the world and being careful for everything, leave us unable to move, or to see the power of God in our lives. Our God is bigger than all the cares and tribulations of this life.

The righteous man isn't fearless, he fears less.

Psalm 56 · Gath

Put thou my tears into thy bottle

Thou tellest my wanderings: put thou my tears into thy bottle: are they not in thy book?Psalm 56:8

God loves His children, and knows our pain and struggles. He savors our prayer and captures our tears and numbers every step that we take, that He may direct our paths. Jesus says the same, speaking directly to us.

The Lord knows our troubles and hears us in adversity. We have a Savior, Who the Bible says, understands what we go through. He will never leave us or forsake us. We who praise His name and lean on His mercy, will never be ashamed.

Psalm 34 · Gath

I sought the LORD, and he heard me

I sought the Lord, and he heard me, and delivered me from all my fears.Psalm 34:4

David's confidence at the time of fear, fleeing from Saul and finding himself being challenged at the gates of Gath led to his deliverance. Psalm 34 tells of this. It is a complement to Psalm 56. The Bible says it is, A Psalm of David, when he changed his behaviour before Abimelech; who drove him away, and he departed.

David credits the Lord with hearing and answering his cry. And for this, David gives joyful thanksgiving.

The confidence of ends in the song of worship, praising His name in Psalm 34.

Psalm 34 · Gath

O taste and see

O taste and see that the Lord is good: blessed is the man that trusteth in him.Psalm 34:8

Give thanks in the congregation when God has moved in your life. Praise His holy name among the people. Teach others to fear the Lord. Tell them of the works He has done in your life.

While we are promised a longer life with more blessings, we are not promised a carefree life that has no suffering. Often the suffering we experience is for our benefit to help us grow in the Lord, and to increase our faith.

Our fear leads to faith, by casting our cares upon the Lord. Our faith leads to proclaiming the truth of Christ, teaching men to fear only God.

1 Samuel 21–22 · Nob

Doeg the Edomite

Now a certain man of the servants of Saul was there that day, detained before the Lord; and his name was Doeg, an Edomite, the chiefest of the herdmen that belonged to Saul.1 Samuel 21:7

David seeks food and weapons at the Tabernacle in Nob, from the unsuspecting priest, Ahimelech. But the priest isn’t the only person there to see David. Doeg is not a man of war. He is a herdsman. But he took the opportunity of seeing David there in Nob, on the run, to curry favor with Saul.

Saul refuses clemency. In his murderous rage he orders the death of the priest and entire lineage. Even Saul’s loyal men hesitate and refuse the order of their King, out of fear of the Lord and harming His anointed priests. But not Doeg the Edomite.

About 22 minutes in

Psalm 52 · Nob

O mighty man

Why boastest thou thyself in mischief, O mighty man? the goodness of God endureth continually.Psalm 52:1

Doeg the shepherd, Doeg the herdsman, who brought out of date intelligence to Saul’s attention, jeopardizing the priest, that led to this situation, took full advantage of the license given him by Saul, and wickedly carried out the command. First slaying 85 priests, and then murdering every man, woman, and child, beast and all, found in the Levite city of Nob.

But Doeg became a byword. Psalm 52 mockingly called Doeg a "mighty man," for slaying men of God, dressed only in linen, and helpless women and children, even babies.

Psalm 52 · Nob

Root thee out of the land of the living

God shall likewise destroy thee for ever, he shall take thee away, and pluck thee out of thy dwelling place, and root thee out of the land of the living. Selah.Psalm 52:5

We can imagine Doeg’s fate, the Bible doesn’t speak specifically of what became of him afterwards, but Psalm 52 gives us some idea of his ultimate fate before God.

If "selah" is meant to be a pause, a moment to reflect on what was just sung or said, is there anywhere more fitting than in that moment, having heard that sobering truth? And the righteous shall see their end and fear God, and laugh at the calamity they brought about on themselves.

Psalm 52 · Nob

Like a green olive tree

But I am like a green olive tree in the house of God: I trust in the mercy of God for ever and ever.Psalm 52:8

As the sole survivor of the high priestly line of Eli, Abiathar brings the official religious authority of Israel to David’s camp, signaling that God has abandoned Saul.

The evil of Saul and the bloodthirst of Doeg, became good for David, who loved God and was called according to His purpose. David trusted in the mercy of the Lord, and was strengthened by Doeg’s actions, and was able to sing of the praises of the Lord because of it.

But we are His. We have humbled ourselves to rely on His holy name.
