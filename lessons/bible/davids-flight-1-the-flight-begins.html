Psalm 59

Thou therefore, O Lord God of hosts, the God of Israel, awake to visit all the heathen: be not merciful to any wicked transgressors. Selah. They return at evening: they make a noise like a dog, and go round about the city.Psalm 59:1-6

1 Samuel 19 · The window

Michal let David down through a window

We often forget the flights of David were the impetus of so many Psalms. Psalm 59 was written as Saul, David's King and father in law sent men to David's house to capture and kill him, while Michal, David's wife and daughter of the King aided in his escape, being lowered from the window to flee.

And Saul sought to smite David even to the wall with the javelin, but he slipped away out of Saul’s presence, and he smote the javelin into the wall: and David fled, and escaped that night. Saul also sent messengers unto David’s house, to watch him, and to slay him in the morning: and Michal David’s wife told him, saying, If thou save not thy life to night, to morrow thou shalt be slain. So Michal let David down through a window: and he went, and fled, and escaped.1 Samuel 19:10-12
1 Samuel 19 · The window

Michal let David down through a window, continued

And Michal took an image, and laid it in the bed, and put a pillow of goats’ hair for his bolster, and covered it with a cloth. And when Saul sent messengers to take David, she said, He is sick. And Saul sent the messengers again to see David, saying, Bring him up to me in the bed, that I may slay him. And when the messengers were come in, behold, there was an image in the bed, with a pillow of goats’ hair for his bolster. And Saul said unto Michal, Why hast thou deceived me so, and sent away mine enemy, that he is escaped? And Michal answered Saul, He said unto me, Let me go; why should I kill thee? So David fled, and escaped, and came to Samuel to Ramah, and told him all that Saul had done to him. And he and Samuel went and dwelt in Naioth.1 Samuel 19:13-18
Psalm 59 · The window

A harrowing time

A harrowing time, likely more than a decade where the future King lives as a fugitive, hunted, lied about and sought by wicked men in King Saul's service.

And we see that David's faithfulness towards God as His deliverer was present from the beginning.

Psalm 59 · The window

Without fault

He trusts God to deliver him from the bloody men coming round his home, barking like dogs, saying cruel lies.

David is innocent of wrongdoing, yet they seek his life. Without fault, pursued unto death, he cries out for the Lord to awake and help him.

Psalm 59 · The window

The prayer of Jesus

This is similar to the prayer of Jesus, the sinless Son of God, in the hours leading up to His arrest.

And he went a little farther, and fell on his face, and prayed, saying, O my Father, if it be possible, let this cup pass from me: nevertheless not as I will, but as thou wilt.Matthew 26:39
Psalm 59 · The window

The joy set before Him

Like David, Jesus was sought by workers of iniquity, bloody men. Falsely accused, and yet knowing the joy set before Him, endured.

Looking unto Jesus the author and finisher of our faith; who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross, despising the shame, and is set down at the right hand of the throne of God.Hebrews 12:2
Psalm 59 · The window

An angel from heaven

Jesus, knowing what was set before Him, prayed.

And there appeared an angel unto him from heaven, strengthening him.Luke 22:43
Psalm 59 · The window

Twelve legions of angels

He, like David, could have asked for deliverance, and a host of angels to come and save Him.

Thinkest thou that I cannot now pray to my Father, and he shall presently give me more than twelve legions of angels?Matthew 26:53
Psalm 59 · The window

Obedient unto death

But for the sake of the cross, Jesus allowed them to take Him.

And being found in fashion as a man, he humbled himself, and became obedient unto death, even the death of the cross.Philippians 2:8
Psalm 59 · The window

Able to deliver

However, the willingness of Christ to be obedient unto death doesn't change the fact of Almighty God being able to deliver in a time of persecution.

David being an image of the persecuted Son, being sinless in the matter with which he was accused, not sinless himself, shows the general application of deliverance when we are wrongly persecuted.

Psalm 59 · The window

Let this mind be in you

If we Let this mind be in you, which was also in Christ Jesus , and have a willingness to suffer tribulation, while trusting on the Lord, like David, to protect us, we will not be ashamed.

It is a faithful saying: For if we be dead with him, we shall also live with him: If we suffer, we shall also reign with him: if we deny him, he also will deny us:2 Timothy 2:11-12
Psalm 59 · The window

Let this mind be in you, continued

Yea, and all that will live godly in Christ Jesus shall suffer persecution.2 Timothy 3:12
Yet if any man suffer as a Christian, let him not be ashamed; but let him glorify God on this behalf.1 Peter 4:16
Psalm 59 · The window

A way to escape

God is able to deliver us;

There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.1 Corinthians 10:13
Psalm 59 · The window

The mind of Christ

David had the mind of Christ. He glorified God in his temptations and trials. He counted God's mercy and judgement as something to grasp, to know for sure that He is, and a deliverer of those that call upon Him.

The God of my mercy shall prevent me: God shall let me see my desire upon mine enemies.Psalm 59:10
Unto thee, O my strength, will I sing: for God is my defence, and the God of my mercy.Psalm 59:17
Psalm 59 · The window

A man after God's own heart

David continues in tribulation for years, maintaining this outlook, as witnessed across the Psalm, from this initial trial, through the time in the wilderness, until God rewarded him with his crown. A man after God's own heart.

The late evening setting of Psalm 59, as the men dispatched by Saul come for David, mirrors the midnight garden setting when the high priests men came for Jesus.

Psalm 59 · The window

David took the window

David knew he would be delivered because he believed God, he knew the line that would eventually produce the incarnation of Christ was promised, the eternal Kingdom. And while Jesus rejected the hosts to save Him, David took the window, the way God made for his escape.

Psalm 59 · The window

All things for good

The betrayal of Saul matches the betrayal of Judas, trusted men turning on the blameless, in service of evil.

But God uses all things for good.

Psalm 59 · The window

The God of my mercy shall prevent me

When David writes, The God of my mercy shall prevent me, he isn't using the modern understanding of the word, to stop or hinder, rather the actual definition of the word; to "go before" or "anticipate." His faith is that the mercy of God would be there with him, providing that means of escape. Knowing that the Lord would not leave him ashamed.

The mercy of God has already mapped out the boundary of the trial, and the window exit would be the means God had already allowed.

Psalm 59 · The window

In the morning

And, the night past, the fleeing David sings of the mercy of God in the morning, even as the dogs are barking around his door.

Just as David found deliverance in the morning, Christ’s voluntary obedience unto death led to the ultimate morning of the resurrection.

Psalm 59 · The window

Hope maketh not ashamed

Because He did not take the window, He broke the power of death entirely, ensuring that for every believer facing tribulation, hope maketh not ashamed;

And hope maketh not ashamed; because the love of God is shed abroad in our hearts by the Holy Ghost which is given unto us.Romans 5:5

Amen

Psalm 56

Every day they wrest my words: all their thoughts are against me for evil. They gather themselves together, they hide themselves, they mark my steps, when they wait for my soul. Shall they escape by iniquity? in thine anger cast down the people, O God.Psalm 56:1-7

Psalm 56 · Gath

When I am afraid

Psalm 56 was written commemorating the flight of David from Saul, when it seemed all Israel was against him and sought David's life.

He prays for mercy from the Lord, in the midst of this tribulation, reminding himself that, when he is afraid, he will trust in God.

Psalm 56 · Gath

A life-verse

For some of us, What time I am afraid, I will trust in thee could be a life-verse.

Being fearful is a sin. Having fear itself is not. This is an important distinction that we should constantly remind ourselves of. We see David throughout the Psalms express fear. He is a mighty man of valor, great in battle, a giant-killer, a man of great faith, and yet he feels fear.

Psalm 56 · Gath

Fear and being fearful

Fear is a natural response to the threats against us and those that we love. Fear is something God has given us in order to react to immediate stimuli and avoid danger. To elevate our awareness in situations where we have to be on guard. Fear saves lives.

But being fearful, the condition where we take that warning system, and become slaves to it, where we allow present troubles of the world and being careful for everything, leave us unable to move, or to see the power of God in our lives.

Psalm 56 · Gath

Despair comes from fearfulness

Despair comes from fearfulness. Sin is at the door of those trapped in it. We are at our worst when we allow the stress of fear to blind us, bind us and send us into spiraling worry, where we begin to feel fear for things that haven't even presented themselves. For future events we have only seen in our vain imagination. When rather than hope in God that all things will work for good, we see only evil and destruction.

Psalm 56 · Gath

Be not afraid

Our God is bigger than all the cares and tribulations of this life. And fearing only God trumps anything else we may be afraid of.

The myth that the KJV has 365 verses saying not to fear isn't true but there are many, many times, where God tells us to be not afraid, for He is with us. The Bible uses variations of "fear not" or "be not afraid" approximately 100 to 150 times, with the broader theme of finding courage because God is with us appearing over 300 times throughout the entire Scripture.

Psalm 56 · Gath

Fear is a test of faith

Fear is a test of faith. Not a test of salvation, it takes an incredibly tiny amount of trust to believe God and accept Christ, and we are not saying that being afraid should shake that trust or make you question your eternal life. David, the apple of God's eye, felt fear. Again, it is a natural reaction to danger.

Psalm 56 · Gath

To have peace and rest in the Lord

But the Christian walk is not about finding enough faith to trust Jesus to save you, but about building upon that foundation a progressive reliance on God to handle everything in that life, that we may live it more abundantly. To have peace and rest in the Lord.

And so, fear is a test of faith. Does it make you fearful, and bring you low, to react in sin, or does it humble you to giving that dread up to be handled by Him, and have confidence in that very trial that God will see you through?

Psalm 56 · Gath

He fears less

Fear is an opportunity to increase your faith and bring your feet to stay in His ways, to walk His paths.

The righteous man isn't fearless, he fears less. Casting all his cares upon God, trusting in His deliverance. His judgments. Knowing that whatever may come, He has you in His hands.

Fear thou not; for I am with thee: be not dismayed; for I am thy God: I will strengthen thee; yea, I will help thee; yea, I will uphold thee with the right hand of my righteousness.Isaiah 41:10
Psalm 56 · Gath

Fear only God

We should only fear God. Let Him handle the rest.

And fear not them which kill the body, but are not able to kill the soul: but rather fear him which is able to destroy both soul and body in hell.Matthew 10:28
Psalm 56 · Gath

Fear not, for I am with thee

God tells us again and again Fear not, for I am with thee. David confesses this truth again and again, and places his safety and security entirely upon the Lord.

In God I will praise his word, in God I have put my trust; I will not fear what flesh can do unto me.Psalm 56:4
Psalm 56 · Gath

Threatened daily

David is threatened daily, by enemies and betrayers within the Kingdom, and without. By Saul and his armies, turncoats and opportunistic men. Hearing lies and rumors repeated against him, spread by the wicked to poison opinions against him.

1 Samuel 21 · Gath

At the gates of Gath

Here in Psalm 56, David has been delivered to the Kingdom of Gath by his flight. He stood at the gates, to seek an audience with King Achish. But, he notices the words of the guards, and the heightened senses sparked by fear cause him to sense potential trouble.

And David arose, and fled that day for fear of Saul, and went to Achish the king of Gath. And the servants of Achish said unto him, Is not this David the king of the land? did they not sing one to another of him in dances, saying, Saul hath slain his thousands, and David his ten thousands? And David laid up these words in his heart, and was sore afraid of Achish the king of Gath. And he changed his behaviour before them, and feigned himself mad in their hands, and scrabbled on the doors of the gate, and let his spittle fall down upon his beard.1 Samuel 21:10-13
1 Samuel 21 · Gath

Lo, ye see the man is mad

In this case fear helped David to react quickly. Acting like a madman when he hears the guards recognize who he is.

Then said Achish unto his servants, Lo, ye see the man is mad: wherefore then have ye brought him to me? Have I need of mad men, that ye have brought this fellow to play the mad man in my presence? shall this fellow come into my house?1 Samuel 21:14-15
Psalm 56 · Gath

He seeks comfort in the Lord

Rather than being taken captive and potentially turned over to Saul, David's feigned madness allows him to escape and continue his flight. His short-term fear protected him in a harrowing situation.

However, long-term, he seeks comfort in the Lord.

Psalm 56 · Gath

Continuing in Psalm 56

He prays the enemies be cast down. To be shown God's strength and wrath. To turn the tables of the fear they may bring out in David, and instead fill them with fear of his God.

Psalm 56 · Gath

Put thou my tears into thy bottle

David writes of his trust in the faithfulness of God, in the face of his fears;

Thou tellest my wanderings: put thou my tears into thy bottle: are they not in thy book? When I cry unto thee, then shall mine enemies turn back: this I know; for God is for me.Psalm 56:8-9
Psalm 56 · Gath

The very hairs of your head

God loves His children, and knows our pain and struggles. He savors our prayer and captures our tears and numbers every step that we take, that He may direct our paths.

Jesus says the same, speaking directly to us;

But even the very hairs of your head are all numbered. Fear not therefore: ye are of more value than many sparrows.Luke 12:7
Psalm 56 · Gath

A Savior who understands

The Lord knows our troubles and hears us in adversity. We have a Savior, Who the Bible says, understands what we go through.

For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin.Hebrews 4:15
Psalm 56 · Gath

I will render praises unto thee

He will never leave us or forsake us. We who praise His name and lean on His mercy, will never be ashamed.

David writes:

In God will I praise his word: in the Lord will I praise his word.Psalm 56:10
Thy vows are upon me, O God: I will render praises unto thee.Psalm 56:12
Psalm 56 · Gath

Confess me before men

God will deliver us that we may sing His praises. Confessing Jesus as the author and finisher of our salvation.

Whosoever therefore shall confess me before men, him will I confess also before my Father which is in heaven.Matthew 10:32
Psalm 56 · Gath

Who shall we fear?

The excellency of Christ, the power of God unto salvation, the right judge, full of righteousness and holy. Who shall we fear? What can befall us? If God be with us, who can stand against us? If we've been saved from the pit of Hell, delivered from the judgment and graciously given eternal life to praise His name, who shall we fear but God?

For thou hast delivered my soul from death: wilt not thou deliver my feet from falling, that I may walk before God in the light of the living?Psalm 56:13

Amen!

Psalm 34 · Gath

He delivered me from all my fears

David's confidence at the time of fear, fleeing from Saul and finding himself being challenged at the gates of Gath led to his deliverance. Psalm 34 tells of this. It is a complement to Psalm 56. The Bible says it is;

A Psalm of David, when he changed his behaviour before Abimelech; who drove him away, and he departed.

I will bless the Lord at all times: his praise shall continually be in my mouth. My soul shall make her boast in the Lord: the humble shall hear thereof, and be glad. O magnify the Lord with me, and let us exalt his name together. I sought the Lord, and he heard me, and delivered me from all my fears.Psalm 34:1-4
Psalm 34 · Gath

O taste and see

David credits the Lord with hearing and answering his cry. The discernment to recognize the threat and allowing David's act to be sufficient for the King to allow him to continue his flight from Saul, results in being able to live another day. And for this, David gives joyful thanksgiving.

The confidence of ends in the song of worship, praising His name in Psalm 34.

O taste and see that the Lord is good: blessed is the man that trusteth in him.Psalm 34:8
Psalm 34 · Gath

Give thanks in the congregation

Give thanks in the congregation when God has moved in your life. Praise His holy name among the people. Teach others to fear the Lord. Tell them of the works He has done in your life.

The Lord is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit.Psalm 34:18
Psalm 34 · Gath

Our fear leads to faith

Our fear leads to faith, by casting our cares upon the Lord. Our faith leads to proclaiming the truth of Christ, teaching men to fear only God. His mercy enduring forever.

It is the way. The truth. The light.

Psalm 34 · Gath

This is our purpose

Walking in the paths of righteousness. Calling upon Him when we are troubled or afraid. Being delivered. Proclaiming the truth of our God, confessing Jesus before men. Letting our light so shine that men may praise God in Heaven.

This is our purpose. This is why we suffer, so that men may praise Him. This is why we are blessed, so that men may praise Him.

Praise God for His lovingkindness and His mercy.
