The Old Testament Retold Throughout Scripture

One History, Many Witnesses

The Old Testament’s narrative, God’s covenant with Abraham, the Exodus, the wilderness, the kings and the prophets, is not told once and set aside. It is retold again and again across Scripture, always to teach the grace and mercy of God, His longsuffering towards His people, and the tendency of those people to be prone to wander, to become puffed up, to harden their hearts and stiffen their necks, and to disobey God and provoke Him.

Now all these things happened unto them for ensamples: and they are written for our admonition, upon whom the ends of the world are come.1 Corinthians 10:11

This lesson follows four of those retellings, chosen because, read in order, they trace a single arc: confession, accusation, repetition, and redemption.

The Pattern in Scripture

This is not unique to these four. Retelling Israel’s history is one of Scripture’s standard tools, used by psalmists, prophets, and apostles alike to call God’s people back to Him.

Corporate Confession

Psalm 78; Psalm 105 and 106; Psalm 135 and 136: the history sung and confessed in worship.

Prophetic Confrontation

Ezekiel 20; Joshua 24; 1 Samuel 12: the history used as an indictment, or a charge to choose.

New Testament Theology

Galatians 3–4; Romans 9–11: the history used by Paul to explain the Law, faith, and the difference between unbelieving Israel and the believing.

We will look closely at just four, chosen because they trace one arc from confession to redemption.

I. Confession

Nehemiah’s Confession

Generations before Stephen ever stood trial, the returned remnant stands in Jerusalem, reads the Law aloud, and confesses this same history as their own failure, verse for verse.

The Exodus and the Wilderness
God chose Abraham, delivered Israel from Egypt with signs and wonders, and led them by cloud and fire to Sinai, giving them the Law, manna, and water.
The Golden Calf and God’s Mercy
They hardened their necks and made a molten calf, yet God’s mercy did not depart from them, and He brought them in to possess the land.
The Cycle of Rebellion
In the land, the pattern repeats for generations: they rebel, God delivers them to their enemies, they cry out, and He hears and saves them again.
The Confession
The prayer ends by justifying God and condemning themselves.
Howbeit thou art just in all that is brought upon us; for thou hast done right, but we have done wickedly:Nehemiah 9:33
II. Accusation

Stephen’s Accusation: Abraham to Moses

Brought before the council of Jews in Jerusalem, Stephen uses the very history Nehemiah’s generation confessed as an accusation against his accusers. Indicting them for their rejection of God’s Messiah, Christ Jesus.

Abraham
Called out of his own country to a land and an inheritance he never lived to receive.
Joseph
Sold by his own brothers in envy, yet God was with him, and made him ruler over Egypt.
Moses
Rejected the first time he tried to deliver his people on his own, but forty years later, was chosen by God to lead them from captivity.

Genesis 12:1–3

Now the Lord had said unto Abram, Get thee out of thy country, and from thy kindred, and from thy father’s house, unto a land that I will shew thee: And I will make of thee a great nation, and I will bless thee, and make thy name great; and thou shalt be a blessing: And I will bless them that bless thee, and curse him that curseth thee: and in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed.Genesis 12:1-3

II. Accusation

Stephen’s Accusation: Idols, the Temple, and the Indictment

Idols
The same Moses who delivered them was refused again. While he was on the mountain, Israel turned back to Egypt’s idols and built a golden calf.
The Temple
David wanted to build God a house but was denied because he was a man of war and had shed blood (); his son Solomon was given the role instead. “The most High dwelleth not in temples made with hands”: the building was never the point, but a shadow of good things to come.

Stephen quotes Amos 5:25–27

Have ye offered unto me sacrifices and offerings in the wilderness forty years, O house of Israel? But ye have borne the tabernacle of your Moloch and Chiun your images, the star of your god, which ye made to yourselves. Therefore will I cause you to go into captivity beyond Damascus, saith the Lord, whose name is The God of hosts.Amos 5:25-27

mark: beyond Damascus

Stephen quotes Isaiah 66:1–2

Thus saith the Lord, The heaven is my throne, and the earth is my footstool: where is the house that ye build unto me? and where is the place of my rest? For all those things hath mine hand made, and all those things have been, saith the Lord: but to this man will I look, even to him that is poor and of a contrite spirit, and trembleth at my word.Isaiah 66:1-2

mark: where is the house that ye build unto me

The Indictment
“Ye stiffnecked... which of the prophets have not your fathers persecuted?” The same history Nehemiah confessed, Stephen turns into a charge.
II. Accusation

Saul, Consenting

The council is cut to the heart, not persuaded, but enraged. Stephen is dragged out of the city and stoned, praying for his killers as he dies.

And cast him out of the city, and stoned him: and the witnesses laid down their clothes at a young man’s feet, whose name was Saul. And they stoned Stephen, calling upon God, and saying, Lord Jesus, receive my spirit. And he kneeled down, and cried with a loud voice, Lord, lay not this sin to their charge. And when he had said this, he fell asleep.Acts 7:58-60

Standing in the crowd, at whose feet the witnesses laid down their clothes, is a young Pharisee named Saul, consenting to Stephen’s death.

Stephen is the first martyr, a powerful witness against unbelieving Israel. Saul will hear this testimony, and soon be the one giving it.

III. Repetition

Paul’s Repetition: The Second Witness

This is the third time I am coming to you. In the mouth of two or three witnesses shall every word be established.2 Corinthians 13:1

Stephen is not the first to preach this against unbelieving Israel. Peter does, and Jesus certainly did in His own ministry. But Stephen’s witness, and his consenting unto death for the cause of Christ, was a powerful testimony to the truth, a truth Saul, now called Paul, would echo. Years later, in a synagogue at Pisidian Antioch, the very man who watched him die for it becomes the second, independently preaching the same history, to the same end: that it all points to Jesus.

The God of this people of Israel chose our fathers, and exalted the people when they dwelt as strangers in the land of Egypt, and with an high arm brought he them out of it.Acts 13:17
And when he had removed him, he raised up unto them David to be their king; to whom also he gave testimony, and said, I have found David the son of Jesse, a man after mine own heart, which shall fulfil all my will. Of this man’s seed hath God according to his promise raised unto Israel a Saviour, Jesus:Acts 13:22-23

Paul walks the synagogue through Egypt, the wilderness, the judges, and the kings, the very shape of the history Stephen preached, and lands it on the same name: Jesus.

Interlude

A Rejection as Old as the Prophets

Before Stephen ever stood trial, Jesus leveled the same charge against the very city that would kill him, and named the pattern going all the way back to the beginning.

Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! because ye build the tombs of the prophets, and garnish the sepulchres of the righteous, And say, If we had been in the days of our fathers, we would not have been partakers with them in the blood of the prophets. Wherefore ye be witnesses unto yourselves, that ye are the children of them which killed the prophets.Matthew 23:29-31
That upon you may come all the righteous blood shed upon the earth, from the blood of righteous Abel unto the blood of Zacharias son of Barachias, whom ye slew between the temple and the altar. Verily I say unto you, All these things shall come upon this generation. O Jerusalem, Jerusalem, thou that killest the prophets, and stonest them which are sent unto thee, how often would I have gathered thy children together, even as a hen gathereth her chickens under her wings, and ye would not! Behold, your house is left unto you desolate.Matthew 23:35-38

Jesus names Abel first, the very same Abel that Hebrews 11 is about to commend by faith. The line runs from the first martyr, to the prophets, to Jesus Himself.

IV. Redemption

Hebrews’ Redemption: By Faith

The same names are recited a fourth time, not as accusation, not as confession, but as a roll call of faith.

Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen. For by it the elders obtained a good report. Through faith we understand that the worlds were framed by the word of God, so that things which are seen were not made of things which do appear. By faith Abel offered unto God a more excellent sacrifice than Cain, by which he obtained witness that he was righteous, God testifying of his gifts: and by it he being dead yet speaketh.Hebrews 11:1-4

Abel, Enoch, and Noah obeyed God by faith before the Law ever existed, proof that faith, not ritual, has always been the standard.

Abraham and Sarah believed a promise neither of them lived to see fully kept, and died still looking for “a city which hath foundations, whose builder and maker is God.”

IV. Redemption

Hebrews’ Redemption: “Time Would Fail Me”

Moses refused the treasures of Egypt, choosing to suffer with God’s people rather than enjoy the pleasures of sin for a season.

And what shall I more say? for the time would fail me to tell of Gedeon, and of Barak, and of Samson, and of Jephthae; of David also, and Samuel, and of the prophets:Hebrews 11:32
(Of whom the world was not worthy:) they wandered in deserts, and in mountains, and in dens and caves of the earth. And these all, having obtained a good report through faith, received not the promise: God having provided some better thing for us, that they without us should not be made perfect.Hebrews 11:38-40

Of whom the world was not worthy. Hebrews 11:38, the same men Stephen’s accusers claimed to honor.

V

The Lesson

Four settings, one history. What changes is not the facts, but the posture toward them.

NehemiahConfession
StephenAccusation
PaulRepetition
HebrewsRedemption

Saul stood by consenting when this history was preached as judgment against a stiffnecked people. Years later he stood up and preached it himself, and in his own epistles, came to hold up the very same men and women as redeemed by faith, a great cloud of witnesses.

The Old Testament story is both an indictment of men, who with the advantage of God’s grace and protection, the oracles of God, still fell short in their sin, but also a powerful example of His redemptive plan for man, through faith in His Word, calling upon the name of Jesus.
