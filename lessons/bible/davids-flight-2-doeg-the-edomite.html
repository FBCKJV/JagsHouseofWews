Psalm 52 · Doeg

David is come to the house of Ahimelech

Psalm 52 is told to us as a Psalm written when Doeg the Edomite came and told Saul, and said unto him, David is come to the house of Ahimelech.

The situation is described in 1 Samuel 21, David has fled Saul, who is determined to kill him. Consumed with pride and evil spirits, the king seeks the life of a man considered a hero among Israel.

1 Samuel 21 · Nob

The shew bread and Goliath's sword

David seeks food and weapons at the Tabernacle in Nob, from the unsuspecting priest, Ahimelech. Ahimelech unwittingly provides David with the shew bread, and Goliath’s sword as David claims he is on a secret mission for the King.

And David said unto Ahimelech the priest, The king hath commanded me a business, and hath said unto me, Let no man know any thing of the business whereabout I send thee, and what I have commanded thee: and I have appointed my servants to such and such a place.1 Samuel 21:2
1 Samuel 21 · Nob

Have ye not read what David did

(An act that Jesus uses to justify His Apostles plucking corn to eat on the Sabbath.)

But he said unto them, Have ye not read what David did, when he was an hungred, and they that were with him; How he entered into the house of God, and did eat the shewbread, which was not lawful for him to eat, neither for them which were with him, but only for the priests?Matthew 12:3-4
1 Samuel 21 · Nob

Doeg, an Edomite

But the priest isn’t the only person there to see David.

Now a certain man of the servants of Saul was there that day, detained before the Lord; and his name was Doeg, an Edomite, the chiefest of the herdmen that belonged to Saul.1 Samuel 21:7

Now, Doeg is not a man of war. He is a herdsman. He oversees the other shepherds and wranglers for the King. But he took the opportunity of seeing David there in Nob, on the run, to curry favor with Saul.

1 Samuel 22 · Doeg

A captain over them

The story continues, showing David’s flight, ending up in Judah at the advice of the prophet Gad, bringing his parents to the King of Moab for protection, and gathering with him loyal men as he went.

And every one that was in distress, and every one that was in debt, and every one that was discontented, gathered themselves unto him; and he became a captain over them: and there were with him about four hundred men.1 Samuel 22:2

(This is a lovely picture of Jesus and salvation, the distressed and debtors gathering unto him.)

1 Samuel 22 · Doeg

Hear now, ye Benjamites

Saul hears tell of these activities;

When Saul heard that David was discovered, and the men that were with him, (now Saul abode in Gibeah under a tree in Ramah, having his spear in his hand, and all his servants were standing about him;) Then Saul said unto his servants that stood about him, Hear now, ye Benjamites; will the son of Jesse give every one of you fields and vineyards, and make you all captains of thousands, and captains of hundreds;1 Samuel 22:6-7
1 Samuel 22 · Doeg

Doeg enters the picture

As Saul assembles his armies, Doeg enters the picture;

Then answered Doeg the Edomite, which was set over the servants of Saul, and said, I saw the son of Jesse coming to Nob, to Ahimelech the son of Ahitub. And he enquired of the Lord for him, and gave him victuals, and gave him the sword of Goliath the Philistine.1 Samuel 22:9-10

We should note that much time has passed since David’s flight to Nob, and Saul has an idea of where David and his men have hidden, in the forest of Hareth, in Judah.

1 Samuel 22 · Doeg

Why have ye conspired against me?

But Doeg brings his knowledge to the King as he is already readying for war. It feels even more opportunistic and craven in light of that passage of time and the context. Doeg’s word causes Saul to summon the priest and his kin;

Then the king sent to call Ahimelech the priest, the son of Ahitub, and all his father’s house, the priests that were in Nob: and they came all of them to the king. And Saul said, Hear now, thou son of Ahitub. And he answered, Here I am, my lord. And Saul said unto him, Why have ye conspired against me, thou and the son of Jesse, in that thou hast given him bread, and a sword, and hast enquired of God for him, that he should rise against me, to lie in wait, as at this day?1 Samuel 22:11-13
1 Samuel 22 · Doeg

Thou shalt surely die, Ahimelech

The hapless Ahimelech then pleads ignorance and asks that Saul understand that he had no way of knowing David fled the King.

Did I then begin to enquire of God for him? be it far from me: let not the king impute any thing unto his servant, nor to all the house of my father: for thy servant knew nothing of all this, less or more. And the king said, Thou shalt surely die, Ahimelech, thou, and all thy father’s house.1 Samuel 22:15-16
1 Samuel 22 · Doeg

Slay the priests

Saul refuses clemency. In his murderous rage he orders the death of the priest and entire lineage.

And the king said unto the footmen that stood about him, Turn, and slay the priests of the Lord: because their hand also is with David, and because they knew when he fled, and did not shew it to me. But the servants of the king would not put forth their hand to fall upon the priests of the Lord.1 Samuel 22:17

Even Saul’s loyal men hesitate and refuse the order of their King, out of fear of the Lord and harming His anointed priests. In the state the King was in, in the context of his twisted, murderous vengeance, the footmen feared God more.

1 Samuel 22 · Doeg

But not Doeg the Edomite

But not Doeg the Edomite.

And the king said to Doeg, Turn thou, and fall upon the priests. And Doeg the Edomite turned, and he fell upon the priests, and slew on that day fourscore and five persons that did wear a linen ephod. And Nob, the city of the priests, smote he with the edge of the sword, both men and women, children and sucklings, and oxen, and asses, and sheep, with the edge of the sword.1 Samuel 22:18-19
1 Samuel 22 · Doeg

Doeg the herdsman

Doeg the shepherd, Doeg the herdsman, who brought out of date intelligence to Saul’s attention, jeopardizing the priest, that led to this situation, took full advantage of the license given him by Saul, and wickedly carried out the command. First slaying 85 priests, and then murdering every man, woman, and child, beast and all, found in the Levite city of Nob.

1 Samuel 22 · Doeg

Abiathar escaped

One of the sons of Ahimelech escapes to see David, telling him of the wicked act done at the hands of Doeg;

And one of the sons of Ahimelech the son of Ahitub, named Abiathar, escaped, and fled after David. And Abiathar shewed David that Saul had slain the Lord’s priests. And David said unto Abiathar, I knew it that day, when Doeg the Edomite was there, that he would surely tell Saul: I have occasioned the death of all the persons of thy father’s house. Abide thou with me, fear not: for he that seeketh my life seeketh thy life: but with me thou shalt be in safeguard.1 Samuel 22:20-23
1 Samuel 22 · Doeg

Abide thou with me

David’s guilt at not taking care of Doeg when he had the opportunity, and the resulting deaths, has him offer Abiathar refuge with him and his troop of men.

David will have Abiathar act in his office, with the ephod, to consult with the Lord several times. Asking Will the men of Keilah deliver me up into his hand? will Saul come down, as thy servant hath heard? And later, enquiring of the Lord, Shall I pursue after this troop? shall I overtake them? Seeking the wisdom of God to direct his paths in times of uncertainty.

1 Samuel 22 · Doeg

God has abandoned Saul

David, in exile, is given the oracles of God through the Levite and ephod, as a King would. Saul, in his rage had cut himself off from this avenue of prophecy. As the sole survivor of the high priestly line of Eli, Abiathar brings the official religious authority of Israel to David’s camp, signaling that God has abandoned Saul.

By using the Urim and Thummim in the ephod, Abiathar provides divine reconnaissance that saves David from Saul’s traps and ensures the defeat of the Amalekites. Abiathar serves as David’s high priest throughout his exile and reign, eventually helping bring the Ark of the Covenant to Jerusalem.

1 Samuel 22 · Doeg

Evil became good for David

During Absalom’s rebellion, Abiathar remains behind in Jerusalem to act as a secret agent, feeding David crucial intelligence through his son Jonathan to help win back the throne.

The evil of Saul and the bloodthirst of Doeg, became good for David, who loved God and was called according to His purpose.

Psalm 52 · Doeg

O mighty man

But Doeg became a byword.

Psalm 52 mockingly called Doeg a "mighty man," for slaying men of God, dressed only in linen, and helpless women and children, even babies. Doeg did what was unthinkable to the actual mighty men of Saul, after causing the entire situation by his obsequious talebearing. Immortalized in his wickedness by the psalmist, David.

Why boastest thou thyself in mischief, O mighty man? the goodness of God endureth continually. Thy tongue deviseth mischiefs; like a sharp razor, working deceitfully. Thou lovest evil more than good; and lying rather than to speak righteousness. Selah.Psalm 52:1-3
Psalm 52 · Doeg

Doeg's fate

We can imagine Doeg’s fate, the Bible doesn’t speak specifically of what became of him afterwards, but Psalm 52 gives us some idea of his ultimate fate before God.

God shall likewise destroy thee for ever, he shall take thee away, and pluck thee out of thy dwelling place, and root thee out of the land of the living. Selah. The righteous also shall see, and fear, and shall laugh at him: Lo, this is the man that made not God his strength; but trusted in the abundance of his riches, and strengthened himself in his wickedness.Psalm 52:5-7
Psalm 52 · Doeg

By his evil, good came

But by his evil, good came. David trusted in the mercy of the Lord, and was strengthened by Doeg’s actions, and was able to sing of the praises of the Lord because of it.

Psalm 52 · The mighty man

The polar opposite of Psalm 51

In Psalm 52, the psalmist presents the polar opposite of the contrite heart of Psalm 51, the mischief-making man who doesn't avail himself to the longsuffering mercy of God.

The wicked and their deceitful tongue, inventing lies, telling slander against God and God's people, boasting of their blessings, not thankful but vain in their imagination, loving evil more than good, these are the men who continually reject the call to fear the Lord.

Psalm 52 · The mighty man

Like just Lot

Those of us who are saved can become incredulous at the prideful stance of the wicked, who reject the fear of God and lean on their own strength. We can often grow confounded and even depressed over the ways of the wicked. Like just Lot, living in Sodom, being afflicted by the wickedness around him

(For that righteous man dwelling among them, in seeing and hearing, vexed his righteous soul from day to day with their unlawful deeds;)2 Peter 2:8
Psalm 52 · The mighty man

The Bible is not afraid to call these people out

The Bible is not afraid to call these people out, directly and to their face. They are boastful, mockingly called "mighty men," liars, evil, haters of righteousness, lovers of slanderous and wicked words, lovers of mischief, having deceitful tongues.

We should not fear to identify exactly the perverted in our midst when they reject and mock the gospel of God and the word we preach.

Psalm 52 · The mighty man

Pulling them out of the fire

Nor should we tiptoe around the heart of the matter; Their imminent destruction and the torment in the fire of Hell that awaits them.

And of some have compassion, making a difference: And others save with fear, pulling them out of the fire; hating even the garment spotted by the flesh.Jude 22-23
Psalm 52 · The mighty man

Hard preaching

Those who do not see the wrath of God revealed from Heaven upon others as a reason to repent and believe, and won't see the mercy and hear the praise of the meek, feeling the compassionate love of believers magnify God's grace, need hard preaching to let them know exactly what they've chosen, in hopes that they might be saved.

That their love of devouring words will lead to God likewise destroying them forever. Literally, being devoured by flame and worm in outer darkness, forever. Eternal suffering at the hands of a just and mighty God.

Psalm 52 · The mighty man

Selah

God shall likewise destroy thee for ever, he shall take thee away, and pluck thee out of thy dwelling place, and root thee out of the land of the living. Selah.Psalm 52:5

If "selah" is meant to be a pause, a moment to reflect on what was just sung or said, is there anywhere more fitting than in that moment, having heard that sobering truth?

God will destroy you. He will take your life and root you out of the land of the living, to place you in a place of eternal destruction.

And the righteous shall see their end and fear God, and laugh at the calamity they brought about on themselves. The end their mischief brought about, turned back upon them.

Whoso diggeth a pit shall fall therein: and he that rolleth a stone, it will return upon him.Proverbs 26:27
Psalm 52 · The mighty man

A parable or a byword

The ironic nature of the bitter end experienced by the boastful sinner is often evident and clear to those that fear God. The humor of the Lord is appreciated by His children. We laugh at their calamity.

When the wicked are made a parable or a byword among the people, it should give other men pause. The wrath revealed from Heaven should give instruction to the simple to turn towards God. It shows the righteous in Christ God's power and promise.

The fear of the Lord is the beginning of knowledge: but fools despise wisdom and instruction.Proverbs 1:7

The warning is ignored by the fool. And God will not be mocked.

But ye have set at nought all my counsel, and would none of my reproof: I also will laugh at your calamity; I will mock when your fear cometh; When your fear cometh as desolation, and your destruction cometh as a whirlwind; when distress and anguish cometh upon you.Proverbs 1:25-27
Psalm 52 · The mighty man

God will not be mocked, continued

Psalm 52 · The mighty man

But we are His

These men that did not trust in God, but made their own puny strength and their worldly, perishing riches their stronghold, will be brought low. By their evil deeds they seek to maintain power and control, and by the same they will be brought low by our God, Who is a consuming fire.

But we are His. We have humbled ourselves to rely on His holy name. To be meek servants, poor in spirit, that we may receive His grace, and the abundance of life in His love.

They waited for Jesus, we look back to His cross and the glory of His resurrection. We shall praise His name among the people. We shall look at His mercy and wrath, and know He is our salvation. We will praise Him forever, as His saints. Amen
