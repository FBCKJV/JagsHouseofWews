The Gospel of John

That Ye Might Believe

Two devices to watch for

In our study of the Gospel of John, we will meet two hallmark literary devices that the author frequently wraps around the teachings of Jesus to unlock deep spiritual truths.

1. The Johannine Misunderstanding Motif

A rhetorical strategy widely recognized by scholars, often triggered by amphibologia, or words with double meanings. Jesus delivers a profound spiritual truth or “dark saying,” a hearer mistakenly takes it in a strictly physical, material way, and Jesus uses that error as a springboard to explain the deeper heavenly reality.

2. Semitic Parallelism

Specifically a blend of synonymous and antithetic parallelism. John uses poetic restatements to set two opposing realities side by side, such as the physical flesh versus the supernatural Spirit, to define and clarify his terms.

A note on where this came from: I noticed both patterns in my own study of John and built the first version of this lesson around them before I knew they had names. They are well known and widely taught. I did not invent them, and I am not claiming to have discovered them. I only found them in the text.

The gospel in one verse

Before we follow the figures, we set down the whole message plainly. John 3:16 holds all of it.

“For God so loved the world”

Salvation begins with the Father’s love and grace, not with anything in us (, ).

“that he gave his only begotten Son”

The Son died for our sins, was buried and rose again the third day (, , ).

“that whosoever believeth in him”

It is received by believing on Him, and it is offered to all (, ).

“should not perish, but have everlasting life”

The believer is quickened, sealed by the Holy Spirit of promise and kept by the power of God (, , , ).

When John says “believe,” he means believing on this Son: God manifest in the flesh, given by the Father, crucified and risen. Every figure in the book restates this verse.

How John teaches

Again and again in John, Jesus says something in a picture. Those who will not believe take the picture literally and are confused or offended. Then Jesus, or John himself, says it again plainly. The plain meaning is nearly always the same: believe on Him.

Every chapter in this study is laid out the same way:

The figure

A picture Jesus uses: a temple, a birth, water, bread, a door, sleep.

The literal hearer

Someone takes the picture at face value and misses the point, or is offended by it.

The revealed meaning

The plain truth, given by Jesus or explained by John. Most often it is a call to believe on Him.

Jesus said it Himself: “These things have I spoken unto you in proverbs” ().

Why speak in figures?

The psalmist foretold that the Messiah would speak in parables and dark sayings, and the other gospels record Jesus explaining why.

I will open my mouth in a parable: I will utter dark sayings of old:Psalm 78:2
Therefore speak I to them in parables: because they seeing see not; and hearing they hear not, neither do they understand.Matthew 13:13

A figure does not hide the truth from anyone who wants it. It shows who is listening in faith. The believer looks past the picture to the One speaking. The unbeliever argues with the picture and walks away. John records Jesus saying the same thing to those who opposed Him:

Why do ye not understand my speech? even because ye cannot hear my word.John 8:43

The believer hears that Christ’s words “are spirit, and they are life” ().

John 1

The Word Made Flesh

John begins by telling us exactly who Jesus is, before a single miracle is recorded.

In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.John 1:1
And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.John 1:14

The Word was with God, and the Word was God. That same Word was made flesh and lived among men. The Son the Father gave is no lesser being. He is God, come in the flesh. Everything else in John rests on this.

No man hath seen God at any time. The only begotten Son has declared Him ().

John 1

Received Him, Believed on Him

In the same chapter John gives us the first figure, the first rejection and the first restatement.

The figure

In him was life; and the life was the light of men. And the light shineth in darkness; and the darkness comprehended it not.John 1:4-5

John pictures Jesus as light shining into a dark world.

The world’s response

He was in the world, and the world was made by him, and the world knew him not. He came unto his own, and his own received him not.John 1:10-11

The world He made did not know Him, and His own people did not receive Him.

The revealed meaning

But as many as received him, to them gave he power to become the sons of God, even to them that believe on his name: Which were born, not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God.John 1:12-13

Then John says it plainly. “Received him” is restated as “believe on his name.” Those who believe are born of God, not by any human effort.

This is the pattern for the whole book: the figure, the response, and the plain meaning.

John 2

Destroy This Temple

After Jesus drives the money changers out of the temple, the Jews demand a sign of His authority. He answers with a figure.

The figure

Jesus answered and said unto them, Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up.John 2:19

Jesus speaks of a temple destroyed and raised up in three days.

The literal hearer

Then said the Jews, Forty and six years was this temple in building, and wilt thou rear it up in three days?John 2:20

His hearers think only of the stone building in front of them, which had taken forty-six years to build.

The revealed meaning

But he spake of the temple of his body. When therefore he was risen from the dead, his disciples remembered that he had said this unto them; and they believed the scripture, and the word which Jesus had said.John 2:21-22

John tells us plainly that He meant His own body. After the resurrection, the disciples remembered His words and believed.

The very first figure in John points to His death and resurrection, the heart of the gospel.

John 3

Ye Must Be Born Again

Nicodemus, a ruler of the Jews, comes to Jesus by night. Jesus answers him with a figure about birth.

The figure

Jesus answered and said unto him, Verily, verily, I say unto thee, Except a man be born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God.John 3:3

To see the kingdom of God, a man must be born a second time.

The literal hearer

Nicodemus saith unto him, How can a man be born when he is old? can he enter the second time into his mother’s womb, and be born?John 3:4

Nicodemus can only think of physical birth, and asks how a grown man could enter his mother’s womb again.

The revealed meaning

Jesus answered, Verily, verily, I say unto thee, Except a man be born of water and of the Spirit, he cannot enter into the kingdom of God. That which is born of the flesh is flesh; and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit.John 3:5-6

Jesus says it again two ways, in back-to-back verses. “Born of water” is restated as “born of the flesh”: our natural birth. “Born of the Spirit” is restated as “is spirit”: the second birth. Every person is born once of the flesh. He must be born again of the Spirit.

Like the wind, the new birth cannot be seen, but its effect is real ().

John 3

As Moses Lifted Up the Serpent

To explain how a man is born again, Jesus reaches back to a story Nicodemus knew well.

The figure

And the Lord said unto Moses, Make thee a fiery serpent, and set it upon a pole: and it shall come to pass, that every one that is bitten, when he looketh upon it, shall live. And Moses made a serpent of brass, and put it upon a pole, and it came to pass, that if a serpent had bitten any man, when he beheld the serpent of brass, he lived.Numbers 21:8-9

When fiery serpents bit Israel in the wilderness, God had Moses set a brass serpent on a pole. Every bitten man who looked at it lived.

The figure applied

And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up:John 3:14

Jesus says He must be lifted up the same way, on the cross.

The revealed meaning

That whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life.John 3:15

Looking at the serpent becomes believing on the Son. The bitten man did nothing but look. The sinner does nothing but believe.

“Look and live” in the wilderness becomes “believe and live” at the cross.

“Eternal life” and “everlasting life” are the same gift. Verse 15 tells us what the serpent pictured. Verse 16 tells us why the Son was lifted up: the Father so loved the world that He gave Him.

He that believeth on him is not condemned: but he that believeth not is condemned already, because he hath not believed in the name of the only begotten Son of God.John 3:18

The line is drawn by one thing only: believing, or not believing, on the Son.

John 4

Living Water

At Jacob’s well, Jesus asks a Samaritan woman for a drink, then offers her water of a different kind.

The figure

Jesus answered and said unto her, If thou knewest the gift of God, and who it is that saith to thee, Give me to drink; thou wouldest have asked of him, and he would have given thee living water.John 4:10

Jesus speaks of living water that only He can give.

The literal hearer

The woman saith unto him, Sir, thou hast nothing to draw with, and the well is deep: from whence then hast thou that living water? Art thou greater than our father Jacob, which gave us the well, and drank thereof himself, and his children, and his cattle?John 4:11-12

She thinks of the well itself. He has no bucket, and the well is deep. Is He greater than Jacob, who dug it?

The revealed meaning

Jesus answered and said unto her, Whosoever drinketh of this water shall thirst again: But whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst; but the water that I shall give him shall be in him a well of water springing up into everlasting life.John 4:13-14

Jesus contrasts the two waters. Well water leaves you thirsty again. The water He gives satisfies once and for ever, springing up into everlasting life.

Even then she asks for it so she won’t have to come back and draw (). John later explains the living water as the Spirit, received by those who believe ().

John 4

Meat to Eat That Ye Know Not Of

The disciples come back from town with food and urge Jesus to eat.

The figure

But he said unto them, I have meat to eat that ye know not of.John 4:32

Jesus says He has food they do not know about.

The literal hearer

Therefore said the disciples one to another, Hath any man brought him ought to eat?John 4:33

His own disciples wonder who brought Him something to eat.

The revealed meaning

Jesus saith unto them, My meat is to do the will of him that sent me, and to finish his work.John 4:34

His food is doing the will of the Father who sent Him and finishing the work He was given. That work is finished at the cross ().

Even His disciples stumble at a figure until He explains it. This prepares us for John 6, where He Himself is the bread.

John 4

I That Speak unto Thee Am He

The woman speaks of the coming Messiah, and Jesus answers her in plain words.

Her hope

The woman saith unto him, I know that Messias cometh, which is called Christ: when he is come, he will tell us all things.John 4:25

She knows the Christ is coming and will tell them all things.

The revelation

Jesus saith unto her, I that speak unto thee am he.John 4:26

This is the first time in John that Jesus says “I am.”

The believers

And many more believed because of his own word; And said unto the woman, Now we believe, not because of thy saying: for we have heard him ourselves, and know that this is indeed the Christ, the Saviour of the world.John 4:41-42

The Samaritans believe because of His own word and confess Him as the Saviour of the world.

She came for water from a well and went home believing on the Christ.

John 5

Equal with God

Jesus heals a lame man on the sabbath, and the Jews seek to kill Him. His answer shows what kind of claim He was making.

His words

But Jesus answered them, My Father worketh hitherto, and I work.John 5:17

Jesus calls God “my Father” and places His own work alongside the Father’s.

What they understood

Therefore the Jews sought the more to kill him, because he not only had broken the sabbath, but said also that God was his Father, making himself equal with God.John 5:18

The unbelievers understood exactly what He meant. To call God His own Father in this way was to make Himself equal with God.

The promise

Verily, verily, I say unto you, He that heareth my word, and believeth on him that sent me, hath everlasting life, and shall not come into condemnation; but is passed from death unto life.John 5:24

Then the plain promise: hear and believe, and you have everlasting life now. You will not come into condemnation, because you have already passed from death unto life.

Unbelief did not misunderstand His claim to be God. It rejected it.

John 6

I Am the Bread of Life

The day after He fed the five thousand, the crowd comes looking for more bread (). Jesus offers them Himself.

The figure

And Jesus said unto them, I am the bread of life: he that cometh to me shall never hunger; and he that believeth on me shall never thirst.John 6:35

He calls Himself the bread of life and explains it in the same breath: coming to Him is eating, and believing on Him is drinking. One meal, and never hunger again.

The literal hearer

The Jews therefore strove among themselves, saying, How can this man give us his flesh to eat?John 6:52

When He says the bread is His flesh, given for the life of the world (), the Jews argue about how a man could give his flesh to eat. Many disciples call it a hard saying ().

The revealed meaning

It is the spirit that quickeneth; the flesh profiteth nothing: the words that I speak unto you, they are spirit, and they are life.John 6:63

Jesus gives the key Himself. The flesh profits nothing. His words are spirit and life, received by believing them.

Peter understood: “thou hast the words of eternal life” ().

John 6

Eating Is Believing

Set verse 40 beside verse 54. Jesus states the same promise twice, once plainly and once in the figure.

Plain words
The figure
every one which seeth the Son, and believeth on him,
Whoso eateth my flesh, and drinketh my blood,
may have everlasting life:
hath eternal life;
and I will raise him up at the last day.
and I will raise him up at the last day.

Same promise, same result. To eat His flesh and drink His blood is to believe on the Son. And the one who comes is kept:

All that the Father giveth me shall come to me; and him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out.John 6:37
And this is the Father’s will which hath sent me, that of all which he hath given me I should lose nothing, but should raise it up again at the last day.John 6:39

None who come are cast out, and of all the Father has given Him, He will lose nothing.

John 7

Whither Will He Go?

At the feast of tabernacles, the rulers send officers to take Jesus, and He speaks of going away.

The figure

Then said Jesus unto them, Yet a little while am I with you, and then I go unto him that sent me. Ye shall seek me, and shall not find me: and where I am, thither ye cannot come.John 7:33-34

He will soon go to the One who sent Him, where they cannot follow.

The literal hearer

Then said the Jews among themselves, Whither will he go, that we shall not find him? will he go unto the dispersed among the Gentiles, and teach the Gentiles? What manner of saying is this that he said, Ye shall seek me, and shall not find me: and where I am, thither ye cannot come?John 7:35-36

The Jews think of a journey on the map, perhaps to teach the Jews scattered among the Gentiles.

The revealed meaning

I came forth from the Father, and am come into the world: again, I leave the world, and go to the Father.John 16:28

Later He tells His disciples plainly: He came from the Father, and He is returning to the Father.

They thought of a trip abroad. He spoke of going back to heaven, by way of the cross and the empty tomb.

John 7

Rivers of Living Water

On the last and greatest day of the feast, Jesus stands and cries out to the crowd.

The figure

In the last day, that great day of the feast, Jesus stood and cried, saying, If any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink. He that believeth on me, as the scripture hath said, out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water.John 7:37-38

He offers a drink to anyone who thirsts, and promises rivers flowing out of the one who believes.

John’s explanation

(But this spake he of the Spirit, which they that believe on him should receive: for the Holy Ghost was not yet given; because that Jesus was not yet glorified.)John 7:39

John stops to tell us what it means. The living water is the Holy Spirit, whom believers would receive once Jesus was glorified.

Coming and drinking is believing, and every believer receives the Spirit. This is the same water He offered the woman at the well.

John 8

Believe That I Am He

Jesus has just declared, “I am the light of the world” (). Again He speaks of going where they cannot follow.

The figure

Then said Jesus again unto them, I go my way, and ye shall seek me, and shall die in your sins: whither I go, ye cannot come.John 8:21

He is going away, and they will die in their sins without Him.

The literal hearer

Then said the Jews, Will he kill himself? because he saith, Whither I go, ye cannot come.John 8:22

The Jews wonder if He means to kill Himself.

The revealed meaning

And he said unto them, Ye are from beneath; I am from above: ye are of this world; I am not of this world. I said therefore unto you, that ye shall die in your sins: for if ye believe not that I am he, ye shall die in your sins.John 8:23-24

He explains: they are from beneath, and He is from above. The only way out of their sins is to believe that He is the I AM.

Here believing on Him and who He is are joined in a single sentence.

John 8

The Truth Shall Make You Free

Jesus speaks to Jews who had begun to believe His words.

The figure

Then said Jesus to those Jews which believed on him, If ye continue in my word, then are ye my disciples indeed; And ye shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free.John 8:31-32

He speaks of being set free.

The literal hearer

They answered him, We be Abraham’s seed, and were never in bondage to any man: how sayest thou, Ye shall be made free?John 8:33

They protest that Abraham’s children have never been anyone’s slaves.

The revealed meaning

Jesus answered them, Verily, verily, I say unto you, Whosoever committeth sin is the servant of sin. And the servant abideth not in the house for ever: but the Son abideth ever. If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed.John 8:34-36

The bondage is sin, and only the Son can set a man free. A servant can be put out of the house. The Son abides for ever, and the freedom He gives is freedom indeed.

They heard a question of ancestry. He spoke of the slavery of sin.

John 8

Before Abraham Was, I Am

The argument turns to Abraham, and Jesus makes His plainest claim yet.

The figure

Your father Abraham rejoiced to see my day: and he saw it, and was glad.John 8:56

Abraham looked ahead in faith to the day of Christ and rejoiced.

The literal hearer

Then said the Jews unto him, Thou art not yet fifty years old, and hast thou seen Abraham?John 8:57

They hear only a question of age. He is not even fifty, so how could He have seen Abraham?

The revealed meaning

Jesus said unto them, Verily, verily, I say unto you, Before Abraham was, I am.John 8:58

Jesus answers with the name God gave Moses at the burning bush: “I AM” (). He did not say “I was.” He has no beginning.

Then took they up stones to cast at him: but Jesus hid himself, and went out of the temple, going through the midst of them, and so passed by.John 8:59

The unbelievers understood the claim to be God, and reached for stones.

John 9

Are We Blind Also?

Jesus says, “I am the light of the world” (), and gives sight to a man born blind. The Pharisees question the man and cast him out.

The figure

And Jesus said, For judgment I am come into this world, that they which see not might see; and that they which see might be made blind.John 9:39

Jesus uses sight and blindness as a picture of who receives Him.

The literal hearer

And some of the Pharisees which were with him heard these words, and said unto him, Are we blind also?John 9:40

The Pharisees take it as an insult to their eyesight.

The revealed meaning

Jesus said unto them, If ye were blind, ye should have no sin: but now ye say, We see; therefore your sin remaineth.John 9:41

Their sin remains, not because they cannot see, but because they claim to see while they reject the Light standing in front of them.

The man born blind received his sight. The men who said “We see” remained in darkness.

John 9

Lord, I Believe

After he is cast out, Jesus finds the healed man and asks him one question.

Jesus heard that they had cast him out; and when he had found him, he said unto him, Dost thou believe on the Son of God? He answered and said, Who is he, Lord, that I might believe on him? And Jesus said unto him, Thou hast both seen him, and it is he that talketh with thee. And he said, Lord, I believe. And he worshipped him.John 9:35-38

He believed and worshipped Him, and Jesus accepted it. Worship belongs to God alone. Then compare how Jesus described the light in John 8 with how He said it again in John 12:

Said again
he that followeth me
whosoever believeth on me
shall not walk in darkness

Following the light is believing on Him.

John 10

I Am the Door

Jesus speaks of a sheepfold, its door, its shepherd and the thieves who climb in another way.

The figure

Verily, verily, I say unto you, He that entereth not by the door into the sheepfold, but climbeth up some other way, the same is a thief and a robber. But he that entereth in by the door is the shepherd of the sheep. To him the porter openeth; and the sheep hear his voice: and he calleth his own sheep by name, and leadeth them out.John 10:1-3

A picture every shepherd in Israel would know: one rightful way into the fold.

The hearers

This parable spake Jesus unto them: but they understood not what things they were which he spake unto them.John 10:6

John calls it a parable and tells us plainly that they did not understand it.

The revealed meaning

Then said Jesus unto them again, Verily, verily, I say unto you, I am the door of the sheep.John 10:7
I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved, and shall go in and out, and find pasture.John 10:9

So Jesus says it again. He is the door. Whoever enters by Him is saved.

“Then said Jesus unto them again” is the dual pattern in John’s own words.

John 10

I Am the Good Shepherd

The figure continues, and this time it points straight to the cross.

The figure

I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep.John 10:11

A good shepherd gives his own life for his sheep.

His death and resurrection

Therefore doth my Father love me, because I lay down my life, that I might take it again. No man taketh it from me, but I lay it down of myself. I have power to lay it down, and I have power to take it again. This commandment have I received of my Father.John 10:17-18

No one takes His life from Him. He lays it down Himself, and He takes it again.

The promise

My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me: And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand. My Father, which gave them me, is greater than all; and no man is able to pluck them out of my Father’s hand.John 10:27-29

His sheep hear, follow and receive eternal life. They shall never perish, held in the Son’s hand and in the Father’s hand.

The security of the sheep rests on the Shepherd who died and rose again.

John 10

I and My Father Are One

At the feast of dedication, the Jews press Him to tell them plainly whether He is the Christ.

His words

I and my Father are one.John 10:30

He has just said that no man can pluck the sheep out of the Father’s hand. Now He says He and the Father are one.

What they understood

Then the Jews took up stones again to stone him. Jesus answered them, Many good works have I shewed you from my Father; for which of those works do ye stone me? The Jews answered him, saying, For a good work we stone thee not; but for blasphemy; and because that thou, being a man, makest thyself God.John 10:31-33

They state His claim in their own words: “thou, being a man, makest thyself God.”

For the second time they took up stones. Even His enemies said it plainly. They understood He claimed to be God. They would not believe it.

John 11

Lazarus Sleepeth

Word comes that Lazarus of Bethany is sick. Jesus waits two days, then tells His disciples they are going back.

The figure

These things said he: and after that he saith unto them, Our friend Lazarus sleepeth; but I go, that I may awake him out of sleep.John 11:11

Jesus speaks of death as sleep.

The literal hearer

Then said his disciples, Lord, if he sleep, he shall do well. Howbeit Jesus spake of his death: but they thought that he had spoken of taking of rest in sleep.John 11:12-13

The disciples think Lazarus is resting and will get better. John notes their mistake.

The revealed meaning

Then said Jesus unto them plainly, Lazarus is dead.John 11:14

So Jesus tells them plainly.

For the believer, death is called sleep because the Lord will wake him.

John 11

Believest Thou This?

Martha meets Jesus outside Bethany. Her brother has been in the grave four days.

The figure

Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live: And whosoever liveth and believeth in me shall never die. Believest thou this?John 11:25-26

Two clauses, one promise. The believer who dies will live again. The believer who lives will never die. Then He asks her directly.

The believer’s answer

She saith unto him, Yea, Lord: I believe that thou art the Christ, the Son of God, which should come into the world.John 11:27

Martha confesses who He is before Lazarus walks out of the tomb. She believed first and saw the glory of God afterward ().

The proof

And when he thus had spoken, he cried with a loud voice, Lazarus, come forth. And he that was dead came forth, bound hand and foot with graveclothes: and his face was bound about with a napkin. Jesus saith unto them, Loose him, and let him go.John 11:43-44

The voice of the Son of God calls a dead man out of the grave.

Many believed on Him (). From that day the rulers plotted His death (). The same question comes to every hearer: believest thou this?

John 12

If I Be Lifted Up

In His last public teaching, Jesus speaks once more of being lifted up.

The figure

And I, if I be lifted up from the earth, will draw all men unto me.John 12:32

Lifted up from the earth, He will draw all men to Himself.

John’s explanation

This he said, signifying what death he should die.John 12:33

John tells us at once what “lifted up” meant: the kind of death He would die, raised up on a cross.

The literal hearer

The people answered him, We have heard out of the law that Christ abideth for ever: and how sayest thou, The Son of man must be lifted up? who is this Son of man?John 12:34

The people cannot fit a Christ who dies into their belief that the Christ abides for ever. They understood He meant death, and stumbled at it.

This is the third time Jesus speaks of being lifted up (, ). Each time it means the cross. By His death He draws all men, and whosoever believeth on Him lives.

John 13

If I Wash Thee Not

At the last supper Jesus rises, takes a towel and begins washing His disciples’ feet.

The figure

Peter saith unto him, Thou shalt never wash my feet. Jesus answered him, If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me.John 13:8

Peter refuses, and Jesus answers that without His washing Peter has no part with Him.

The literal hearer

Simon Peter saith unto him, Lord, not my feet only, but also my hands and my head.John 13:9

Peter swings the other way and asks for a full bath.

The revealed meaning

Jesus saith to him, He that is washed needeth not save to wash his feet, but is clean every whit: and ye are clean, but not all. For he knew who should betray him; therefore said he, Ye are not all clean.John 13:10-11

One who has been washed is clean every whit and only needs his feet washed. The washing that makes a man clean happens once. Judas was the exception, because he never believed.

Later that night: “ye may believe that I am he” ().

John 14

I Am the Way

Jesus comforts His troubled disciples: “ye believe in God, believe also in me” (). He is going to prepare a place for them ().

The figure

And whither I go ye know, and the way ye know.John 14:4

He tells them they know where He is going and the way there.

The literal hearer

Thomas saith unto him, Lord, we know not whither thou goest; and how can we know the way?John 14:5

Thomas thinks of a road and a destination he cannot see.

The revealed meaning

Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.John 14:6

The way is not a road. It is a Person. No one comes to the Father except through Him.

Notice the dual phrasing in verse 1: believe in God, believe also in Me.

John 14

Shew Us the Father

Philip asks for the one thing he thinks would settle everything.

The figure

If ye had known me, ye should have known my Father also: and from henceforth ye know him, and have seen him.John 14:7

To know Jesus is to know the Father. They have already seen Him.

The literal hearer

Philip saith unto him, Lord, shew us the Father, and it sufficeth us.John 14:8

Philip wants a visible sight of the Father.

The revealed meaning

Jesus saith unto him, Have I been so long time with you, and yet hast thou not known me, Philip? he that hath seen me hath seen the Father; and how sayest thou then, Shew us the Father?John 14:9

To see the Son is to see the Father. There are few plainer statements of His deity in all of scripture.

And the promise to the believer: “because I live, ye shall live also” ().

John 15

I Am the True Vine

The last of the seven figures, spoken to the eleven on the way to Gethsemane.

The figure

I am the true vine, and my Father is the husbandman.John 15:1

Jesus is the vine, and the Father tends it.

The revealed meaning

I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing.John 15:5

A branch lives and bears fruit only by being joined to the vine. Without Him we can do nothing.

The theme of the vine is fruit. Read in light of what John has already promised the believer: never cast out (), never perish ().

John 16

A Little While

Jesus prepares His disciples for His death and resurrection, and they cannot follow what He means.

The figure

A little while, and ye shall not see me: and again, a little while, and ye shall see me, because I go to the Father.John 16:16

Soon they will not see Him, and soon after they will see Him again.

The confused hearers

Then said some of his disciples among themselves, What is this that he saith unto us, A little while, and ye shall not see me: and again, a little while, and ye shall see me: and, Because I go to the Father? They said therefore, What is this that he saith, A little while? we cannot tell what he saith.John 16:17-18

Even the disciples ask one another what He can mean.

The revealed meaning

Verily, verily, I say unto you, That ye shall weep and lament, but the world shall rejoice: and ye shall be sorrowful, but your sorrow shall be turned into joy. A woman when she is in travail hath sorrow, because her hour is come: but as soon as she is delivered of the child, she remembereth no more the anguish, for joy that a man is born into the world. And ye now therefore have sorrow: but I will see you again, and your heart shall rejoice, and your joy no man taketh from you.John 16:20-22

His death would bring them sorrow, like a woman in labour. His resurrection would turn it to a joy no man could take away.

“A little while” was three days: the cross, the grave and the risen Lord.

John 16

Now Speakest Thou Plainly

Jesus names the way He has been teaching, and then speaks without a figure.

In proverbs

These things have I spoken unto you in proverbs: but the time cometh, when I shall no more speak unto you in proverbs, but I shall shew you plainly of the Father.John 16:25

He says He has been speaking in proverbs, and promises to speak plainly.

Plainly

I came forth from the Father, and am come into the world: again, I leave the world, and go to the Father.John 16:28

No figure at all: He came from the Father and returns to the Father.

The believers

His disciples said unto him, Lo, now speakest thou plainly, and speakest no proverb. Now are we sure that thou knowest all things, and needest not that any man should ask thee: by this we believe that thou camest forth from God.John 16:29-30

The disciples recognize the plain speech and believe that He came forth from God.

Jesus Himself called His sayings proverbs. Reading His figures as figures is following His own description of how He taught.

John 17

Keep Through Thine Own Name

On the night before the cross, Jesus prays to the Father for those the Father has given Him.

Life eternal

And this is life eternal, that they might know thee the only true God, and Jesus Christ, whom thou hast sent.John 17:3

Eternal life is knowing the only true God and the Son He sent.

Kept

And now I am no more in the world, but these are in the world, and I come to thee. Holy Father, keep through thine own name those whom thou hast given me, that they may be one, as we are. While I was with them in the world, I kept them in thy name: those that thou gavest me I have kept, and none of them is lost, but the son of perdition; that the scripture might be fulfilled.John 17:11-12

He asks the Father to keep them. None of them is lost except Judas, the son of perdition, who never believed.

With Him

Father, I will that they also, whom thou hast given me, be with me where I am; that they may behold my glory, which thou hast given me: for thou lovedst me before the foundation of the world.John 17:24

His will is that every one the Father gave Him be with Him where He is.

The Son asked the Father to keep them, and the Father always hears the Son.

John 18

I Am He

Judas leads a band of soldiers and officers into the garden with lanterns, torches and weapons.

Jesus therefore, knowing all things that should come upon him, went forth, and said unto them, Whom seek ye? They answered him, Jesus of Nazareth. Jesus saith unto them, I am he. And Judas also, which betrayed him, stood with them. As soon then as he had said unto them, I am he, they went backward, and fell to the ground.John 18:4-6

At the words “I am,” armed men go backward and fall to the ground. Then He gives Himself up willingly, and asks only that His disciples be let go:

Jesus answered, I have told you that I am he: if therefore ye seek me, let these go their way:John 18:8

He was not taken by force. He laid down His life, just as He said ().

John 19

It Is Finished

Every figure has pointed here: the temple destroyed, the serpent lifted up, the bread given, the shepherd laying down His life.

When Jesus therefore had received the vinegar, he said, It is finished: and he bowed his head, and gave up the ghost.John 19:30

The work the Father gave Him to do is complete (, ). Nothing is left for the sinner to add. Three days later the temple of His body was raised, as He had said.

For I delivered unto you first of all that which I also received, how that Christ died for our sins according to the scriptures; And that he was buried, and that he rose again the third day according to the scriptures:1 Corinthians 15:3-4

Christ died for our sins, was buried and rose again. This is the gospel the believer believes.

John 20

My Lord and My God

Thomas was absent when the risen Lord first appeared to the disciples.

The literal demand

The other disciples therefore said unto him, We have seen the Lord. But he said unto them, Except I shall see in his hands the print of the nails, and put my finger into the print of the nails, and thrust my hand into his side, I will not believe.John 20:25

Thomas will believe only what he can see and touch.

The revelation

Then saith he to Thomas, Reach hither thy finger, and behold my hands; and reach hither thy hand, and thrust it into my side: and be not faithless, but believing. And Thomas answered and said unto him, My Lord and my God.John 20:27-28

Eight days later, Thomas confesses the risen Jesus as Lord and God, exactly what John declared in the first verse.

Said again, for us

Jesus saith unto him, Thomas, because thou hast seen me, thou hast believed: blessed are they that have not seen, and yet have believed.John 20:29

The last dual saying is for every reader who was not there: blessed are those who believe without seeing.

And many other signs truly did Jesus in the presence of his disciples, which are not written in this book: But these are written, that ye might believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God; and that believing ye might have life through his name.John 20:30-31

John tells us why he wrote the whole book: that we might believe, and that believing we might have life.

John 21

What Is That to Thee?

By the sea of Tiberias, Peter asks the risen Lord what will happen to John.

His words

Jesus saith unto him, If I will that he tarry till I come, what is that to thee? follow thou me.John 21:22

Jesus tells Peter that John’s future is not his concern. Peter is to follow.

The literal hearers

Then went this saying abroad among the brethren, that that disciple should not die: yet Jesus said not unto him, He shall not die; but, If I will that he tarry till I come, what is that to thee?John 21:23

The brethren turned His words into a rumor that John would never die. John corrects it by repeating exactly what Jesus said, not what they assumed.

Even in the last chapter, John shows the difference between reading what the Lord said and reading into it.

Believe, and live

Looking back over the whole book, the only work John ever names is believing. He states the condition for life again and again, and never adds anything to it.

  • “This is the work of God, that ye believe on him whom he hath sent.”
  • Sons of God by believing on His name.
  • Whosoever believeth should not perish.
  • Heareth and believeth, hath everlasting life, and is passed from death unto life.
  • He that believeth on me hath everlasting life.
  • He that believeth shall live and never die.
  • Believing, ye might have life through His name.

Condemnation in John rests on one thing: “because he hath not believed” ().

Kept for ever

The believer’s security is not a single proof text. John repeats it across the whole gospel in five kinds of promise.

Never

never thirst, never hunger, never see death, never perish, never die

Hath now

everlasting life possessed today, condemnation already past

Not lost

not cast out, nothing lost, none plucked from His hand

Washed, sealed and kept

clean every whit, sealed by the Spirit, kept by the power of God

Raised and received

raised at the last day, received to Himself, with Him where He is

Life that could be lost would not be everlasting. The believer is kept by the Father’s will, the Son’s hand and the Son’s resurrection.

The literalist and the believer

At every turn the literal hearer is shown to be wrong, and the believer looks past the figure to the One speaking.

The literalist argues with the figure. The believer receives the word and understands.

The plain truth

  • God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son.
  • The Son is God. The Word was God, and Thomas called Him “My Lord and my God.”
  • He died for our sins, was buried and rose again the third day.
  • Whosoever believeth in Him should not perish, but have everlasting life, by grace through faith with nothing added.
  • The believer is quickened and sealed by the Spirit and kept by the power of God.
  • He spoke in figures and dark sayings that sorted His hearers.
  • The literalist stumbled over the figure. The believer heard words that are spirit and life.
But these are written, that ye might believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God; and that believing ye might have life through his name.John 20:31
