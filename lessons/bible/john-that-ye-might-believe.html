Chapter by chapter through the figures, dark sayings and paired lines John uses to point every reader to one plain truth: believe on the Son.

Two literary devices to watch for

John wraps the teachings of Jesus in two recurring devices. Both point the reader to the same plain truth: believe on the Son ().

1. The Johannine Misunderstanding Motif

Jesus speaks a spiritual truth in a figure, a carnal hearer takes it literally, and the plain meaning is given.

2. Semitic Parallelism

John pairs lines the way Hebrew writers did. The second line restates or contrasts the first.

Device 1

The Johannine Misunderstanding Motif

Jesus speaks a spiritual truth in a parable, figure or “dark saying,” often uses a word with a double meaning (amphibologia). A carnal hearer takes it literally and physically. Jesus, or John, then uses their misunderstanding to explain the spiritual meaning.

The figure

A picture Jesus uses: temple, birth, water, bread, door, sleep.

The literal hearer

Someone takes it at face value and is confused or offended.

The revealed meaning

The plain truth, most often a call to believe on Him.

Device 2

Semitic Parallelism

John often writes the way Hebrew thinkers and poets wrote: by pairing lines.

Synonymous parallelism

He says the same truth twice in slightly different words.

Antithetic parallelism

He sets two opposites next to each other so each one makes the other clearer.

The second line is not just decoration. It defines, sharpens, or completes the first.

Device 2

1. Synonymous Parallelism

2. Antithetic Parallelism

Meaning

Jesus saith to him, He that is washed needeth not save to wash his feet, but is clean every whit: and ye are clean, but not all. For he knew who should betray him; therefore said he, Ye are not all clean.John 13:10-11
John 13

If I Wash Thee Not, part 3

Peter heard a bath. Jesus meant the cleansing only He gives. The one who is washed (saved) is clean every whit and needs it once; the feet that walk in the world need daily cleansing. Judas had water on his feet but was never washed: he never believed.

Now I tell you before it come, that, when it is come to pass, ye may believe that I am he.John 13:19

He foretells the betrayal so that its fulfillment proves He is “I am” (see the five “I am he” sayings).

That Thou Doest, Do Quickly, continued

Meaning

John 13

John 13

Whither I Go, continued

Meaning

Jesus answered him, Wilt thou lay down thy life for my sake? Verily, verily, I say unto thee, The cock shall not crow, till thou hast denied me thrice.John 13:38

The same saying He gave the Jews (), now to His own. Peter thought of a place he could walk to; Jesus was going to the cross and to the Father. Peter could not even follow Him past the fire in the courtyard (). This sets up chapter 14: “Let not your heart be troubled.”

Figure

John 15

I Am the True Vine

“Arise, let us go hence” (). The teaching continues, likely on the way toward the garden: the vine and branches, love one another, and the world will hate you.

Figure

I am the true vine, and my Father is the husbandman.John 15:1

Meaning

I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing.John 15:5
John 15

I Am the True Vine, continued

The last of the seven figures. Its theme is fruit, and fruit here is spreading the truth and winning souls:

Fruit

Ye have not chosen me, but I have chosen you, and ordained you, that ye should go and bring forth fruit, and that your fruit should remain: that whatsoever ye shall ask of the Father in my name, he may give it you.John 15:16

Fruit unto life eternal

And he that reapeth receiveth wages, and gathereth fruit unto life eternal: that both he that soweth and he that reapeth may rejoice together.John 4:36
John 15

I Am the True Vine, part 3

The believer’s eternal security is already given: “him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out” (); “And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand” ().

But the unfruitful servant is taken away:

Taken away

Every branch in me that beareth not fruit he taketh away: and every branch that beareth fruit, he purgeth it, that it may bring forth more fruit.John 15:2

If a man abide not in me, he is cast forth as a branch, and is withered; and men gather them, and cast them into the fire, and they are burned.John 15:6
John 16

A Little While

His last teaching before the cross: the Comforter’s work, sorrow turned to joy, and “be of good cheer; I have overcome the world” ().

Figure

A little while, and ye shall not see me: and again, a little while, and ye shall see me, because I go to the Father.John 16:16

Confused hearers, some of His disciples

Then said some of his disciples among themselves, What is this that he saith unto us, A little while, and ye shall not see me: and again, a little while, and ye shall see me: and, Because I go to the Father? They said therefore, What is this that he saith, A little while? we cannot tell what he saith.John 16:17-18
John 16

A Little While, continued

Meaning

Verily, verily, I say unto you, That ye shall weep and lament, but the world shall rejoice: and ye shall be sorrowful, but your sorrow shall be turned into joy. A woman when she is in travail hath sorrow, because her hour is come: but as soon as she is delivered of the child, she remembereth no more the anguish, for joy that a man is born into the world. And ye now therefore have sorrow: but I will see you again, and your heart shall rejoice, and your joy no man taketh from you.John 16:20-22

“A little while” was three days: the cross, the grave, the risen Lord.

John 16

Now Speakest Thou Plainly

These things have I spoken unto you in proverbs: but the time cometh, when I shall no more speak unto you in proverbs, but I shall shew you plainly of the Father. At that day ye shall ask in my name: and I say not unto you, that I will pray the Father for you: For the Father himself loveth you, because ye have loved me, and have believed that I came out from God. I came forth from the Father, and am come into the world: again, I leave the world, and go to the Father. His disciples said unto him, Lo, now speakest thou plainly, and speakest no proverb. Now are we sure that thou knowest all things, and needest not that any man should ask thee: by this we believe that thou camest forth from God.John 16:25-30
John 16

Now Speakest Thou Plainly, continued

Here Jesus names the very method this study follows. He has been speaking in proverbs, figures with a spiritual meaning, and now He says it plainly: He came from the Father and goes back to the Father. The disciples hear the plain word and answer the way every figure in John aims: “by this we believe.” Reading His figures as figures, and looking for the plain meaning He gives, is following Jesus’ own description of how He taught.

John 17

Keep Through Thine Own Name

The prayer of Jesus the Son to God the Father on behalf of the apostles, the night before the cross.

While I was with them in the world, I kept them in thy name: those that thou gavest me I have kept, and none of them is lost, but the son of perdition; that the scripture might be fulfilled. And now come I to thee; and these things I speak in the world, that they might have my joy fulfilled in themselves. I have given them thy word; and the world hath hated them, because they are not of the world, even as I am not of the world. I pray not that thou shouldest take them out of the world, but that thou shouldest keep them from the evil. They are not of the world, even as I am not of the world.John 17:12-16
John 17

Keep Through Thine Own Name, continued

Jesus spoke in the world and gave the apostles the word of God. The world hated that word, and hated them for it. But the apostles heard it and believed, because they are not of the world.

John 18

I Am He

Over the brook Cedron into the garden. Judas comes with a band of men and officers, and Jesus is arrested; Peter cuts off Malchus’s ear. Trials before Annas, Caiaphas, and Pilate; Peter denies Him three times; the crowd chooses Barabbas.

As soon then as he had said unto them, I am he, they went backward, and fell to the ground.John 18:6
Jesus answered, I have told you that I am he: if therefore ye seek me, let these go their way:John 18:8
John 18

I Am He, continued

“I am he” is more than Jesus singling Himself out to the guards. It is the name of God (), His declaration that He is the Christ, and the truth of that Word knocks armed men to the ground. He is not taken by force; He lays down His life: “No man taketh it from me, but I lay it down of myself. I have power to lay it down, and I have power to take it again. This commandment have I received of my Father” ().

John 19

It Is Finished

Scourged and crowned with thorns: “Behold the man!” (). Crucified at Golgotha between two others under the title JESUS OF NAZARETH THE KING OF THE JEWS. He commits His mother to John, dies, His side is pierced, and Joseph of Arimathaea and Nicodemus bury Him.

When Jesus therefore had received the vinegar, he said, It is finished: and he bowed his head, and gave up the ghost.John 19:30
John 19

It Is Finished, continued

Every figure pointed here: temple, serpent, bread, shepherd. The work is complete: “My meat is to do the will of him that sent me, and to finish his work” (); “I have glorified thee on the earth: I have finished the work which thou gavest me to do” (). Nothing is left for the sinner to add:

For I delivered unto you first of all that which I also received, how that Christ died for our sins according to the scriptures; And that he was buried, and that he rose again the third day according to the scriptures:1 Corinthians 15:3-4

My Lord and My God, part 3

John 21

What Is That to Thee?

Overwhelmed, Peter leaves the ministry for his old life: “Simon Peter saith unto them, I go a fishing. They say unto him, We also go with thee” (). Six others join him at the sea of Tiberias, and they fish all night and catch nothing. Jesus appears on the shore, fills the net with 153 fish, and serves them breakfast.

John 21

What Is That to Thee?, continued

Then three times He asks Peter, “lovest thou me?”, giving him the chance to answer each of his three denials. He foretells Peter’s martyrdom, Peter asks about John, and the book closes with one final misunderstanding.

John 21

What Is That to Thee?, part 3

John corrects the rumor by repeating exactly what Jesus said. Read what the Lord said, not read something into it.
