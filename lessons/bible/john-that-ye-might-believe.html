That Ye Might Believe

The Father gave His Son, who died, was buried and rose again. Whosoever believeth on Him hath everlasting life. Traced chapter by chapter through the figures, dark sayings and dual phrasings of the Gospel of John.

Swipe or use the arrows to move through the study. Tap any gold reference to read the verse. The Chapters button opens the contents.

Two devices to watch for

In our study of the Gospel of John, we will meet two hallmark literary devices that the author frequently wraps around the teachings of Jesus to unlock deep spiritual truths.

1. The Johannine Misunderstanding Motif

A rhetorical strategy widely recognized by scholars, often triggered by amphibologia, or words with double meanings. Jesus delivers a profound spiritual truth or “dark saying,” a hearer mistakenly takes it in a strictly physical, material way, and Jesus uses that error as a springboard to explain the deeper heavenly reality.

2. Semitic Parallelism

Specifically a blend of synonymous and antithetic parallelism. John uses poetic restatements to set two opposing realities side by side, such as the physical flesh versus the supernatural Spirit, to define and clarify his terms.

A note on where this came from: I noticed both patterns in my own study of John and built the first version of this lesson around them before I knew they had names. They are well known and widely taught. I did not invent them, and I am not claiming to have discovered them. I only found them in the text.

The gospel in one verse

Before we follow the figures, we set down the whole message plainly. John 3:16 holds all of it.

“For God so loved the world”
Salvation begins with the Father’s love and grace, not with anything in us (John 3:17, Ephesians 2:8).
“that he gave his only begotten Son”
The Son died for our sins, was buried and rose again the third day (1 Corinthians 15:3–4, John 10:17–18, John 19:30).
“that whosoever believeth in him”
It is received by believing on Him, and it is offered to all (John 3:15, John 12:32–33).
“should not perish, but have everlasting life”
The believer is quickened, sealed by the Holy Spirit of promise and kept by the power of God (Ephesians 2:5, Ephesians 1:13, 1 Peter 1:5, John 10:28–29).

When John says “believe,” he means believing on this Son: God manifest in the flesh, given by the Father, crucified and risen. Every figure in the book restates this verse.

How John teaches

Again and again in John, Jesus says something in a picture. Those who will not believe take the picture literally and are confused or offended. Then Jesus, or John himself, says it again plainly. The plain meaning is nearly always the same: believe on Him.

Every chapter in this study is laid out the same way:

The figure

A picture Jesus uses: a temple, a birth, water, bread, a door, sleep.

The literal hearer

Someone takes the picture at face value and misses the point, or is offended by it.

The revealed meaning

The plain truth, given by Jesus or explained by John. Most often it is a call to believe on Him.

Jesus said it Himself: “These things have I spoken unto you in proverbs” (John 16:25).

Why speak in figures?

The psalmist foretold that the Messiah would speak in parables and dark sayings, and the other gospels record Jesus explaining why.

A figure does not hide the truth from anyone who wants it. It shows who is listening in faith. The believer looks past the picture to the One speaking. The unbeliever argues with the picture and walks away. John records Jesus saying the same thing to those who opposed Him:

The believer hears that Christ’s words “are spirit, and they are life” (6:63).

Seven figures and the bare “I am”

Two kinds of “I am” run through John. Seven times Jesus joins “I am” to a figure that describes who He is. Other times He says simply “I am,” the name God gave Moses at the burning bush (Exodus 3:14).

The seven figures

  • 6:35The bread of life
  • John 8:12The light of the world
  • John 10:9The door
  • John 10:11The good shepherd
  • John 11:25The resurrection, and the life
  • John 14:6The way, the truth, and the life
  • John 15:1The true vine

“I am he”

  • John 4:26To the woman at the well
  • John 8:24Believe, or die in your sins
  • John 8:28Known when He is lifted up
  • John 8:58Before Abraham was
  • John 13:19That ye may believe
  • John 18:5–6They fell to the ground

Chapter by chapter

Tap a chapter to go straight to it. The Chapters button at the bottom brings you back here.

    1
    John 1

    The Word Made Flesh

    John begins by telling us exactly who Jesus is, before a single miracle is recorded.

    The Word was with God, and the Word was God. That same Word was made flesh and lived among men. The Son the Father gave is no lesser being. He is God, come in the flesh. Everything else in John rests on this.

    No man hath seen God at any time. The only begotten Son has declared Him (John 1:18).

    1
    John 1

    Received Him, Believed on Him

    In the same chapter John gives us the first figure, the first rejection and the first restatement.

    The figure

    John pictures Jesus as light shining into a dark world.

    The world’s response

    The world He made did not know Him, and His own people did not receive Him.

    The revealed meaning

    Then John says it plainly. “Received him” is restated as “believe on his name.” Those who believe are born of God, not by any human effort.

    This is the pattern for the whole book: the figure, the response, and the plain meaning.

    2
    John 2

    Destroy This Temple

    After Jesus drives the money changers out of the temple, the Jews demand a sign of His authority. He answers with a figure.

    The figure

    Jesus speaks of a temple destroyed and raised up in three days.

    The literal hearer

    His hearers think only of the stone building in front of them, which had taken forty-six years to build.

    The revealed meaning

    John tells us plainly that He meant His own body. After the resurrection, the disciples remembered His words and believed.

    The very first figure in John points to His death and resurrection, the heart of the gospel.

    3
    John 3

    Ye Must Be Born Again

    Nicodemus, a ruler of the Jews, comes to Jesus by night. Jesus answers him with a figure about birth.

    The figure

    To see the kingdom of God, a man must be born a second time.

    The literal hearer

    Nicodemus can only think of physical birth, and asks how a grown man could enter his mother’s womb again.

    The revealed meaning

    Jesus says it again two ways, in back-to-back verses. “Born of water” is restated as “born of the flesh”: our natural birth. “Born of the Spirit” is restated as “is spirit”: the second birth. Every person is born once of the flesh. He must be born again of the Spirit.

    Like the wind, the new birth cannot be seen, but its effect is real (John 3:7–8).

    3
    John 3

    As Moses Lifted Up the Serpent

    To explain how a man is born again, Jesus reaches back to a story Nicodemus knew well.

    The figure

    When fiery serpents bit Israel in the wilderness, God had Moses set a brass serpent on a pole. Every bitten man who looked at it lived.

    The figure applied

    Jesus says He must be lifted up the same way, on the cross.

    The revealed meaning

    Looking at the serpent becomes believing on the Son. The bitten man did nothing but look. The sinner does nothing but believe.

    “Look and live” in the wilderness becomes “believe and live” at the cross.

    3
    John 3

    Said, Then Said Again

    Verse 16 repeats verse 15 almost word for word, then opens it wider to show where the gift came from.

    John 3:15The serpent explained
    John 3:16The gospel stated
    (as the serpent was lifted up, 3:14)
    For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son,
    That whosoever believeth in him
    that whosoever believeth in him
    should not perish,
    should not perish,
    but have eternal life.
    but have everlasting life.

    “Eternal life” and “everlasting life” are the same gift. Verse 15 tells us what the serpent pictured. Verse 16 tells us why the Son was lifted up: the Father so loved the world that He gave Him.

    The line is drawn by one thing only: believing, or not believing, on the Son.

    4
    John 4

    Living Water

    At Jacob’s well, Jesus asks a Samaritan woman for a drink, then offers her water of a different kind.

    The figure

    Jesus speaks of living water that only He can give.

    The literal hearer

    She thinks of the well itself. He has no bucket, and the well is deep. Is He greater than Jacob, who dug it?

    The revealed meaning

    Jesus contrasts the two waters. Well water leaves you thirsty again. The water He gives satisfies once and for ever, springing up into everlasting life.

    Even then she asks for it so she won’t have to come back and draw (John 4:15). John later explains the living water as the Spirit, received by those who believe (John 7:39).

    4
    John 4

    Meat to Eat That Ye Know Not Of

    The disciples come back from town with food and urge Jesus to eat.

    The figure

    Jesus says He has food they do not know about.

    The literal hearer

    His own disciples wonder who brought Him something to eat.

    The revealed meaning

    His food is doing the will of the Father who sent Him and finishing the work He was given. That work is finished at the cross (John 19:30).

    Even His disciples stumble at a figure until He explains it. This prepares us for John 6, where He Himself is the bread.

    4
    John 4

    I That Speak unto Thee Am He

    The woman speaks of the coming Messiah, and Jesus answers her in plain words.

    Her hope

    She knows the Christ is coming and will tell them all things.

    The revelation

    This is the first time in John that Jesus says “I am.”

    The believers

    The Samaritans believe because of His own word and confess Him as the Saviour of the world.

    She came for water from a well and went home believing on the Christ.

    5
    John 5

    Equal with God

    Jesus heals a lame man on the sabbath, and the Jews seek to kill Him. His answer shows what kind of claim He was making.

    His words

    Jesus calls God “my Father” and places His own work alongside the Father’s.

    What they understood

    The unbelievers understood exactly what He meant. To call God His own Father in this way was to make Himself equal with God.

    The promise

    Then the plain promise: hear and believe, and you have everlasting life now. You will not come into condemnation, because you have already passed from death unto life.

    Unbelief did not misunderstand His claim to be God. It rejected it.

    6
    John 6

    I Am the Bread of Life

    The day after He fed the five thousand, the crowd comes looking for more bread (6:26). Jesus offers them Himself.

    The figure

    He calls Himself the bread of life and explains it in the same breath: coming to Him is eating, and believing on Him is drinking. One meal, and never hunger again.

    The literal hearer

    When He says the bread is His flesh, given for the life of the world (6:51), the Jews argue about how a man could give his flesh to eat. Many disciples call it a hard saying (6:60).

    The revealed meaning

    Jesus gives the key Himself. The flesh profits nothing. His words are spirit and life, received by believing them.

    Peter understood: “thou hast the words of eternal life” (6:68–69).

    6
    John 6

    Eating Is Believing

    Set verse 40 beside verse 54. Jesus states the same promise twice, once plainly and once in the figure.

    6:40Plain words
    6:54The figure
    every one which seeth the Son, and believeth on him,
    Whoso eateth my flesh, and drinketh my blood,
    may have everlasting life:
    hath eternal life;
    and I will raise him up at the last day.
    and I will raise him up at the last day.

    Same promise, same result. To eat His flesh and drink His blood is to believe on the Son. And the one who comes is kept:

    None who come are cast out, and of all the Father has given Him, He will lose nothing.

    7
    John 7

    Whither Will He Go?

    At the feast of tabernacles, the rulers send officers to take Jesus, and He speaks of going away.

    The figure

    He will soon go to the One who sent Him, where they cannot follow.

    The literal hearer

    The Jews think of a journey on the map, perhaps to teach the Jews scattered among the Gentiles.

    The revealed meaning

    Later He tells His disciples plainly: He came from the Father, and He is returning to the Father.

    They thought of a trip abroad. He spoke of going back to heaven, by way of the cross and the empty tomb.

    7
    John 7

    Rivers of Living Water

    On the last and greatest day of the feast, Jesus stands and cries out to the crowd.

    The figure

    He offers a drink to anyone who thirsts, and promises rivers flowing out of the one who believes.

    John’s explanation

    John stops to tell us what it means. The living water is the Holy Spirit, whom believers would receive once Jesus was glorified.

    Coming and drinking is believing, and every believer receives the Spirit. This is the same water He offered the woman at the well.

    8
    John 8

    Believe That I Am He

    Jesus has just declared, “I am the light of the world” (John 8:12). Again He speaks of going where they cannot follow.

    The figure

    He is going away, and they will die in their sins without Him.

    The literal hearer

    The Jews wonder if He means to kill Himself.

    The revealed meaning

    He explains: they are from beneath, and He is from above. The only way out of their sins is to believe that He is the I AM.

    Here believing on Him and who He is are joined in a single sentence.

    8
    John 8

    The Truth Shall Make You Free

    Jesus speaks to Jews who had begun to believe His words.

    The figure

    He speaks of being set free.

    The literal hearer

    They protest that Abraham’s children have never been anyone’s slaves.

    The revealed meaning

    The bondage is sin, and only the Son can set a man free. A servant can be put out of the house. The Son abides for ever, and the freedom He gives is freedom indeed.

    They heard a question of ancestry. He spoke of the slavery of sin.

    8
    John 8

    Before Abraham Was, I Am

    The argument turns to Abraham, and Jesus makes His plainest claim yet.

    The figure

    Abraham looked ahead in faith to the day of Christ and rejoiced.

    The literal hearer

    They hear only a question of age. He is not even fifty, so how could He have seen Abraham?

    The revealed meaning

    Jesus answers with the name God gave Moses at the burning bush: “I AM” (Exodus 3:14). He did not say “I was.” He has no beginning.

    The unbelievers understood the claim to be God, and reached for stones.

    9
    John 9

    Are We Blind Also?

    Jesus says, “I am the light of the world” (John 9:5), and gives sight to a man born blind. The Pharisees question the man and cast him out.

    The figure

    Jesus uses sight and blindness as a picture of who receives Him.

    The literal hearer

    The Pharisees take it as an insult to their eyesight.

    The revealed meaning

    Their sin remains, not because they cannot see, but because they claim to see while they reject the Light standing in front of them.

    The man born blind received his sight. The men who said “We see” remained in darkness.

    9
    John 9

    Lord, I Believe

    After he is cast out, Jesus finds the healed man and asks him one question.

    He believed and worshipped Him, and Jesus accepted it. Worship belongs to God alone. Then compare how Jesus described the light in John 8 with how He said it again in John 12:

    John 8:12The figure
    John 12:46Said again
    he that followeth me
    whosoever believeth on me
    shall not walk in darkness
    should not abide in darkness

    Following the light is believing on Him.

    10
    John 10

    I Am the Door

    Jesus speaks of a sheepfold, its door, its shepherd and the thieves who climb in another way.

    The figure

    A picture every shepherd in Israel would know: one rightful way into the fold.

    The hearers

    John calls it a parable and tells us plainly that they did not understand it.

    The revealed meaning

    So Jesus says it again. He is the door. Whoever enters by Him is saved.

    “Then said Jesus unto them again” is the dual pattern in John’s own words.

    10
    John 10

    I Am the Good Shepherd

    The figure continues, and this time it points straight to the cross.

    The figure

    A good shepherd gives his own life for his sheep.

    His death and resurrection

    No one takes His life from Him. He lays it down Himself, and He takes it again.

    The promise

    His sheep hear, follow and receive eternal life. They shall never perish, held in the Son’s hand and in the Father’s hand.

    The security of the sheep rests on the Shepherd who died and rose again.

    10
    John 10

    I and My Father Are One

    At the feast of dedication, the Jews press Him to tell them plainly whether He is the Christ.

    His words

    He has just said that no man can pluck the sheep out of the Father’s hand. Now He says He and the Father are one.

    What they understood

    They state His claim in their own words: “thou, being a man, makest thyself God.”

    For the second time they took up stones. Even His enemies said it plainly. They understood He claimed to be God. They would not believe it.

    11
    John 11

    Lazarus Sleepeth

    Word comes that Lazarus of Bethany is sick. Jesus waits two days, then tells His disciples they are going back.

    The figure

    Jesus speaks of death as sleep.

    The literal hearer

    The disciples think Lazarus is resting and will get better. John notes their mistake.

    The revealed meaning

    So Jesus tells them plainly.

    For the believer, death is called sleep because the Lord will wake him.

    11
    John 11

    Believest Thou This?

    Martha meets Jesus outside Bethany. Her brother has been in the grave four days.

    The figure

    Two clauses, one promise. The believer who dies will live again. The believer who lives will never die. Then He asks her directly.

    The believer’s answer

    Martha confesses who He is before Lazarus walks out of the tomb. She believed first and saw the glory of God afterward (John 11:40).

    The proof

    The voice of the Son of God calls a dead man out of the grave.

    Many believed on Him (John 11:45). From that day the rulers plotted His death (John 11:53). The same question comes to every hearer: believest thou this?

    12
    John 12

    If I Be Lifted Up

    In His last public teaching, Jesus speaks once more of being lifted up.

    The figure

    Lifted up from the earth, He will draw all men to Himself.

    John’s explanation

    John tells us at once what “lifted up” meant: the kind of death He would die, raised up on a cross.

    The literal hearer

    The people cannot fit a Christ who dies into their belief that the Christ abides for ever. They understood He meant death, and stumbled at it.

    This is the third time Jesus speaks of being lifted up (John 3:14, John 8:28). Each time it means the cross. By His death He draws all men, and whosoever believeth on Him lives.

    13
    John 13

    If I Wash Thee Not

    At the last supper Jesus rises, takes a towel and begins washing His disciples’ feet.

    The figure

    Peter refuses, and Jesus answers that without His washing Peter has no part with Him.

    The literal hearer

    Peter swings the other way and asks for a full bath.

    The revealed meaning

    One who has been washed is clean every whit and only needs his feet washed. The washing that makes a man clean happens once. Judas was the exception, because he never believed.

    Later that night: “ye may believe that I am he” (John 13:19).

    14
    John 14

    I Am the Way

    Jesus comforts His troubled disciples: “ye believe in God, believe also in me” (John 14:1). He is going to prepare a place for them (John 14:2–3).

    The figure

    He tells them they know where He is going and the way there.

    The literal hearer

    Thomas thinks of a road and a destination he cannot see.

    The revealed meaning

    The way is not a road. It is a Person. No one comes to the Father except through Him.

    Notice the dual phrasing in verse 1: believe in God, believe also in Me.

    14
    John 14

    Shew Us the Father

    Philip asks for the one thing he thinks would settle everything.

    The figure

    To know Jesus is to know the Father. They have already seen Him.

    The literal hearer

    Philip wants a visible sight of the Father.

    The revealed meaning

    To see the Son is to see the Father. There are few plainer statements of His deity in all of scripture.

    And the promise to the believer: “because I live, ye shall live also” (John 14:19).

    15
    John 15

    I Am the True Vine

    The last of the seven figures, spoken to the eleven on the way to Gethsemane.

    The figure

    Jesus is the vine, and the Father tends it.

    The revealed meaning

    A branch lives and bears fruit only by being joined to the vine. Without Him we can do nothing.

    The theme of the vine is fruit. Read John 15:6 in light of what John has already promised the believer: never cast out (6:37), never perish (John 10:28).

    16
    John 16

    A Little While

    Jesus prepares His disciples for His death and resurrection, and they cannot follow what He means.

    The figure

    Soon they will not see Him, and soon after they will see Him again.

    The confused hearers

    Even the disciples ask one another what He can mean.

    The revealed meaning

    His death would bring them sorrow, like a woman in labour. His resurrection would turn it to a joy no man could take away.

    “A little while” was three days: the cross, the grave and the risen Lord.

    16
    John 16

    Now Speakest Thou Plainly

    Jesus names the way He has been teaching, and then speaks without a figure.

    In proverbs

    He says He has been speaking in proverbs, and promises to speak plainly.

    Plainly

    No figure at all: He came from the Father and returns to the Father.

    The believers

    The disciples recognize the plain speech and believe that He came forth from God.

    Jesus Himself called His sayings proverbs. Reading His figures as figures is following His own description of how He taught.

    17
    John 17

    Keep Through Thine Own Name

    On the night before the cross, Jesus prays to the Father for those the Father has given Him.

    Life eternal

    Eternal life is knowing the only true God and the Son He sent.

    Kept

    He asks the Father to keep them. None of them is lost except Judas, the son of perdition, who never believed.

    With Him

    His will is that every one the Father gave Him be with Him where He is.

    The Son asked the Father to keep them, and the Father always hears the Son.

    18
    John 18

    I Am He

    Judas leads a band of soldiers and officers into the garden with lanterns, torches and weapons.

    At the words “I am,” armed men go backward and fall to the ground. Then He gives Himself up willingly, and asks only that His disciples be let go:

    He was not taken by force. He laid down His life, just as He said (John 10:18).

    19
    John 19

    It Is Finished

    Every figure has pointed here: the temple destroyed, the serpent lifted up, the bread given, the shepherd laying down His life.

    The work the Father gave Him to do is complete (John 4:34, John 17:4). Nothing is left for the sinner to add. Three days later the temple of His body was raised, as He had said.

    Christ died for our sins, was buried and rose again. This is the gospel the believer believes.

    20
    John 20

    My Lord and My God

    Thomas was absent when the risen Lord first appeared to the disciples.

    The literal demand

    Thomas will believe only what he can see and touch.

    The revelation

    Eight days later, Thomas confesses the risen Jesus as Lord and God, exactly what John declared in the first verse.

    Said again, for us

    The last dual saying is for every reader who was not there: blessed are those who believe without seeing.

    John tells us why he wrote the whole book: that we might believe, and that believing we might have life.

    21
    John 21

    What Is That to Thee?

    By the sea of Tiberias, Peter asks the risen Lord what will happen to John.

    His words

    Jesus tells Peter that John’s future is not his concern. Peter is to follow.

    The literal hearers

    The brethren turned His words into a rumor that John would never die. John corrects it by repeating exactly what Jesus said, not what they assumed.

    Even in the last chapter, John shows the difference between reading what the Lord said and reading into it.

    Believe, and live

    Looking back over the whole book, the only work John ever names is believing. He states the condition for life again and again, and never adds anything to it.

    • 6:28–29“This is the work of God, that ye believe on him whom he hath sent.”
    • John 1:12Sons of God by believing on His name.
    • John 3:16Whosoever believeth should not perish.
    • John 5:24Heareth and believeth, hath everlasting life, and is passed from death unto life.
    • 6:47He that believeth on me hath everlasting life.
    • John 11:25–26He that believeth shall live and never die.
    • John 20:31Believing, ye might have life through His name.

    Condemnation in John rests on one thing: “because he hath not believed” (John 3:18).

    The plain truth

    • God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son.
    • The Son is God. The Word was God, and Thomas called Him “My Lord and my God.”
    • He died for our sins, was buried and rose again the third day.
    • Whosoever believeth in Him should not perish, but have everlasting life, by grace through faith with nothing added.
    • The believer is quickened and sealed by the Spirit and kept by the power of God.
    • He spoke in figures and dark sayings that sorted His hearers.
    • The literalist stumbled over the figure. The believer heard words that are spirit and life.
