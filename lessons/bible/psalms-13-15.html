Psalm 13

Waiting for His perfect timing

✦ ✦ ✦
Psalm 13 · How long?

How long wilt thou forget me?

How long wilt thou forget me, O Lord? for ever? how long wilt thou hide thy face from me? How long shall I take counsel in my soul, having sorrow in my heart daily? how long shall mine enemy be exalted over me?Psalm 13:1-2

Psalm 13 beautifully captures a very real feeling of the Christian life, the impatient waiting for God's perfect timing, turning into an acknowledgement that He has dealt bountifully with us.

It can be easy to slip into despair when facing trials. It can be easy for us as humans, weak in the flesh, to see the enemy seemingly triumph. To pray and wait upon the Lord, but feel like He may have forgotten you in regards to your trouble.

Cross references • Psalm 13:1

The Lord is not slack

We've discussed the idea that even as martyred saints in Heaven, this impatient call for the Lord to hurry and fulfill His judgment, occurs. How much more can this take hold in our finite flesh?

As Peter reminds us;

But, beloved, be not ignorant of this one thing, that one day is with the Lord as a thousand years, and a thousand years as one day. The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.2 Peter 3:8-9
Psalm 13 · Consider and hear me

They that wait upon the Lord

Consider and hear me, O Lord my God: lighten mine eyes, lest I sleep the sleep of death; Lest mine enemy say, I have prevailed against him; and those that trouble me rejoice when I am moved.Psalm 13:3-4

God calls upon His faithful to wait. And in that waiting, there is promise as well.

But they that wait upon the Lord shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings as eagles; they shall run, and not be weary; and they shall walk, and not faint.Isaiah 40:31
Cross references • Psalm 13:3

To temperance patience

In our patience, God gives us added strength. It is part of a ladder of virtue in the Christian life, elevating us to godliness.

And beside this, giving all diligence, add to your faith virtue; and to virtue knowledge; And to knowledge temperance; and to temperance patience; and to patience godliness; And to godliness brotherly kindness; and to brotherly kindness charity. For if these things be in you, and abound, they make you that ye shall neither be barren nor unfruitful in the knowledge of our Lord Jesus Christ.2 Peter 1:5-8
Psalm 13 · I have trusted in thy mercy

He hath dealt bountifully with me

But I have trusted in thy mercy; my heart shall rejoice in thy salvation. I will sing unto the Lord, because he hath dealt bountifully with me.Psalm 13:5-6

In the end, our prayers for God to act, and reminders that He has promised to tend to our welfare, and bring us peace from our enemies must inevitably lead to our recognition of His mercies and blessings. Of His salvation.

Because, as we call out to Him, it is impossible to not remember the very reason we are trusting in Him, beginning with eternal life granted through Jesus. And with the heavenly perspective of God's grace, it is incredibly difficult to continue in complaint. Because he hath dealt bountifully with us. We've already received more than we could ever deserve.

Cross references • Psalm 13:5

My grace is sufficient for thee

And lest I should be exalted above measure through the abundance of the revelations, there was given to me a thorn in the flesh, the messenger of Satan to buffet me, lest I should be exalted above measure. For this thing I besought the Lord thrice, that it might depart from me. And he said unto me, My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness. Most gladly therefore will I rather glory in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me. Therefore I take pleasure in infirmities, in reproaches, in necessities, in persecutions, in distresses for Christ’s sake: for when I am weak, then am I strong. I am become a fool in glorying; ye have compelled me: for I ought to have been commended of you: for in nothing am I behind the very chiefest apostles, though I be nothing.2 Corinthians 12:7-11

It's also good to remember that, if a prayer remains "unanswered" by our measure, it very well may be answered in His. That His grace is sufficient. We've already been spared, hell and death, and given the glory of eternal life in Christ.

Application • Complaint

Remember the Israelites

Remember the Israelites, who when delivered out of Egypt and bondage, complained incessantly in the wilderness. They complained about a lack of water. They complained about the daunting task of taking the land, despite God promising to drive the inhabitants out before them. They believed the bad spies report, and ignored Joshua and Caleb. They complained about manna, and they complained about quail. And God had to constantly remind them that He had delivered them in the first place.

Sometimes if we feel the need to complain to God, we should remember that he's delivered us in the first place. That His grace is sufficient. Reminding ourselves of what we're going through currently is nothing in comparison to the abundance we've already received.

Cross references • Psalm 13:6

The sufferings of this present time

For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, they are the sons of God. For ye have not received the spirit of bondage again to fear; but ye have received the Spirit of adoption, whereby we cry, Abba, Father. The Spirit itself beareth witness with our spirit, that we are the children of God: And if children, then heirs; heirs of God, and joint-heirs with Christ; if so be that we suffer with him, that we may be also glorified together. For I reckon that the sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory which shall be revealed in us.Romans 8:14-18
Application • Waiting

Patience builds godliness

Keeping that eternal perspective gives us faith in the Lord's ability to deliver us. The suffering of this present time is nothing. Life is but a vapor.

So, having persistent prayer and letting God know how you feel while waiting for His perfect timing to occur is fine. But keep that perspective in mind. And understand that patience builds godliness. And everything to His glory, amen.

Psalm 14 · All have sinned

There is none that doeth good

The fool hath said in his heart, There is no God. They are corrupt, they have done abominable works, there is none that doeth good. The Lord looked down from heaven upon the children of men, to see if there were any that did understand, and seek God. They are all gone aside, they are all together become filthy: there is none that doeth good, no, not one.Psalm 14:1-3

The opening 3 verses of Psalm 14 stand as the definitive and true statements of the doctrine that all men have sinned and fallen short of the glory of God. Quoted in part by Paul in Romans 3:10–12

Cross references • Psalm 14:1–3

There is none righteous

As it is written, There is none righteous, no, not one: There is none that understandeth, there is none that seeketh after God. They are all gone out of the way, they are together become unprofitable; there is none that doeth good, no, not one.Romans 3:10-12

It serves as a universal appeal to the need for the gospel, and the reconciliation of fallen men with a holy God. There is none good but God. All have gone astray.

The opening verse is an even deeper truth, showing the self-deception that leads fools to claim there is no God. They may say this, but ultimately every man knows that God is, and the judge of all things.

Psalm 14 · The workers of iniquity

They eat up my people as bread

Have all the workers of iniquity no knowledge? who eat up my people as they eat bread, and call not upon the Lord. There were they in great fear: for God is in the generation of the righteous. Ye have shamed the counsel of the poor, because the Lord is his refuge.Psalm 14:4-6

Like the scribes and Pharisees that Jesus preaches against in Matthew 23, these fools, the workers of iniquity, devour the poor. They eat up the people as bread.

They call not upon the Lord. They shame the counsel of the poor, they profane the hope of the righteous, they deny the Lord that bought them.

Psalm 14 · Jacob shall rejoice

The salvation of Israel out of Zion

Oh that the salvation of Israel were come out of Zion! when the Lord bringeth back the captivity of his people, Jacob shall rejoice, and Israel shall be glad.Psalm 14:7

Again, the eschatological application is clearly seen in the text, and the prophetic words of David's prayer is ultimately about Jesus.

He is the salvation come out of Zion, and at His coming, Jesus brings all the risen saints, death and Hell defeated.

This was the hope David had. And the hope that God's judgment and resurrection of the righteous and wrath to the wicked, was the refuge of the poor and the oppressed.

This is our future, rejoice.

Psalm 15 · Who shall abide?

A life of separation

Lord, who shall abide in thy tabernacle? who shall dwell in thy holy hill?Psalm 15:1

The Christian life is one of separation. Of leaving behind the things of this world, and the wickedness men do, separated unto His gospel, and proclaiming His truth.

Epistles in the New Testament show us examples of godly living. Admonishments against the behavior that the lost may do, but we should strive to reject even the company of those that behave as the heathen.

But now I have written unto you not to keep company, if any man that is called a brother be a fornicator, or covetous, or an idolater, or a railer, or a drunkard, or an extortioner; with such an one no not to eat.1 Corinthians 5:11
Cross references • Psalm 15:3

Backbitings, whisperings

Again, think ye that we excuse ourselves unto you? we speak before God in Christ: but we do all things, dearly beloved, for your edifying. For I fear, lest, when I come, I shall not find you such as I would, and that I shall be found unto you such as ye would not: lest there be debates, envyings, wraths, strifes, backbitings, whisperings, swellings, tumults: And lest, when I come again, my God will humble me among you, and that I shall bewail many which have sinned already, and have not repented of the uncleanness and fornication and lasciviousness which they have committed.2 Corinthians 12:19-21
Psalm 15 · Speaketh the truth in his heart

He that backbiteth not

He that walketh uprightly, and worketh righteousness, and speaketh the truth in his heart. He that backbiteth not with his tongue, nor doeth evil to his neighbour, nor taketh up a reproach against his neighbour.Psalm 15:2-3

Here, David gives a similar refrain. Against backbiting, talking behind someone's back, railing and gossiping. Against evil done to your neighbor. The very traits David spends multiple Psalms praying imprecatory prayers against workers of iniquity for doing, should not be spoken of among God's children.

Psalm 15 · A vile person is contemned

He that sweareth to his own hurt

In whose eyes a vile person is contemned; but he honoureth them that fear the Lord. He that sweareth to his own hurt, and changeth not.Psalm 15:4

We should condemn vile persons, not partake in promoting them, or their pride in sin. We should honor and fear the Lord. In all our ways, acknowledging Him.

We should keep our vows and promises, especially when it is not to our favor. Where we suffer loss for our honesty's sake.

Psalm 15 · Usury

He shall never be moved

He that putteth not out his money to usury, nor taketh reward against the innocent. He that doeth these things shall never be moved.Psalm 15:5

We should avoid usury, and taking advantage of the poor and desperate. Instead, with gratitude to the Lord, lend freely. By our actions we establish ourselves in truth. Building upon the rock, by being doers of the word.

We shall do these things and shall never be moved.

Application • The mind of a King

The apple of God's eye

Imagine you are a King, and powerful among the people, yet your empathy and thoughts are for justice and the poor, widows, fatherless. Making your priorities about others.

David, far from being sinless, yet this was the mind within him. His power wasn't for him, but from God to help the helpless.

This is what made him the apple of God's eye. And it is the calling we are to answer, as princes of the Kingdom. The same instructions in humility and decency that Solomon, David's heir, taught in Proverbs. The same as the teachings and example of Christ Jesus in His ministry as a Servant.

May this be our starting position, every morning. In our prayers and supplication before the throne, seeking the benefit of others and the glorification of God.
