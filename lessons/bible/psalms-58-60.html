Psalm 58 · Imprecatory prayer

Hard preaching

Psalm 58 serves not only as another example of imprecatory prayer, but as it's meant to be read or sung among the congregation, it serves as an example of hard preaching.

Leather lung, face-ripping, red-hot preaching. Hard preaching by the prophets of old, holy men moved by the Spirit, speaking directly to the sin among the people, is a Biblical standard. It is God's desire for man to be pricked by the Spirit and led to the fear of the Lord by it.

Application • Hard preaching

Peace, peace

Those who hate hard preaching, who seek to be soothed from behind the pulpit with sermons of peace, peace, when there is no peace...They aren't right with God.

Either you are convicted towards repentance and acknowledgement to God of your sin, or you have already seen the wisdom of it, and have enough fear of God and discernment to shout "Amen!"

Psalm 58 · Do ye indeed speak righteousness?

O congregation

Do ye indeed speak righteousness, O congregation? do ye judge uprightly, O ye sons of men?Psalm 58:1

David opens with a scathing rhetorical question. He is calling them out. You play church and are there when the assembly is called out, but are you there spiritually? Do you actually have the judgment your piousness is meant to indicate?

Psalm 58 · The violence of your hands

In heart ye work wickedness

Yea, in heart ye work wickedness; ye weigh the violence of your hands in the earth.Psalm 58:2

The heart is desperately wicked. If you aren't conformed to the will of God for your life, walking in the Spirit so as to not fulfill the lusts of the flesh, seeking the profit of others, judging the poor and afflicted, what are you even doing?

He that is not with me is against me; and he that gathereth not with me scattereth abroad.Matthew 12:30

If you aren't on God's program, and aren't working for the Lord to do his will, you are against Him. Hard preaching is there to act as a goad, reminder to those who grow comfortable and complacent. It's also there to call out the wicked deeds of men.

Psalm 58 · Estranged from the womb

Speaking lies

David goes on to describe what the wicked are like, simile and metaphor, designed to prick the heart.

The wicked are estranged from the womb: they go astray as soon as they be born, speaking lies.Psalm 58:3

The wicked seem to go astray from birth, speaking lies.

Now some in the Reformed movement take this to teach the wicked heresy of total depravity from the womb. "Vipers in diapers," they call it. David's hyperbolic language is shown to be such, when he says they speak lies. Innocent babies cannot speak lies, they cannot speak.

Psalm 58 · The voice of charmers

The deaf adder

Their poison is like the poison of a serpent: they are like the deaf adder that stoppeth her ear; Which will not hearken to the voice of charmers, charming never so wisely.Psalm 58:4-5

The poetic point of how the wicked seem to be, continues. Comparing them to the deaf adder, the snake that resists the music of the charmer.

In ancient Near East culture, especially among the Egyptians, snake charming was not merely a street performance for the tourists, but a valued service that provided medical care for bites, treatment for venom, and exterminator services to rid the house of the creeping threat.

Application • Hard preaching

Deaf before the preaching

The lies of the wicked are as the venom of asps and cobras. And they appear deaf before the preaching of the Lord.

There are many who sit among the church pews today. Not hearing the word preached. Who doesn't want to hear the whole counsel of God.

David has a plea to God for vengeance. He has a desire they meet His judgment, if not the simple way by hearing, then a visceral way by His power.

Psalm 58 · The young lions

Break their teeth

Break their teeth, O God, in their mouth: break out the great teeth of the young lions, O Lord.Psalm 58:6

They might harken to a curb stomp from God when hard preaching does nothing to their hearts of stone.

David continues cursing them and their impenetrable hearts.

Psalm 58 · Let them melt away

As a snail which melteth

Let them melt away as waters which run continually: when he bendeth his bow to shoot his arrows, let them be as cut in pieces. As a snail which melteth, let every one of them pass away: like the untimely birth of a woman, that they may not see the sun.Psalm 58:7-8

Let them melt away as waters, let their bows be broken, and they be cut to pieces. Let them melt away as the snail in the sun. Let them be as a miscarriage, that they not ever see the Sun.

This is hard preaching. How many pastors and preachers back away from it, today? Especially in the age of YouTube and social media clips. Hate campaigns by lewd fellows of the baser sort, stirred up against a local church or its man of God for using similar language against the enemies of God, have led many to silence.

Praise God it has led others to double down and endure persecution for His sake.

Application • Imprecatory psalms

Not rare

David's Psalms contain wonderful and uplifting messages about the glory of God, the blessedness of the man who walks in His righteousness, the faith in Him to deliver during our toughest times, and the praise God only deserves when He sees us through. Positive preaching, a majority of the time.

But imprecatory Psalms, cursing the enemies of God and promising His swift and terrible justice upon them aren't absent. They aren't rare. They come up in the catalogue, often, given as an example. We shouldn't be offended or shocked at such graphic and harsh language. We should never make excuses for sin or give comfort to the workers of iniquity.

Psalm 58 · Before your pots can feel the thorns

As with a whirlwind

Before your pots can feel the thorns, he shall take them away as with a whirlwind, both living, and in his wrath.Psalm 58:9

The day of the Lord is coming. Nomads and travelers used dry desert thorns, brambles as kindling for campfires because they caught fire instantly and burned with intense, sudden heat. They'd cook in their pots above the flame, but God's wrath will be so sudden, like a whirlwind upon the wicked, with His wrath poured out.

The suddenness of His wrath is a key doctrine in scripture;

Therefore shall his calamity come suddenly; suddenly shall he be broken without remedy.Proverbs 6:15
Cross references • Psalm 58:9

Peace and safety

The sudden destruction is linked to the soft preaching of peace for the wicked;

For when they shall say, Peace and safety; then sudden destruction cometh upon them, as travail upon a woman with child; and they shall not escape.1 Thessalonians 5:3

That day will come upon them quickly. Just as it did before the flood.

And as it was in the days of Noe, so shall it be also in the days of the Son of man. They did eat, they drank, they married wives, they were given in marriage, until the day that Noe entered into the ark, and the flood came, and destroyed them all.Luke 17:26-27
Cross references • Psalm 58:9

As in a moment

This truth is foundational. God is a rewarder of those who diligently seek Him, but to those that despise His judgment, swift destruction.

How are they brought into desolation, as in a moment! they are utterly consumed with terrors.Psalm 73:19

We who are His are not meant to shrink from His judgments and wrath, we aren't to be embarrassed by it, or made to feel cowed into denouncing a vengeful God. We are meant to rejoice.

Psalm 58 · The righteous shall rejoice

A God that judgeth in the earth

The righteous shall rejoice when he seeth the vengeance: he shall wash his feet in the blood of the wicked. So that a man shall say, Verily there is a reward for the righteous: verily he is a God that judgeth in the earth.Psalm 58:10-11

Crying out for His wrath upon the ungodly is as natural for the believer as praising God for His longsuffering and mercy. Love requires it.

Let love be without dissimulation. Abhor that which is evil; cleave to that which is good.Romans 12:9

True love and righteous judgment is loving good, hating evil and rejoicing at His judgement.

Cross references • Psalm 58:10

The winepress

We wait with patience not only our redemption, but God's judgment against the wicked.

And they cried with a loud voice, saying, How long, O Lord, holy and true, dost thou not judge and avenge our blood on them that dwell on the earth?Revelation 6:10

Jesus Himself will execute the judgment against the wicked. We shall wash our feet in the blood of them He destroys with His holy word.

And the winepress was trodden without the city, and blood came out of the winepress, even unto the horse bridles, by the space of a thousand and six hundred furlongs.Revelation 14:20
Application • Hard preaching

The same God

Hellfire and brimstone is as biblical as love thy enemies. Jesus is as much a God of vengeance as the Father. There is no variance in God. Jesus the same yesterday today and forever.

Because I have called, and ye refused; I have stretched out my hand, and no man regarded; But ye have set at nought all my counsel, and would none of my reproof: I also will laugh at your calamity; I will mock when your fear cometh;Proverbs 1:24-26

The Prince of Peace is the same God.

So as faithful Christians let us not pull back from hard preaching and imprecatory prayers towards the ungodly. Let us rejoice in the wrath against the wicked, even as we patiently await our salvation. Amen

Psalm 60 · Syria and Edom

David's wars

Psalm 60 presents an interesting perspective of events during the reign of King David. It was written to commemorate the time of war, when Joab strove with Syria in the north, or Aram-naharaim and with Aram-zobah as it's called in the superscription, and then returned to help defeat Edom in the South.

We are given details of the victories in 2 Samuel 8, 10, and again here in 1 Chronicles 18;

And David smote Hadarezer king of Zobah unto Hamath, as he went to stablish his dominion by the river Euphrates. And David took from him a thousand chariots, and seven thousand horsemen, and twenty thousand footmen: David also houghed all the chariot horses, but reserved of them an hundred chariots. And when the Syrians of Damascus came to help Hadarezer king of Zobah, David slew of the Syrians two and twenty thousand men. Then David put garrisons in Syriadamascus; and the Syrians became David’s servants, and brought gifts. Thus the Lord preserved David whithersoever he went. And David took the shields of gold that were on the servants of Hadarezer, and brought them to Jerusalem.1 Chronicles 18:3-7
Cross references • Psalm 60

The valley of salt

At some point, after these lands were put into subjection, attention was turned to Moab and Edom, south of the Kingdom of Israel.

Moreover Abishai the son of Zeruiah slew of the Edomites in the valley of salt eighteen thousand. And he put garrisons in Edom; and all the Edomites became David’s servants. Thus the Lord preserved David whithersoever he went. So David reigned over all Israel, and executed judgment and justice among all his people. And Joab the son of Zeruiah was over the host; and Jehoshaphat the son of Ahilud, recorder.1 Chronicles 18:12-15
Cross references • Psalm 60

Dedicated unto the LORD

We are told of these decisive wins in a multi front war against all the adversaries of Israel. They defeated the Philistines, taking Gath and the Moabites, who became David’s servants.

In the victory against Syria, and the others, David gained territory, tribute and treasure.

And David took the shields of gold that were on the servants of Hadarezer, and brought them to Jerusalem. Likewise from Tibhath, and from Chun, cities of Hadarezer, brought David very much brass, wherewith Solomon made the brasen sea, and the pillars, and the vessels of brass.1 Chronicles 18:7-8
Them also king David dedicated unto the Lord, with the silver and the gold that he brought from all these nations; from Edom, and from Moab, and from the children of Ammon, and from the Philistines, and from Amalek.1 Chronicles 18:11

Other Kings, hearing of these feats, turned and offered gifts and tribute, and of this wealth, Solomon built the Temple.

Psalm 60 · Thou hast cast us off

The wine of astonishment

O God, thou hast cast us off, thou hast scattered us, thou hast been displeased; O turn thyself to us again. Thou hast made the earth to tremble; thou hast broken it: heal the breaches thereof; for it shaketh. Thou hast shewed thy people hard things: thou hast made us to drink the wine of astonishment.Psalm 60:1-3

But Psalm 60 begins on a low note. David cries out that God has cast off His people and turned against their armies. The earth quaked in His displeasure, and David asks to be healed. The King believes that they suffered losses and made the people see the Lord's displeasure by whatever had transpired to have David this despondent. Drunk on the wine of astonishment.

This is a vivid allegory, the Bible often uses drunkenness or the effects of wine to illustrate bewilderment or astonishment. David wants God to know, message received.

Psalm 60 · Because of the truth

A banner

Thou hast given a banner to them that fear thee, that it may be displayed because of the truth. Selah. That thy beloved may be delivered; save with thy right hand, and hear me.Psalm 60:4-5

The banner of the Lord is for those that fear Him and it is displayed because of the truth. That the power of God be displayed in His aid in the battles that come.

That all who see this strength come to fear Him.

David asks that Israel be delivered by the strength of the Lord, by His power, in this time of crisis.

Psalm 60 · I will rejoice

God hath spoken in his holiness

God hath spoken in his holiness; I will rejoice, I will divide Shechem, and mete out the valley of Succoth. Gilead is mine, and Manasseh is mine; Ephraim also is the strength of mine head; Judah is my lawgiver; Moab is my washpot; over Edom will I cast out my shoe: Philistia, triumph thou because of me.Psalm 60:6-8

Without the Psalms giving a running journal of David's mind and heart during the events documented in the historical books, we might think that he and his mighty men were an unstoppable war machine creating an empire. Not a meek and lowly man, depending entirely on the strength of an almighty God.

God hath spoken in his holiness; I will rejoice.

It is the humility of David the Psalmist where we see the truth. His dedication to the Lord in confession and meekness, relying on his faith to deliver them. Not his armies and mighty men.

Psalm 60 · Victory

That which seemed good

The end result was the defeat of Ammon, the retreat and regathering of Syria, only to see their planned invasion stopped and their armies destroyed.

Trusting in the Lord to do that which seemed good, led to all of these military wins.

And David made peace with the enemies and established garrisons in their cities, and collected tribute from all the nations who now became his servants.

Cross references • Psalm 60:8

A Star out of Jacob

This was a fulfillment of prophecy, made centuries before.

I shall see him, but not now: I shall behold him, but not nigh: there shall come a Star out of Jacob, and a Sceptre shall rise out of Israel, and shall smite the corners of Moab, and destroy all the children of Sheth. And Edom shall be a possession, Seir also shall be a possession for his enemies; and Israel shall do valiantly. Out of Jacob shall come he that shall have dominion, and shall destroy him that remaineth of the city.Numbers 24:17-19

This prophecy was given to the wicked Balaam, and applies here, in the Kingdom of David, as well as the future reign of the King of Kings, from the root of Jesse, the son of David, Jesus Christ.

Psalm 60 · The strong city

Who will lead me into Edom?

Who will bring me into the strong city? who will lead me into Edom? Wilt not thou, O God, which hadst cast us off? and thou, O God, which didst not go out with our armies?Psalm 60:9-10

David defeats Moab and stations garrisons throughout all of Edom so that "all the Edomites became David's servants."

And when Isaac blessed Jacob (Israel) instead of Esau (the ancestor of the Edomites), he prophesied that nations, specifically Esau's descendants of Edom would serve Jacob:

Therefore God give thee of the dew of heaven, and the fatness of the earth, and plenty of corn and wine: Let people serve thee, and nations bow down to thee: be lord over thy brethren, and let thy mother’s sons bow down to thee: cursed be every one that curseth thee, and blessed be he that blesseth thee.Genesis 27:28-29
Application • Humility

He never presumes

The foretold blessings become a reality under David. But even as David sees the prophecies of old fulfilled by the almighty God, he never presumes. He always approaches the Lord contrite, asking for deliverance. Never assuming that it's his.

It's amazing that even in the thick of it, as the scriptures come to life, a man who is so highly favored by the Lord remains humble. And petitions the Lord with prayer, rejoicing in song amongst the congregation.

Psalm 60 · Vain is the help of man

Through God we shall do valiantly

Give us help from trouble: for vain is the help of man. Through God we shall do valiantly: for he it is that shall tread down our enemies.Psalm 60:11-12

So whatever fear or setbacks they may have had David trusts God to deliver them and He does. Not only bringing great victory and fulfilling prophecy but giving provision for the creation of the temple under Solomon.

All things working for good to those that love God. This is the greatest lesson of the Psalms. God provides His people protection and blessings, when they humble themselves and come before Him. Let us have that heart of David. Let us seek God early knowing that he is a rewarder of those that seek him. Amen
