Psalm 49 · Rich and poor

Hear this, all ye people

Hear this, all ye people; give ear, all ye inhabitants of the world: Both low and high, rich and poor, together. My mouth shall speak of wisdom; and the meditation of my heart shall be of understanding. I will incline mine ear to a parable: I will open my dark saying upon the harp.Psalm 49:1-4

Psalm 49 is a meditation on the futility of life, in the building of riches, honor and name, when death awaits everyone, rich or poor, wise or fool.

This reflection on life's vanities ties to Solomon's writings in Ecclesiastes, from the call to harken to the preacher to the vanity of life and the only remedy, fearing the Lord and receiving His blessing.

Cross references • Psalm 49:10

How dieth the wise man?

For there is no remembrance of the wise more than of the fool for ever; seeing that which now is in the days to come shall all be forgotten. And how dieth the wise man? as the fool.Ecclesiastes 2:16

This echoes the same sentiment found in verse 10;

For he seeth that wise men die, likewise the fool and the brutish person perish, and leave their wealth to others.Psalm 49:10
As he came forth of his mother’s womb, naked shall he return to go as he came, and shall take nothing of his labour, which he may carry away in his hand.Ecclesiastes 5:15
Cross references • Psalm 49:3–4

Get wisdom

Solomon learned at the feet of his father, and after careful study of all the things his wealth and power availed him of, with the wisdom above other men, granted by God's grace, he would come to the same conclusions David sang of.

The call to incline your ear to hear wisdom, to listen to the dark sayings and parables. To seek understanding is a major theme of Solomon's Proverbs;

Wisdom is the principal thing; therefore get wisdom: and with all thy getting get understanding.Proverbs 4:7

David teaches us the same lessons in Psalms. The wisdom of God is most valuable, because in that knowledge we may find eternal life.

Psalm 49 · Wherefore should I fear?

The iniquity of my heels

Wherefore should I fear in the days of evil, when the iniquity of my heels shall compass me about?Psalm 49:5

David expresses confidence in his salvation. He is not afraid when the time comes to be given the wages of his sin, the iniquity of his heels, the death that awaits us all, the consequences of our own making.

Why should we fear in the days of evil, that comes our own making? He knows his redemption is a gift of God. But the man trusting his riches has no hope at all in that day. They cannot redeem themselves or their brothers, and cannot pay the ransom due God for their sins.

Psalm 49 · The ransom

None can redeem his brother

They that trust in their wealth, and boast themselves in the multitude of their riches; None of them can by any means redeem his brother, nor give to God a ransom for him: (For the redemption of their soul is precious, and it ceaseth for ever:) That he should still live for ever, and not see corruption.Psalm 49:6-9

Again, this idea that man's riches are worthless in the judgment, that they do nothing for the dead, is repeated by Solomon;

Riches profit not in the day of wrath: but righteousness delivereth from death.Proverbs 11:4
For riches are not for ever: and doth the crown endure to every generation?Proverbs 27:24
Psalm 49 · Like the beasts that perish

This their way is their folly

Their inward thought is, that their houses shall continue for ever, and their dwelling places to all generations; they call their lands after their own names. Nevertheless man being in honour abideth not: he is like the beasts that perish. This their way is their folly: yet their posterity approve their sayings. Selah. Like sheep they are laid in the grave; death shall feed on them; and the upright shall have dominion over them in the morning; and their beauty shall consume in the grave from their dwelling.Psalm 49:11-14

Their way is folly. The wise man dies like the fool, like the brute, and all their wealth is left for others. But the precious redemption of the soul, that one lives forever and shall not see corruption, that is of God, through faith in the Lord Christ Jesus.

For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus; Who gave himself a ransom for all, to be testified in due time.1 Timothy 2:5-6
Psalm 49 · Be not thou afraid

He shall carry nothing away

Be not thou afraid when one is made rich, when the glory of his house is increased; For when he dieth he shall carry nothing away: his glory shall not descend after him. Though while he lived he blessed his soul: and men will praise thee, when thou doest well to thyself.Psalm 49:16-18

David laments the fact that worldly prosperity deceives the wealthy, their own riches fooling them into a false sense of security. This is the larger context of the preaching of Christ;

The eye of a needle

Then said Jesus unto his disciples, Verily I say unto you, That a rich man shall hardly enter into the kingdom of heaven. And again I say unto you, It is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle, than for a rich man to enter into the kingdom of God. When his disciples heard it, they were exceedingly amazed, saying, Who then can be saved? But Jesus beheld them, and said unto them, With men this is impossible; but with God all things are possible.Matthew 19:23-26

The only hope of man, rich or poor, is God. Only in Him is salvation possible.

Better is little with the fear of the Lord than great treasure and trouble therewith.Proverbs 15:16
Cross references • Psalm 49:16–17

Neither poverty nor riches

The blessings of this life are not a hope for the next. The praise of men is vanity before the Throne of an almighty and holy God.

Agur's prayer in Proverbs directly addresses this wisdom;

Remove far from me vanity and lies: give me neither poverty nor riches; feed me with food convenient for me: Lest I be full, and deny thee, and say, Who is the Lord? or lest I be poor, and steal, and take the name of my God in vain.Proverbs 30:8-9

Better to be satisfied with what the Lord has given us, and trust and fear Him, than to allow wealth to take us from fearing the Lord, or poverty that brings bitterness to our soul.

Psalm 49 · From the power of the grave

God will redeem my soul

Psalm 49 perfectly encapsulates these wise sayings, showing that David's hope is not in riches, but in the salvation of the Lord.

But God will redeem my soul from the power of the grave: for he shall receive me. Selah.Psalm 49:15

So, no matter the station you have been born to, or the prosperity you've been blessed with, fear God and follow His ways, seeking His wisdom and be blessed by His redemption. Learn from his dark sayings and parables, before it is too late.

Psalm 49 · Understandeth not

Man that is in honour

He shall go to the generation of his fathers; they shall never see light. Man that is in honour, and understandeth not, is like the beasts that perish.Psalm 49:19-20
Cross references • Psalm 49:4

I will open my mouth in parables

The call by David to hear and understand his "dark sayings and parables," brings to mind the way Jesus taught in His ministry.

All these things spake Jesus unto the multitude in parables; and without a parable spake he not unto them: That it might be fulfilled which was spoken by the prophet, saying, I will open my mouth in parables; I will utter things which have been kept secret from the foundation of the world.Matthew 13:34-35
Cross references • Psalm 49:4

A spiritual filter

Jesus revealed to His disciples that parables act as a spiritual filter. To the humble seeker of wisdom, they provide deeper understanding; to the spiritually blind, they remain obscure "dark sayings."

And the disciples came, and said unto him, Why speakest thou unto them in parables? He answered and said unto them, Because it is given unto you to know the mysteries of the kingdom of heaven, but to them it is not given. For whosoever hath, to him shall be given, and he shall have more abundance: but whosoever hath not, from him shall be taken away even that he hath. Therefore speak I to them in parables: because they seeing see not; and hearing they hear not, neither do they understand.Matthew 13:10-13
Cross references • Psalm 49:4

I shall shew you plainly

The unfolding revelation to man throughout scripture was never meant to keep wisdom hidden. Especially, not forever.

Unlike the religions of man, with their esoteric knowledge, vague and shallow sayings fed in dribs and drabs, with no clarity ever given, God's word opens itself up, and Jesus ultimately shows what all these "dark sayings" and prophetic parables actually mean.

These things have I spoken unto you in proverbs: but the time cometh, when I shall no more speak unto you in proverbs, but I shall shew you plainly of the Father.John 16:25
Cross references • Psalm 49:4

He expounded all things

During His earthly ministry, even Jesus' most faithful disciples required His explanation of the parables He spoke in, to the crowds and Pharisees.

And with many such parables spake he the word unto them, as they were able to hear it. But without a parable spake he not unto them: and when they were alone, he expounded all things to his disciples.Mark 4:33-34

But the full revelation of scripture came in the days following His resurrection, when all things were finally made known.

Cross references • Psalm 49:4

And in the psalms, concerning me

To the men He met on the way, in Luke 24, Jesus, beginning at Moses and all the prophets, he expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning himself.

And to the gathering of the disciples, He did the same;

And he said unto them, These are the words which I spake unto you, while I was yet with you, that all things must be fulfilled, which were written in the law of Moses, and in the prophets, and in the psalms, concerning me. Then opened he their understanding, that they might understand the scriptures,Luke 24:44-45
Application • The scriptures

The words of eternal life

He explicitly references the three divisions of the Old Testament, (the Law, the Prophets, and the Psalms, like Psalm 49) and grants them the spiritually supernatural ability to finally see past the "dark sayings" to understand the plain truth of the Gospel.

These things are revealed to us by the Spirit, Who teaches us all things. We are able to see the entire picture, fully revealed in Christ, and the understanding imparted by the Apostles in the Epistles for added clarity and depth. Only the future Revelation of Christ Jesus is still through a glass darkly, (although the gist is pretty clear on timing and events).

We see that the Old Testament scriptures aren't an empty recitation of genealogy, geography and events. They aren't the vain contemplations of man. They are the words of eternal life, and all these were written to point the way to Jesus, and provide us with understanding.

Application • The scriptures

For our admonition

Now all these things happened unto them for ensamples: and they are written for our admonition, upon whom the ends of the world are come.1 Corinthians 10:11

Not only does God's word exist to show us His ways, but ultimately it is the tool He has given us to fulfill the Great Commission, leading others to the truth.

Application • The Great Commission

Ye are witnesses

And said unto them, Thus it is written, and thus it behoved Christ to suffer, and to rise from the dead the third day: And that repentance and remission of sins should be preached in his name among all nations, beginning at Jerusalem. And ye are witnesses of these things.Luke 24:46-48

We are witnesses of these things. We have the testimony of God, revealed. We are given the wisdom and power of His word to teach repentance towards God and remission of sins by His Son, in Jesus' name.

Not in dark sayings and parables, although they help reveal the truth, but by the power of our witness that all is revealed in Christ. Amen.
