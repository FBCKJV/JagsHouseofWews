Psalm 21 · The king shall joy

In thy salvation

The king shall joy in thy strength, O Lord; and in thy salvation how greatly shall he rejoice! Thou hast given him his heart’s desire, and hast not withholden the request of his lips. Selah. For thou preventest him with the blessings of goodness: thou settest a crown of pure gold on his head.Psalm 21:1-3

The dominant theme of the book of Psalms is placing all your faith in God alone, to redeem, to save from danger, to exact vengeance and recompense, to be the strong tower and shield, a ready comfort, faithful to His promises, generous with His mercy and gracious in His gifts.

Trusting in the Lord for every category, especially for our eternal life.

Psalm 21 · Eternal life

Length of days for ever and ever

He asked life of thee, and thou gavest it him, even length of days for ever and ever.Psalm 21:4

Those that have called upon the name of the Lord for salvation, who trust in His promises, are given eternal life. Forever. This is the confidence we have. The sure hope that we place our trust in.

Psalm 21 · The king trusteth in the Lord

He shall not be moved

His glory is great in thy salvation: honour and majesty hast thou laid upon him. For thou hast made him most blessed for ever: thou hast made him exceeding glad with thy countenance. For the king trusteth in the Lord, and through the mercy of the most High he shall not be moved. Thine hand shall find out all thine enemies: thy right hand shall find out those that hate thee.Psalm 21:5-8

And we trust that the wicked will be dealt with according to His perfect and holy judgment.

Psalm 21 · The wicked

The fire shall devour them

Thou shalt make them as a fiery oven in the time of thine anger: the Lord shall swallow them up in his wrath, and the fire shall devour them. Their fruit shalt thou destroy from the earth, and their seed from among the children of men. For they intended evil against thee: they imagined a mischievous device, which they are not able to perform. Therefore shalt thou make them turn their back, when thou shalt make ready thine arrows upon thy strings against the face of them.Psalm 21:9-12

Just as sure as our exaltation and glory is guaranteed, that through the Lord's mercy we shall not be moved, we can know the wicked who reject Him will be swallowed up in His wrath. That His fire shall devour them, as the smoke of their torment rises forever.

Eternal life and salvation, contrasted with eternal wrath and fire.

Psalm 21 · Praise

Be thou exalted, Lord

Be thou exalted, Lord, in thine own strength: so will we sing and praise thy power.Psalm 21:13

We who trusted in Him, sing praises and worship Him. We exalt the Lord, we sing praise to His power. Amen

✦ ✦ ✦
Psalm 23 · The good shepherd

The Lord is my shepherd

The Lord is my shepherd; I shall not want.Psalm 23:1

Psalm 23 is perhaps the second most famous passage of scripture, behind John 3:16, and like it, contains the gospel promise, the good news of the Lord towards believers.

Psalm 23 · The good shepherd

By faith in the Good Shepherd, alone

The world knows the Psalm because it has become the standard go to, in fiction and in real life, recited at funerals by priests and reverends, in every religion that claims the name of Christ. Sadly, for most, the departed have no idea that the promises are not automatic, and aren't achieved by their works, their character or how "good" they are. They are by faith in the Good Shepherd, alone.

Of course, Jesus is that shepherd.

I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep.John 10:11
Psalm 23 · I shall not want

I shall not want

He gave His life that we may be forgiven, and rose again that we may have eternal life. He did this for those that follow Him, and who have been entrusted to Him by the Father. Believers are His flock.

And because He is our shepherd, we shall not want.

What an amazing promise, which God Who cannot lie has given us. We shall not want. He provides all things:

But my God shall supply all your need according to his riches in glory by Christ Jesus.Philippians 4:19
Psalm 23 · I shall not want

All things that pertain unto life

Not riches as this world judges, necessarily, but all our needs are met by Him by Christ Jesus.

According as his divine power hath given unto us all things that pertain unto life and godliness, through the knowledge of him that hath called us to glory and virtue:2 Peter 1:3

You may feel financially burdened, or pressed by health concerns. You might be struggling in your faith, to live by trust in God to deliver. But ultimately, the truth is that all we need, everything that pertains to life and godliness, is given freely, that we may have life and more abundantly in His name.

Psalm 23 · Green pastures

Beside the still waters

He maketh me to lie down in green pastures: he leadeth me beside the still waters.Psalm 23:2

The green pastures and still waters are generally equated to Heaven in the way the world uses Psalm 23, and certainly, what little we know of Paradise is the peace of being in the presence of the Lord. But this stillness is a promise of this life, for a believer.

And the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus.Philippians 4:7
Psalm 23 · Green pastures

All things work together for good

Our burdens aren't removed, but the yoke of carrying them is made light by His grace. Because in the end, whatever we are going through will be for our good.

And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose.Romans 8:28
Psalm 23 · For his name's sake

He restoreth my soul

He restoreth my soul: he leadeth me in the paths of righteousness for his name’s sake.Psalm 23:3

We are renewed in Him, and regenerated by the Spirit in faith, our soul restored. We are led in His ways, and shown the truth from His word, for His name's sake. In Jesus we are made whole, and by His power revealed in us, by His word, we are shown the deeper things of God.

Psalm 23 · I will fear no evil

The valley of the shadow of death

Yea, though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil: for thou art with me; thy rod and thy staff they comfort me.Psalm 23:4

This allegory is considered death itself by the secular world, but note the imagery of a shadow. The Bible says a shadow is not the very image of the things that cast it.

We may be in a low point in our lives, a valley, and maybe even feel the shadow of death on our flesh as we endure this season in our lives, but the strength to fear no evil, to glory in our tribulation, comes from knowing the good Shepherd is with us. His rod and staff, our comfort.

Psalm 23 · I will fear no evil

Thy rod and thy staff

The rod of correction, the staff that guides us as we go astray. His protection, our safety. Knowing He laid down His life for us. That He will seek and save His lost sheep, and bring us home by His power.

What the unsaved world uses as a false comfort in death should be a comfort in this life for saved believers.

Psalm 23 · My cup runneth over

A table before me

Thou preparest a table before me in the presence of mine enemies: thou anointest my head with oil; my cup runneth over.Psalm 23:5

The table He has set for us, the bounty of His blessings, the deliverance from those who seek to destroy us, that they may know we fear the Lord, and glorify Him, is in this life. In the presence of our enemies.

Yes, there is the idea of a feast set for us in the end times prophecy, a wedding feast for the Bridegroom. Served by Christ Jesus:

Cross references • Psalm 23:5

A feast of fat things

And in this mountain shall the Lord of hosts make unto all people a feast of fat things, a feast of wines on the lees, of fat things full of marrow, of wines on the lees well refined.Isaiah 25:6
Blessed are those servants, whom the lord when he cometh shall find watching: verily I say unto you, that he shall gird himself, and make them to sit down to meat, and will come forth and serve them.Luke 12:37

...but the primary application in Psalm 23 is the peace found in this life. At this time. Our cup runneth over with His grace, even now. The table has been set in Heaven, but the bounty we are given starts the moment you call upon His name in faith.

Psalm 23 · All the days of my life

Goodness and mercy

The final verse places this context firmly in the "now" of the believer:

Surely goodness and mercy shall follow me all the days of my life: and I will dwell in the house of the Lord for ever.Psalm 23:6

Goodness and mercy follow us, today. We can rest assured that He has delivered us, and be blessed in this life, knowing we have eternity with Him in the next.

We shall fear no evil, because we have eternal life and shall never perish. We will dwell in the house of the Lord forever, so rejoice even in perils and trials. Rest in the green pastures by the still waters. We have a good Shepherd Who loves us. Amen

Psalm 24 · The Creator

The earth is the Lord's

The earth is the Lord’s, and the fulness thereof; the world, and they that dwell therein. For he hath founded it upon the seas, and established it upon the floods.Psalm 24:1-2

Psalm 24 is a meditation on the truth of the Christ, the Lord, King of Hosts. Creator of all things, and the truth of the way of righteousness for those that trust in Him.

The gospel message of the Psalms, sung in the Temple, performed by the chief musicians and the congregation is the inspired doctrines of God, and the focus is entirely Jesus.

They may have looked through a glass darkly, but the concepts of Christ are hammered home, in verse after verse, chorus and stanzas of praise for the name of God, the Holy One, the Lord.

Psalm 24 · The hill of the Lord

Who shall ascend?

Who shall ascend into the hill of the Lord? or who shall stand in his holy place? He that hath clean hands, and a pure heart; who hath not lifted up his soul unto vanity, nor sworn deceitfully.Psalm 24:3-4

Here in the 24th Psalm, David asks Who shall ascend into the hill of the LORD? or who shall stand in his holy place?

Those with clean hands and pure hearts, who hasn't put their trust in vanity or lying lips, but who trusted the God of their salvation.

Psalm 24 · Imputed righteousness

Righteousness from the God of his salvation

He shall receive the blessing from the Lord, and righteousness from the God of his salvation.Psalm 24:5

We are made clean of our sins, our hearts purified by the imputed righteousness of God. We receive these blessings by grace through faith, and access to the Kingdom, not by our own righteousness but by submitting to the righteousness that is God's.

Cross references • Psalm 24:5

Righteousness without works

But to him that worketh not, but believeth on him that justifieth the ungodly, his faith is counted for righteousness. Even as David also describeth the blessedness of the man, unto whom God imputeth righteousness without works, Saying, Blessed are they whose iniquities are forgiven, and whose sins are covered. Blessed is the man to whom the Lord will not impute sin.Romans 4:5-8
Cross references • Psalm 24:5

Made the righteousness of God in him

There are false religions that deny the imputation of righteousness by faith in Christ. They say that imputation is not taught in scripture. This is a lie.

For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.2 Corinthians 5:21
Cross references • Psalm 24:4–5

Their own righteousness

Our righteousness is as a filthy rag, our works could never justify us before a holy and perfect God. But these men believing vanity, and swearing deceitfully, teach that man needs to establish their own righteousness through works of the law, and many are misled by this lie.

For I bear them record that they have a zeal of God, but not according to knowledge. For they being ignorant of God’s righteousness, and going about to establish their own righteousness, have not submitted themselves unto the righteousness of God. For Christ is the end of the law for righteousness to every one that believeth.Romans 10:2-4
Application • Imputed righteousness

The Council of Trent

Roman Catholicism rejects the idea that we are made righteous by receiving righteousness from the God of our salvation. The Council of Trent (Canon 11 on Justification) anathematized the idea that people are justified “by the sole imputation of the justice of Christ, or by the sole remission of sins” and that righteousness is "infused" by works, sacraments and "grace transforming a person," so that their own righteousness is established, rather than submitting to the righteousness of God.

Psalm 24 · The generation

Them that seek him

This is the generation of them that seek him, that seek thy face, O Jacob. Selah.Psalm 24:6

But let God be true, and every man a liar. The generation of them that seek His face shall not be ashamed. All who come to the saving knowledge of Christ are made pure, in Him. Righteous, in Him.

Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us, by the washing of regeneration, and renewing of the Holy Ghost; Which he shed on us abundantly through Jesus Christ our Saviour; That being justified by his grace, we should be made heirs according to the hope of eternal life.Titus 3:5-7
Application • Imputed righteousness

Remembered no more

The truth of imputed righteousness of God being given the believer is found throughout scripture. And thank God for His grace and mercy. That our sins are washed clean, justified by the blood, and remembered no more.

Psalm 24 · The King of glory

Who is this King of glory?

Lift up your heads, O ye gates; and be ye lift up, ye everlasting doors; and the King of glory shall come in. Who is this King of glory? The Lord strong and mighty, the Lord mighty in battle. Lift up your heads, O ye gates; even lift them up, ye everlasting doors; and the King of glory shall come in. Who is this King of glory? The Lord of hosts, he is the King of glory. Selah.Psalm 24:7-10

The final verses of Psalm 24 are pure prophecy, the adoration and glory of the King.

The opening of the gates to receive Him. The Lord strong and mighty. Who is the King of glory? We know His name is Jesus. King of kings, Lord of Hosts. The everlasting doors of the Kingdom of God open, and receive Him. All glory is given to Him. The Creator Who is blessed forever, amen.

Psalm 25 · I lift up my soul

Let none that wait on thee be ashamed

Unto thee, O Lord, do I lift up my soul. O my God, I trust in thee: let me not be ashamed, let not mine enemies triumph over me. Yea, let none that wait on thee be ashamed: let them be ashamed which transgress without cause.Psalm 25:1-3

The powerful prayer of Psalm 25 is a model for all that trust in the Lord, and a doctrinally-rich reminder of why we should. That we who wait on the Lord will not be ashamed.

Verse after verse, the psalmist has us call upon the name of the Lord, as we read or sing through the Psalm. And verse after verse we are reminded of His mercy and faithfulness.

We lift up our souls, every fiber of our being, unto the Lord, and He delivers us from our enemies. Those that trust in Him are never ashamed, but those that reject His truth are.

Psalm 25 · Teach me thy paths

Shew me thy ways

Shew me thy ways, O Lord; teach me thy paths. Lead me in thy truth, and teach me: for thou art the God of my salvation; on thee do I wait all the day.Psalm 25:4-5

We are shown His ways, taught His statutes, walk His paths, and we are preserved in this life, and established in the next.

Psalm 25 · Thy tender mercies

Remember not the sins of my youth

Remember, O Lord, thy tender mercies and thy lovingkindnesses; for they have been ever of old. Remember not the sins of my youth, nor my transgressions: according to thy mercy remember thou me for thy goodness’ sake, O Lord.Psalm 25:6-7

The meek and his seed shall inherit the earth, and in meekness we humbly admit our sins and ask for God's mercy, to forget them. His lovingkindness is established from old, and the God of our salvation keeps us by His power, unto redemption. What an amazing God we have. How can we not serve Him in truth. Truth of Who He is, who we are, and the reconciliation we have with and in Him, despite our sins.

Psalm 25 · The meek

He will teach sinners in the way

Good and upright is the Lord: therefore will he teach sinners in the way. The meek will he guide in judgment: and the meek will he teach his way. All the paths of the Lord are mercy and truth unto such as keep his covenant and his testimonies. For thy name’s sake, O Lord, pardon mine iniquity; for it is great.Psalm 25:8-11

The Lord teaches sinners like us in His way, and the meek share this truth with the world.

Psalm 25 · His covenant

The secret of the Lord

What man is he that feareth the Lord? him shall he teach in the way that he shall choose. His soul shall dwell at ease; and his seed shall inherit the earth. The secret of the Lord is with them that fear him; and he will shew them his covenant.Psalm 25:12-14

His secret, His covenant, is shown to us. And that good news is spread abroad by those that fear Him, that others may know the mystery of the gospel, and be saved.

Psalm 25 · Mine eyes are ever toward the Lord

Out of the net

Mine eyes are ever toward the Lord; for he shall pluck my feet out of the net.Psalm 25:15
In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths.Proverbs 3:6
This I say then, Walk in the Spirit, and ye shall not fulfil the lust of the flesh.Galatians 5:16

Looking to Jesus the author and finisher of our salvation, we are lifted from the nets, the snares of sin set for us by the wicked. Walking in the Spirit. Acknowledging the ways of the Lord.

Psalm 25 · Have mercy upon me

Bring thou me out of my distresses

Turn thee unto me, and have mercy upon me; for I am desolate and afflicted. The troubles of my heart are enlarged: O bring thou me out of my distresses.Psalm 25:16-17

With importunity and persistent prayer, we remind Him of the goodness He has shown us, and ourselves of the grace we have found in Him. Our troubles become lighter, as His yoke is easy. He brings us out of our distress.

Psalm 25 · Let me not be ashamed

Forgive all my sins

Look upon mine affliction and my pain; and forgive all my sins. Consider mine enemies; for they are many; and they hate me with cruel hatred. O keep my soul, and deliver me: let me not be ashamed; for I put my trust in thee. Let integrity and uprightness preserve me; for I wait on thee. Redeem Israel, O God, out of all his troubles.Psalm 25:18-22

The pain we feel, He understands. The affliction that besets us is often due to sin. Seeking forgiveness, because our troubles may be chastening by a loving Father. Getting right so we may be lifted up by Him. Delivered, because we put our trust in Him. Never ashamed, because His word is true. Waiting on the day of the Lord, and the redemption of all Israel. All His people who trusted in the Lord. Amen

Psalm 25 · A note on the Hebrew

An alphabetic acrostic

In the original Hebrew, David used the format of an "alphabetic acrostic psalm," where stanzas begin with consecutive letters, to act as a mnemonic device, and form of poetry, that David uses multiple times in Psalms.

This stylized form of writing is "lost" in translation, some textual critics may say, but the strained gnat of it all makes them miss the power of what we have in English.

The repeated calls to trust the Lord, seek forgiveness, humbly petition and wait with patience is all beautifully expressed in Psalm 25 as we read it here. The prose of the Authorized English loses nothing to the power of God's word.

So, while it is interesting to note the format, and discuss the ways it was used (here, imperfectly with missing and skipped letters) it isn't necessary for the truth to prick our hearts and give us comfort.

Psalm 25 · A note on the Hebrew

The broken acrostic

However, recognizing Psalm 25 as an attempted alphabetic acrostic in the original Hebrew, intended to run through the 22 letters of the alphabet, does give us the opportunity to see something of ourselves in it.

In this Psalm, the alphabetic pattern is incomplete, the letter vav is missing, resh appears twice, and the final verse falls outside the sequence. The broken acrostic still carries the psalm’s prayer, and focuses on the Lord's mercy and power.

Could the broken format look to the broken-hearted sinner, imperfectly calling upon a perfect God? Could it be that by breaking the expected format, David shows the same brokenness of spirit we have in this life as we wait upon the redemption of Christ in the end?

I don't know. But it sounds good. Selah.
