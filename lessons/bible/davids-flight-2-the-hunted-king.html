David is threatened daily, by enemies and betrayers within the Kingdom, and without. By Saul and his armies, turncoats and opportunistic men.

1 Samuel 23 · Ziph

Doth not David hide himself with us?

Then came up the Ziphites to Saul to Gibeah, saying, Doth not David hide himself with us in strong holds in the wood, in the hill of Hachilah, which is on the south of Jeshimon? Now therefore, O king, come down according to all the desire of thy soul to come down; and our part shall be to deliver him into the king’s hand.1 Samuel 23:19-20

Shortly after making his refuge in the woods of Ziph, Saul's son Jonathan comes to David and assures his beloved friend that his father will not find him.

The Ziphites and Saul pursue David, but he makes his escape to Maon.

About 2 minutes in

Psalm 54 · Ziph

Strangers are risen up against me

For strangers are risen up against me, and oppressors seek after my soul: they have not set God before them. Selah.Psalm 54:3

Psalm 54 is another prayerful song that was penned by David during his flight from Saul. We can sense the urgency of David's prayer to God, with the plaintive Save me, and Hear my prayer.

There are times in our life when the relative safety we have is suddenly threatened. When even strangers rise up against us, and we have unexpected enemies that we weren't even aware of... when in desperation we should turn to God.

Psalm 54 · Ziph

God is mine helper

Behold, God is mine helper: the Lord is with them that uphold my soul.Psalm 54:4

Behold, God is my helper. This simple truth is reassuring in our time of trouble. And blessing on those who have our backs, who give us comfort and support, should be in our prayers as well. David says, the Lord is with them that uphold my soul.

In the Psalm, we see David praising those that help him, (mirroring King Saul's blessing upon the Ziphites for revealing David's hiding spot. The irony here is interesting. Especially given the context).

Psalm 54 · Ziph

He hath delivered me out of all trouble

For he hath delivered me out of all trouble: and mine eye hath seen his desire upon mine enemies.Psalm 54:7

This sudden threat rising against the future King, God's anointed, troubles him enough to bring him to call for God's mercy, to deliver him with His strength, to reward his enemies for the evil they sought to do to him. To bless those that stand with him.

What a model for believers to have, so when our fear rises, we have the assurance of this Psalm to guide us in prayer, along with the results; God delivers us from all trouble, He takes vengeance on our enemies for us. All that we may give praise to His holy name.

1 Samuel 24 · Engedi

In the sides of the cave

And he came to the sheepcotes by the way, where was a cave; and Saul went in to cover his feet: and David and his men remained in the sides of the cave. And the men of David said unto him, Behold the day of which the Lord said unto thee, Behold, I will deliver thine enemy into thine hand, that thou mayest do to him as it shall seem good unto thee. Then David arose, and cut off the skirt of Saul’s robe privily.1 Samuel 24:3-4

This is the context of Psalm 57. David is among lions, physically and metaphorically. The mighty men with him, armed and dangerous. Encouraging David to kill Saul.

David has the opportunity to take matters into his own hands, to be his own avenger. To slay the King and let whatever may come of it unfold. And instead, David trusts in the Lord and does the right thing.

About 10 minutes in

1 Samuel 24 · Engedi

The LORD judge between me and thee

The Lord judge between me and thee, and the Lord avenge me of thee: but mine hand shall not be upon thee.1 Samuel 24:12

He treats his enemy to a sign of peace. David makes known to Saul and his armies exactly the heart he has towards his King.

Reminding Saul of his exaggerated hatred for David, a humble flea, pleading his innocence, and appealing to God, David has negotiated the way of peace, and left it to the Lord to decide his fate. And Saul weeps.

The King asks only mercy for his sons when David comes into his power, and then rides away. Defeated by the truth and the faith of David, who did the right thing, and left his fate to the Lord.

Psalm 57 · Engedi

Be merciful unto me

Be merciful unto me, O God, be merciful unto me: for my soul trusteth in thee: yea, in the shadow of thy wings will I make my refuge, until these calamities be overpast.Psalm 57:1

The psalmist cries out for mercy, twice pleading to God to bestow favor on him. Not grace, unmerited favor, but mercy because David has trusted in Him, making his refuge in the Lord.

Mercy is often said to be not receiving what you deserve, while grace is receiving what you don't deserve. God shows His mercy on us that we are not destroyed for our wickedness. Grace is then receiving eternal life and made partakers of the inheritance of Christ.

Note David isn't boasting. He is throwing himself at the mercy of the court. The mighty Judge.

Psalm 57 · Engedi

They are fallen themselves

They have prepared a net for my steps; my soul is bowed down: they have digged a pit before me, into the midst whereof they are fallen themselves. Selah.Psalm 57:6

Saul, who is irrationally envious of David, and seeking his life has prepared a net for the younger man's steps. The King has happened upon the caves, digging a pit to capture David, but Saul himself has fallen into it. The hunter is in the snare of the hunted.

He has done nothing wrong to deserve what's happening, he has trusted in God, blameless before Him. Even this slight move against the King makes David humble before God. His trust and meekness is rewarded.

Psalm 57 · Engedi

God shall send forth his mercy and his truth

He shall send from heaven, and save me from the reproach of him that would swallow me up. Selah. God shall send forth his mercy and his truth.Psalm 57:3

He then gives the sure hope he has, having yet to be rescued or brought out of calamity, seeing the thing far off, that God shall send forth His mercy and truth.

The Bible tells us that hope isn't a wish, or a desire for an outcome, but the thing itself we are placing our trust in, sight unseen. It is this good report of the faith of David, that we are taught the value of hope.

Psalm 57 · Engedi

Be thou exalted, O God

Be thou exalted, O God, above the heavens; let thy glory be above all the earth.Psalm 57:5

Even in desperation, in danger, pleading his case to the God he's trusted, David gives Him praise. How can we not when we pray? We have all been guilty of haphazard prayer.

And for this, David didn't praise himself. He didn't pat himself on the back for having done the right thing. He gives all credit to God. He sings unto Him among the nations. He exalts God above the heavens and His glory above the earth.

It may be easier in desperate times to remember our place, but it shouldn't be forgotten just because you're in His hand.

The Psalms of David's Flight

The thread through it all

  • Trust in fear. Fear is an opportunity to increase your faith and bring your feet to stay in His ways, to walk His paths.
  • Mercy. His faith is that the mercy of God would be there with him, providing that means of escape.
  • Vengeance is the Lord's. We do not need to fight a battle, but trust in Him to fight for us.
  • The morning. Just as David found deliverance in the morning, Christ’s voluntary obedience unto death led to the ultimate morning of the resurrection.
The thread through it all

He will repay

He shall reward evil unto mine enemies: cut them off in thy truth.Psalm 54:5

Vengeance is Mine, saith the Lord. He will repay. We not only believe He is, and a rewarder of those that diligently seek Him, but that He is our shield and strength against our enemies. We do not need to fight a battle, but trust in Him to fight for us.

When we look at David, especially his behavior towards Saul during this time, especially this incident, and we get the context of his prayerful posture during this time of trial, and great faith towards his Redeemer to judge righteous judgment, and deliver him from evil, we can understand why God considered David a man after His own heart.
