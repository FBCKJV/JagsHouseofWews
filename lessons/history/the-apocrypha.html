The Letter of Aristeas

A forgery

The “Letter of Aristeas” that supposedly commissioned “scribes from each of the 12 tribes” to translate the OT into Greek, appears to have been a forgery written by the Alexandrian apostate Jew, Philo, in the first century AD, to justify his heresies.

No 12 tribes

The “12 tribes” did not exist in 250 BC, and there were no scribes in among the long scattered and destroyed 10 tribes of the Northern Kingdom, as all scribes were from the Levites.

No evidence

There is no evidence of the existence of a Greek translation, other than a copy of Deuteronomy 23-28 in Greek, having zero indication that it was from a larger work, and dated to 150 BC, a century later than the supposed Septuagint. No other evidence outside of Origen exists.

Never considered scripture

Apocrypha was never considered scripture. Not by Jews. Not by the early Christians.

Other 2nd–3rd century figures (e.g., Irenaeus, Tertullian, Clement of Alexandria) show familiarity with and occasional citation of some deuterocanonical texts, but none produce a formal canon list that includes them as Scripture equivalent to the protocanonical books.
