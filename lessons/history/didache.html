Section One

Before We Begin

What the Didache is, and where it stands next to the Word of God.

✦ ✦ ✦

A Witness, Not Scripture

Didache (DID-ah-kay) simply means “Teaching.”

What it is

One of the earliest Christian writings outside the New Testament. A window into what early assemblies believed and practiced. Where it agrees with the Bible, it shows the doctrine was held from the beginning.

What it is not

It is not inspired. It does not add to the canon. Where it goes beyond the Bible, the Bible remains the final authority.

Didache quotations appear in quote blocks. Scripture appears in gold. Quotations are from the public-domain Roberts-Donaldson and Lightfoot translations.

Section Two

Origins

When it was written, where, and how a lost book was found again.

✦ ✦ ✦

When and Where

Written about 70 to 110 AD

No popes, no regional hierarchy, no priesthood over the people. Just local assemblies, each led by its own bishops and deacons. That simplicity is itself a mark of an early date.

Compiled in Syria or Palestine

The bread prayer speaks of grain scattered “over the hills.” That describes hillside wheat farming, common in Syria and Palestine.

Lost and Found

For centuries the West knew the Didache only by name.

c. 70–110
The Didache is written.
4th century
A papyrus copy is made in Egypt. Fragments of it are later dug up at Oxyrhynchus.
1056
“Leon the Notary” copies it into a manuscript now called the Codex Hierosolymitanus.
1873
Philotheos Bryennios discovers that manuscript in a library in Constantinople.
1883
Bryennios publishes the text, and it spreads around the world.

The ancient papyrus fragments agree with the medieval copy where they overlap, showing the text was handed down faithfully.

Section Three · Chapters 1–6

The Two Ways

The first lessons taught to new converts coming straight out of paganism, before they were baptized.

✦ ✦ ✦

Two Roads, One Choice

“There are two ways, one of life and one of death; and there is a great difference between the two ways.”Didache 1:1
  • The Way of Life (chapters 1–4) applies the Ten Commandments and the Sermon on the Mount to life in the Roman world.
  • The Way of Death (chapter 5) describes the life of the unregenerate.
  • There is no third road.

Love God, Love Your Neighbor

“The way of life, then, is this: First, you shall love God who made you; second, love your neighbor as yourself; and all things whatsoever you would not should be done to you, do not also to another.”Didache 1:2

The Didache starts exactly where Jesus said the whole law hangs, then gives the Golden Rule in its “do not” form.

The Other Cheek and the Open Hand

“If any one give thee a blow on thy right cheek, turn to him the other also, and thou shalt be perfect… Give to every one that asks thee, and ask it not back.”Didache 1:4–5
“Let thine alms sweat in thy hands, until thou know to whom thou shouldst give.”Didache 1:6

Generous, but not careless. Hold the gift until you know it will do good.

Guarding Life and Guarding the Tongue

“Thou shalt not murder a child by abortion nor kill that which is begotten.”Didache 2:2

Rome tolerated abortion and the abandonment of infants. The early church named both as murder, standing on the sixth commandment.

“Thou shalt not be double-minded nor double-tongued; for to be double-tongued is a snare of death. Thy speech shall not be false, nor empty, but fulfilled by deed.”Didache 2:4–5

Omens, Charms, and Idols

“My child, be not an observer of omens, since it leads the way to idolatry; neither an enchanter, nor an astrologer, nor a purifier, nor be willing to look at these things; for from all these idolatry is begotten.”Didache 3:4
  • Superstition is not harmless. It is the doorway to idolatry.
  • The Didache forbids not only practicing these things but even being willing to look at them.

The Way of Death

“And the way of death is this: First of all it is evil and full of curse; murders, adulteries, lusts, fornications, thefts, idolatries, witchcrafts, sorceries, robberies, false witnessings, hypocrisies, double-heartedness, deceit, pride…”Didache 5:1
“…hating truth, loving a lie… not pitying a poor man, not laboring for the afflicted…”Didache 5:2

Like the tares among the wheat, false professors sit beside true believers until the harvest reveals them.

The Yoke of the Lord

“For if thou art able to bear the whole yoke of the Lord, thou wilt be perfect; but if thou art not able, what thou art able that do.”Didache 6:2

A gentle word for babes in Christ: grow at the pace God gives, but keep growing.

“But against that which is sacrificed to idols be exceedingly on thy guard; for it is a service of dead gods.”Didache 6:3
Section Four · Chapters 7–10

The Ordinances

Baptism, fasting and prayer, and the Thanksgiving meal.

✦ ✦ ✦

Taught First, Then Baptized

“And concerning baptism, thus baptize ye: Having first said all these things, baptize into the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, in living water.”Didache 7:1

“All these things” means the Two Ways. Instruction came first.

“And before the baptism let the baptizer fast, and the baptized, and whatever others can; but thou shalt command the baptized to fast one or two days before.”Didache 7:4

A candidate who is taught, and commanded to fast for a day or two, is a believer who can understand and obey. There is no place here for infant baptism.

Into the Water

The Didache gives an order of preference.

  • Living water: running water, like a river or stream.
  • Other water: still water, like a pool.
  • Cold water before warm water.
  • Only if there is not enough water of any kind to baptize in: pour water three times on the head.
“But if thou hast not either, pour out water thrice upon the head into the name of Father and Son and Holy Spirit.”Didache 7:3

Why this matters. Pouring is a last-resort exception for scarcity, not an equal choice. The very need for an exception shows that going down into the water was the normal practice.

Fasting and Prayer

“But let not your fasts be with the hypocrites; for they fast on the second and fifth day of the week; but fast ye on the fourth day and the Preparation (Friday).”Didache 8:1

The synagogue fasted Monday and Thursday. The church chose Wednesday and Friday, a deliberate line of separation.

“Neither pray ye as the hypocrites, but as the Lord commanded in His Gospel, thus pray ye… Thrice in the day pray ye thus.”Didache 8:2–3

Three set times gave new believers a habit of prayer, without the endless chanting of the heathen.

The Thanksgiving Meal

A shared meal of worship. Chapter 10 begins “after ye are filled.”

“We thank thee, our Father, for the holy vine of David Thy servant, which Thou madest known to us through Jesus Thy Servant.”Didache 9:2
“Even as this broken bread was scattered over the hills, and was gathered together and became one, so let Thy Church be gathered together from the ends of the earth into Thy kingdom.”Didache 9:4
  • The bread prayer gives thanks for the life and knowledge made known through Jesus.
  • Wheat scattered across the hills becomes one loaf, a picture of God’s people scattered over the world, gathered into one kingdom.
  • The prayers say nothing about the bread or cup becoming Christ’s body and blood.

Guarding the Table

“But let no one eat or drink of your Thanksgiving, but they who have been baptized into the name of the Lord; for concerning this also the Lord hath said: Give not that which is holy to the dogs.”Didache 9:5
“Let grace come, and let this world pass away. Hosanna to the God of David! If any one is holy, let him come; if any one is not so, let him repent. Maranatha! Amen.”Didache 10:6

Maranatha means “Our Lord, come!” The meal ended looking for His return.

Section Five · Chapters 11–15

Church Order

How a local church with no headquarters protected itself and cared for its ministers.

✦ ✦ ✦

Testing Traveling Preachers

“Whosoever, therefore, cometh and teacheth you all these things that have been said before, receive him. But if the teacher himself turn aside and teach another teaching to the destruction of this, hear him not.”Didache 11:1–2

The checklist

  • Does his teaching match what you were taught?
  • Does he move on after a day or two? “If he remain three days, he is a false prophet.” (11:5)
  • Does he ask for money for himself? “If he ask money, he is a false prophet.” (11:6)
  • Does he live like the Lord? Only then is he a true prophet. (11:8)

Beware the Christ-Peddler

“But if he who cometh wishes to settle among you, and is a craftsman, let him work and eat. But if he has no craft, according to your wisdom provide how he shall live as a Christian among you, but not in idleness. But if he will not act thus, he is a Christ-peddler. Beware of such.”Didache 12:3–5

The Greek word is Christemporos: someone who trades on the name of Christ to live off the church.

The Laborer Is Worthy

Having warned against freeloaders, the Didache balances the scale.

“But every true prophet that wills to abide among you is worthy of his support. So also a true teacher is himself worthy, as the workman, of his support.”Didache 13:1–2

This is the principle of the ox. God told Israel not to muzzle the ox while it threshed the grain, and Paul applies that law to those who labor in the word.

The Firstfruits

“Every first-fruit, therefore, of the products of wine-press and threshing-floor, of oxen and of sheep, you shall take and give to the prophets… If you make a batch of dough, take the first-fruit and give according to the commandment… and of money and clothing and every possession, take the first-fruit, as it may seem good to you, and give according to the commandment.”Didache 13:3, 5–7
  • The first and best of every increase: the farm, the kitchen, the purse, the closet.
  • “According to the commandment” ties this to the Old Testament firstfruits and tithes that supported the Levites.

“Your High Priests” and the Poor

“…give to the prophets, for they are your high priests. But if you have no prophet, give it to the poor.”Didache 13:3–4

A careful reading. “High priests” describes the role of receiving firstfruits, as the Levitical priests once did. It is not a priestly office in the church. Christ alone is our High Priest, and every believer is a priest unto God.

And the firstfruits are never simply kept back. If there is no minister to support, they go to the poor.

Reconciled Before Worship

“But on the Lord’s Day do ye assemble and break bread, and give thanks, after having confessed your transgressions, that your sacrifice may be pure. But let every one that hath a dispute with his fellow not assemble with you, until they be reconciled, that your sacrifice be not defiled.”Didache 14:1–2
  • Confession here is brother to brother, settling wrongs before worship.
  • No priest stands in the middle. The “sacrifice” is the church’s thanksgiving and praise.

Two Offices, Chosen by the Church

“Appoint, therefore, for yourselves, bishops and deacons worthy of the Lord, men meek, and not lovers of money, and truthful and proven.”Didache 15:1
  • Only two offices: bishops (pastors) and deacons.
  • “For yourselves”: the congregation chooses. No outside bishop or headquarters appoints them.
“And reprove one another, not in anger, but in peace, as ye have it in the Gospel.”Didache 15:3
Section Six · Chapter 16

Last Things

First what the text plainly says, then what we can reasonably draw from it.

The only complete manuscript breaks off at 16:8. Whatever came next is lost.

✦ ✦ ✦

Watch, and Gather Often

“Watch for your life’s sake. Let not your lamps be quenched, nor your loins unloosed; but be ye ready, for ye know not the hour in which our Lord cometh.”Didache 16:1
“But often shall ye come together, seeking the things which are befitting to your souls: for the whole time of your faith will not profit you, if ye be not made complete in the last time.”Didache 16:2

The World-Deceiver

“For in the last days false prophets and corrupters shall be multiplied, and the sheep shall be turned into wolves, and love shall be turned into hate.”Didache 16:3
“And then shall appear the world-deceiver as Son of God, and shall do signs and wonders, and the earth shall be delivered into his hands, and he shall do iniquitous things which have never yet come to pass since the beginning.”Didache 16:4

Stated in the text

The church is warned that it will face this deceiver.

The Fiery Trial

“Then shall the creation of men come into the fire of trial, and many shall be stumbled and shall perish; but they that endure in their faith shall be saved by the Curse itself.”Didache 16:5
  • This is persecution under men, the great sifting of the professing church.
  • Those who only went through the motions fall away. True believers endure.

“Saved by the Curse itself.” A puzzling phrase. It is most often understood as Christ, who was made a curse for us. Some translators render it “saved from the curse.”

The Signs, the Trumpet, the Resurrection

“And then shall appear the signs of the truth; first, the sign of an outspreading in heaven; then the sign of the sound of the trumpet; and the third, the resurrection of the dead; yet not of all, but as it was said: The Lord shall come and all His saints with Him. Then shall the world see the Lord coming upon the clouds of heaven.”Didache 16:6–8

Public, audible, and visible. And it comes after the world-deceiver and the fiery trial.

The Order the Text Gives

Stated in the text

The order of events in Didache 16, as written.

  • False prophets multiply and love grows cold.
  • The world-deceiver appears and rules the earth.
  • The fiery trial: false professors fall away, true believers endure.
  • Signs in heaven, then the trumpet.
  • The resurrection of the dead, but not of all: the saints.
  • The world sees the Lord coming on the clouds.
  • The manuscript breaks off here.

The church is on earth through the deceiver’s reign, and the trumpet and resurrection of the saints come after it. That is a post-tribulation gathering, in the same order as Matthew 24.

What We Can Reasonably Draw Out

Inference

Before the wrath. The fiery trial comes from men under the deceiver, not from God. The saints are raised at the trumpet before the world sees the Lord coming. God’s wrath itself is not described in what survives, so “pre-wrath” is the natural reading, but the wrath portion is less detailed.

Inference

A later resurrection. “Not of all” means some of the dead are not raised yet. With Scripture, that fits a first resurrection of the saints and a later one before the Great White Throne.

Inference

A future kingdom. The Didache never mentions a thousand years. But it prays for the Church to be gathered “into Thy kingdom” and for “this world to pass away.” The kingdom is still awaited.

Measured Against Modern Views

ViewAgainst Didache 16
Preterism (fulfilled in 70 AD)Conflicts. The deceiver, trumpet, and resurrection are all still future.
Postmillennialism (the world improves first)Conflicts. The world grows worse until the end.
Amillennialism (one general resurrection)Strained. “Not of all” sits awkwardly with a single resurrection.
Pre-tribulation rapture (church removed first)Conflicts. The church is warned it will face the deceiver.
Post-tribulation / pre-wrath premillennialismFits the stated order most naturally.
Section Seven

The Old Paths

What a first-century manual still teaches a local church today.

✦ ✦ ✦

Walking in the Old Paths

  • Teach before you baptize. New believers need grounding in the way of life.
  • Guard the pulpit. Test every teacher by the Word and by his life.
  • Give your firstfruits. Support those who labor in the word, and care for the poor.
  • Be reconciled. Settle what is between you and your brother before you worship.
  • Watch. Gather often, endure, and keep your lamp burning.

Maranatha

“Let grace come, and let this world pass away. Hosanna to the God of David! If any one is holy, let him come; if any one is not so, let him repent. Maranatha! Amen.”Didache 10:6
