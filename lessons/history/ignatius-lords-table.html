I

Section One

Who Was Ignatius?

A pastor in Antioch, writing on his way to die.

A Prisoner’s Letters

  • Ignatius led the church at Antioch, where the disciples were first called Christians.
  • Around 107 to 110 AD he was arrested and taken under guard to Rome to be thrown to the beasts.
  • On the road he wrote seven short letters to churches, and one to Polycarp.
Same rule as the DidacheIgnatius is a valuable early witness. He is not Scripture. Where he agrees with the Bible, he confirms it. Where he goes beyond it, the Bible corrects him.
II

Section Two

The Real Enemy

Ignatius was not fighting about bread. He was fighting about the body of Jesus.

The Docetists

From the Greek dokeō, “to seem.”

  • They taught that Jesus was pure spirit who only seemed to have a human body.
  • So He only seemed to suffer, only seemed to die, and only seemed to rise.
  • John had already called this the spirit of antichrist.
The real question in Smyrna
Did Jesus Christ come in the flesh? Everything Ignatius says about the Lord’s Table answers that question.
III

Section Three · Smyrnaeans 7:1

The Proof Text

The verse most often quoted to prove transubstantiation.

What Ignatius Wrote

“They abstain from the Eucharist and from prayer, because they confess not the Eucharist to be the flesh of our Saviour Jesus Christ, which suffered for our sins, and which the Father, of His goodness, raised up again.”

Ignatius, Smyrnaeans 7:1
  • Who abstained? The Docetists.
  • Why? Because the meal remembered a body they said never existed.
  • Ignatius is exposing their denial of the Incarnation, not defining the bread.

A Figure of Speech

Ignatius calls the meal by the name of what it represents. Grammarians call this metonymy: naming the sign for the thing signified.

The flagIf a man refuses to salute a nation’s flag because he denies that nation has a right to exist, his protest is against the nation, not the cloth. The Docetists refused the meal because they denied the body it remembered.

Jesus spoke the same way. No one thinks He is made of wood or grows on a trellis.

A Memorial of Thanksgiving

“This do in remembrance of me.”

  • The Greek word is anamnēsis: a memorial, a calling to mind.
  • The elements point back to a finished act on Calvary, not to something happening in the eating.
  • And they point forward: “ye do shew the Lord’s death till he come.”
IV

Section Four

Jesus at the Table

What the first Supper shows about every Supper since.

His Body Was Holding the Bread

  • When Jesus said “This is my body,” His own body was whole and sitting at the table, holding the bread in His hands.
  • After the blessing, He still called the cup “this fruit of the vine.”
  • After the blessing, Paul still calls it “this bread.”
Stated in Scripture
The Lord Himself and the apostle Paul both named the elements as bread and fruit of the vine after they were blessed.

Expect This Answer

Ignatius calls it the flesh “which suffered for our sins, and which the Father raised up again.” It is tempting to say, “So the bread was crucified?”

Don’t lean on thatA Roman Catholic will simply agree that the body present is the same one that suffered and rose. That argument answers a view they don’t hold.

Stand here instead

  • His sacrifice was offered once for all, and is finished.
  • He sat down at the right hand of God.
  • His body is in heaven until He returns.
V

Section Five

In His Own Words

Let Ignatius define “flesh” and “blood” for himself.

Faith Is the Flesh, Love Is the Blood

“Be renewed in faith, that is the flesh of the Lord, and in love, that is the blood of Jesus Christ.”

Ignatius, Trallians 8:1

“I desire the bread of God, the heavenly bread, the bread of life, which is the flesh of Jesus Christ… and I desire the drink of God, namely His blood, which is incorruptible love and eternal life.”

Ignatius, Romans 7:3

In Ignatius’s own vocabulary, eating the flesh and drinking the blood describe living by faith and abiding in love.

Eating Is Believing

Ignatius is echoing John 6. Jesus Himself explains what coming and eating mean.

  • He that cometh shall never hunger. He that believeth shall never thirst.
  • He that believeth hath everlasting life. “I am that bread of life.”
  • “The flesh profiteth nothing: the words that I speak unto you, they are spirit, and they are life.”
VI

Section Six

The Altar Is the Assembly

Where the “altar” really is in Ignatius’s letters.

Christ Is Our Altar

Some use Ignatius’s word “altar” to turn the thanksgiving meal into a new and continual sacrifice. Scripture says we already have an altar, and it is Christ, who suffered without the gate.

“Let no man deceive himself: if any one be not within the altar, he is deprived of the bread of God.”

Ignatius, Ephesians 5:2

“Within the altar” means within the gathered church. To be outside it is to separate yourself from the local congregation, and so lose your place at the memorial.

One Eucharist, One Altar

“Take ye heed, then, to have but one Eucharist. For there is one flesh of our Lord Jesus Christ, and one cup to show forth the unity of His blood; one altar; as there is one bishop, along with the presbytery and deacons, my fellow-servants.”

Ignatius, Philadelphians 4:1
  • The point is unity: one meal, one cup, one gathered body.
  • “Eucharist” means the whole thanksgiving gathering, which the Docetists were boycotting.
  • The “one altar” is the one local assembly.

What “Catholic” Meant

“Wherever the bishop shall appear, there let the multitude of the people also be; even as, wherever Jesus Christ is, there is the Catholic Church.”

Ignatius, Smyrnaeans 8:2
  • This is the first known use of the phrase. Katholikos means “whole” or “universal.”
  • It is defined by where Christ is, not by Rome.
  • In his letter to the church at Rome, Ignatius never mentions a bishop there at all.

Measured by Scripture

Ignatius sets one bishop above the elders (presbyters). That is already a step beyond the New Testament.

Stated in Scripture
Paul calls the same men “elders” and “bishops” (overseers). In the Bible these are one office, not two.

Even so, Ignatius’s bishop was the pastor of one church in one city, not a ruler over many churches.

VII

Section Seven

The Answer

Putting it together when Smyrnaeans 7:1 comes up.

In One Breath

“Ignatius is not defining the substance of the bread. He is exposing the hypocrisy of the Docetists. They cannot join a thanksgiving meal meant to memorialize a physically broken body when they deny Jesus ever had a physical body to break. You are reading a 13th-century Roman Catholic definition of transubstantiation back into a 2nd-century defense of the literal, physical Incarnation.”

  • The enemy was Docetism, not a low view of the bread.
  • Ignatius defines flesh and blood as faith and love.
  • Jesus and Paul still called it bread and fruit of the vine.
  • Christ’s sacrifice is finished, once for all.
