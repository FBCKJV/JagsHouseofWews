1Core gospel foundation

This chart treats classic Independent Fundamental Baptist doctrine as the closest practical match to New Testament church order and gospel preaching. These four points are non-negotiable — variations that subtract from or add conditions to them are treated as serious error.

Grace through faith alone

No front-loaded or back-loaded works. Faith is the empty hand that receives Christ.

Tap for Ephesians 2:8–9 →

Provisional / general atonement

Christ died for the whole world. The invitation is genuinely “whosoever will.”

Tap for John 3:16 →

Once Saved Always Saved

The believer is sealed and kept by God. Apostasy proves a person was never saved.

Tap for John 10:28–29 →

Human responsibility

Man is genuinely responsible and able to respond in free response to the gospel call.

Tap for Romans 10:13 →
Reformed Calvinism and Free-Will Arminianism are viewed as two sides of the same coin — both move away from this simple, provisional gospel center.

2Baptist distinctives

Most historic Baptists formally affirm the BAPTISTS acrostic. Differences appear in application and in the doctrines underneath.

B

Biblical Authority

Scripture alone is final authority. No creed or confession may bind the conscience above the text. Preservation implies a pure English text (KJV).

Common drift: Confessionalism elevates 1689/Westminster language above plain reading. Multiple versions undermine preservation.

A

Autonomy of the Local Church

Each local assembly is independent under Christ. No hierarchy, convention, or association has authority over the church.

Common drift: SBC Cooperative Program and association pressures. Some Reformed networks function semi-presbyterially.

P

Priesthood of the Believer

Every believer has direct access to God and the right/responsibility to interpret Scripture under the Spirit.

Common drift: Heavy confessional gate-keeping or elder-rule systems can functionally limit this.

T

Two Ordinances

Baptism (immersion of believers) and the Lord's Supper only. Neither is salvific.

Common drift: Some Free Will / Separate groups add foot-washing as a third ordinance.

I

Individual Soul Liberty

No civil or ecclesiastical power may coerce conscience in matters of faith.

Common drift: Generally retained, though some Landmark or confessional circles tighten practical application.

S

Saved Church Membership

Only those who give credible evidence of regeneration and believer's baptism are received.

Common drift: Practically weakened where open membership or weak discipline exists.

T

Two Church Offices

Pastor (bishop/elder) and deacons. The pastor is under-shepherd; plurality of equal elders is not required by the text.

Common drift: Reformed Baptists often insist on plurality of elders as normative.

S

Security of the Believer

Eternal security / OSAS — the truly saved are kept by the power of God. Apostasy proves the person was never saved.

Common drift: Arminian groups teach conditional security. Some Calvinists reframe it as perseverance that can look like works.

Holding a detailed confession (e.g., the 1689 London Baptist Confession) as binding tends to drift from pure biblical authority — the confession becomes a lens that can override the plain reading of individual texts.

3Soteriology — the gospel core

Grace through faith plus OSAS is the foundation. Systems that condition salvation on human performance (Arminian) or restrict the gospel offer and redefine security in terms of perseverance works (Calvinist) both miss the mark.

IFB baseline Southern Baptist Reformed / Particular Landmark Free Will / Arminian

Salvation by faith alone

IFB baselineBy grace through faith, not of works (Eph 2:8–9). No Lordship front-loading.
Southern BaptistGenerally affirms sola fide. Growing Calvinist minority introduces nuances.
Reformed / ParticularAffirms justification by faith alone, yet ties true faith so tightly to perseverance that assurance is disturbed.
LandmarkGenerally holds faith alone; some circles emphasize local-church baptism heavily.
Free Will / ArminianOften mixes faith with ongoing obedience or the possibility of falling away — effectively adding works.

Atonement scope

IFB baselineGeneral / provisional. Christ died for the world (John 3:16; 1 John 2:2; 1 Tim 2:4–6).
Southern BaptistMajority Traditionalist/Provisionist view is general atonement. Calvinist minority holds limited atonement.
Reformed / ParticularLimited / particular atonement — Christ died only for the elect. This restricts the free offer of the gospel.
LandmarkUsually general atonement, consistent with IFB on this point.
Free Will / ArminianGeneral atonement, but paired with conditional security.

Election & free will

IFB baselineGod elects according to His foreknowledge; man is genuinely responsible and able to respond (Rom 10:13).
Southern BaptistMixed. Many hold a soft view of election; growing 5-point minority. Most still preach “whosoever will.”
Reformed / ParticularUnconditional election and irresistible grace. The free offer is effectively only for the elect.
LandmarkUsually closer to IFB: responsibility and free response emphasized.
Free Will / ArminianStrong free-will emphasis, often at the expense of God's sovereignty — salvation becomes man-centered.

Eternal security (OSAS)

IFB baselineOnce saved, always saved. Sealed by the Spirit (Eph 1:13–14; John 10:28–29).
Southern BaptistOfficially affirms eternal security. Vast majority of churches teach OSAS.
Reformed / ParticularPerseverance of the saints — the elect will persevere; those who fall away were never elect. Can functionally resemble works-based assurance.
LandmarkGenerally holds eternal security / OSAS, consistent with IFB.
Free Will / ArminianConditional security — a believer can lose salvation through willful sin or apostasy. Treated here as a grave error.

Apostasy passages

IFB baselineHebrews 6, 10, etc. describe those who tasted but were never regenerated. “They went out because they were not of us” (1 John 2:19).
Southern BaptistMost interpret consistently with eternal security. Some softer voices exist.
Reformed / ParticularUsually interpreted as evidence of non-election. The warning passages function as means God uses to keep the elect.
LandmarkGenerally the same as IFB.
Free Will / ArminianTaken as proof that true believers can fall from grace and be lost. Misreads the texts.

Works & assurance

IFB baselineGood works follow salvation but never secure or prove it. Assurance rests on Christ's finished work and the Spirit's witness.
Southern BaptistGenerally similar. Some Calvinist circles push Lordship or fruit-inspection that unsettles weak believers.
Reformed / ParticularStrong emphasis on fruit and perseverance as evidence. Can produce a practical works-righteousness of introspection.
LandmarkUsually closer to IFB simplicity.
Free Will / ArminianOngoing obedience is often treated as a condition of remaining saved.

4Ecclesiology, Scripture & separation

A New Testament church is local, autonomous, evangelistic, and under the pure preserved Word.

IFB baseline Southern Baptist Reformed / Particular Landmark Free Will / Arminian

Local church autonomy

IFB baselineAbsolute under Christ. No convention, board, or association has authority over the local assembly. Fellowship is voluntary.
Southern BaptistOfficially autonomous, yet the Cooperative Program and convention structures create practical interdependence and pressure.
Reformed / ParticularAutonomous in theory. Many practice elder-rule and association accountability that can limit congregational freedom.
LandmarkVery strong emphasis on local-church authority — often the strongest among Baptist groups.
Free Will / ArminianGenerally autonomous; associations exist but the local church remains primary.

Succession / Landmarkism

IFB baselineMany IFB hold a practical Baptist succession or “trail of blood” view — emphasizing continuity of New Testament order rather than an unbroken institutional chain.
Southern BaptistGenerally rejects strict Landmark successionism. Accepts broader evangelical baptism in many cases.
Reformed / ParticularRejects Landmark succession. Sees continuity through doctrine and the true church.
LandmarkCore distinctive: only Baptist churches (in succession) are true churches. Closed communion; rejects “alien immersion.”
Free Will / ArminianUsually does not hold Landmark succession.

Communion

IFB baselineTypically close or closed — local-church members or those of like faith and order. Not open to the unbaptized or those in known error.
Southern BaptistVaries widely — open, close, and closed all exist. Trend toward more open practice.
Reformed / ParticularOften close (baptized believers of like faith). Some stricter.
LandmarkClosed communion — restricted to members of the local church (or churches of like faith and order).
Free Will / ArminianVaries; many practice open or close communion.

Offices

IFB baselinePastor (bishop/elder) and deacons. One primary teaching pastor is the New Testament pattern; plurality is permitted but not required.
Southern BaptistPastor and deacons standard. Some churches move toward elder plurality.
Reformed / ParticularStrong preference for plurality of elders as the normative model.
LandmarkUsually pastor and deacons, consistent with historic Baptist practice.
Free Will / ArminianPastor and deacons; some groups have additional practices.

Scripture & preservation

IFB baselineVerbal plenary inspiration + providential preservation. In English this leads to the King James Version. Modern critical-text versions are rejected.
Southern BaptistInerrancy officially affirmed. Majority use modern versions (ESV, NIV, CSB); KJV preference exists but is minority.
Reformed / ParticularInerrancy held. Almost universally use modern translations based on the critical text. Confessions treated as highly authoritative summaries.
LandmarkMany Landmark churches are KJV-preferred or KJVO, similar to IFB.
Free Will / ArminianGenerally accept modern versions; some older groups retain KJV.

Evangelism & missions

IFB baselineEssential mark of a NT church. Aggressive soul-winning, local evangelism, and faith-mission support of church planting (Acts 1:8; Matt 28:18–20).
Southern BaptistStrong missions emphasis through the Cooperative Program and IMB. Methods and theological breadth vary.
Reformed / ParticularMissions affirmed, but limited atonement and emphasis on the elect can dampen aggressive “whosoever” evangelism in practice.
LandmarkStrong missions emphasis in many Landmark/Missionary Baptist groups, often through their own associations.
Free Will / ArminianEvangelism affirmed; conditional security can affect how the gospel is presented and followed up.

Ecclesiastical separation

IFB baselineClear separation from apostasy, liberalism, and neo-evangelical compromise (2 Cor 6:14–18; 2 Thess 3:6, 14). First- and second-degree separation practiced.
Southern BaptistOfficially conservative since the Conservative Resurgence, yet remains in cooperative structures including broader evangelicalism. Separation generally weaker.
Reformed / ParticularDoctrinal separation on Calvinistic distinctives is often strong; fundamentalist-style separation from neo-evangelicalism is less consistent.
LandmarkOften strong separation, especially regarding baptism and church order.
Free Will / ArminianVaries widely; many are less separatist.

Confessionalism

IFB baselineNo binding creed beyond Scripture. Statements of faith are descriptive, not magisterial. Elevating a confession drifts from sola Scriptura.
Southern BaptistBaptist Faith & Message is a consensus document, not a rigid confession. Churches may differ within limits.
Reformed / ParticularHighly confessional (1689 London Confession or similar). The confession functions as a secondary authority and interpretive grid.
LandmarkOften use historic Baptist confessions or abstracts, but emphasis remains on local church and Scripture.
Free Will / ArminianSome have treatises or confessions; less rigid than Reformed Baptists.

5Where others miss the mark

1

The gospel center must not be moved

Reformed Calvinism (by limiting the atonement and reframing security around inevitable perseverance) and Arminian Free-Will systems (by making security conditional) both move the gospel off center. This affects how the gospel is preached, how assurance is given, and how the church understands its mission.

2

Biblical authority vs. confessional drift

When a detailed confession becomes the functional authority or required interpretive grid, the church has begun to drift from sola Scriptura. Historic Baptist practice treated confessions as useful summaries, not binding secondary standards.

3

Preservation and the English Bible

If God inspired His Word and promised to preserve it, then in the English-speaking world that preserved Word is found in the King James Version. Churches that treat modern critical-text versions as equal or superior have accepted a different doctrine of preservation.

4

Evangelism and missions are not optional

A New Testament church is a soul-winning, missionary-sending body. Any system that dampens the free offer of the gospel or shifts energy away from aggressive evangelism has missed a central mark of the early church.

5

Separation protects the testimony

Ecclesiastical and personal separation are obedience to clear commands, not mean-spiritedness. Cooperation with unbelief or persistent doctrinal error eventually erodes the gospel and the purity of the church.
