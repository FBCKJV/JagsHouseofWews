vv. 1–4

Keturah

Then Again Abraham Took a Wife

Then again Abraham took a wife, and her name was Keturah. And she bare him Zimran, and Jokshan, and Medan, and Midian, and Ishbak, and Shuah. And Jokshan begat Sheba, and Dedan. And the sons of Dedan were Asshurim, and Letushim, and Leummim. And the sons of Midian; Ephah, and Epher, and Hanoch, and Abidah, and Eldaah. All these were the children of Keturah.Genesis 25:1-4

Abraham was 175 when he died. He was 137 when Sarah died, and here it says he remarried and had at least 6 sons with Keturah, and they begat many sons, but the physical inheritance and the spiritual blessing, given Abraham by God concerning Jesus, Abraham passes down to Isaac. The child of faith.

vv. 5–6

All That He Had unto Isaac

Gifts to the Sons of the Concubines

And Abraham gave all that he had unto Isaac. But unto the sons of the concubines, which Abraham had, Abraham gave gifts, and sent them away from Isaac his son, while he yet lived, eastward, unto the east country.Genesis 25:5-6
vv. 7–8

Gathered to His People

An Hundred Threescore and Fifteen Years

And these are the days of the years of Abraham’s life which he lived, an hundred threescore and fifteen years. Then Abraham gave up the ghost, and died in a good old age, an old man, and full of years; and was gathered to his people.Genesis 25:7-8

Abraham died and was gathered to his people, in heaven. It couldn't be into his own "bosom" after all. But the patriarch is given an end of days and an account of his death, which the Bible uses to signify the humanity of the fathers and the prophets.

vv. 19–21

Rebekah His Wife Conceived

Because She Was Barren

And these are the generations of Isaac, Abraham’s son: Abraham begat Isaac: And Isaac was forty years old when he took Rebekah to wife, the daughter of Bethuel the Syrian of Padanaram, the sister to Laban the Syrian. And Isaac intreated the Lord for his wife, because she was barren: and the Lord was intreated of him, and Rebekah his wife conceived.Genesis 25:19-21

Isaac, at 60 years old, finally becomes a father. In an echo of his own birth, Rebekah is as barren as Sarah was. Yet God intervenes, and the couple conceive twin boys.

vv. 22–23

The Elder Shall Serve the Younger

Two Nations Are in Thy Womb

And the children struggled together within her; and she said, If it be so, why am I thus? And she went to enquire of the Lord. And the Lord said unto her, Two nations are in thy womb, and two manner of people shall be separated from thy bowels; and the one people shall be stronger than the other people; and the elder shall serve the younger.Genesis 25:22-23

As God says to Rebekah, there are two nations striving within her. One shall be strong, and the elder will serve the younger.

Cross references • Genesis 25:23

Jacob Have I Loved

Malachi 1:2-3

I have loved you, saith the Lord. Yet ye say, Wherein hast thou loved us? Was not Esau Jacob’s brother? saith the Lord: yet I loved Jacob, And I hated Esau, and laid his mountains and his heritage waste for the dragons of the wilderness.Malachi 1:2-3

Romans 9:11-13

(For the children being not yet born, neither having done any good or evil, that the purpose of God according to election might stand, not of works, but of him that calleth;) It was said unto her, The elder shall serve the younger. As it is written, Jacob have I loved, but Esau have I hated.Romans 9:11-13

Now, the Bible says that God hated Esau, and Jacob He loved. This is referring to the nations that come from each, Jacob giving birth to the 12 patriarchs of Israel, Esau the nations of Edomites. Much is made of this distinction, but the fate of Edom is not necessarily reflected in the life of its patriarch, Esau. In the next chapter we will look more closely at that.

vv. 24–26

Esau and Jacob

His Hand Took Hold on Esau's Heel

And when her days to be delivered were fulfilled, behold, there were twins in her womb. And the first came out red, all over like an hairy garment; and they called his name Esau. And after that came his brother out, and his hand took hold on Esau’s heel; and his name was called Jacob: and Isaac was threescore years old when she bare them.Genesis 25:24-26

Esau, the first born, hair red as the pottage he'd sell his birthright for. Red is the meaning of Edom. Jacob, second born, holding on to Esau's foot, as they are taken from their mother.

vv. 27–28

A Plain Man, Dwelling in Tents

A Cunning Hunter, a Man of the Field

And the boys grew: and Esau was a cunning hunter, a man of the field; and Jacob was a plain man, dwelling in tents. And Isaac loved Esau, because he did eat of his venison: but Rebekah loved Jacob.Genesis 25:27-28

God wastes no time on irrelevant details. Isaac's life story is briefly skimmed over. Only the details of the inheritance and blessing matter to the story God is telling. Of the elder serving the younger. Of the law and of faith.

And so, we come to adult Esau and Jacob, men established as a hunter, and a man of the field, while the younger tends flocks, living in tents. No great hero and hunter, proficient with weapons like his elder brother, but a plain man.

vv. 29–30

That Same Red Pottage

For I Am Faint

And Jacob sod pottage: and Esau came from the field, and he was faint: And Esau said to Jacob, Feed me, I pray thee, with that same red pottage; for I am faint: therefore was his name called Edom.Genesis 25:29-30

Jacob makes a red pottage, a lentil soup. Esau, hungry and tired, saying "Feed me, I pray thee, with that same red pottage; for I am faint."

Most interpret this to just mean normal hunger. Not truly fainting or starving in a serious sense. Just ravenous from being out in the field hunting, and wanting to please his flesh by eating the pottage.

vv. 31–34

Esau Despised His Birthright

Sell Me This Day Thy Birthright

And Jacob said, Sell me this day thy birthright. And Esau said, Behold, I am at the point to die: and what profit shall this birthright do to me? And Jacob said, Swear to me this day; and he sware unto him: and he sold his birthright unto Jacob. Then Jacob gave Esau bread and pottage of lentiles; and he did eat and drink, and rose up, and went his way: thus Esau despised his birthright.Genesis 25:31-34

Jacob says he will feed his brother in exchange for his birthright. And despising his birthright, that is not caring about it, willing to trade it for a momentary satisfaction of the flesh, Esau agrees.

Cross references • Genesis 25:34

One Morsel of Meat

Hebrews 12:16-17

Lest there be any fornicator, or profane person, as Esau, who for one morsel of meat sold his birthright. For ye know how that afterward, when he would have inherited the blessing, he was rejected: for he found no place of repentance, though he sought it carefully with tears.Hebrews 12:16-17

That later, though he repented of giving away his inheritance by law to his younger brother, having a change of heart too late, and losing the eventual blessings meant for him.

Application • The birthright

Like Esau

A Grave Warning

Christians who live carnally, after the flesh, despising their birthright received through Christ, losing blessings of God for their own morsel of meat, a simple bowl of pottage, are like Esau. And while we still retain sonship, and gain a share in the kingdom, the blessings we lose here in this life and in the time to come are lost or diminished, regardless of how tearfully we may regret it when our flesh dies. It's a grave warning.

We know, having hindsight what a fool Esau, now Edom was. Yet how many times do we do the same, for much less than even a bowl of lentils?

Application • The elder and the younger

Faith over Flesh

A Pattern for the Salvation to Come

For now, the parallels to Cain and Abel, Sarah and Hagar, Isaac and Ishmael, Jews and Gentiles, even Jesus and Barabbas, the free will choices given to show that God favors faith over the law, spirit over the flesh, blesses them that trust His promises and do His will, giving them the inheritance for their faith, and the casting out of those who are after the flesh, even if by law they have a birthright claim.

The entire Old Testament serves as a pattern for the salvation to come offered in Christ. Both the choice to believe, placing faith above flesh, explaining the theology of the New Testament with examples from the Old. And continually we see the eldest by law passed over, to serve the younger, who by faith receives both the birthright and blessing.

Application • Spiritual Edom

Not Replacement, Rejection

They Sold Their Birthright

When applied to the Jews and Gentiles, this is called "replacement theology," which is a misnomer. Like Esau, Jews sought an earthly kingdom from an earthly messiah, to satisfy their fleshly desires. They sold their birthright, despising their inheritance, by choosing to kill Christ. By rejecting the blessing that had carried them from Canaan to Egypt, back to the promised land. Through wars, invasions and captivity, they lost everything, for the younger, the Gentiles, to gain.

It's not replacement, it's rejection. Spiritual Edom. And like Edom after the flesh, they were made desolate by the Lord.

Modern, dispensationalist Christians who have elevated Jews to coequal status, even giving them honor as "older brothers," claiming that inheritance of the flesh, in the law means they retain the birthright and blessing they rejected when they reject Christ, are carnal and risk going the way of Edom themselves.

Application • Spiritual Edom

Too Late

They question the judgment of God and ignore these warnings found throughout the Bible, in exchange for...what? The false teachers get paid.
