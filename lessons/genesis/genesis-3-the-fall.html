Genesis Chapter 3

The Fall  •  The Curse  •  The First Promise

v. 1 The Serpent's Opening Move

"Yea, Hath God Said?" — Planting Doubt in God's Word

1Now the serpent was more subtil than any beast of the field which the LORD God had made. And he said unto the woman, Yea, hath God said, Ye shall not eat of every tree of the garden?

The serpent does not begin with a lie — he begins with a question. He does not deny God's word; he causes Eve to doubt it. The first weapon of the enemy is not atheism but uncertainty about what God has said. 2 Cor. 11:3
vv. 2–5 The Appeal — Become Like God

The Lie: "Ye Shall Not Surely Die"

2And the woman said unto the serpent, We may eat of the fruit of the trees of the garden: 3But of the fruit of the tree which is in the midst of the garden, God hath said, Ye shall not eat of it, neither shall ye touch it, lest ye die.

4And the serpent said unto the woman, Ye shall not surely die: 5For God doth know that in the day ye eat thereof, then your eyes shall be opened, and ye shall be as gods, knowing good and evil.

The serpent's appeal: you are being held back. God is withholding something good from you. You can be as God. Pride was the first sin in heaven — Isa. 14:13–14 — the same bait is set for man.
v. 6 The Lust of the Eyes — Eve's Choice, Then Adam's

She Saw • She Took • She Gave — He Did Eat

6And when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was pleasant to the eyes, and a tree to be desired to make one wise, she took of the fruit thereof, and did eat, and gave also unto her husband with her; and he did eat.

Eve was deceived — she convinced herself through three appeals: good for food (lust of the flesh), pleasant to the eyes (lust of the eyes), desired to make wise (pride of life) — 1 John 2:16. Adam was NOT deceived — 1 Tim. 2:14. He received God's command directly before Eve was created — Gen. 2:16–17. Adam chose sin with open eyes.
The Cover-Up
v. 7 Their Own Works — Insufficient Before a Holy God

Fig Leaves — Man's Attempt to Cover His Own Sin

7And the eyes of them both were opened, and they knew that they were naked; and they sewed fig leaves together, and made themselves aprons.

Man's first religious instinct after sin: cover yourself by your own works. Fig leaves could not atone. No human effort, religion, or righteousness can cover the guilt of sin before a holy God. Isa. 64:6
vv. 8–10 The LORD God Walking — They Hid

"Where Art Thou?" — God Comes Seeking the Sinner

8And they heard the voice of the LORD God walking in the garden in the cool of the day: and Adam and his wife hid themselves from the presence of the LORD God amongst the trees of the garden.

9And the LORD God called unto Adam, and said unto him, Where art thou?

10And he said, I heard thy voice in the garden, and I was afraid, because I was naked; and I hid myself.

God knew exactly where Adam was. The question is not for God's information — it is for Adam's conviction. God is the one who comes seeking the sinner. This is the gospel pattern from the very beginning. Luke 19:10
vv. 11–13 The Blame Game — No One Takes Responsibility

Adam Blames God. Eve Blames the Serpent. All Are Guilty.

11And he said, Who told thee that thou wast naked? Hast thou eaten of the tree, whereof I commanded thee that thou shouldest not eat?

12And the man said, The woman whom thou gavest to be with me, she gave me of the tree, and I did eat.

13And the LORD God said unto the woman, What is this that thou hast done? And the woman said, The serpent beguiled me, and I did eat.

Adam blames the woman — and subtly blames God who gave her. Eve blames the serpent. But God holds each accountable in turn. We are each guilty of our own choices. Rom. 14:12  •  Ezek. 18:20
The Judgment of God
vv. 14–15 The Serpent Cursed  •  The First Prophecy of Christ

The Proto-Evangelium — The Seed of the Woman Shall Bruise His Head

14And the LORD God said unto the serpent, Because thou hast done this, thou art cursed above all cattle, and above every beast of the field; upon thy belly shalt thou go, and dust shalt thou eat all the days of thy life:

15 And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel. The very first word of gospel in all Scripture. Before one curse falls on man, God announces the remedy. The seed of the woman — not of a man — points to the virgin birth. Gal. 4:4. He shall bruise thy head — fatal, final blow at the cross and resurrection. Col. 2:15  •  Rom. 16:20. Thou shalt bruise his heel — the suffering of Christ. Final end of the serpent: Rev. 20:10
v. 16 Eve Cursed — And All Women

Sorrow in Childbirth  •  Desire and Rule

16Unto the woman he said, I will greatly multiply thy sorrow and thy conception; in sorrow thou shalt bring forth children; and thy desire shall be to thy husband, and he shall rule over thee.

The curse falls on the role God designed — childbearing becomes sorrow. The creation order of male headship is now complicated by the effects of sin. Every woman who has ever suffered in childbirth bears the mark of the Fall. Hope: 1 Tim. 2:15
vv. 17–19 Adam Cursed — And All Men

Thorns and Thistles  •  Sweat  •  Dust to Dust

17And unto Adam he said, Because thou hast hearkened unto the voice of thy wife, and hast eaten of the tree, of which I commanded thee, saying, Thou shalt not eat of it: cursed is the ground for thy sake; in sorrow shalt thou eat of it all the days of thy life;

18Thorns also and thistles shall it bring forth to thee; and thou shalt eat the herb of the field;

19In the sweat of thy face shalt thou eat bread, till thou return unto the ground; for out of it wast thou taken: for dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return.

Physical death enters here — every grave is the direct consequence of this moment. Every man who has ever broken his back for daily bread bears the mark of the Fall. The thorns Adam now cultivates foreshadow the crown of thorns on the Last Adam — Christ bore the curse in full. Gal. 3:13  •  Rom. 5:12
The Gospel Enacted
v. 21 The LORD Makes Coats of Skins

Blood Must Be Shed — God Clothes the Sinner

21 Unto Adam also and to his wife did the LORD God make coats of skins, and clothed them. Man covered himself with leaves — his own works. God replaced those leaves with skins. An animal had to die. Blood had to be shed. This is the first sacrifice in Scripture — and it is God who performs it. God himself provides the covering for sin at the cost of innocent life. Heb. 9:22  •  John 1:29  •  Isa. 61:10
Spiritual Death — Cast Out
vv. 22–24 Expelled — Separated from God's Presence

Driven Out  •  The Flaming Sword  •  The Tree of Life Guarded

22And the LORD God said, Behold, the man is become as one of us, to know good and evil: and now, lest he put forth his hand, and take also of the tree of life, and eat, and live for ever:

23Therefore the LORD God sent him forth from the garden of Eden, to till the ground from whence he was taken.

24So he drove out the man; and he placed at the east of the garden of Eden Cherubims, and a flaming sword which turned every way, to keep the way of the tree of life.

Spiritual death — separation from the presence of God where He walked with man. The way to the tree of life is now barred. It will not be opened again until the cross tears the veil — Matt. 27:51 — and the last chapter of Scripture restores what was lost: Rev. 22:14
The Whole Story Genesis 3 in One Frame

What the Fall Cost — What Christ Restores

Man doubted God's word. Man desired to be God. Man chose sin. Man tried to cover himself. Man hid from God. Man blamed others. Man was cursed. Man was driven out.

But before the first curse was pronounced — God announced a Deliverer. Before the first animal died — God showed that blood would cover sin. Before the gate closed — God was already planning to open it again.

Romans 5:19 — For as by one man's disobedience many were made sinners, so by the obedience of one shall many be made righteous.

Cross References  •  Romans 5

Adam's Choice — Death Through One Man, Life Through One Man

Romans 5:12

"Wherefore, as by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned:"

→ Sin entered through Adam's wilful choice. He was the federal head of the human race. His act condemned all who come from him.

Romans 5:14

"Nevertheless death reigned from Adam to Moses, even over them that had not sinned after the similitude of Adam's transgression, who is the figure of him that was to come."

→ Adam is called a "figure" — a type — of Christ. The first Adam plunged the race into death; the Last Adam would lift it back to life.

Romans 5:17

"For if by one man's offence death reigned by one; much more they which receive abundance of grace and of the gift of righteousness shall reign in life by one, Jesus Christ."

→ The "much more" of grace — what Adam lost, Christ restores in abundance. Death reigned through Adam; the redeemed shall reign in life through Christ.

Romans 5:18–19

"Therefore as by the offence of one judgment came upon all men to condemnation; even so by the righteousness of one the free gift came upon all men unto justification of life. For as by one man's disobedience many were made sinners, so by the obedience of one shall many be made righteous."

→ The remedy is perfectly shaped to match the wound. One act of disobedience — condemnation. One act of obedience at the cross — justification.

1 Corinthians 15:21–22

"For since by man came death, by man came also the resurrection of the dead. For as in Adam all die, even so in Christ shall all be made alive."

→ Every human being stands in one of two federal heads: in Adam (dead), or in Christ (alive). There is no neutral ground.

1 Timothy 2:14

"And Adam was not deceived, but the woman being deceived was in the transgression."

→ Eve was deceived. Adam was not. Adam chose to follow his wife into sin with full knowledge of God's command. His representative role is therefore the greater.

Adam received the command directly from God before Eve existed. He was not deceived — he sinned deliberately. This is why Scripture assigns the Fall to Adam, not Eve. But the glory of the gospel is that the same principle that condemned us — one man representing many — is the very mechanism God uses to save us. In Adam, all die. In Christ, all who believe are made alive.
