vv. 1–4

One Language, and One Speech

Let Us Make Us a Name

And the whole earth was of one language, and of one speech. And it came to pass, as they journeyed from the east, that they found a plain in the land of Shinar; and they dwelt there. And they said one to another, Go to, let us make brick, and burn them thoroughly. And they had brick for stone, and slime had they for morter. And they said, Go to, let us build us a city and a tower, whose top may reach unto heaven; and let us make us a name, lest we be scattered abroad upon the face of the whole earth.Genesis 11:1-4

About 100 years after the flood, in the year of Peleg’s birth (for in his days was the earth divided), the people imagined a vain thing. A tower to Heaven. That they could work their way to Heaven and be like God.

vv. 5–7

The Lord Came Down

Go To, Let Us Go Down

And the Lord came down to see the city and the tower, which the children of men builded. And the Lord said, Behold, the people is one, and they have all one language; and this they begin to do: and now nothing will be restrained from them, which they have imagined to do. Go to, let us go down, and there confound their language, that they may not understand one another’s speech.Genesis 11:5-7

The Lord God, the Father, says, “let us go down…”

vv. 8–9

The Lord Scattered Them

Therefore Is the Name of It Called Babel

So the Lord scattered them abroad from thence upon the face of all the earth: and they left off to build the city. Therefore is the name of it called Babel; because the Lord did there confound the language of all the earth: and from thence did the Lord scatter them abroad upon the face of all the earth.Genesis 11:8-9

And they scattered the people and confused their language. We are meant to be in nations. Nations by clan. By blood. Not one world government, full of vanity. Not even by a civic nationalism of paper and statutes that erases who we are, making a name for ourselves, lest we be scattered abroad upon the face of the whole earth.

Genesis 10:8–10 • The Founder of Babel

Nimrod

A Mighty Hunter Before the Lord

And Cush begat Nimrod: he began to be a mighty one in the earth. He was a mighty hunter before the Lord: wherefore it is said, Even as Nimrod the mighty hunter before the Lord. And the beginning of his kingdom was Babel, and Erech, and Accad, and Calneh, in the land of Shinar.Genesis 10:8-10

Nimrod gets a bad rap. Having founded Babel and being a mighty man, a hunter before the Lord, it is assumed he’s the impetus for the construction of the tower. Most of the assumption is based on his proximity, greatness, being a gibbor or mighty man, and his lineage forming the great cities listed in Genesis 10: Babel, Erech, Accad, and Calneh, in the land of Shinar. Some translate his name as “deserter,” others “he rebelled,” and take this to build an entire story around him, the tower, and great wickedness against God. Yet scripture doesn’t give a negative accounting of him.

vv. 10–11

The Generations of Shem

Shem Begat Arphaxad Two Years After the Flood

These are the generations of Shem: Shem was an hundred years old, and begat Arphaxad two years after the flood: And Shem lived after he begat Arphaxad five hundred years, and begat sons and daughters.Genesis 11:10-11

And then we are given the generations of Shem. The genealogy of man after the flood is again given to establish where and when we are for the next phase of the story being told: the origins and chronology of the people whom God dealt with. Adam to Noah. Noah to Abram.

Many overlook this as simply a history or chronological device, and try to extract much deeper meaning from it than is there, often at the expense of believing it literally, especially the timing of years.

vv. 24–26

Nahor and Terah

The Life Spans of Man Shrinking

And Nahor lived nine and twenty years, and begat Terah: And Nahor lived after he begat Terah an hundred and nineteen years, and begat sons and daughters. And Terah lived seventy years, and begat Abram, Nahor, and Haran.Genesis 11:24-26

Note that the life spans of man are shrinking as we go from Shem to Abram, approaching the 120 year average God decided back in Genesis 6. Abraham would be 175 when he died. A shadow of the 900 years most of the earlier patriarchs lived to.

vv. 27–32

The Generations of Terah

Terah, Father of Abram

Now these are the generations of Terah: Terah begat Abram, Nahor, and Haran; and Haran begat Lot. And Haran died before his father Terah in the land of his nativity, in Ur of the Chaldees. And Abram and Nahor took them wives: the name of Abram’s wife was Sarai; and the name of Nahor’s wife, Milcah, the daughter of Haran, the father of Milcah, and the father of Iscah. But Sarai was barren; she had no child. And Terah took Abram his son, and Lot the son of Haran his son’s son, and Sarai his daughter in law, his son Abram’s wife; and they went forth with them from Ur of the Chaldees, to go into the land of Canaan; and they came unto Haran, and dwelt there. And the days of Terah were two hundred and five years: and Terah died in Haran.Genesis 11:27-32

The generations of Shem lead to Terah, father of Abraham and grandfather of Lot.

Application • Nations

God Is No Respecter of Persons

The Same Offer of Salvation to All

Then Peter opened his mouth, and said, Of a truth I perceive that God is no respecter of persons: But in every nation he that feareth him, and worketh righteousness, is accepted with him.Acts 10:34-35

God is not a respecter of persons, the Bible says repeatedly. He does not care if you are great or small, rich or poor, man or woman, Greek or Jew, where you’re from or who your parents are. He gives us all the same offer of salvation and adoption in Christ. As individuals, we have free will.

Application • Nations

God Does Judge Nations

Judging Ungodly Nations, Including Our Own

God does judge nations. He does favor some over others at times, and destroys many who go too far astray. He curses the land stained by blood of violence, sodomy, fornication. Of people, collectively, to the fourth generation.

We are to know the judgment of God and emulate it. Not being a respecter of persons, giving the Gospel to whomever we meet, but judging ungodly nations for what they are. Including our own. Every nation carries that burden, where the godly among them must turn towards God so that He may heal their land.

Application • 2 Chronicles 7:14

This Is Not “Doing Nothing”

Discipline, Motivation and Perseverance

Have faith. Obey the commandments. As much as possible, live peaceably among men. Be a godly husband, raise godly children and share the gospel, winning souls. Give comfort to those mourning, be a good judge for the fatherless and true widows.

This is not “doing nothing.” It is hard work, or else it wouldn’t be so difficult to get people to do it. Like most effective things, it requires discipline, motivation and perseverance. It’s easier to express impotent outrage online.

Application • Ephesians 6:12

We Wrestle Not Against Flesh and Blood

Fearful for Nothing Except God

As a people, if Christians turn towards God and obeying Him, fearful for nothing except God, He will hear us and heal our land, and bring blessings to all our nation, believers or not.

Stop Doing Nothing and Believe

Just being loving and kind, while maintaining the separation and sanctification God asks of us, is a dissident, revolutionary act. When we live blamelessly, it heaps coals upon their heads. But that all requires our faith. So stop doing nothing and believe.
