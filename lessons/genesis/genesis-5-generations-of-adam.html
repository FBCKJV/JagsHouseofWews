vv. 1–2

The Book of the Generations

In the Likeness of God

This is the book of the generations of Adam. In the day that God created man, in the likeness of God made he him; Male and female created he them; and blessed them, and called their name Adam, in the day when they were created.Genesis 5:1-2

Moses opens this chapter the same way he opens a genealogy anywhere in Scripture, with a “book,” a formal, deliberate record. This is not legend passed down loosely around a fire. It is a document, a toledoth, meant to be taken as literal history, the same way we take the record of names, dates, and reigns anywhere else in the Old Testament.

vv. 3–5

Adam: The First Entry

In His Own Likeness, After His Image

And Adam lived an hundred and thirty years, and begat a son in his own likeness, after his image; and called his name Seth: And the days of Adam after he had begotten Seth were eight hundred years: and he begat sons and daughters: And all the days that Adam lived were nine hundred and thirty years: and he died.Genesis 5:3-5

Verse 1 said Adam was made in God’s likeness. Verse 3 says Seth was born in Adam’s likeness: fallen, mortal, sinful. That is the whole doctrine of the fall in one verse: what is passed down is not innocence but Adam’s own broken image. And then the refrain begins: and he died. God’s word to Adam in the garden was not a bluff.

vv. 6–8

Seth

Nine Hundred Twelve Years, and He Died

And Seth lived an hundred and five years, and begat Enos: And Seth lived after he begat Enos eight hundred and seven years, and begat sons and daughters: And all the days of Seth were nine hundred and twelve years: and he died.Genesis 5:6-8
vv. 9–11

Enos

Then Began Men to Call Upon the Lord

And Enos lived ninety years, and begat Cainan: And Enos lived after he begat Cainan eight hundred and fifteen years, and begat sons and daughters: And all the days of Enos were nine hundred and five years: and he died.Genesis 5:9-11

Enos’s own birth was the moment marks. With the third generation from Adam, public, corporate calling on the name of the Lord begins, a confession made with the mouth, standing alongside the blood faith of Abel.

vv. 12–14

Cainan

Nine Hundred Ten Years, and He Died

And Cainan lived seventy years, and begat Mahalaleel: And Cainan lived after he begat Mahalaleel eight hundred and forty years, and begat sons and daughters: And all the days of Cainan were nine hundred and ten years: and he died.Genesis 5:12-14
vv. 15–17

Mahalaleel

Eight Hundred Ninety-Five Years, and He Died

And Mahalaleel lived sixty and five years, and begat Jared: And Mahalaleel lived after he begat Jared eight hundred and thirty years, and begat sons and daughters: And all the days of Mahalaleel were eight hundred ninety and five years: and he died.Genesis 5:15-17
vv. 18–20

Jared

Nine Hundred Sixty-Two Years, and He Died

And Jared lived an hundred sixty and two years, and he begat Enoch: And Jared lived after he begat Enoch eight hundred years, and begat sons and daughters: And all the days of Jared were nine hundred sixty and two years: and he died.Genesis 5:18-20

Jared lived longer than all but Methuselah, and he is the father of the one man in this chapter for whom the refrain never comes. Watch what happens in the very next entry.

vv. 21–24

Enoch, the Seventh from Adam

And Enoch Walked With God: And He Was Not; For God Took Him

And Enoch lived sixty and five years, and begat Methuselah: And Enoch walked with God after he begat Methuselah three hundred years, and begat sons and daughters: And all the days of Enoch were three hundred sixty and five years: And Enoch walked with God: and he was not; for God took him.Genesis 5:21-24

Every other name in this chapter closes the same way: and he died. Enoch’s does not. He is the seventh from Adam, not to be confused with Cain’s son Enoch, third from Adam in the other line (), and Scripture is careful to tell us this was no ordinary death. calls it a translation, by faith, and testifies that before it happened, he pleased God.

Cross references • Genesis 5:24

Hebrews 11 and Jude: Enoch’s Testimony

Hebrews 11:5

By faith Enoch was translated that he should not see death; and was not found, because God had translated him: for before his translation he had this testimony, that he pleased God.Hebrews 11:5

Two things are named here: the mechanism (faith) and the reward (translation, so that he never faced death at all). Before it happened, his testimony was already recorded: he pleased God.

Jude 14-15

And Enoch also, the seventh from Adam, prophesied of these, saying, Behold, the Lord cometh with ten thousands of his saints, To execute judgment upon all, and to convince all that are ungodly among them of all their ungodly deeds which they have ungodly committed, and of all their hard speeches which ungodly sinners have spoken against him.Jude 14-15

Jude, writing near the end of the New Testament, quotes a sermon preached before the flood. Enoch’s prophecy of the Lord’s coming in judgment is the same hope every believer since has held: Christ’s return with His saints.

Cross references • Genesis 5:24

Hebrews 11 and Jude: Enoch’s Testimony (continued)

John 8:51

Verily, verily, I say unto you, If a man keep my saying, he shall never see death.John 8:51

What happened to Enoch bodily is the promise held spiritually for every believer in Christ: those who keep His word will never see death in the sense that matters, separation from God forever.

Enoch is not a strange exception tucked into a genealogy. He is proof, planted in the earliest pages of Scripture, that walking with God by faith is a life death cannot ultimately touch.

vv. 25–27

Methuselah

The Oldest Man Who Ever Lived

And Methuselah lived an hundred eighty and seven years, and begat Lamech: And Methuselah lived after he begat Lamech seven hundred eighty and two years, and begat sons and daughters: And all the days of Methuselah were nine hundred sixty and nine years: and he died.Genesis 5:25-27

Nine hundred sixty-nine years: no man in Scripture, before or since, lived longer. Even the longest life this side of Eden still ends the same way every other life in this chapter ends. The refrain catches up to everyone but one.

vv. 28–31

Lamech

This Same Shall Comfort Us

And Lamech lived an hundred eighty and two years, and begat a son: And he called his name Noah, saying, This same shall comfort us concerning our work and toil of our hands, because of the ground which the Lord hath cursed. And Lamech lived after he begat Noah five hundred ninety and five years, and begat sons and daughters: And all the days of Lamech were seven hundred seventy and seven years: and he died.Genesis 5:28-31

This is a different Lamech than Cain’s descendant of who boasted of vengeance seventy and sevenfold. This Lamech, of the line of Seth, names his son in hope, looking for relief from the curse on the ground pronounced back in . He could not have known how, but Noah’s name proved prophetic the day the flood lifted and a new world began.

v. 32

Noah

Shem, Ham, and Japheth

And Noah was five hundred years old: and Noah begat Shem, Ham, and Japheth.Genesis 5:32

Ten generations, one continuous line, from the dust of Eden to the three sons who would repopulate the whole earth after the flood. The chapter that opened “in the likeness of God” closes by naming the men through whom every nation on earth descends.

The Gospel, Hidden in the Meaning of the Names

Man is appointed mortal sorrow, but the Blessed God shall come down teaching that His death shall bring the despairing rest.Read straight down the ten names

Read straight down the family tree, in Hebrew, the ten names carrying us from Adam to Noah string together into that sentence: the gospel itself, laid into a genealogy centuries before Christ came down, taught among us, died, and gave rest to the despairing. Whether every root is pressed with equal precision or not, the pattern is hard to miss: the God who inspired this list wastes nothing, not even the names.

Eight Times Repeated

And He Died: The Refrain of the Chapter

The Wages of Sin, Written in a Genealogy

8times: and he died

They Lived, They Had Children, and They Died

Long-lived though they were, every man in this chapter but one lived, had sons and daughters, and died. Sons of God calling on the name of the Lord and sons of men who did not: all of them sinned, and all of them, in due time, died. This is not poetry or picture; it is the plain, literal accounting of years from creation to Noah. Taking the figurative as figurative and the literal as literal, these ages are given to us as literal history, the same rule of interpretation that lets us call Christ’s parables parables without turning His genealogies into parables too.

Cross references • Genesis 5, “and he died”

Romans 6:23: The Wages of Sin Is Death

Romans 6:23

For the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.Romans 6:23

Genesis 5 is Romans 6:23 written out in eight literal case studies. Every long life in the chapter still ends exactly where Paul says sin’s wages lead.

Romans 5:12

Wherefore, as by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned:Romans 5:12

Adam’s sin did not stay Adam’s problem. It passed to every name on this genealogy, sons of God and sons of men alike, whether they called upon the name of the Lord or rejected His salvation.

The refrain “and he died” is not filler between the numbers. It is the whole doctrine of sin and death, stated eight times over, so that the ninth time, when it doesn’t come, we notice.

Adam to Abraham

Following the given years of Genesis 5 forward, and then adding the generations of from Shem down to Terah, father of Abraham, we arrive at roughly 2,008 years from Adam to Abraham. This isn’t a modern estimate imposed on the text; it’s simple addition of the years the text itself gives us.

Matthew 1:17

Abraham to Christ

Fourteen Generations, Three Times Over

So all the generations from Abraham to David are fourteen generations; and from David until the carrying away into Babylon are fourteen generations; and from the carrying away into Babylon unto Christ are fourteen generations.Matthew 1:17

Matthew closes the loop Genesis 5 opens. Between the literal years of Genesis and the counted generations of Matthew 1:17, Scripture hands us the recorded span from creation to Christ, inspired and preserved. Add the roughly two thousand years since, and the commonly accepted span places the earth at just slightly more than six thousand years old, give or take a century or two.

A Pattern of Salvation in the Early Patriarchs

Knowledge, Blood, Confession, and a Walk With God

Reading these early chapters together, a pattern for salvation emerges in the lives of these men:

  1. AdamKnows God his Creator, and knows his own sin.
  2. AbelBrings the blood of atonement, offered in faith for redemption.
  3. Seth and EnosShow that with the mouth, confession is made: calling upon the name of the Lord.
  4. EnochGives the testimony that he walked with God, that he lived separated, pleasing God.

Each of us passes through these same steps when we come to Christ and live for Him, an illustration God gave in His word, through the lives of these men, long before the cross.

A Prophet Who Preached, and a Promise to Every Believer

Enoch was more than a name in a list. He was a prophet. preserves his preaching, a witness in the Spirit to Christ’s coming victory in the day of the Lord, given ages before the flood, let alone the cross. And what happened to him is the promise held out to every believer since: , “if a man keep my saying, he shall never see death.”

What we can say plainly: Enoch walked with God, prophesied of Christ, and was pleasing to God, and he did not see death. Beyond that, Genesis leaves the mechanics a mystery, and we do well to leave them there too, drawing our application from what is written rather than from what isn’t.

A caution

Not Every Old Book Is Scripture

The “Book of Enoch” Is Not Scripture

There are rabbit trails worth naming and leaving alone. Ancient Jewish fables and the pseudepigraphal “Book of Enoch” claim to fill in what Genesis leaves silent, but the book contradicts itself and contains plain fabrications. It is not Scripture, whatever curiosity it satisfies. What we can know, and should hold to, is simply this: Enoch walked with God, prophesied of Christ, and pleased God, and by faith, we’re meant to draw the same inspiration to live pleasing to God ourselves, in the Spirit, however our own days end.
