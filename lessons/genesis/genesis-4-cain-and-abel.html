Genesis Chapter 4

Cain & Abel  •  The First Murder  •  Calling on the LORD

vv. 1–2Two Sons Born

Cain and Abel

1And Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived, and bare Cain, and said, I have gotten a man from the LORD.

2And she again bare his brother Abel. And Abel was a keeper of sheep, but Cain was a tiller of the ground.

Eve's declaration shows her faith that the promised seed of Genesis 3:15 would come from the LORD. Two brothers — two vocations — and two very different hearts before God.
vv. 3–5Two Offerings — One Accepted, One Rejected

Abel's Offering Accepted — Cain's Rejected

3And in process of time it came to pass, that Cain brought of the fruit of the ground an offering unto the LORD.

4And Abel, he also brought of the firstlings of his flock and of the fat thereof. And the LORD had respect unto Abel and to his offering:

5But unto Cain and to his offering he had not respect. And Cain was very wroth, and his countenance fell.

Abel brought blood — the firstlings of the flock. Cain brought the fruit of his own labour from the cursed ground. God had already shown in Genesis 3:21 that a blood covering was required — Heb. 9:22. The difference was not the quality of the gift but the nature of it — and the faith behind it.
vv. 6–7God Warns Cain

"Sin Lieth at the Door" — Grace Before Judgment

6And the LORD said unto Cain, Why art thou wroth? and why is thy countenance fallen?

7If thou doest well, shalt thou not be accepted? and if thou doest not well, sin lieth at the door. And unto thee shall be his desire, and thou shalt rule over him.

God gives Cain a direct warning before he acts. Sin is pictured as a crouching beast at the threshold — desiring to master him, but he can still rule over it. Cain chose not to. God never sends judgment without first sending warning. 1 Pet. 5:8
The First Murder
v. 8Cain Rises Against Abel

The World's First Murder — Brother Against Brother

8And Cain talked with Abel his brother: and it came to pass, when they were in the field, that Cain rose up against Abel his brother, and slew him.

The first death in human history is not natural — it is murder. And the victim is a righteous man killed by a religious man whose worship was rejected. Matt. 23:35 — Jesus names Abel as the first of a long line of righteous martyrs.
vv. 9–12God's Judgment — The Blood Cries Out

"Am I My Brother's Keeper?" — The Voice of Blood

9And the LORD said unto Cain, Where is Abel thy brother? And he said, I know not: Am I my brother's keeper?

10And he said, What hast thou done? the voice of thy brother's blood crieth unto me from the ground.

11And now art thou cursed from the earth, which hath opened her mouth to receive thy brother's blood from thy hand;

12When thou tillest the ground, it shall not henceforth yield unto thee her strength; a fugitive and a vagabond shalt thou be in the earth.

Cain lies to God as Adam hid from God. But the blood itself is a witness — Heb. 12:24 — the blood of Jesus speaketh better things than that of Abel. Abel's blood cried for vengeance; Christ's blood speaks peace.
vv. 13–16Cain's Punishment — Departure from God's Presence

Cain Went Out from the Presence of the LORD

13And Cain said unto the LORD, My punishment is greater than I can bear.

14Behold, thou hast driven me out this day from the face of the earth; and from thy face shall I be hid; and I shall be a fugitive and a vagabond in the earth; and it shall come to pass, that every one that findeth me shall slay me.

15And the LORD said unto him, Therefore whosoever slayeth Cain, vengeance shall be taken on him sevenfold. And the LORD set a mark upon Cain, lest any finding him should kill him.

16And Cain went out from the presence of the LORD, and dwelt in the land of Nod, on the east of Eden.

Cain's complaint is about his suffering — not about what he has done. He does not repent; he grieves his consequences. Note that even in judgment God shows restraint — setting a mark of protection on Cain. Yet the defining sentence of v16 is the saddest in the chapter: he went out from the presence of the LORD.
The Line of Cain — Civilization Without God
vv. 17–22Cain's Descendants — City, Culture, Craft

Progress Without God — Building, Music, Metalwork

17And Cain knew his wife; and she conceived, and bare Enoch: and he builded a city, and called the name of the city, after the name of his son, Enoch.

18And unto Enoch was born Irad: and Irad begat Mehujael: and Mehujael begat Methusael: and Methusael begat Lamech.

19And Lamech took unto him two wives: the name of the one was Adah, and the name of the other Zillah.

20And Adah bare Jabal: he was the father of such as dwell in tents, and of such as have cattle.

21And his brother's name was Jubal: he was the father of all such as handle the harp and organ.

22And Zillah, she also bare Tubalcain, an instructer of every cutting instrument of brass and iron: and the sister of Tubalcain was Naamah.

The line of Cain builds civilization — cities, livestock, music, metalwork. None of it is evil in itself. But all of it is constructed apart from God. Man can fill the earth with art and industry and still be walking away from the presence of the LORD.
vv. 23–24Lamech's Boast — Violence Escalates

Lamech — The Way of Cain Goes Further

23And Lamech said unto his wives, Adah and Zillah, Hear my voice; ye wives of Lamech, hearken unto my speech: for I have slain a man to my wounding, and a young man to my hurt.

24If Cain shall be avenged sevenfold, truly Lamech seventy and sevenfold.

Lamech takes God's promise of protection over Cain and twists it into a boast of personal vengeance. He has murdered and glories in it. This is sin unchecked — from Cain's silent deed in a field to Lamech's proud song. Rom. 1:32
The Line of Seth — Hope Restored
vv. 25–26Seth Appointed — Men Call on the LORD

"Then Began Men to Call upon the Name of the LORD"

25And Adam knew his wife again; and she bare a son, and called his name Seth: For God, said she, hath appointed me another seed instead of Abel, whom Cain slew.

26And to Seth was born a son; and he called his name Enos: then began men to call upon the name of the LORD.

Against the backdrop of Cain's city and Lamech's boast, God quietly raises up another seed. Seth — appointed in Abel's place — begins the godly line through which the promised Redeemer will come. And the mark of that line is this: they call upon the name of the LORD.

Cross References  •  Genesis 4:10

Hebrews 11:4 — He Being Dead Yet Speaketh

Hebrews 11:4

"By faith Abel offered unto God a more excellent sacrifice than Cain, by which he obtained witness that he was righteous, God testifying of his gifts: and by it he being dead yet speaketh."

→ The difference between the two offerings was faith. Abel's sacrifice was accepted not because it was larger or costlier, but because it was offered in faith — faith that required blood, pointing to the coming atonement. Though murdered, Abel still speaks through his faith recorded in Scripture.

Hebrews 12:24

"And to Jesus the mediator of the new covenant, and to the blood of sprinkling, that speaketh better things than that of Abel."

→ Abel's blood cried out from the ground for justice and vengeance. The blood of Jesus speaks better things — it cries for mercy, for peace, for forgiveness. Both bloods speak; only one silences the accusation of the Law.

Romans 3:25

"Whom God hath set forth to be a propitiation through faith in his blood, to declare his righteousness for the remission of sins that are past, through the forbearance of God;"

→ Every Old Testament blood sacrifice pointed forward to the propitiation Christ would make. Abel's faith looked forward to the same cross we look back on.

Abel did not earn God's favour by working harder than Cain. He came God's way — by faith, with blood. The entire book of Hebrews is built on this pattern: the old sacrifices were shadows; Christ is the substance. Abel understood by faith what the Law would later codify and the cross would finally fulfil.

Cross References  •  Genesis 4:16

1 John 3:12 — Not as Cain

1 John 3:11–12

"For this is the message that ye heard from the beginning, that we should love one another. Not as Cain, who was of that wicked one, and slew his brother. And wherefore slew he him? Because his own works were evil, and his brother's righteous."

→ John names the root of Cain's murder: his works were evil and his brother's were righteous. Righteousness in another does not produce love in a corrupt heart — it produces hatred. This is the spirit that has always killed the prophets.

1 John 3:15

"Whosoever hateth his brother is a murderer: and ye know that no murderer hath eternal life abiding in him."

→ John draws the line from Cain to the heart. Murder did not begin in the field — it began with the hatred before it.

John 8:44

"Ye are of your father the devil, and the lusts of your father ye will do. He was a murderer from the beginning, and abode not in the truth, because there is no truth in him."

→ The spirit behind Cain's act is identified: the devil was a murderer from the beginning. Cain, John says in 1 John 3:12, "was of that wicked one."

Cain went out from the presence of the LORD — the saddest verse of the chapter. His legacy is not only the first murder but a pattern John warns the church against: religious performance without genuine faith, and hatred of the righteous because their righteousness is a rebuke to our own evil works.

Cross References  •  Genesis 4:24

Jude 11 — The Way of Cain

Jude 10–11

"But these speak evil of those things which they know not: but what they know naturally, as brute beasts, in those things they corrupt themselves. Woe unto them! for they have gone in the way of Cain, and ran greedily after the error of Balaam for reward, and perished in the gainsaying of Core."

→ Jude uses "the way of Cain" as a defined category of apostasy — approaching God on your own terms, rejecting the blood, hating the righteous, and ultimately being driven from God's presence. Lamech's boast in v24 is the fruit of what Cain's way produces across generations.

Compare: Matthew 18:21–22

"Then came Peter to him, and said, Lord, how oft shall my brother sin against me, and I forgive him? till seven times? Jesus saith unto him, I say not unto thee, Until seven times: but, Until seventy times seven."

→ Jesus directly inverts Lamech's boast. Lamech: avenge seventy and sevenfold. Christ: forgive seventy times seven. The way of Cain multiplies vengeance; the way of Christ multiplies forgiveness.

The way of Cain is the pattern of all religion that approaches God on its own terms, scorns the blood, hates the righteousness of others, and is driven further from God's presence. Jude warns the church that this spirit appears among false teachers in every age.

Cross References  •  Genesis 4:26

Romans 10:13 — Whosoever Shall Call upon the Name of the Lord

Romans 10:13

"For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved."

→ What began at Seth's generation — calling upon the name of the LORD — is still the only way of salvation. The promise in Genesis 4:26 finds its fullest declaration here in Paul's letter to Rome.

Romans 10:9–10

"That if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved. For with the heart man believeth unto righteousness; and with the mouth confession is made unto salvation."

→ Calling upon the name of the Lord flows from a heart that believes and a mouth that confesses. The same faith Abel had, Seth's generation expressed publicly in calling on the LORD.

Joel 2:32

"And it shall come to pass, that whosoever shall call on the name of the LORD shall be delivered: for in mount Zion and in Jerusalem shall be deliverance, as the LORD hath said, and in the remnant whom the LORD shall call."

→ This promise spans both Testaments — Joel, Romans 10:13, and Acts 2:21 all repeat it. What Seth's generation began, the Church continues.

Acts 2:21

"And it shall come to pass, that whosoever shall call on the name of the Lord shall be saved."

→ Peter quotes Joel on the Day of Pentecost — the same promise first glimpsed in Genesis 4:26 is now proclaimed to all nations. The door has always been the same: call upon the LORD.

Genesis 4 ends not with Lamech's boast but with a quiet, eternal answer: men began to call upon the name of the LORD. Against every city Cain built and every song Lamech sang of blood, God was raising up a people who would simply call on Him. That calling — by faith, upon His name — is still the only way of salvation today.
