So these three men ceased to answer Job, because he was righteous in his own eyes.Job 32:1

Repeating many of the charges leveled against Job by the three friends, while proclaiming his own wisdom, Elihu's understanding is shallow and, as the reader knows, untrue.

Job 32 · Elihu

His wrath was kindled

Then was kindled the wrath of Elihu the son of Barachel the Buzite, of the kindred of Ram: against Job was his wrath kindled, because he justified himself rather than God. Also against his three friends was his wrath kindled, because they had found no answer, and yet had condemned Job. Now Elihu had waited till Job had spoken, because they were elder than he. When Elihu saw that there was no answer in the mouth of these three men, then his wrath was kindled.Job 32:2-5

An angry young man has entered the conversation, Elihu. And the Bible tells us his wrath was kindled 4 times.

The young man is angry at Job, thinking the poor man suffering before him had justified himself, rather than God. He is angry at Job's friends, who accused Job but didn't answer him when he defended himself.

Job 32 · Elihu

I am young, and ye are very old

And Elihu the son of Barachel the Buzite answered and said, I am young, and ye are very old; wherefore I was afraid, and durst not shew you mine opinion.Job 32:6

Starting off with a false humility, claiming to respect his elders, that he held off from giving his opinion until they were done, but now he must share his opinions.

He was chomping at the bit to tell his elders off, who are very old (not just older). Elihu is rash, while counting himself reserved. He is rude, while claiming to be patient and respectful.

He is a picture of impertinent youth.

Job 32 · Elihu

God thrusteth him down

Lest ye should say, We have found out wisdom: God thrusteth him down, not man.Job 32:13

Four times we are told Elihu is angry, and three times he tells the men that he is going to give his opinion (, , ). An opinion no one asked for.

He wants Job to know, God thrust him down. He wants the friends (who didn't hesitate to accuse Job), to know they are weak and afraid to say it. To condemn Job utterly.

Job 32 · Elihu

Wine which hath no vent

For I am full of matter, the spirit within me constraineth me. Behold, my belly is as wine which hath no vent; it is ready to burst like new bottles. I will speak, that I may be refreshed: I will open my lips and answer. Let me not, I pray you, accept any man’s person, neither let me give flattering titles unto man. For I know not to give flattering titles; in so doing my maker would soon take me away.Job 32:18-22

He describes his need to unload his opinion as wine fermented in a sealed cask, the gases exploding the seams. He cannot wait to tell them what he thinks.

He rationalizes and spiritualizes his rashness, by saying he won't give flattering titles to them, won't show them respect they are due, because his maker, the Lord, wouldn't like it. Convenient!

The pride and arrogance of youth is what he is actually filled with. How often do we see this in chatrooms and even in churches?

Job 32 · Elihu

Young men, do not be as Elihu

Young men, do not be as Elihu. He waited on the elders, it's true, but not out of respect or seeking after wisdom, but to bide his time so he could give his opinion. He is a special boy.

Young men with no experience, babes in Christ, convinced they know more than their elders. Who can't wait to tell them their opinions, because the older people just don't get it, in their eyes.

Job 32 · Elihu

You aren't a special boy

As we will see, Elihu actually offers little to the conversation. It really is just his pride.

If you are like Elihu, if you jump in to give your opinion thinking your newfound wisdom and knowledge, repeating things you recently heard, thinking you are blowing minds, stop. Consider him, and this lesson. You aren't a special boy, and you have limited understanding. Have real humility, not the fake kind Elihu shows here.

Job 33 · Elihu

Hear my speeches

Wherefore, Job, I pray thee, hear my speeches, and hearken to all my words. Behold, now I have opened my mouth, my tongue hath spoken in my mouth. My words shall be of the uprightness of my heart: and my lips shall utter knowledge clearly. The spirit of God hath made me, and the breath of the Almighty hath given me life. If thou canst answer me, set thy words in order before me, stand up. Behold, I am according to thy wish in God’s stead: I also am formed out of the clay. Behold, my terror shall not make thee afraid, neither shall my hand be heavy upon thee.Job 33:1-7

Elihu, having spent all of the previous chapter telling Job that he's heard all of the conversation, and is going to speak his opinion on the matter, and how much he is looking forward to give it, then spends another 7 verses prattling on about how he is going to speak, and Job better listen up.

It is vain speech, the lips and his breath and his words and how Elihu is going to answer in God's stead, he's going to really tell Job what's up, erm actually.

Job 33 · Elihu

The least charitable account

Surely thou hast spoken in mine hearing, and I have heard the voice of thy words, saying, I am clean without transgression, I am innocent; neither is there iniquity in me.Job 33:8-9

He gives his own recap of Job's points. This is more than Job actually said. The context is Job facing so great a trouble in his life, that Job doesn't understand what he'd done to deserve so much pain. Not that he was without sin. Elihu is giving the least charitable account.

Elihu lectures the older Job for being so audacious as to ask why of God.

Job 33 · Elihu

As if this young man can comprehend

He's lost all his wealth, his 10 children, the respect of his wife, his friends, the men of the city, even the youth who live in the wilderness because of their baser and lewd families disrespect him, holding Job as a byword and mocking him...

...but Elihu has decided that Job is not just to ask of the Lord, as if this young man can begin to comprehend the suffering Job's been through.

Job 33 · Elihu

In a dream, in a vision of the night

For God speaketh once, yea twice, yet man perceiveth it not. In a dream, in a vision of the night, when deep sleep falleth upon men, in slumberings upon the bed; Then he openeth the ears of men, and sealeth their instruction, That he may withdraw man from his purpose, and hide pride from man. He keepeth back his soul from the pit, and his life from perishing by the sword.Job 33:14-18

Elihu begins to pontificate about how God actually speaks to men in dreams, and describes the outcome of old age as punishment to humble man.

God speaks to us by inspired words, and prophets. Clear teachings. Dreams may be involved, but Elihu's understanding is overly spiritual and limited, and his idea of old age being a humbling curse is a conceit based on his relative youth. How he sees his elders.

Job 33 · Elihu

Not the full story

Elihu's point about God speaking through dreams and visions or through pain and affliction to turn people from pride and the pit isn't entirely wrong theologically (Scripture elsewhere affirms God can use suffering redemptively , ; ). But applying it directly to Job's case assumes the suffering is for specific sin or pride, which the book's prologue shows isn't the full story.

Job's trials stem from a heavenly challenge, not personal wrongdoing. But Elihu's unable to hold enough grace and empathy to relate to Job in charity. In faith.

Job 33 · Elihu

The messenger of God?

If there be a messenger with him, an interpreter, one among a thousand, to shew unto man his uprightness: Then he is gracious unto him, and saith, Deliver him from going down to the pit: I have found a ransom.Job 33:23-24

Elihu sees himself as the messenger of God in v23. And that all this was God's way of giving Job Elihu's wisdom. Of bringing him the truth of God through Elihu, to spare Job from the pit, from death. To be enlightened.

It is an absurdly grandiose view of himself. Hold thy peace, and I shall teach thee wisdom. Quiet Boomer, let me tell you how it is!

Job 33 · Elihu

Hold thy peace

Mark well, O Job, hearken unto me: hold thy peace, and I will speak. If thou hast anything to say, answer me: speak, for I desire to justify thee. If not, hearken unto me: hold thy peace, and I shall teach thee wisdom.Job 33:31-33

I also want to point out that all the build up Elihu uses before he starts to speak directly, all his "I am going to speak, and words will come off my tongue and my lips will move and I will breathe, so listen up!" set up, and even the arrogant end to the chapter....

...where Elihu tells Job to hold his peace....unless he has something to say, shows the lack of conviction and confidence that betrays his own brashness.

Job 33 · Elihu

Unless you don't want to

Elihu is super confident, and you better listen up, unless you don't want to. He knows he is over his head, and these slips show an insight into his character that adds depth to the story and character in a purely literary sense.

Job is such an awesome study on people and how we can tend to be.

Job 33 · Elihu

Let no man despise thy youth

Let no man despise thy youth; but be thou an example of the believers, in word, in conversation, in charity, in spirit, in faith, in purity.1 Timothy 4:12

This isn't a command to elders to give any special honor to Timothy, to the young. It's a warning to Timothy to be an example by his behavior, that no man would see him and hold his limited years against him. To not be like Elihu.

Young (or any) believers gain influence through quiet godliness, love, and purity, not puffed-up words that lack empathy for profound grief.

Job 33 · Elihu

Humble presence

Job's story shows that suffering often defies neat explanations, and the wisest response is often humble presence rather than overconfident correction.

Everyone else's misfortune isn't an opportunity to show how smart or wise or spiritual you are. No one profits from that. It leads to men despising your youth, not establishing you as more wise than they are

Job 34 · Elihu

Regurgitating obvious points

For Job hath said, I am righteous: and God hath taken away my judgment. Should I lie against my right? my wound is incurable without transgression. What man is like Job, who drinketh up scorning like water? Which goeth in company with the workers of iniquity, and walketh with wicked men. For he hath said, It profiteth a man nothing that he should delight himself with God.Job 34:5-9

The young man, Elihu, continues on his monologue, accusing Job of being unrighteous, unwise and wicked.

There are some valid points made about God not being a respecter of persons, holding the kings and the common man to the same fate of death and judgment (). All the accusers of Job have said some right things. What makes Elihu's portion so obnoxious is that he is regurgitating obvious points, while proclaiming his own wisdom in knowing them. A lack of humility Eliphaz, Bildad and Zophar maintained.

Job 34 · Elihu

A parrot, not an intellectual

Again, for Christians, this is an example of what not to do. Even if you have knowledge, it is of the Lord. The more basic and obvious, the more humility you should have in passing it on.

Assuming that commonly held wisdom is already understood by those you are dealing with. Not pontificating and acting like you invented the doctrine. That your ability to recite dogmas makes you an intellectual, rather than a parrot.

Job 34 · Elihu

Tried unto the end

Job hath spoken without knowledge, and his words were without wisdom. My desire is that Job may be tried unto the end because of his answers for wicked men. For he addeth rebellion unto his sin, he clappeth his hands among us, and multiplieth his words against God.Job 34:35-37

Elihu haranguing Job, says that he hopes Job suffers even more than he has, that he not only deserves it, but more that Job is in rebellion against God for even questioning why this happened.

The misapplication of wisdom, the lack of maturity and grace, the presumption and pride, these traits make many young people look foolish in the eyes of their elders.

Job 35 · Elihu

My righteousness is more than God's?

Thinkest thou this to be right, that thou saidst, My righteousness is more than God’s?Job 35:2

Elihu continues to accuse Job, and again, builds a strawman to do it. Job never uttered the words "My righteousness is more than God's" anywhere in the book.

Job repeatedly defends his own personal integrity and blamelessness before God, while fully acknowledging God's supreme righteousness and power. In his statements, Job repeatedly recognizes no one can claim perfect righteousness with God. But Elihu puts words and intent into Job's mouth to attack him.

Job 35 · Elihu

A disinterested deity

If thou sinnest, what doest thou against him? or if thy transgressions be multiplied, what doest thou unto him? If thou be righteous, what givest thou him? or what receiveth he of thine hand? Thy wickedness may hurt a man as thou art; and thy righteousness may profit the son of man.Job 35:6-8

Elihu's take on what does it matter to God if you sin or are righteous, overly spiritualizes God into a disinterested deity. This theory of theology then allows Elihu to twist what Job said, and for the young foolish boy to claim Job's words are vanity. Ironically.

This is also a very common tactic online, especially with young theology bros. They learn the proof texts and apologetics for their presuppositions, and can only apply them to certain expected responses.

Job 35 · Elihu

So the special boy can pounce

They need the person they are "debating" to fall into the trap of defending the false representation of their own argument, so the special boy can pounce.

One reason this is so common is the prefab doctrines and canned arguments work against the unstable and unlearned. Good faith Christians take the bait, and so the argument is derailed into being about what the special boy needs it to be, so that his regurgitating is effective.

Job 35 · Elihu

The Titus 3:10 rule

A man that is an heretick after the first and second admonition reject;Titus 3:10

Which is why it is very important in these situations to know what the Bible says, and to keep the discussion focused on that. And to remember the Titus 3:10 rule;

The second they enter into putting words in your mouth, or reframing what you've said in a dishonest way, they are in bad faith and cannot be reasonable. "So you're saying...." only works if it is actually an honest assessment of what was being said. Otherwise, it is an insult.

Job 36 · Elihu

I have yet to speak on God's behalf

Suffer me a little, and I will shew thee that I have yet to speak on God’s behalf. I will fetch my knowledge from afar, and will ascribe righteousness to my Maker. For truly my words shall not be false: he that is perfect in knowledge is with thee.Job 36:2-4

Elihu's lecture to Job continues, and he lets Job know he is just getting warmed up. To sit back and relax, because someone with perfect knowledge, speaking on behalf of God with wisdom from afar, is present with them.

And for all this boasting, Elihu talks about turning to God with humility.

Wew.

Job 36 · Elihu

This hast thou chosen

Take heed, regard not iniquity: for this hast thou chosen rather than affliction.Job 36:21

He gives a long-winded explanation of the prosperity gospel, repeating what the three friends had already said, and condemns Job, saying he's worse than the hypocrite. That even the wicked understand the wrath they stored up for themselves.

But Job, Elihu says, has chosen this affliction.

In all ways, Elihu is an embarrassing display of youthful pride.

Job 36 · Elihu

The cattle also concerning the vapour

With clouds he covereth the light; and commandeth it not to shine by the cloud that cometh betwixt. The noise thereof sheweth concerning it, the cattle also concerning the vapour.Job 36:32-33

As he goes on, Elihu runs out of steam, pun intended. He starts a parable about God bringing rain, and the thunder and lightning that come with it. And by the end he's like, "um the cattle...also concerning um, the vapor..."

But it is ironic that his blustering about his wisdom is relying on just what he is seeing, and spiritualizing it. He is running out of things to say, and just starts describing the weather. It always makes me chuckle.

Job 36 · Elihu

A storm forming around them

What's interesting, as a story, is that the more Elihu speaks of the weather as allegory, as we really see in the next chapter, we come to realize as the reader he is actually seeing a storm forming around them. He unknowingly is announcing the voice of God that will be coming from a whirlwind.

Job 37 · Elihu

There is a whirlwind coming

Out of the south cometh the whirlwind: and cold out of the north. By the breath of God frost is given: and the breadth of the waters is straitened. Also by watering he wearieth the thick cloud: he scattereth his bright cloud: And it is turned round about by his counsels: that they may do whatsoever he commandeth them upon the face of the world in the earth.Job 37:9-12

And, at last, young Elihu is silenced. But not before his observations of the weather, roiling around them as a storm builds, fuel his final sayings.

I picture the 4 men standing over pitiful Job, as Elihu calls out the ways God is like the advancing storm. The dark and heavy clouds pushing out the bright clouds, the sky becoming filled with lightning, and the voice of thunder. A cold wind blowing from the north, as the whirlwind approaches from the South.

Job 37 · Elihu

Teach us what we shall say unto him

Teach us what we shall say unto him; for we cannot order our speech by reason of darkness. Shall it be told him that I speak? if a man speak, surely he shall be swallowed up.Job 37:19-20

Elihu is right about the power of God (). But as Elijah found, God is not nature. He is the still small voice speaking to us through His creation.

I believe as the storm intensifies, Elihu's fear begins to overtake his pride. He turns to Job, the man he spent so much wind slandering, and asks;

His fear of God is the beginning of wisdom. The Lord has arrived to answer Job.
