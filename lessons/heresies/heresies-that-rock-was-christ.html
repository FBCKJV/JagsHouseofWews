Matthew 16  ·  Church Fathers Testimony

The Rock is Jesus Christ,
Not Peter

What the earliest church actually taught

15He saith unto them, But whom say ye that I am?
16And Simon Peter answered and said, Thou art the Christ, the Son of the living God.
17And Jesus answered and said unto him, Blessed art thou, Simon Bar-jona: for flesh and blood hath not revealed it unto thee, but my Father which is in heaven.
18And I say also unto thee, That thou art Peter, and upon this rock I will build my church; and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it.
01 Origen of Alexandria c. 184–253 A.D.
If we also say, 'You are the Christ, the Son of the living God,' we become Peter, and it can be said to us: 'Upon this rock I will build my Church.'
02 St. John Chrysostom c. 349–407 A.D.
Upon this rock I will build my Church, that is, upon the faith of his confession.
03 St. Hilary of Poitiers c. 310–367 A.D.
This faith is the foundation of the Church, for the Church is built upon this rock.
04 St. Ambrose of Milan c. 339–397 A.D.
The rock is the faith upon which the Church is built.
05 St. Augustine of Hippo 354–430 A.D.
Christ did not say: you are the rock, but you are Peter. The rock was Christ. Peter was called rock because he confessed the Rock. The rock is the confession of faith Peter made: 'You are the Christ, the Son of the living God.'
06 Theodoret of Cyrus c. 393–458 A.D.
Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jonah because you confessed the Truth. I say to you that you are Peter, and upon this rock of the confession of faith I will build My Church.
07 Eusebius of Caesarea c. 260–339 A.D.
The Church is built upon this confession of faith.
08 Clement of Alexandria c. 150–215 A.D.
Christ is called the Rock… because He is the foundation of all teaching.
09 Cyprian of Carthage c. 200–258 A.D.
The rest of the apostles were what Peter was, equal in honor and power.
10 Jerome c. 347–420 A.D.
Upon this rock of confession the Church shall be built.
11 Basil of Caesarea c. 329–379 A.D.
Do not say that the Church was built upon Peter… it was built upon the faith.
12 Gregory of Nyssa c. 335–395 A.D.
His Christocentric theology excludes the idea that the foundation of the Church is a man. He refers to Christ as the Rock, not Peter.
13 Tertullian c. 155–240 A.D.
The Rock is Christ, not Peter.
