Teacher’s Lesson Plan

Fundamentalism vs. Vain Philosophy

Where the Scripture presents mystery, the fundamentalist stops.

Text
1 Timothy 3:16; 1 John 5:6–10, 20; 1 Corinthians 2:12–13; Colossians 2:1–18
Memory verse
Colossians 2:8
Length
About 55 minutes, 26 slides in seven sections
Materials
The interactive deck, KJV Bibles for each student, a printed copy of the timeline for anyone who wants to take it home
Beware lest any man spoil you through philosophy and vain deceit, after the tradition of men, after the rudiments of the world, and not after Christ.Colossians 2:8

The Big Idea

Biblical fundamentalism is superior to theology. Heresy answered with heresy is not how we find the truth. The history of the early church shows error answered with counter-error, both sides pulling on the same chains of Greek and Latin philosophy. The fundamentalist refuses to take either side. He stands on what God said, stops where God stopped, and accepts the mystery by faith.

Aims

By the end of the lesson, students should be able to:

  • Explain why answering one error with a “closer” error is still error.
  • Trace the thesis-and-antithesis back-and-forth from the Gnostics to Nicaea and beyond, and show that each step refused to stop where Scripture stops.
  • State the difference between “Sola Scriptura” (the Bible as final arbiter, with tradition at the table) and pure Bible (the Bible as the only authority).
  • Distinguish clear primary doctrine from the secondary matters of Romans 14.
  • Rest on the witness of God in 1 John 5 and the warning of Colossians 2 rather than on the vocabulary of men.

I. The Mystery of Godliness

Slides 2–3 · 5 min

Open by reading 1 Timothy 3:16 aloud. Point to the first three words: without controversy. God calls it a mystery and says there is no controversy about it. Men made the controversy.

Key statement

Where the text of scripture presents mystery, the fundamentalist stops, where the philosopher and the theologian continue, pressing on in their vain imagination.

Teaching points
  • Truth comes by the revelation of scripture, not by the reasoning of men.
  • Some things our finite brain cannot comprehend. We accept those things by faith.
  • Deuteronomy 29:29: the secret things belong unto the LORD; the revealed things belong to us. We live on the revealed things.
Ask the class

“Is it a weakness or a strength to say ‘I don’t know how, but God said it’?”

II. Heresy Answered with Heresy

Slides 4–5 · 6 min

Describe what students will see if they read the debates: the position of the Father, the godhood of the incarnate Jesus, the Trinity, the person of the Spirit and the unity of it all, argued endlessly in the fallible and weak terms of men.

Key statement

Biblical fundamentalism is superior to theology. Heresy answered with heresy is not how we find the truth.

Teaching points
  • The trap of the “closer” answer: saved, Bible-believing Christians see an answer to a clear heresy that is closer to the truth, and accept that doctrine as solid.
  • They lean on the understanding of traditions, the consensus of the crowd (Proverbs 3:5–6).
  • Closer to the truth is not the truth.
Transition

“Let’s see how this actually played out in history, because it did not happen once. It happened over and over.”

III. The Back-and-Forth Away from the Bible

Slides 6–8 · 10 min

Show the image. The cracked stone statue is the thesis, the action. The gold statue is the antithesis, the reaction. Both are pulling on the same chains. The light of Scripture falls between them and neither one is standing in it.

Open the nine steps of the timeline one at a time. For each, ask the same question: where should they have stopped?

  1. Gnostic Spark (c. 90–150): Greek dualism; Docetism. Abandoned John 1:14. Note 1 John 4:2–3: John was already fighting this in his own epistle.
  2. Apologists’ Logos Theory (c. 150–165): answering pagans and Jews with Greek Logos philosophy instead of the plain text; the Son a secondary, generated instrument. The Gnostics brought Greek philosophy in the back door; the apologists brought it in the front door. Birthed subordinationism and set the terms for everything that followed.
  3. Adoptionism (c. 190–260): protecting monotheism by human logic; Jesus a mere man adopted at baptism.
  4. Modalism / Sabellianism (c. 200–250): over-correcting the other way; Father, Son, and Holy Ghost as masks of one person.
  5. Tertullian (c. 210): Latin legal and physical terms, “one substance, three persons”; the Son a portion derived from the whole.
  6. Origen (c. 230–250): Neo-Platonic “Eternal Generation”; “begotten” forced out of its scriptural context.
  7. Arius (c. 318–325): the logical conclusion of subordinationism; “there was a time when the Son was not.”
  8. Nicaea (325): Arianism answered with more philosophy; homoousios and “eternally begotten” locked into a state-enforced creed.
  9. Post-Nicene Fragmentation (325–381): new vocabulary, new warring camps; human creeds generating more human error.
Key statement

Each step refused to stop where Scripture stops. Thesis produced antithesis; both remained inside Greek or Latin philosophical categories.

Teacher’s caution

Keep this section moving. The point is the pattern, not the personalities. Do not let it become a church-history lecture. We are showing the error of those who cite these men, not building on them.

IV. Where Scripture Stops, We Stop

Slides 9–10 · 5 min
Key statement

The fundamentalist does not feel obligated to supply the missing metaphysical machinery that finite minds invent to close the gap.

Teaching points
  • We don’t need to take sides in the back-and-forth debates of differing heretical schools of thought. We are not on the stone side or the gold side.
  • Never accept presuppositions and systematics that come prepackaged, regurgitating apologetics because others before us accepted them whole cloth.
  • Always search the scripture to see if it is so (Acts 17:11).
  • If you cannot draw a clear, direct understanding from plain language and context, it is ok, and in fact wise, to leave it to faith and admit your ignorance as fallible, finite man.

V. Pure Bible, Not “Sola Scriptura”

Slides 11–13 · 7 min

This is the section most likely to surprise students, because “Sola Scriptura” sounds like our position. Slow down here.

Key statement

That sliver of daylight given to the authority of tradition and man’s philosophy invites error just as much as the other attempts to answer bad doctrine.

Teaching points
  • The theologian uses the logic of men, the writings of church fathers and reformers and past generations, to argue the Bible should be the final arbiter. Tradition still sits at the table.
  • The fundamentalist takes scripture compared with scripture as the only means of finding truth, and the Bible as the only authority for faith and practice.
  • 2 Timothy 3:16–17: the man of God is throughly furnished by scripture.
  • Romans 14: on secondary matters men may disagree and be fully persuaded in their own minds, without judging another man’s servant. Primary doctrine is clear; God is not the author of confusion (1 Corinthians 14:33). Where we see through a glass darkly (1 Corinthians 13:12), there is room for brotherly disagreement.
Ask the class

“What’s the difference between a judge who has the last word and a judge who is the only one allowed to speak?”

VI. The Witness of God Is Greater

Slides 14–20 · 9 min

Now show what Scripture actually says. Have students read each verse from their own Bibles before you comment.

  • 1 John 5:7. The Bible does not demand that we figure out the metaphysical mechanics of how three can be one. It states the fact of their heavenly record and their absolute unity. No mode, essence, hypostatic union, or filioque. Just Father, Word, and Holy Ghost, and these three are one.
  • 1 John 5:6. God anchors the reality of Christ to historical facts: born of water, the incarnation; came by blood, the death and resurrection. The Spirit bears witness. There is no need to explain what we know is true. It is just true.
  • 1 John 5:9. The theologian, the seminarian, and the other “experts” seek our confidence in their arguments, theories, and jargon. But the witness of God is greater.
  • 1 John 5:10. The believer has the witness in himself. We have the Spirit of truth, who teaches us all things (John 14:26) and does not speak of Himself (John 16:13).
  • 1 Corinthians 2:12–13. Not the words which man’s wisdom teacheth, but which the Holy Ghost teacheth; comparing spiritual things with spiritual. What more do we need?
  • 1 John 5:20. The Son of God has given us an understanding, that we may know Him that is true. Don’t let men wise in their own conceits (Romans 12:16) tell you that you need to be “educated” as they are to know the truth.

VII. Beware Lest Any Man Spoil You

Slides 21–24 · 6 min
  • Colossians 2:1–4. Beguiling men with enticing words seek to lead us from the truth. Paul’s warning applies to us as much as to Colosse and Laodicea. Our hearts are comforted with full assurance of understanding, even as we acknowledge the mystery of God, in whom are hid all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge.
  • Colossians 2:6–9. We have received Christ and are rooted in Him. We can never deny Him, but we can be spoiled by deceivers: the tradition of men, the rudiments of the world, the theology and disputations of denominations and schools of thought. The fulness of the Godhead is stated as a fact, in Christ. God declares it, then warns against the very methods later generations would use to elaborate it.
  • Matthew 15:9. Creeds and confessions that add to and take away from the things of Christ, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men.
  • Colossians 2:16–18. The tip of the iceberg: false teachers using their own imagination to invent how we should live, intruding into things they have not seen, condemning those who trust entirely in Christ and His word.

Closing

Slides 25–26 · 7 min

Read the closing slide, then say the memory verse together twice. End on the line: God Himself testifies with us the truth of His Son. Amen.

Discussion questions
  1. 1 Timothy 3:16 calls the mystery of godliness “without controversy.” Why did men turn it into centuries of controversy?
  2. Look at the cycle. At what point should each side have simply stopped and said, “Thus saith the Lord, and no further”?
  3. What is the difference between the Bible as the “final arbiter” and the Bible as the only authority? Why does the sliver of daylight matter?
  4. Name a doctrine you were taught that you have never checked against the plain text. How would you search the scripture “whether those things were so”?
  5. Where is the line between primary doctrine and the secondary matters of Romans 14? How do we keep brotherly love on the second without compromising the first?

Questions You May Be Asked

“Isn’t ‘Trinity’ an extra-biblical word too?”
Whatever word a man uses as shorthand, the content has to be exactly what 1 John 5:7 says and nothing more: the Father, the Word, and the Holy Ghost, and these three are one. The problem is not shorthand. The problem is the machinery men attach to it and then require others to confess.
“So was Nicaea wrong to say Jesus is God?”
Jesus is God because the Bible says so: “the Word was God” (John 1:1), “this is the true God” (1 John 5:20), “in him dwelleth all the fulness of the Godhead bodily” (Colossians 2:9). We don’t need a council to tell us. The error was answering Arius with philosophical vocabulary and an imperial creed instead of with the text, which put the authority in the council and the vocabulary rather than in the word of God.
“If everyone just reads the Bible for himself, won’t everyone believe something different?”
God is not the author of confusion (1 Corinthians 14:33). Primary doctrine is clear. The believer has the witness in himself (1 John 5:10) and the Spirit of truth to guide him (John 16:13). On secondary matters, Romans 14 already tells us what to do: be fully persuaded in your own mind and don’t judge another man’s servant. The creeds did not produce unity either. They produced Semi-Arians, Homoiousians, and Anomoeans.
“What does ‘begotten’ mean, then?”
Let scripture interpret scripture. Psalm 2:7 says, “Thou art my Son; this day have I begotten thee,” and Acts 13:33 tells us how God fulfilled it: “in that he hath raised up Jesus again.” Read the word in its own context before reaching for a philosopher’s definition.
“Aren’t you just rejecting education?”
No. We are rejecting the claim that a man needs the theologian’s education to know the truth. The Bereans were commended for searching the scriptures daily (Acts 17:11), not for consulting the scholars. Study hard. Just study the Book.
