Biblical balance over systems

Dealing with the doctrines of atonement, salvation, and election are approached with a strong emphasis on the plain, literal reading of Scripture, prioritizing biblical balance over systematic frameworks like Calvinism or Arminianism.

This view seeks to uphold God's sovereignty in providing redemption while affirming human responsibility in responding to it, without over-philosophizing the interplay.

Christ's propitiatory death

The atonement is described in the Bible as Christ's propitiatory death on the cross—His shed blood serving as the ultimate fulfillment of the Old Testament mercy seat (hilastērion)—which objectively reconciles the world and all creation to God.

This work satisfies divine justice and pays the cost for redemption, a propitiation for not our sins only, but the sins of the whole world, removing the things that prevented God's grace from being extended to humanity.

And he is the propitiation for our sins: and not for ours only, but also for the sins of the whole world.1 John 2:2
Key aspects

Objective and cosmic scope

The atonement accomplishes a real, foundational, cosmic in scope, total reconciliation (e.g., ; ), making peace possible between God and creation regardless of individual response.

It is effective in this sense "even if no one ever believed," as it deals with sin's imputed guilt, the fallen world, the tally of the wages of sin - universally and individually - by providing the necessary condition for salvation's offer to all (e.g., ; ).

Reconciled to himself

To wit, that God was in Christ, reconciling the world unto himself, not imputing their trespasses unto them; and hath committed unto us the word of reconciliation.2 Corinthians 5:19
And, having made peace through the blood of his cross, by him to reconcile all things unto himself; by him, I say, whether they be things in earth, or things in heaven.Colossians 1:20
Key aspects

Universal provision

Christ's sacrifice is sufficient for the sins of the whole world, enabling a genuine gospel invitation to every person, without limitation in extent or intent.

Who gave himself a ransom for all, to be testified in due time.1 Timothy 2:6
Key aspects

Substitutionary and propitiatory

While sharing elements with Penal Substitutionary Atonement (PSA), fundamentalism avoids strict Reformed theology formulations because the Bible emphasizes broad reconciliation over effectual application limited to a predestined group.

The cross is not merely potential but achieves an actual change in God's relational stance toward sinners.

This perspective integrates substitution (Christ dying in place of sinners) with broader biblical themes like victory over death, but centers on the blood's role in propitiation as the grounds for mercy.

Deliverance from sin

Salvation is understood as deliverance from sin's penalty, power, and presence, received by grace through faith alone in Christ's finished work.

Personal application

While the death provides reconciliation objectively (—"reconciled to God by the death of his Son"), full experiential salvation—eternal life, justification, and regeneration—comes through belief in Christ's resurrection and risen life ("saved by his life").

Faith is a free, personal response to the gospel, not irresistibly imposed or conditionally foreseen.

For if, when we were enemies, we were reconciled to God by the death of his Son, much more, being reconciled, we shall be saved by his life.Romans 5:10

Necessity of faith

The atonement makes salvation available to "whosoever will," but it is not automatic or universal; individuals must trust in the crucified and risen Savior (e.g., ; ).

We reject universalism while maintaining the gospel's general accessibility.

That if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved.Romans 10:9
For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.John 3:16

Holistic process

Salvation encompasses initial justification (forgiveness through the cross) and ongoing sanctification (transformation by His life, walking in the Spirit), leading to the ultimate glorification, which all believers are elect to at the resurrection.

The Bible stresses the simplicity of the gospel: Christ died for sins, rose again, and offers eternal life to all who believe, without requiring works or predestined selection.

Not selection for salvation

Election is never presented as an individual, personal selection by God for salvation.

We reject both Calvinist (unconditional predestination to eternal life) and Arminian (conditional election based on foreseen faith) models as two sides of the same deterministic coin.

Purpose-oriented

Election refers to God's sovereign choosing or ordaining for a specific role, service, benefit, calling, or future result (e.g., holiness, ministry, or conformity to Christ's image).

It is not synonymous with being saved in scripture; saved believers are elect unto certain things, but election itself pertains to vocation or purpose within God's plan (e.g., ; ).

You have to be saved to be elect. Not elect to be saved. It is conditional, by faith. Unconditional election is a lie.

Romans 8:29-30 and Ephesians 1:4

For whom he did foreknow, he also did predestinate to be conformed to the image of his Son, that he might be the firstborn among many brethren. Moreover whom he did predestinate, them he also called: and whom he called, them he also justified: and whom he justified, them he also glorified.Romans 8:29-30
According as he hath chosen us in him before the foundation of the world, that we should be holy and without blame before him in love:Ephesians 1:4

Corporate or categorical

Election is corporate or categorical - emphasis: Often applied to groups (e.g., Israel as a chosen nation, the church as a royal priesthood) or to believers as a class set apart for holy living and witness (; ).

But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people; that ye should shew forth the praises of him who hath called you out of darkness into his marvellous light:1 Peter 2:9
Who hath saved us, and called us with an holy calling, not according to our works, but according to his own purpose and grace, which was given us in Christ Jesus before the world began,2 Timothy 1:9
Romans 8:29

Foreknowledge

In passages like Romans 8:29 ("whom he did foreknow"), this is not God previewing who would believe (Arminian) or decreeing based on sovereign will alone (Calvinist).

It may mean a broader determination that those fitting the category of believers will receive blessings (e.g., glorification), or specifically in Romans 8, a prophetic past tense assuring NT believers of the same outcomes believed by OT saints, the promise of what will happen to believers (to be justified and then glorified).

Relational intimacy

"Foreknow" carries relational intimacy with God, especially the Old Testament saints had trusted, rather than the false idea of election by the foresight of “God knows who will believe!” determinism.

Arminianism and Calvinism both start from a false premise of what these terms mean, and all their theology falls apart when compared with scripture.

Proclaiming the gospel to everyone

✦ ✦ ✦

Overall, this Fundamentalist framework promotes assurance for believers through God's faithful purposes, while preserving the mystery of divine sovereignty and human free will.

It critiques overly rigid systems for potentially undermining the Bible's universal language (e.g., "world," "all") or the call to personal faith, aiming instead for a practical, evangelistic theology that encourages proclaiming the gospel to everyone.

This summary reflects a commitment to Scripture's plain sense, as discussed, without endorsing or critiquing external traditions like Eastern Orthodoxy or Reformed views.
