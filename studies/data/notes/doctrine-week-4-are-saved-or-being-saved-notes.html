The Greek term σῳζομένοις is a present passive participle (dative masculine plural) from the verb σῴζω (“to save”). It literally means “to those saved.” The present tense in Greek participles can indicate either an ongoing action or a state resulting from a completed action, and the passive voice emphasizes that the action is performed by an external agent—God—rather than by the subjects themselves.

The King James Bible renders σῳζομένοις as “are saved,” using the simple present tense to emphasize a completed action with an assured future state. This translation aligns with the doctrine of eternal security: salvation is a definitive, irreversible act accomplished by God, and “are saved” presents it as a settled, present reality for the believer.

Modern versions (NASB, ESV, NIV, etc.) typically translate it as “are being saved,” using the English present progressive. While this reflects one possible aspect of the Greek present tense and highlights ongoing effects, the progressive form can introduce ambiguity, suggesting a process that is incomplete or could potentially be lost.

In 1 Corinthians 1:18 the word of the cross is the power of God precisely to those who stand in the state of having been saved—God’s effective, completed work secures the believer. In 1 Corinthians 15:2 Paul writes, “by which also ye are saved, if ye keep in memory what I preached unto you.” The “if” clause is not a condition for retaining salvation but the evidence of genuine faith; true believers will hold fast because they are already saved (Philippians 1:6; John 10:28–29).

The present passive participle here carries its common New Testament sense of a current state resulting from a past, completed action. Scripture repeatedly presents salvation as a finished act (Romans 8:30; Ephesians 2:8–9) that guarantees eternal security for everyone truly in Christ. Therefore, in both 1 Corinthians 1:18 and 15:2 the rendering “are saved” more accurately conveys the assured, completed, and eternally secure state of the believer, while “are being saved,” though grammatically possible, risks undermining that certainty by implying an unfinished or losable process. The word σῳζομένοις is best understood as describing a permanent present condition of salvation fully accomplished and eternally kept by God.
