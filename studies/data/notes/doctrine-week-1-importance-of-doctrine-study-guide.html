Why Doctrine Matters

For Baptists, doctrine is the bedrock of faith, derived only from the KJV Bible (in English) as God’s preserved and inerrant Word. It distinguishes truth from error, throughly equips believers for godly living, and protects against heresy, ensuring the church remains faithful to Christ, giving unity to believers and fellowship between independent churches (1 Timothy 4:16; 2 Timothy 4:3)

Jesus’ Doctrine from the Father and the Religious Leaders’ Hatred

  1. Jesus Stressed the Doctrine Given by the Father
  • Jesus emphasized that His doctrine was not His own but came directly from God the Father, teaching with divine authority.
  • John 7:16: “Jesus answered them, and said, My doctrine is not mine, but his that sent me.”
  1. The people and the Religious Leaders Heard and Were Astonished by His Doctrine
  • The Jewish religious leaders, including Pharisees and scribes, heard Jesus’ doctrine and were amazed, yet questioned His authority, revealing their growing opposition.
  • Matthew 22:33: “And when the multitude heard this, they were astonished at his doctrine.” (Context: Pharisees present, reacting to Jesus’ teachings on the resurrection and commandments, Matthew 22:23-34.)
  1. The Religious Leaders Sought to Kill Jesus Because of His Doctrine
  • The religious leaders’ astonishment turned to hatred, as Jesus’ doctrine exposed their hypocrisy, leading them to plot His death.
  • John 7:19: “Did not Moses give you the law, and yet none of you keepeth the law? Why go ye about to kill me?” (Context: After Jesus taught His doctrine in John 7:16-18, the Jews, including religious leaders, sought to kill Him for His teachings, as seen in their reaction in John 7:1, 25.)

Differences Between Doctrine, Theology, Opinion, Tradition and Heresy

  • Doctrine: Clear Biblical teaching from the KJV. Doctrines are authoritative and essential for salvation, matters of faith and practice (2 Timothy 3:16). Example: Salvation by faith alone (Ephesians 2:8-9).
  • Theology: The study and systematic organization of biblical truths, often involving human interpretation. While useful, it is secondary to doctrine and not authoritative in KJVO IFB churches, which rely solely on Scripture (2 Timothy 2:15). Example: The Trinity (1 John 5:7)
  • Opinion: Personal beliefs not explicitly taught in Scripture, often non-essential (e.g., timing of ordinances). Opinions should not divide believers (Romans 14:1).
  • Tradition: To do things a certain way that may or may not be explicitly taught, but which have been done by our forefathers in the faith. Example: Standing for the reading of the word.
  • Heresy: False teaching contradicting core KJV doctrines, such as denying Christ’s deity or salvation by works. Heresy is condemned and leads to spiritual destruction (2 Peter 2:1; Galatians 1:8).
