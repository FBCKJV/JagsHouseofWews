Paul has been talking about Israel.

He has just said God has the right to show mercy to whom He wills and to harden whom He wills. In context that is not “some random humans elected to heaven, some elected to hell.” It is God showing mercy to the nations through the gospel and hardening unbelieving Israel—the same Israel that had the covenants, the law, the promises, and still stumbled over the Messiah:

Romans 9:30–33 (KJV)

«“What shall we say then? That the Gentiles, which followed not after righteousness, have attained to righteousness, even the righteousness which is of faith.

But Israel, which followed after the law of righteousness, hath not attained to the law of righteousness.

Wherefore? Because they sought it not by faith, but as it were by the works of the law. For they stumbled at that stumblingstone;

As it is written, Behold, I lay in Sion a stumblingstone and rock of offence: and whosoever believeth on him shall not be ashamed.”»

That is why the question in 9:14 is “Is there injustice with God?” It is the covenant people asking why God would treat them the way He treated Pharaoh: raise them up, harden them in their rebellion, and use that hardening to make His name known among the Gentiles.

Romans 9:14–18 (KJV)

«“What shall we say then? Is there unrighteousness with God? God forbid.

For he saith to Moses, I will have mercy on whom I will have mercy, and I will have compassion on whom I will have compassion.

So then it is not of him that willeth, nor of him that runneth, but of God that sheweth mercy.

For the scripture saith unto Pharaoh, Even for this same purpose have I raised thee up, that I might shew my power in thee, and that my name might be declared throughout all the earth.

Therefore hath he mercy on whom he will have mercy, and whom he will he hardeneth.”»

So the objector in 9:19 is not a reprobate crying, “I never had a chance.” It is a self-righteous Jew crying, “How can God find fault with us? We are the chosen nation. Who resists His will?”

Romans 9:19–21 (KJV)

«“Thou wilt say then unto me, Why doth he yet find fault? For who hath resisted his will?

Nay but, O man, who art thou that repliest against God? Shall the thing formed say to him that formed it, Why hast thou made me thus?

Hath not the potter power over the clay, of the same lump to make one vessel unto honour, and another unto dishonour?”»

In other words: “God promised us. He cannot set us aside and graft in those dirty Gentiles. That is not how the story is supposed to go.”

That is why Paul answers with the potter. He is not quoting a philosophy textbook on compatibilism.

He is echoing Jeremiah 18. In Jeremiah the clay is a nation, and the potter remolds it in response to whether that nation turns. The point is not “the pot has no say.” The point is “the Potter has the right to do something you did not expect with a vessel that refused to repent.”

Jeremiah 18:6–10 (KJV)

«“O house of Israel, cannot I do with you as this potter? saith the LORD. Behold, as the clay is in the potter's hand, so are ye in mine hand, O house of Israel.

At what instant I shall speak concerning a nation, and concerning a kingdom, to pluck up, and to pull down, and to destroy it;

If that nation, against whom I have pronounced, turn from their evil, I will repent of the evil that I thought to do unto them.

And at what instant I shall speak concerning a nation, and concerning a kingdom, to build and to plant it;

If it do evil in my sight, that it obey not my voice, then I will repent of the good, wherewith I said I would benefit them.”»

Keep reading and the Calvinist objector disappears.

• 9:30–32: Gentiles obtained righteousness by faith; Israel did not because they pursued it by works.

Romans 9:30–32 (KJV)

«“What shall we say then? That the Gentiles, which followed not after righteousness, have attained to righteousness, even the righteousness which is of faith.

But Israel, which followed after the law of righteousness, hath not attained to the law of righteousness.

Wherefore? Because they sought it not by faith, but as it were by the works of the law.”»

• 10:21: “All day long I have held out my hands to a disobedient and contrary people.” That is not a people God never wanted.

Romans 10:21 (KJV)

«“But to Israel he saith, All day long I have stretched forth my hands unto a disobedient and gainsaying people.”»

• 11:11: They stumbled so that salvation would come to the Gentiles—to make Israel jealous.

Romans 11:11 (KJV)

«“I say then, Have they stumbled that they should fall? God forbid: but rather through their fall salvation is come unto the Gentiles, for to provoke them to jealousy.”»

• 11:20–23: They were broken off because of unbelief. If they do not continue in unbelief, they can be grafted in again.

Romans 11:20–23 (KJV)

«“Well; because of unbelief they were broken off, and thou standest by faith. Be not highminded, but fear:

For if God spared not the natural branches, take heed lest he also spare not thee.

Behold therefore the goodness and severity of God: on them which fell, severity; but toward thee, goodness, if thou continue in his goodness: otherwise thou also shalt be cut off.

And they also, if they abide not still in unbelief, shall be graffed in: for God is able to graff them in again.”»

The same “hardened” people are not stuck in a decree of reprobation. Their condition is contingent on faith.

If the objector were a man damned by an eternal decree he could never resist, Paul’s answer in chapter 11 would be cruel nonsense. You do not tell a vessel prepared for destruction, “Stop persisting in unbelief and you will be grafted back in.”

So the Calvinist has to treat 9:19–23 as a self-contained philosophy of individual election and then quietly change the subject when Paul keeps talking about Israel, unbelief, jealousy, and restoration.

The objector Paul actually answers is the one standing in the story: the covenant member who thinks God’s mercy to the nations and His judicial hardening of unbelieving Israel is unfair.

That objection makes sense in the argument of Romans 9–11. The “why did God create me to be damned” objection does not. Paul is not defending God against the charge of authoring unbelief. He is defending God’s right to use a rebellious nation’s unbelief to save the world—and then invite that same nation back if they will believe.
