How God Reveals Himself to Man (Week 2)

General Revelation vs. Specific Revelation

  • Key Question: How does God make Himself known to us?
  • Have you ever looked at a sunset or read a Bible verse and felt God’s presence? Today, we’ll explore how God speaks through nature, His actions, and His Word.

God’s revelation is divided into two types: General (available to all) and Specific (targeted through Scripture and Jesus).

Part 1: Understanding General and Specific Revelation

  1. General Revelation:
  • Definition: God revealing Himself through creation and the human conscience, God’s Law written on men’s hearts.
  • Accessible to all people (Romans 1:18-20).
  1. For the wrath of God is revealed from heaven against all ungodliness and unrighteousness of men, who hold the truth in unrighteousness; Because that which may be known of God is manifest in them; for God hath shewed it unto them. For the invisible things of him from the creation of the world are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made, even his eternal power and Godhead; so that they are without excuse:
  • Psalm 19:1-4: The heavens declare the glory of God; and the firmament sheweth his handywork. Day unto day uttereth speech, and night unto night sheweth knowledge. There is no speech nor language, where their voice is not heard. Their line is gone out through all the earth, and their words to the end of the world.
  1. Characteristics: Universal, non-verbal, points to God’s existence but not specific details like salvation.
  2. All creation is a parable for us to know God.
  • Always let your conscience be your guide: General revelation manifests through the innate moral conscience God has placed within every person
  • Romans 2:14-15: For when the Gentiles, which have not the law, do by nature the things contained in the law, these, having not the law, are a law unto themselves: Which shew the work of the law written in their hearts, their conscience also bearing witness, and their thoughts the mean while accusing or else excusing one another.
  • This internal moral compass is part of general revelation.

Nature reflects God's universal truth and righteousness, guiding all people to recognize His moral order, and the understanding that things are “wrong” or “right;” so that all may know God is real.

  • Without excuse:
  • “What about that guy on that deserted island? What about people who have never heard of Jesus?”
  1. Specific Revelation:
  • Definition: God revealing Himself through direct means of His Word - like Scripture, Prophets and Jesus Christ (Hebrews 1:1-2).
  • Example: John 1:1—“In the beginning was the Word.” Jesus is God’s ultimate revelation.
  • Characteristics: Detailed, verbal, reveals God’s plans for salvation, commandments, how to live our life, prophecy for the future (times past and time to come).
  1. 2 Timothy 3:16-17: All scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness: That the man of God may be perfect, thoroughly furnished unto all good works.
  • Specific Revelation builds on General Revelation, giving clarity to man that we might know God’s will for us collectively and individually.

Part 2: God’s Revelation Through Creation

Psalm 19:1-3 The heavens declare the glory of God; and the firmament sheweth his handywork. Day unto day uttereth speech, and night unto night sheweth knowledge. There is no speech nor language, where their voice is not heard.

Creation (sky, stars, nature) declares God’s glory and power.

  • Romans 1:20: People are “without excuse” because creation shows God’s invisible qualities.
  • Job 12:7-9: But ask now the beasts, and they shall teach thee; and the fowls of the air, and they shall tell thee: Or speak to the earth, and it shall teach thee: and the fishes of the sea shall declare unto thee. Who knoweth not in all these that the hand of the Lord hath wrought this?
  • Animals, birds, and the earth testify to God’s wisdom and sovereignty.
  • Example: A bird’s migration shows God’s design.
  • Miracle of birth. DNA.
  • All nature as a parable

Part 3: God’s Revelation Through Wrath

Romans 1:18-32 and Psalm 7:11:

  • God’s wrath reveals His justice and hatred of sin (Romans 1:18-32).
  • Psalm 7:11: God is a “righteous judge.” His wrath shows His moral standard.
  • Wrath is not arbitrary but a response to sin, showing God’s holiness.
  • Seeing His wrath upon the wicked, even natural consequences for sin (STD’s, hangovers from drunkenness, adultery/divorce) teaches unbelievers that there is a just Creator.
  • Even the secular/pagan idea of “karma” shows there is a just God.
  • God’s wrath reveals His righteousness and ultimate victory over evil.
  • Connect wrath to justice - “How does knowing God judges fairly affect our view of Him?”
  • Revelation 6:10; And they cried with a loud voice, saying, How long, O Lord, holy and true, dost thou not judge and avenge our blood on them that dwell on the earth?

Psalm 7:11-13; God judgeth the righteous, and God is angry with the wicked every day. If he turn not, he will whet his sword; he hath bent his bow, and made it ready. He hath also prepared for him the instruments of death; he ordaineth his arrows against the persecutors.

Hebrews 10:30; For we know him that hath said, Vengeance belongeth unto me, I will recompense, saith the Lord. And again, The Lord shall judge his people.

God judges nations by their works

Jeremiah 25:31-32:A noise shall come even to the ends of the earth; for the LORD hath a controversy with the nations, he will plead with all flesh; he will give them that are wicked to the sword, saith the LORD. Thus saith the LORD of hosts, Behold, evil shall go forth from nation to nation, and a great whirlwind shall be raised up from the coasts of the earth.

“Why is it important to teach about God’s wrath alongside His love?”

  • Romans 12:9; Let love be without dissimulation. Abhor that which is evil; cleave to that which is good.

Part 4: God’s Revelation Through Mercy

Psalm 103:8 and Ephesians 2:4:

  • Psalm 103:8: God is “merciful and gracious, slow to anger.”
  • Ephesians 2:4: God’s “great love” and mercy save us despite our sin.
  • Romans 11:30-31:For as ye in times past have not believed God, yet have now obtained mercy through their (the Jews) unbelief: Even so have these also now not believed, that through your mercy they also may obtain mercy.

It is important to understand that when we show others the mercy and grace God has shown us, it reveals the nature of God to them. They hear our praise for answered prayers, or see a changed life, and come to better understanding of His longsuffering nature, and the glory He receives for loving His people.

Part 5: God’s Revelation Through The Word (Jesus and Scripture)

  • Hebrews 1:1-2: God spoke through prophets, but now through Jesus, His Son.
  • God, who at sundry times and in divers manners spake in time past unto the fathers by the prophets, Hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son, whom he hath appointed heir of all things, by whom also he made the worlds
  • John 1:1-3:
  • In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. The same was in the beginning with God. All things were made by him; and without him was not any thing made that was made.
  • Jesus is “The Word,” God’s ultimate revelation, fully divine.
  • 2 Peter 1:20-21:
  • Knowing this first, that no prophecy of the scripture is of any private interpretation. For the prophecy came not in old time by the will of man: but holy men of God spake as they were moved by the Holy Ghost.
  • Scripture is inspired by God, “God breathed,” written by men “carried” by the Holy Spirit.
  1. Burden of the Lord
  2. Prophets were the pen, God the author
  • This ensures Scripture’s trustworthiness as God’s Specific Revelation.
  • Why is it comforting to know Scripture comes from God, not just human ideas
