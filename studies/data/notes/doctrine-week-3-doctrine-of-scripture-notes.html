The Doctrine of Scripture (Week 3)

Part 1: Inspiration of Scripture

As we covered last week: Inspiration means Scripture is “God-breathed,” authored by God through human writers moved by the Holy Spirit, ensuring its divine origin and authority.
Key Verses:

  • 2 Timothy 3:16-17: “All scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness: That the man of God may be perfect, thoroughly furnished unto all good works.”
  • Explanation: “Inspiration” (Greek: theopneustos, God-breathed) means every word of Scripture comes from God’s mouth. It’s useful for teaching, correcting, and equipping believers.
  • 2 Peter 1:20-21: “Knowing this first, that no prophecy of the scripture is of any private interpretation. For the prophecy came not in old time by the will of man: but holy men of God spake as they were moved by the Holy Ghost.”
  • Explanation: The Holy Spirit “moved” (carried along) human authors, ensuring their words were God’s, not mere human ideas.
  • Jeremiah 36:2: “Take thee a roll of a book, and write therein all the words that I have spoken unto thee against Israel, and against Judah, and against all the nations, from the day I spake unto thee, from the days of Josiah, even unto this day.”
  • Explanation: God directly commanded Jeremiah to write His words

Part 2: Preservation of Scripture

Definition: Preservation means God ensures His Word remains intact and available across generations, never lost or corrupted beyond recovery.
Key Verses:

  • Psalm 12:6-7: “The words of the LORD are pure words: as silver tried in a furnace of earth, purified seven times. Thou shalt keep them, O LORD, thou shalt preserve them from this generation for ever.”
  • Explanation: God’s promise and a divine role is to preserve His words forever
  • Matthew 24:35: “Heaven and earth shall pass away, but my words shall not pass away.”
  • Explanation: Jesus affirms that God’s Word endures eternally, outlasting even creation itself.
  • Isaiah 40:8: “The grass withereth, the flower fadeth: but the word of our God shall stand for ever.”

Part 3: Inerrancy of Scripture

Definition: Inerrancy means Scripture is without error in all it affirms, as it is God’s truth, perfectly reliable in every detail.
Key Verses:

  • John 17:17: “Sanctify them through thy truth: thy word is truth.”
  • Explanation: Jesus declares God’s Word is truth itself, inherently free from error, guiding believers to holiness.
  • Psalm 119:89-96; For ever, O Lord, thy word is settled in heaven. Thy faithfulness is unto all generations: thou hast established the earth, and it abideth. They continue this day according to thine ordinances: for all are thy servants. Unless thy law had been my delights, I should then have perished in mine affliction. I will never forget thy precepts: for with them thou hast quickened me. I am thine, save me: for I have sought thy precepts. The wicked have waited for me to destroy me: but I will consider thy testimonies. I have seen an end of all perfection: but thy commandment is exceeding broad.

Implications:

  • Man does not live by bread alone, every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God.

  • We need (and have) an every word Bible, that is sufficient to make us perfect (meaning complete), thoroughly furnished (meaning everything we need).

  • We know it is unchanging, settled in Heaven before creation.

  • The rock upon which we build our Christian life.

  • All unsaved men will be judged by His word: Revelation 20:12;

  • And I saw the dead, small and great, stand before God; and the books were opened: and another book was opened, which is the book of life: and the dead were judged out of those things which were written in the books, according to their works.

  • Faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God

  • We who hear the Shepherd's voice, and respond to it, are responding to these inspired, preserved and inerrant words, and trusting in them.

  • Again and again in scripture, God pleads with man to believe Him.

  • A Testament is testimony. A promise based on the word, or the truthfulness, of the one making the promises.

  • There are three testifying in Heaven, the Father, the Spirit and the Word.

  • Under God’s law, at least 2 witnesses were needed. God gives us Three. Plus 4 gospels. Plus hundreds of prophecies that came to pass “That we may know He is the Lord.”

ALL DOCTRINE MUST COME FROM CLEAR SCRIPTURE

  • Not from traditions handed down verbally, which could change
  • Not by a council or group of men deciding what is true and what isn’t
  • Not by “dark sayings” and parables. These can enhance understanding and give context to clear doctrines, but they shouldn’t be the sole source of doctrine
  • Unsaved people cannot understand scripture, so going to them for doctrine is foolish
  • 1 Corinthians 2:14 But the natural man receiveth not the things of the Spirit of God: for they are foolishness unto him: neither can he know them, because they are spiritually discerned.
  • Anyone who doesn’t believe the gospel, rejects Jesus, etc, cannot teach us
  • Context matters! When someone posts a verse to claim a doctrine is true, you should not just accept their word. Always check the context and compare it with other scripture to see if it contradicts clear doctrine.
  • God is not the author of confusion
  • Search the scriptures to see if it is so
  • If you are presented with a contradiction, it is not up to you to square it for the person who introduced it. IT IS ON THEM TO.
