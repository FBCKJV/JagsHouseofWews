Acts 15:8-11

And God, which knoweth the hearts, bare them witness, giving them the Holy Ghost, even as he did unto us; And put no difference between us and them, purifying their hearts by faith. Now therefore why tempt ye God, to put a yoke upon the neck of the disciples, which neither our fathers nor we were able to bear? But we believe that through the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ we shall be saved, even as they.

"Mid-Acts" Dispensationalism holds that by chapter 15, a different gospel is made known unto Gentiles, that the Jews keeping the law of Moses still, despite believing, were under a dispensation of the law still. That the Gospel of Grace was given to Paul to preach to the Gentiles. That these verses "prove it."

A plain reading of the text shows that Peter held the same Gospel of Grace, and that it is not given by works of the law, but by the grace of the LORD (made distinct in the text, using the same capitalized distinction reserved for YHWH. A divine Lord, absolute authority, Greek is Kyrios).

We believe that through the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ we shall be saved, even as they. This sentence refutes any of the idea that Jews had a different gospel or standard, or that Peter's preaching to the circumcision was any different than Paul's ministry to the Gentiles.

There is no "Mid-Acts Dispensation." All men, from Adam through today have been saved by the grace of God the Father, through faith in His deliverance, which is by His Son, Jesus Christ.
