Brief Outline: The Single Gospel Through-Line in American Evangelicalism (and Its Preservation in the IFB Tradition)

1. Colonial & Early Republic Era (1740s–1830s)

- First Great Awakening (1730s–40s): Jonathan Edwards, George Whitefield, and Tennent brothers preach salvation by free grace through faith alone; instantaneous justification the moment a sinner trusts Christ.

- Second Great Awakening (1790s–1830s): Though methods vary (camp meetings, “new measures”), the core message across Presbyterians, Baptists, and most Methodists remains the same: man is lost, Christ’s blood is the only ground of pardon, believe and be saved now and forever.

→ The gospel is overwhelmingly “Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and thou shalt be saved” (no works, no second blessing, no Sabbath, no tongues required).

2. Mid-19th Century Consensus (1840–1880)

- Revivalists (Finney, Phoebe Palmer, etc.) introduce emotional intensity and sometimes questionable methods, but mainstream evangelical pulpits (Baptist, Presbyterian, Congregational, and most Methodist) still proclaim one gospel: justification by faith alone, received in a moment.

- Eternal security is debated (Calvinists vs. many Methodists), but the ground and instrument of salvation are not: Christ’s finished work + faith = eternal life.

3. Late 19th–Early 20th Century (1880–1930)

- D. L. Moody, R. A. Torrey, and Billy Sunday carry the exact same “believe and be saved” message to millions in urban crusades. No second blessing, no tongues, no investigative judgment, no loss of salvation taught as part of the gospel offer.

- This is the high-water mark of broad evangelical unity on the gospel itself.

4. Fracturing Period (1890–1950)

- Radical Holiness → Pentecostalism (1901–1916): adds “second work of grace” and tongues as normative or required.

- Seventh-day Adventism & Jehovah’s Witnesses arise with new mediators/authorities and works-righteousness elements.

- Mainline liberalism and neo-evangelical compromise begin eroding the old landmarks.

5. Fundamentalist & Independent Baptist Response (1920s–present)

- Fundamentalists (and especially the independent Baptist stream—J. Frank Norris, John R. Rice, Sword of the Lord, Jack Hyles, etc.) deliberately refuse the new additions and subtractions.

- They consciously preserve the pre-1890 evangelical gospel:

- Salvation is by grace alone through faith alone in Christ alone

- It is received in a moment, the instant one believes

- It is eternal (the believer possesses everlasting life right now)

- Nothing else (tongues, Sabbath, perfection, sacraments, lordship commitment, etc.) is part of the saving transaction.

Conclusion & Through-Line

From Whitefield in 1740 to Moody in 1880 to Billy Sunday in 1915 to John R. Rice in 1950 to a faithful Independent Fundamental Baptist preacher in 2025, the gospel invitation has remained unchanged:

“Christ died for your sins, was buried, and rose again. Believe on Him alone right now and you have everlasting life—freely, forever, and without fail.”

That is the single unbroken thread that the historic IFB movement still holds high today.
