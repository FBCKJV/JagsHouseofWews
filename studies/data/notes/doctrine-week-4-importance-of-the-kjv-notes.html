There is often much made of the great scholarship and masterful work of the KJB Translators... and certainly with good reason. But the truth of the matter is (not to detract from the labours of those men in any way) it should be noted, definitely more often than it is, that there was a great company of men who laboured much harder and sacrificed much more than they did to give us the word of God.

William Tyndale, a courageous reformer, devoted his life to translating the Holy Scriptures into English, ensuring the common man could access God’s pure Word. His work laid the foundation for the Authorized Version, preserving biblical truth against Roman Catholic corruption. Betrayed and condemned, Tyndale was martyred in 1536, strangled and burned at the stake by Rome’s authorities for his unwavering commitment. As he faced death, he boldly declared, “Lord, open the King of England’s eyes,” a final plea for the spread of God’s truth, cementing his legacy as a faithful hero.

It is estimated that the KJB contains well over 80% of William Tyndale's work. Taking into consideration the purification that took place between Tyndale's work and the KJB, the KJB Translators actually made relatively few changes to the text. Much of the work was done by men who risked... and lost... their lives to give us the Book.

The KJV committees had rules established by King James for how they would translate, and what they could use of previous translations:

Translation Rule #14 - "These translations to be used, when they agree better with the text than the Bishops' Bible: Tyndale's, Coverdale's, Matthew's, Whitchurch's [Great], Geneva."

So let's give some honour to the men who rarely get the credit they deserve for giving us the word of God we hold in our hands:

In addition to William Tyndale

Myles Coverdale (c. 1488–1569): Worked on Coverdale Bible (1535), Matthew Bible (1537), Great Bible (1539), and Geneva Bible notes. Not martyred, died peacefully.

John Rogers (c. 1500–1555): Edited Matthew Bible (1537) under pseudonym “Thomas Matthew.” Martyred, burned at the stake by Roman Catholic authorities under Queen Mary.

Thomas Cromwell (c. 1485–1540): Patron of Great Bible (1539), issued injunctions for English Bibles in churches. Martyred, beheaded for treason/heresy by Henry VIII’s order.

William Whittingham (c. 1524–1579): Led Geneva Bible (1560) translation. Not martyred, died peacefully.

Matthew Parker (1504–1575): Led Bishops’ Bible (1568). Not martyred, died peacefully.

And the other unknown who laboured with the aforementioned, along with the KJB editors who over the centuries ultimately brought us the precious PCE. Thank you, Lord, for this great company of men!

Psalm 68:11 - The Lord gave the word: great was the company of those that published it.
