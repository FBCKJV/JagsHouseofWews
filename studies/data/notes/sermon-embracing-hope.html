Luke 2:25-33

And, behold, there was a man in Jerusalem, whose name was Simeon; and the same man was just and devout, waiting for the consolation of Israel: and the Holy Ghost was upon him. And it was revealed unto him by the Holy Ghost, that he should not see death, before he had seen the Lord's Christ.

Simeon was there when Mary and Joseph brought in the baby Jesus to be circumcised 8th day as was custom, and he took the child up in his arms, AND embraced Him, blessing God, saying, Lord, now lettest thou thy servant depart in peace, according to thy word: For mine eyes have seen thy salvation, Which thou hast prepared before the face of all people; A light to lighten the Gentiles, and the glory of thy people Israel.

The title of my sermon tonight is Embracing Hope.

Simeon is an example of an OT saint, faithful and waiting patiently for the coming of Christ and was rewarded to see his faith fulfilled. Literally Embracing the hope he had watched for. And Simeon wasn’t alone. Looking down to verse 36; we meet Anna, of the tribe of Asher, a prophetess of great age, long widowed, who the Bible says departed not from the temple, but served God with fastings and prayers night and day. And there, seeing the Christ child, she gave thanks to the Lord, and spake of Him to all them that looked for redemption in Jerusalem.

I remember being a boy, around 8 and watching Charlie Brown Christmas and hearing Linus recite Luke 2, detailing the miraculous birth of Jesus in the city of David to a virgin in a manger, angels announcing Him to shepherds, saying “That’s what Christmas is about Charlie Brown” - a cartoon kid Preaching from the King James Bible on national TV. LIKE MANY This was my first exposure to scripture. It moved me. I remember as a boy seeking it out and reading this chapter for the first time and realizing then that there were always people who had the hope of Christ, of being saved by faith in the promises of the Lord.

  • This blew my young mind. And later, accepting Jesus, getting saved, reading the Bible and learning that, as the Bible says, to Him all of the prophets gave witness. Jesus telling the Pharisees If ye believed Moses, ye would have believed me; for he wrote of me.
  • That God, in sundry times and diverse manners spoke to the fathers by the prophets..That throughout time God spoke in various ways to them. That they have faith on the hope set before them.
  • So when Simeon saw Jesus and called him: A light to lighten the Gentiles, he was quoting the old testament prophet, Isaiah written 700yrs before. 🤯

Throughout the OT God made promises to man - and they believed God and it was counted for righteousness…And some they saw fulfilled immediately, as well as prophetic warnings relevant then and came to pass, and those they saw through a glass darkly, that carried promises for the far future. Of the coming of Christ, the Messiah - and of His death on the cross. And of His resurrection, and the eventual resurrection of believers unto eternal life, redemption..End times prophecy that has yet to be fulfilled.

The Bible tells us to look at our forerunners in faith, and be like them:

The OT was written for our admission, to learn how to live by faith. To show the same diligence to the full assurance of hope unto the end: To be followers of them who through faith and patience inherit the promises.

  • God made promises to Abraham, nearly 4,000 years before Christ…
  • Surely blessing I will bless thee, and multiplying I will multiply thee.
  • That in his seed Which is Christ, all nations would be blessed.

The Bible says after he had patiently endured, Abraham obtained the promise from God Who cannot lie. He gives us His word, the Bible, to give us faith, to strengthen it. By seeing His promises kept again and again in supernatural ways, unfolding over thousands of years.

We like Abraham should have a strong consolation, to know for sure His promise is true…AND lay hold upon the hope set before us, Like Simeon, who got to literally embrace his hope. Which hope The Bible Says we have as an anchor of the soul

Hebrews 11:1 Faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen. Hope is a tangible thing, though we have not seen it or touched it, we know that it IS. As solid as this pulpit. As real as the pineapple pizza downstairs. We haven’t seen it, but we know that it’s there.

Hall of faith is filled with the stories of the people who believed God and had the hope of salvation, esteeming the reproach of Christ, They had respect for the recompense of that reward: Abel, Enoch, Noah, Abraham, Sarah, Issac, Jacob, Moses, Rahab,…ALL Looking forward to the cross and their salvation. Just as we look back.

Hebrew 11:13

These all died in faith, not having received the promises, but having seen them afar off, and were persuaded of them, and embraced them….

What really fascinates me is the idea of being alive to see your hope fulfilled. Like Simeon and Anna. Or consider the apostles, many who had been waiting for the Messiah, and found Him in Christ Jesus. They were blessed to witness first hand His ministry, hearing the preaching and witnessing the miracles, and seeing the death, burial and resurrection of Christ, according to scriptures they had heard and believed. The gospel on which we now stand (1 Cor 15). That all who trust on Jesus have the hope of salvation, resurrection, eternal life.

And so Just as the apostles watched as Jesus ascend into Heaven on Pentecost, 50 days after the resurrection, and were told by angels - to watch, for He shall so come in like manner as ye have seen him go - We are called to likewise WATCH & WAIT on that HOPE

To have this hope you must accept that free gift, given by the grace of the Father, paid for by the precious blood of our Saviour. Sealed by the Spirit unto that glorious day of redemption….that holy Spirit of promise…the earnest of our inheritance…

The Bible says We are bought with a price. And so we have a downpayment towards that day. A token of that hope, the Spirit testifying within us that we ARE children of God.

So we watch for the Day of Redemption. The resurrection. Many think it may be in our lifetime. That like Simeon, Anna,and the Apostles who were alive and saw their hope, we may be blessed to be alive for the rapture, the gathering of the elect, those saved in Christ.

  • Having the promise of eternal life in glorified bodies: In a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trump…
  • When our corruptible and broken flesh becomes incorruptible and we put on immortality (1 Corinthians 15:52)
  • The dead in Christ rising first then, we who are alive and remain 🤞
  • To be caught up together in the clouds, The Rapture - to meet the Lord in the air: and so shall we ever be with the Lord. (1 Thessalonians 4:16-17)

My hope is built on nothing less than Jesus' blood and righteousness; I dare not trust the sweetest frame, but wholly lean on Jesus' name.

STORY

On Christ the solid rock I stand All other ground is sinking sand

Perhaps we will be alive to witness our hope firsthand. But either way we embrace that hope within us. As the apostle John wrote,

Beloved, now are we the sons of God, and it doth not yet appear what we shall be: but we know that, when he shall appear, we shall be like him; for we shall see him as he is. And every man that hath this hope in him purifieth himself, even as He is pure. (1 John 3:2-3)

That’s the hope we embrace. Whether we are alive and remain or, like our forefathers, we die in faith, not having received it, but seeing our hope afar off. And so let us embrace it with full confidence. Let it be the anchor to our soul. Let it be on Jesus, on the solid rock we stand. Embracing Him, embracing that blessed hope. Amen
