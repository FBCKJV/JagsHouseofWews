Overview of the book of Proverbs - Instruction to the heirs of the Kingdom

I - The book of Proverbs is a gift of God given to man, with a very specific purpose:

Proverbs 1:2-6

To know wisdom and instruction; to perceive the words of understanding; To receive the instruction of wisdom, justice, and judgment, and equity; To give subtilty to the simple, to the young man knowledge and discretion. A wise man will hear, and will increase learning; and a man of understanding shall attain unto wise counsels: To understand a proverb, and the interpretation; the words of the wise, and their dark sayings.

That’s the purpose, and this instruction is for everyone: the simple, the young, the wise and the mature person of understanding can all learn from these sayings

The book of Proverbs are primarily the recorded sayings of Solomon, to whom God gave an abundance of wisdom;

2 Chronicles 1:7-12

In that night did God appear unto Solomon, and said unto him, Ask what I shall give thee.

Give me now wisdom and knowledge, that I may go out and come in before this people: for who can judge this thy people, that is so great? And God said to Solomon, Because this was in thine heart, and thou hast not asked riches, wealth, or honour, nor the life of thine enemies, neither yet hast asked long life;...: wisdom and knowledge is granted unto thee; and I will give thee riches, and wealth, and honour, such as none of the kings have had that have been before thee, neither shall there any after thee have the like.

1 Kings 4:31-34

For he was wiser than all men; than Ethan... And he spake three thousand proverbs: and his songs were a thousand and five....And there came of all people to hear the wisdom of Solomon, from all kings of the earth, which had heard of his wisdom.

While as King, Solomon displayed wisdom in ruling, making Israel great again, but ruling in his personal life, not so much.

In 1 Kings 11 we read that king Solomon loved many strange women...he had seven hundred wives and three hundred concubines: For it came to pass, when Solomon was old, that his wives turned away his heart after other gods: and his heart was not perfect with the Lord his God, as was the heart of David his father.

The wisest man in history, fell prey to the very thing he counseled against.

And Solomon used this wisdom to instruct his sole heir, his only recorded son, Rehoboam:

Solomon may have written these admonitions to Rehoboam in repentance and regret. He even may have been in the midst of struggling with his sin as he gave them.

Parents of teens will see their own bad habits creeping into their children’s behavior, and when we correct them, they often will level charges of hypocrisy to justify themselves, while we are simply looking to help them avoid our own mistakes. Imagine Solomon telling his son not to chase the strange woman of folly when he had entire palaces built to hold his concubines from every nation.

The Bible tells us that his son, King Rehoboam had eighteen wives and threescore (60) concubines and that “He desired many wives.” but It also says “he dealt wisely” as king.

(In contrast, Jeroboam, who ruled the split northern kingdom of Israel had but one wife, yet he ruled wickedly and brought in a false religion of idols representing God, with unqualified priests making offerings to them, invented holidays and feasts. Catholicism 1.0)

We all fall short, and there are no perfect parents, role models or preachers, we are still called to give ear to the ways of the Lord, and in fear of Him, gain wisdom, and to instruct others in His way, despite our own shortcomings.

II - All that to say: With great wisdom comes the great responsibility - to impart the Lord’s ways to our children, be they physical or spiritual;

The record of Solomon’s sayings were directed to the instruction of his only son and heir, Rehoboam. Preparing him to rule one day.

Solomon writes, My son, hear the instruction of thy father, and forsake not the law of thy mother, these truths are from God, and it is every parent's responsibility to teach them to our children, that they be an ornament of grace upon their head, and chains about their necks.

Fear of the Lord and the wisdom that comes from it - is the greatest inheritance we can give our children. 3 John 1:4 I have no greater joy than to hear that my children walk in truth.

It is also a command, with promise;

Proverbs 22:6 Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it.

Raising godly children is a grave responsibility. They are not ours, but His, we are blessed to be stewards, and instruct them as heirs. Not only are we to instruct children in the wisdom of God, but we are to chasten them, correcting them corporally.

Proverbs 13:24 - He that spareth his rod hateth his son: but he that loveth him chasteneth him betimes. I guess My Mom REALLY Loved me

Proverbs 23:13-14 - Withhold not correction from the child: for if thou beatest him with the rod, he shall not die. Thou shalt beat him with the rod, and shalt deliver his soul from hell.

Proverbs 19:18 - Chasten thy son while there is hope, and let not thy soul spare for his crying.

Spanking your child when they are young, doing it “betimes” meaning early, while they are very young, isn’t justifying abuse. A whack on the backside when “No” wasn’t sufficient will often teach the lesson. The establishment of corporal punishment teaches consequences more than trying to reason with a child, as children are inherently foolish:

Proverbs 22:15 - Foolishness is bound in the heart of a child; but the rod of correction shall drive it far from him.

Modern “parenting” has abandoned the wisdom of God, and created a generation of spoiled monsters, afraid of nothing, acting out to the shame of their parents.

Proverbs 17:25 - A foolish son is a grief to his father, and bitterness to her that bare him.

Proverbs 29:15 - The rod and reproof give wisdom: but a child left to himself bringeth his mother to shame.

Proverbs 10:1 - ... a foolish son is the heaviness of his mother.

The admonition to chasten your children is tempered with the understanding it is to be done with love. As Ephesians 6:4 commands: And, ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord.

The fear of a parent is the first lesson a child receives in fearing the Lord. Fear in this sense is a healthy respect for the authority of the Lord, His terrible judgments and position of authority in our lives, that there are consequences for our actions. Instruction at home establishes this understanding, as parents are proxies for God.

As Solomon’s father, David wrote, Psalm 34:11 - Come, ye children, hearken unto me: I will teach you the fear of the Lord.

When you read through Proverbs you see that many of the lessons are repeated, again and again, rephrased but carrying the same meaning. It's like those sayings we remember from our parents, who told us a million times. The Father uses repetitions to help get it through our hard hearts and thick skulls, the same way our folks did. Proverbs 9:10 - The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom: and the knowledge of the holy is understanding.

Our stewardship over the children God may bless us with, is meant to reflect the Father’s love for His children in Christ.

Hebrews 12:5-11 - My son, despise not thou the chastening of the Lord, nor faint when thou art rebuked of him: For whom the Lord loveth he chasteneth, and scourgeth every son whom he receiveth.

Understanding of what's right, isn't a guarantee of being righteous in adulthood, but it certainly is a great inoculation against the snares and stumbling stones most people get caught in. We may wander, as we are all prone to. But those guardrails, if established early, can help keep us from venturing too far.

Early admonition, a scourging betimes, the willingness to both instruct a child in the way of righteousness and giving them the rod of correction early, will help them form the foundation for fear of the Lord.

III - Lady Wisdom vs the strange woman, Folly

Solomon’s instruction on chasing after Lady Wisdom, pursuing knowledge, prudence and discretion, and his warning of being misled by the strange woman of folly, serves as both a metaphor for obtaining wisdom and avoiding foolishness, as well as having a practical application against chasing after strange women in a literal sense, avoiding adultery, something the king with a thousand wives and concubines knows all about.

Solomon says in Proverbs 7 that the strange woman speaks sweet sayings, and is seductive in her ways, but her end is bitter.

Proverbs 7:

My son, keep my words, and lay up my commandments with thee. Keep my commandments, and live; and my law as the apple of thine eye. Bind them upon thy fingers, write them upon the table of thine heart. Say unto wisdom, Thou art my sister; and call understanding thy kinswoman: That they may keep thee from the strange woman, from the stranger which flattereth with her words.

Solomon then gives a parable, about a simple young man, in the dark of night, heading to the house of Lady Wisdom (v10)

And, behold, there met him a woman with the attire of an harlot, and subtil of heart.

(She is loud and stubborn; her feet abide not in her house: Now is she without, now in the streets, and lieth in wait at every corner.) …So she caught him, and kissed him, and with an impudent face - she says to him she has given peace offerings and paid her vows, lying, saying essentially that she is ritually clean. Enticing him.

(v15) Therefore came I forth to meet thee, diligently to seek thy face, and I have found thee.

I have decked my bed with coverings of tapestry, with carved works, with fine linen of Egypt. I have perfumed my bed with myrrh, aloes, and cinnamon. Come, let us take our fill of love until the morning: let us solace ourselves with loves.

Folly, the fruit of foolishness, of doing the wrong thing when you know better, is incredibly seductive. She flatters - “I was diligently seeking you!” She entices “I decked my bed in preparation for you. Come let us take our fill of love!” it’s one night, only to the morning, right? Where’s the harm?

As they say: Sin will take you further than you want to go, keep you longer than you want to stay, and cost you more than you want to pay. In an adulterous relationship, engaging in sin - in our chasing folly; What may start in impulsive lust and seen as temporary, leads to trouble, with long lasting repercussions and consequences. Deadly. Just as the wages of sin is death, the path of folly is death. It shifts suddenly, making it easy to get lost, and the Bible says it is full of snares and stumbling stones. When you think you know where you are and what lies ahead, but instead find yourself caught in the snare of sin.

This strange woman will also use the allure of getting away with what you know is wrong to further entice you:

For the goodman is not at home, he is gone a long journey: He hath taken a bag of money with him, and will come home at the day appointed. Nibbling at the bait of flattery, at the promise of quick pleasure, closing the trap with the idea of getting away with something you know is wrong, topped with the covetousness of indulging in the rich man’s wealthy lifestyle. It’s a trap.

(v21) With her much fair speech she caused him to yield, with the flattering of her lips she forced him. He goeth after her straightway, as an ox goeth to the slaughter, or as a fool to the correction of the stocks; Till a dart strike through his liver; as a bird hasteth to the snare, and knoweth not that it is for his life. The dart is a dagger. The fool taken in by Folly, a young man taken in by a harlot, and robbed and murdered. A person led away into sin, and slain by it.

(v24) Hearken unto me now therefore, O ye children, and attend to the words of my mouth. Let not thine heart decline to her ways, go not astray in her paths. For she hath cast down many wounded: yea, many strong men have been slain by her. Her house is the way to hell, going down to the chambers of death.

In contrast, Lady Wisdom shows us God's paths, which are straight and knowable, a path of life. The Way is laid out and the light of that path is His word.

Proverbs 9:1-5 - Wisdom hath builded her house, She hath hewn out her seven pillars: She hath killed her beasts; she hath mingled her wine; She hath also furnished her table. She hath sent forth her maidens: she crieth Upon the highest places of the city, Whoso is simple, let him turn in hither: As for him that wanteth understanding, she saith to him, Come, eat of my bread, And drink of the wine which I have mingled.

The repeated urgent calls from Wisdom (her maidens sent out, crying aloud in public places, inviting the simple/foolish to turn and live) parallels the gospel invitation found throughout scripture, and its urgency.

The wedding feast parable (Matt 22 / Luke 14) where the Lord tells his servants to go out to the highways and hedges, bidding both bad and good to come to the supper He has prepared for His Son, is a direct echo of Proverbs 9. Wisdom calling the simple to her feast, just as we compel others to come and eat the bread and drink of the cup of the New Testament in Christ.

Jesus is Wisdom personified (eternal, always existing, present at creation, better than riches, calling all to the feast.) The imagery of bread/wine as the body/blood prefigures or is an archetype of Christ. Jesus is the Wisdom of God (1 Cor 1:24, 30; Col 2:3), the Bread of Life (John 6), living water (John 7:37–38), and the ultimate fulfillment of the “Way, truth, and life” (John 14:6). The marriage supper of the Lamb that all believers are invited to (Rev 19) is the same feast Lady Wisdom has prepared.

This is in stark contrast with Folly (seductive, deceptive, offering sweet-but-deadly pleasures, leading to death and Hell) all echoing the warnings against sin’s allure. The wages of sin, DEATH.

Fearing the Lord, and thus seeking wisdom, hearing His word and following His way is the process by which we learn the gospel and believe.

IV - The gospel promise of being delivered by the Lord is found throughout Proverbs.

Beginning in chapter 1, Solomon places first things first:

Proverbs 1:7 The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge: but fools despise wisdom and instruction.

The call to learn fear of the Lord also carries His promises:

Proverbs 1:33 But whoso hearkeneth unto me shall dwell safely, and shall be quiet from fear of evil.

Proverbs 3:5-6 Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding. In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths.

Throughout Proverbs we are told that the end of sin is death and destruction. And as we read the warnings and advice, if we have the fear of the Lord, if we are wise, we are convicted. The sayings against lying, laziness, being a talebearer or a gossip, holding anger for our neighbor, drinking, gluttony, being froward, a scoffer…all show we have all fallen short of the glory of God.

Proverbs 24:9 tells us that even the thoughts of foolishness are sin! We all have had foolish thoughts. - Some of you are having them right as we speak!

It is impossible to study Proverbs and not come away with the understanding that we are all sinners.

But God commendeth His love towards us, that while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.

Proverbs 8:17 I love them that love me; and those that seek me early shall find me.

Proverbs 8:35-36 For whoso findeth me findeth life, and shall obtain favour of the LORD. But he that sinneth against me wrongeth his own soul: all they that hate me love death.

Proverbs 3:33 The curse of the LORD is in the house of the wicked: but he blesseth the habitation of the just.

Proverbs 9:10-11 The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom: and the knowledge of the holy is understanding. For by me thy days shall be multiplied, and the years of thy life shall be increased.

Proverbs 10:3 The LORD will not suffer the soul of the righteous to famish: but he casteth away the substance of the wicked.

Proverbs 11:8 The righteous is delivered out of trouble, and the wicked cometh in his stead.

Proverbs 11:21 Though hand join in hand, the wicked shall not be unpunished: but the seed of the righteous shall be delivered.

Proverbs 14:26 In the fear of the LORD is strong confidence: and his children shall have a place of refuge.

Proverbs 15:29 The LORD is far from the wicked: but he heareth the prayer of the righteous.

Proverbs 18:10-11 - The name of the LORD is a strong tower: The righteous runneth into it, and is safe. The rich man's wealth is his strong city, And as an high wall in his own conceit.

Again and again, we find the promise of the Lord to all who harken to Him, and His wrath reserved to those who reject Him. This promise of deliverance is contrasted with the earthly things a man may put his trust in: the rich man's trust is in his wealth and the works he does. As Jesus said; It is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle, than for a rich man to enter into the kingdom of God.

We must choose humility, seeking that strong tower of the Lord as our only strength, rather than the haughty pride of the man who places his trust in the walls he has built for himself.

Proverbs 22:4 By humility and the fear of the LORD are riches, and honour, and life.

Proverbs 29:25 The fear of man bringeth a snare: but whoso putteth his trust in the LORD shall be safe.

As you can see, the promises of the Gospel are repeated again and again.

Proverbs 30:4

Who hath ascended up into heaven, or descended? who hath gathered the wind in his fists? who hath bound the waters in a garment? who hath established all the ends of the earth? what is his name, and what is his son's name, if thou canst tell?

V - Instruction for the prince (and princesses) of the Kingdom

My opinion is that the wisdom for princes and kings is also intended for the saved. That as co-heirs with Christ, who will rule and reign with Him during the millennium, and during any stewardship He blesses us with in this life, we need to understand these principles. To study to be approved unto God.

Proverbs 25:1: These are also proverbs of Solomon, which the men of Hezekiah king of Judah copied out.

Hezekiah, the righteous son of the wicked king Ahaz, a descendant of Solomon who came roughly 250 years and 13 generations later, brought revival to Judah. He repaired the Temple, removed idols, restored Passover, and called the people back to the Lord. In that zeal he commanded these proverbs to be written down and preserved by his scribes, as instruction of righteousness and to pass on to future kings, including us. Even the least in the Kingdom of Heaven is a prince, who will judge angels.

Study the word like princes and princesses who are under tutors, waiting for the day of adoption. The resurrection, when we come into our power as glorified children of God.

Galatians 4:1-2 Now I say, That the heir, as long as he is a child, differeth nothing from a servant, though he be lord of all; But is under tutors and governors until the time appointed of the father.

The primary context Paul is writing here, is understanding that the OT law serves to bring a man to understand our need for a savior, but the application also mirrors the process of a prince coming into his own by instruction, until the determined time of the King.

As Paul writes in Romans 8:23 ...even we ourselves groan within ourselves, waiting for the adoption, to wit, the redemption of our body. The day of redemption, the resurrection, is the day of our adoption; the time of the giving of the inheritance, to rule and reign with Jesus.

The Greek word Paul uses for 'adoption,' huiothesia, carries a heavier legal meaning than our modern idea of adoption. In that context, it wasn't about bringing an outsider into a family; it was the formal ceremony, the "placing as a son," where an heir reached the age of maturity, the father publicly recognized them as a legal adult, granting them the full authority, a signet ring, and power to manage the estate, or the lands appointed them. We become children of God when we are born again, but notice the adoption is at the resurrection. On the day of redemption.

So, when we see proverbs about kings or princes, about judgement and mercy, who to be influenced by, who to seek counsel from...it is all instructions from THE King, God our Father, to His children, who will inherit the Kingdom.

VI - Closing

The wisdom given to Solomon was and is the greatest wisdom possessed of man, before or since. And we have been blessed by God to have much of this inspired wisdom providentially preserved in God's word.

Although Solomon is said to have composed 3,000 proverbs, only 800 bearing his name appear in the book of Proverbs, these are the inspired word of God and given for our admonition and instruction.

We also were given the book of Ecclesiastes, or the wisdom of the prophet of God, in which Solomon set out to test the truth in all things of this world, only to find all is vanity, except in the worship of the Lord. True philosophy from the Spirit.

We have only one song of the 1,005 King Solomon composed, the Song of Songs, and its deep meaning, and reflections on Christ and believers.

We have the cream of the wisdom given to Solomon by God, at our fingertips. Able to study and know it. What a blessing.

But of the other proverbs and hundreds of songs and all his deep knowledge of flora and fauna and things of this earth and this life, we have no record. Only those sayings which pertain to God.

Let this be a lesson for us, who are the co-heirs with Christ. To apply the wisdom, and discernment that is found in God’s word. To learn His ways, which are not our ways.

In all our ways acknowledge Him, and He will direct our paths.
