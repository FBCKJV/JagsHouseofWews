Two Literary Devices to Watch For

John wraps the teachings of Jesus in two recurring devices. Both point the reader to the same plain truth: believe on the Son (John 20:31).

1. The Johannine Misunderstanding Motif

Jesus speaks a spiritual truth in a parable, figure or “dark saying,” often uses a word with a double meaning (amphibologia). A carnal hearer takes it literally and physically. Jesus, or John, then uses their misunderstanding to explain the spiritual meaning.

  • The figure — a picture Jesus uses: temple, birth, water, bread, door, sleep.
  • The literal hearer — someone takes it at face value and is confused or offended.
  • The revealed meaning — the plain truth, most often a call to believe on Him.
These things have I spoken unto you in proverbs: but the time cometh, when I shall no more speak unto you in proverbs, but I shall shew you plainly of the Father. — John 16:25

Device 1 in Action: The Seven “I Am” Figures

Jesus uses this motif to declare His divinity in a picture, and each carries the gospel inside it: believe on Him.

  1. The bread of life
    And Jesus said unto them, I am the bread of life: he that cometh to me shall never hunger; and he that believeth on me shall never thirst. — John 6:35

    Note: Coming is compared to eating; believing to drinking. The literal hearer: “The Jews therefore strove among themselves, saying, How can this man give us his flesh to eat?” (6:52).

  2. The light of the world
    Then spake Jesus again unto them, saying, I am the light of the world: he that followeth me shall not walk in darkness, but shall have the light of life. — John 8:12

    Note: Restated in 12:46: “I am come a light into the world, that whosoever believeth on me should not abide in darkness.” The literal hearer: “And some of the Pharisees which were with him heard these words, and said unto him, Are we blind also?” (9:40).

  3. The door
    I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved, and shall go in and out, and find pasture. — John 10:9

    Note: One way into the fold. “This parable spake Jesus unto them: but they understood not what things they were which he spake unto them.” (10:6). So He said it again a different way.

  4. The good shepherd
    I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep. — John 10:11

    Note: The figure points to the cross: “Therefore doth my Father love me, because I lay down my life, that I might take it again. No man taketh it from me, but I lay it down of myself. I have power to lay it down, and I have power to take it again. This commandment have I received of my Father.” (10:17-18).

  5. The resurrection, and the life
    Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live: And whosoever liveth and believeth in me shall never die. Believest thou this? — John 11:25–26

    Note: Martha thought of the last day: “Martha saith unto him, I know that he shall rise again in the resurrection at the last day.” (11:24). Jesus pointed her to Himself, the Life standing before her.

  6. The way, the truth, and the life
    Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me. — John 14:6

    Note: Thomas thought of a road: “Thomas saith unto him, Lord, we know not whither thou goest; and how can we know the way?” (14:5). The way is a Person.

  7. The true vine
    I am the true vine, and my Father is the husbandman. — John 15:1

    Note: Life and fruit come only by being joined to Him: “I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing.” (15:5).

The Bare “I Am” — The Name of God

And God said unto Moses, I AM THAT I AM: and he said, Thus shalt thou say unto the children of Israel, I AM hath sent me unto you. — Exodus 3:14
  1. I that speak unto thee am he (John 4)
    • The hearer (4:25): “The woman saith unto him, I know that Messias cometh, which is called Christ: when he is come, he will tell us all things.”
    • His words (4:26): “Jesus saith unto her, I that speak unto thee am he.”
    • The meaning (4:28-29): “The woman then left her waterpot, and went her way into the city, and saith to the men, Come, see a man, which told me all things that ever I did: is not this the Christ?”

    Note: She looked for a Messiah someday. He was standing in front of her.

  2. Ye shall die in your sins (John 8:24)
    • His words (8:24): “I said therefore unto you, that ye shall die in your sins: for if ye believe not that I am he, ye shall die in your sins.”
    • The hearers, the Jews (8:25): “Then said they unto him, Who art thou? And Jesus saith unto them, Even the same that I said unto you from the beginning.”
    • The meaning (8:27): “They understood not that he spake to them of the Father.”

    Note: They asked for a name; He had already given it. Believing that He is “I am” is life or death.

  3. When ye have lifted up the Son of man (John 8:28)
    • His words (8:28): “Then said Jesus unto them, When ye have lifted up the Son of man, then shall ye know that I am he, and that I do nothing of myself; but as my Father hath taught me, I speak these things.”
    • The hearers (8:30): “As he spake these words, many believed on him.”
    • The meaning (12:32-33): “And I, if I be lifted up from the earth, will draw all men unto me. This he said, signifying what death he should die.”

    Note: The cross would prove who He is.

  4. Before Abraham was, I am (John 8:58)
    • The figure (8:56): “Your father Abraham rejoiced to see my day: and he saw it, and was glad.”
    • The literal hearer (8:57): “Then said the Jews unto him, Thou art not yet fifty years old, and hast thou seen Abraham?”
    • His words (8:58): “Jesus said unto them, Verily, verily, I say unto you, Before Abraham was, I am.”
    • Their response (8:59): “Then took they up stones to cast at him: but Jesus hid himself, and went out of the temple, going through the midst of them, and so passed by.”

    Note: They counted His age in years. He spoke as the eternal God.

  5. That ye may believe that I am he (John 13:19)
    • The figure (13:18): “I speak not of you all: I know whom I have chosen: but that the scripture may be fulfilled, He that eateth bread with me hath lifted up his heel against me.”
    • His words (13:19): “Now I tell you before it come, that, when it is come to pass, ye may believe that I am he.”
    • The hearers (13:22): “Then the disciples looked one on another, doubting of whom he spake.”
    • The hearers again (13:28): “Now no man at the table knew for what intent he spake this unto him.”

    Note: The disciples missed it at the table. Once the betrayal came to pass, the fulfilled word would prove He is “I am.”

  6. I am he (John 18)
    • His question (18:4): “Jesus therefore, knowing all things that should come upon him, went forth, and said unto them, Whom seek ye?”
    • The hearers (18:5): “They answered him, Jesus of Nazareth. Jesus saith unto them, I am he. And Judas also, which betrayed him, stood with them.”
    • Their response (18:6): “As soon then as he had said unto them, I am he, they went backward, and fell to the ground.”
    • The meaning (18:8-9): “Jesus answered, I have told you that I am he: if therefore ye seek me, let these go their way: That the saying might be fulfilled, which he spake, Of them which thou gavest me have I lost none.”

    Note: They came for a man from Nazareth. At the name of God they fell, and He gave Himself up to keep His own.

2. Semitic Parallelism

John often writes the way Hebrew thinkers and poets wrote—by pairing lines. He either says the same truth twice in slightly different words (synonymous parallelism), or sets two opposites next to each other so each one makes the other clearer (antithetic parallelism).

The second line is not just decoration. It defines, sharpens, or completes the first.

1. Synonymous Parallelism

John restates the same truth in a second phrasing.

John 3:15–16

That whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life.
For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.

John 6:40 and 6:54

Plain statement (6:40): “every one which seeth the Son, and believeth on him, may have everlasting life.”

Figured statement (6:54): “Whoso eateth my flesh, and drinketh my blood, hath eternal life.”

Same promise, two different wordings.

2. Antithetic Parallelism

John places two opposites side by side so each defines the other.

Example – John 3:6

That which is born of the flesh is flesh;
and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit.

Flesh produces only flesh.
Spirit produces only spirit.

The contrast makes the point unmistakable.

John 1 — The Word Made Flesh

Setting: John’s prologue: the eternal Word who is God, made flesh. Then John the Baptist points to Jesus, “Behold the Lamb of God” (1:29), and the first disciples follow: Andrew, Peter, Philip, Nathanael.

In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. — 1:1
And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us… — 1:14

Note: John declares who Jesus is before any miracle: God come in the flesh.

Received Him, Believed on Him

  • Figure (1:4-5): “the life was the light of men… and the darkness comprehended it not.”
  • Response (1:10-11): “the world knew him not… his own received him not.”
  • Meaning (1:12-13): “as many as received him… even to them that believe on his name.”

Note: “Received him” is restated as “believe on his name.” This sets the pattern for the whole book.

John 2 — Destroy This Temple

Setting: The wedding at Cana, water to wine, His first miracle (2:11). Then up to Jerusalem at Passover, where He drives the money changers out of the temple and the Jews demand a sign (2:18).

  • Figure (2:19): “Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up.”
  • Literal hearer, the Jews (2:20): “Forty and six years was this temple in building…”
  • Meaning (2:21-22): “But he spake of the temple of his body… and they believed.”

Note: The first figure given by Jesus in John points to His death and resurrection.

John 3 — Ye Must Be Born Again

Nicodemus is a ruler of the Jews, a Pharisee, and he comes to Jesus by night. He is the literal Larry of scripture.

  • Figure (3:3): “Except a man be born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God.”
  • Literal hearer, Nicodemus (3:4): “How can a man be born when he is old?”
  • Meaning (3:5-6): “That which is born of the flesh is flesh; and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit.”

Note: Parallelism: “born of water” = “born of the flesh” (natural birth); “born of the Spirit” = the second birth.

As Moses Lifted Up the Serpent

  • Figure (John 3:14): “as Moses lifted up the serpent… even so must the Son of man be lifted up.” Numbers and the brazen serpent: look and live.
  • Meaning (3:15): “That whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life.”

Note: “Look and live” becomes “believe and live.” John 3:16 restates verse 15 and adds to this good news its source: the Father’s love.

3:18 draws the line at believing alone: “He that believeth on him is not condemned: but he that believeth not is condemned already, because he hath not believed in the name of the only begotten Son of God.”

John the Baptist repeats this in John 3:36:

He that believeth on the Son hath everlasting life: and he that believeth not the Son shall not see life; but the wrath of God abideth on him. — John 3:36

John 4 — Living Water

Setting: Passing through Samaria, Jesus sits wearied at Jacob’s well at Sychar about noon. A Samaritan woman comes to draw water while the disciples are off buying food. The chapter ends back in Cana with the nobleman’s son healed (4:46-54).

  • Figure (4:10): “he would have given thee living water.”
  • Literal hearer, the Samaritan woman (4:11-12): “thou hast nothing to draw with, and the well is deep.”
  • Meaning (4:13-14): “whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst.”

Note: Antithetic contrast of two waters. John 7:39 identifies the living water as the Spirit: “(But this spake he of the Spirit, which they that believe on him should receive: for the Holy Ghost was not yet given; because that Jesus was not yet glorified.)”

Meat to Eat

  • Figure (4:32): “I have meat to eat that ye know not of.”
  • Literal hearers, the disciples (4:33): “Hath any man brought him ought to eat?”
  • Meaning (4:34): “My meat is to do the will of him that sent me, and to finish his work.”

Note: Even disciples stumble at figures. The work is finished at the cross: “When Jesus therefore had received the vinegar, he said, It is finished: and he bowed his head, and gave up the ghost.” (19:30).

I That Speak unto Thee Am He

Jesus saith unto her, I that speak unto thee am he. — 4:26

Note: The first “I am” in John. “And many more believed because of his own word; And said unto the woman, Now we believe, not because of thy saying: for we have heard him ourselves, and know that this is indeed the Christ, the Saviour of the world.” (4:41-42).

John 5 — Equal with God

Setting: A feast in Jerusalem. At the pool of Bethesda, Jesus heals a man infirm 38 years: “Rise, take up thy bed, and walk” (5:8). It is the sabbath, and the Jews seek to kill Him.

  • His words (5:17): “My Father worketh hitherto, and I work.”
  • What the Jews understood (5:18): “making himself equal with God.”
  • Promise (5:24): “He that heareth my word, and believeth… hath everlasting life… is passed from death unto life.”

Note: Unbelief did not misunderstand His claim to deity. It rejected it.

John 6 — I Am the Bread of Life

Setting: Jesus feeds the 5,000 with five barley loaves and two small fishes, then walks on the sea that night. Next day the crowd finds Him in Capernaum wanting more bread. It ends with many disciples walking away (6:66) and Peter: “to whom shall we go?” (6:68).

  • Figure (6:35): “I am the bread of life: he that cometh to me shall never hunger; and he that believeth on me shall never thirst.”
  • Literal hearers, the Jews (6:52): “How can this man give us his flesh to eat?”
  • Meaning (6:63): “the words that I speak unto you, they are spirit, and they are life.”

Note: Coming = eating; believing = drinking. The flesh profits nothing.

Eating Is Believing (parallelism)

  • Plain (6:40): “every one which seeth the Son, and believeth on him, may have everlasting life.”
  • Figure (6:54): “Whoso eateth my flesh, and drinketh my blood, hath eternal life.”

Note: Same promise, same result: “I will raise him up at the last day.” Kept: “All that the Father giveth me shall come to me; and him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out.” (6:37). “And this is the Father’s will which hath sent me, that of all which he hath given me I should lose nothing, but should raise it up again at the last day.” (6:39).

John 7 — Whither Will He Go?

Setting: The feast of tabernacles. His own brethren do not believe; He goes up quietly and teaches in the temple midway through. Officers are sent to take Him. On the last great day of the feast He cries out about living water.

  • Figure (7:33-34): “Ye shall seek me, and shall not find me: and where I am, thither ye cannot come.”
  • Literal hearers, the Jews (7:35-36): “will he go unto the dispersed among the Gentiles?”
  • Meaning (16:28): “I leave the world, and go to the Father.”

Note: They thought of a journey; He spoke of returning to heaven by the cross.

Rivers of Living Water

  • Figure (7:37-38): “He that believeth on me… out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water.”
  • John’s explanation (7:39): “But this spake he of the Spirit, which they that believe on him should receive.”

Note: Coming and drinking is believing; the rivers that flow from every believer is the Spirit. Not literally water.

John 8 — Believe That I Am He

Setting: Opens with the woman taken in adultery: “Neither do I condemn thee: go, and sin no more” (8:11). Then in the temple treasury, Jesus declares Himself the light of the world, and a long, rising argument with the Pharisees and Jews follows.

  • Figure (8:21): “I go my way… whither I go, ye cannot come.”
  • Literal hearers, the Jews (8:22): “Will he kill himself?”
  • Meaning (8:23-24): “if ye believe not that I am he, ye shall die in your sins.”

Note: Antithesis (Device 2): “And he said unto them, Ye are from beneath; I am from above: ye are of this world; I am not of this world.” (8:23). They thought He meant to kill Himself; He meant He was going back to where He came from, above. Then believing and His identity are joined in one sentence: “for if ye believe not that I am he, ye shall die in your sins” (8:24).

The Truth Shall Make You Free

  • Figure (8:31-32): “the truth shall make you free.”
  • Literal hearers, the Jews which believed on him (8:31, 33): “We be Abraham’s seed, and were never in bondage.”
  • Meaning (8:34-36): “If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed.”

Note: They heard ancestry; He meant slavery to sin.

Before Abraham Was, I Am

  • Figure (8:56): “Abraham rejoiced to see my day.”
  • Literal hearers, the Jews (8:57): “Thou art not yet fifty years old.”
  • Meaning (8:58): “Before Abraham was, I am.”

Note: The name of God - Exodus 3:14. They certainly understood the claim: “Then took they up stones to cast at him: but Jesus hid himself, and went out of the temple, going through the midst of them, and so passed by.” (8:59).

John 9 — Are We Blind Also?

Setting: Jesus sees a man blind from his birth. He makes clay, anoints his eyes, and sends him to wash in the pool of Siloam, and he comes seeing. It is the sabbath: the Pharisees question the man and his parents and cast him out. Jesus finds him.

  • Figure (9:39): “that they which see not might see; and that they which see might be made blind.”
  • Literal hearers, some of the Pharisees (9:40): “Are we blind also?”
  • Meaning (9:41): “now ye say, We see; therefore your sin remaineth.”

Note: The blind man received sight; those who claimed to see stayed in darkness.

Lord, I Believe

Dost thou believe on the Son of God?… And he said, Lord, I believe. And he worshipped him. — 9:35–38

Note: Parallelism: “he that followeth me shall not walk in darkness” (8:12) = “whosoever believeth on me should not abide in darkness” (12:46).

John 10 — I Am the Door

Setting: Continues straight from chapter 9: Jesus speaks to the same Pharisees who cast out the healed man, the false shepherds. Later, at the feast of dedication in winter, the Jews surround Him in Solomon’s porch: “If thou be the Christ, tell us plainly” (10:24).

  • Figure (10:1-3): “He that entereth not by the door into the sheepfold… is a thief and a robber.”
  • Hearers, the Pharisees (10:6): “they understood not what things they were which he spake.”
  • Meaning (10:7, 9): “I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved.”

Note: “Then said Jesus unto them again” is the dual pattern in John’s own words.

I Am the Good Shepherd

  • Figure (10:11): “the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep.”
  • Death and resurrection (10:17-18): “I lay down my life, that I might take it again.”
  • Promise (10:28-29): “they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

Note: The sheep’s security rests on the Shepherd who died and rose.

I and My Father Are One

  • His words (10:30): “I and my Father are one.”
  • What the Jews understood (10:33): “thou, being a man, makest thyself God.”

Note: Even His enemies stated the claim plainly. They would not believe it and sought to kill Him.

John 11 — Lazarus Sleepeth

Setting: Lazarus of Bethany, brother of Mary and Martha, is sick. Jesus stays two more days; by the time He comes, Lazarus has lain in the grave four days. “Jesus wept” (11:35). After He raises him, the council plots His death.

  • Figure (11:11): “Our friend Lazarus sleepeth; but I go, that I may awake him.”
  • Literal hearers, the disciples (11:12-13): “Lord, if he sleep, he shall do well.”
  • Meaning (11:14): “Then said Jesus unto them plainly, Lazarus is dead.”

Note: For the believer, death is sleep because the Lord will wake him.

Believest Thou This?

  • Figure (11:23): “Jesus saith unto her, Thy brother shall rise again.”
  • Literal hearer, Martha (11:24): “Martha saith unto him, I know that he shall rise again in the resurrection at the last day.”
  • Meaning (11:25-26): “Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live: And whosoever liveth and believeth in me shall never die. Believest thou this?”
  • Believer’s answer (11:27): “She saith unto him, Yea, Lord: I believe that thou art the Christ, the Son of God, which should come into the world.”
  • Proof (11:43-44): “And when he thus had spoken, he cried with a loud voice, Lazarus, come forth. And he that was dead came forth, bound hand and foot with graveclothes: and his face was bound about with a napkin. Jesus saith unto them, Loose him, and let him go.”

Note: Martha held the resurrection as a doctrine for the last day. Jesus pointed her to Himself: the Resurrection was standing in front of her, right then. Then He proved it at the tomb. Even a believer can miss the plain point Jesus reveals.

John 12 — If I Be Lifted Up

Setting: Six days before Passover, Mary anoints His feet at Bethany. Next day the triumphal entry on a young ass. Certain Greeks ask to see Jesus, and He answers, “The hour is come” (12:23). This chapter closes His public ministry.

  • Figure (12:32): “And I, if I be lifted up from the earth, will draw all men unto me.”
  • John’s explanation (12:33): “This he said, signifying what death he should die.”
  • Literal hearers, the people (12:34): “Christ abideth for ever: and how sayest thou, The Son of man must be lifted up?”

Note: The third “lifted up.” The first: “And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up:” (3:14). The second: “When ye have lifted up the Son of man, then shall ye know that I am he” (8:28). Each time He speaks of the cross, each time He confirms He is the Christ.

John 13 — If I Wash Thee Not

Setting: The last supper, the night before the cross. Jesus washes the disciples’ feet, points out Judas with the sop, and Judas goes out: “and it was night” (13:30). He foretells Peter’s denial.

If I Wash Thee Not

  • Jesus names it a figure (13:7): “Jesus answered and said unto him, What I do thou knowest not now; but thou shalt know hereafter.”
  • Figure (13:8): “Peter saith unto him, Thou shalt never wash my feet. Jesus answered him, If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me.”
  • Literal hearer, Peter (13:9): “Simon Peter saith unto him, Lord, not my feet only, but also my hands and my head.”
  • Meaning (13:10-11): “Jesus saith to him, He that is washed needeth not save to wash his feet, but is clean every whit: and ye are clean, but not all. For he knew who should betray him; therefore said he, Ye are not all clean.”

Note: Peter heard a bath. Jesus meant the cleansing only He gives. The one who is washed (saved) is clean every whit and needs it once; the feet that walk in the world need daily cleansing. Judas had water on his feet but was never washed: he never believed.

That Ye May Believe That I Am He

Now I tell you before it come, that, when it is come to pass, ye may believe that I am he. — 13:19

Note: He foretells the betrayal so that its fulfillment proves He is “I am” (see the bare “I Am” section).

That Thou Doest, Do Quickly

  • His words (13:27): “And after the sop Satan entered into him. Then said Jesus unto him, That thou doest, do quickly.”
  • Literal hearers, the disciples (13:28-29): “Now no man at the table knew for what intent he spake this unto him. For some of them thought, because Judas had the bag, that Jesus had said unto him, Buy those things that we have need of against the feast; or, that he should give something to the poor.”
  • Meaning (13:30): “He then having received the sop went immediately out: and it was night.”

Note: The disciples heard an errand. Jesus was sending the betrayer out to set the cross in motion. Judas walked away from the Light, and it was night.

Whither I Go

  • Figure (13:33): “Little children, yet a little while I am with you. Ye shall seek me: and as I said unto the Jews, Whither I go, ye cannot come; so now I say to you.”
  • Literal hearer, Peter (13:36-37): “Simon Peter said unto him, Lord, whither goest thou? Jesus answered him, Whither I go, thou canst not follow me now; but thou shalt follow me afterwards. Peter said unto him, Lord, why cannot I follow thee now? I will lay down my life for thy sake.”
  • Meaning (13:38): “Jesus answered him, Wilt thou lay down thy life for my sake? Verily, verily, I say unto thee, The cock shall not crow, till thou hast denied me thrice.”

Note: The same saying He gave the Jews: “Ye shall seek me, and shall not find me: and where I am, thither ye cannot come.” (7:34), now to His own. Peter thought of a place he could walk to; Jesus was going to the cross and to the Father. Peter could not even follow Him past the fire in the courtyard: “Peter then denied again: and immediately the cock crew.” (18:27). This sets up chapter 14: “Let not your heart be troubled.”

John 14 — I Am the Way

Setting: Still in the upper room. The disciples are troubled: He is leaving, and Peter will deny Him. Jesus comforts them with the Father’s house of many mansions and promises the Comforter.

  • Figure (14:4): “whither I go ye know, and the way ye know.”
  • Literal hearer, Thomas (14:5): “Thomas saith unto him, Lord, we know not whither thou goest; and how can we know the way?”
  • Meaning (14:6): “I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.”

Note: The way is a Person, not a road. Dual phrasing in 14:1: “Let not your heart be troubled: ye believe in God, believe also in me.”

Shew Us the Father

  • Figure (14:7): “If ye had known me, ye should have known my Father also.”
  • Literal hearer, Philip (14:8): “Lord, shew us the Father, and it sufficeth us.”
  • Meaning (14:9): “he that hath seen me hath seen the Father.”

Note: One of the plainest statements of His deity. Promise: “Yet a little while, and the world seeth me no more; but ye see me: because I live, ye shall live also.” (14:19).

John 15 — I Am the True Vine

Setting: “Arise, let us go hence” (14:31). The teaching continues, likely on the way toward the garden: the vine and branches, love one another, and the world will hate you.

  • Figure (15:1): “I am the true vine, and my Father is the husbandman.”
  • Meaning (15:5): “I am the vine, ye are the branches… without me ye can do nothing.”

Note: The last of the seven figures. Its theme is fruit, and fruit here is spreading the truth and winning souls: “Ye have not chosen me, but I have chosen you, and ordained you, that ye should go and bring forth fruit, and that your fruit should remain” (15:16); “he that reapeth receiveth wages, and gathereth fruit unto life eternal” (4:36). The believer’s eternal security is already given: “him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out” (6:37); “And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand” (10:28). But the unfruitful servant is taken away: “Every branch in me that beareth not fruit he taketh away: and every branch that beareth fruit, he purgeth it, that it may bring forth more fruit.” (15:2). See also 15:6: “If a man abide not in me, he is cast forth as a branch, and is withered; and men gather them, and cast them into the fire, and they are burned.”

John 16 — A Little While

Setting: His last teaching before the cross: the Comforter’s work, sorrow turned to joy, and “be of good cheer; I have overcome the world” (16:33).

  • Figure (16:16): “A little while, and ye shall not see me: and again, a little while, and ye shall see me.”
  • Confused hearers, some of His disciples (16:17-18): “What is this that he saith, A little while? we cannot tell what he saith.”
  • Meaning (16:20-22): “your sorrow shall be turned into joy… and your joy no man taketh from you.”

Note: “A little while” was three days: the cross, the grave, the risen Lord.

Now Speakest Thou Plainly

These things have I spoken unto you in proverbs: but the time cometh, when I shall no more speak unto you in proverbs, but I shall shew you plainly of the Father. At that day ye shall ask in my name: and I say not unto you, that I will pray the Father for you: For the Father himself loveth you, because ye have loved me, and have believed that I came out from God. I came forth from the Father, and am come into the world: again, I leave the world, and go to the Father. His disciples said unto him, Lo, now speakest thou plainly, and speakest no proverb. Now are we sure that thou knowest all things, and needest not that any man should ask thee: by this we believe that thou camest forth from God. — 16:25–30

Note: Here Jesus names the very method this study follows. He has been speaking in proverbs, figures with a spiritual meaning, and now He says it plainly: He came from the Father and goes back to the Father. The disciples hear the plain word and answer the way every figure in John aims: “by this we believe.” Reading His figures as figures, and looking for the plain meaning He gives, is following Jesus’ own description of how He taught.

John 17 — Keep Through Thine Own Name

Setting: The prayer of Jesus the Son to God the Father on behalf of the apostles, the night before the cross.

While I was with them in the world, I kept them in thy name: those that thou gavest me I have kept, and none of them is lost, but the son of perdition; that the scripture might be fulfilled. And now come I to thee; and these things I speak in the world, that they might have my joy fulfilled in themselves. I have given them thy word; and the world hath hated them, because they are not of the world, even as I am not of the world. I pray not that thou shouldest take them out of the world, but that thou shouldest keep them from the evil. They are not of the world, even as I am not of the world. — John 17:12–16

Note: Jesus spoke in the world and gave the apostles the word of God. The world hated that word, and hated them for it. But the apostles heard it and believed, because they are not of the world.

John 18 — I Am He

Setting: Over the brook Cedron into the garden. Judas comes with a band of men and officers, and Jesus is arrested; Peter cuts off Malchus’s ear. Trials before Annas, Caiaphas, and Pilate; Peter denies Him three times; the crowd chooses Barabbas.

As soon then as he had said unto them, I am he, they went backward, and fell to the ground. — 18:6
Jesus answered, I have told you that I am he: if therefore ye seek me, let these go their way: — 18:8

Note: “I am he” is more than Jesus singling Himself out to the guards. It is the name of God (Exodus 3:14), His declaration that He is the Christ, and the truth of that Word knocks armed men to the ground. He is not taken by force; He lays down His life: “No man taketh it from me, but I lay it down of myself. I have power to lay it down, and I have power to take it again. This commandment have I received of my Father.” (10:18).

John 19 — It Is Finished

Setting: Scourged and crowned with thorns: “Behold the man!” (19:5). Crucified at Golgotha between two others under the title JESUS OF NAZARETH THE KING OF THE JEWS. He commits His mother to John, dies, His side is pierced, and Joseph of Arimathaea and Nicodemus bury Him.

When Jesus therefore had received the vinegar, he said, It is finished: and he bowed his head, and gave up the ghost. — 19:30

Note: Every figure pointed here: temple, serpent, bread, shepherd. The work is complete: “Jesus saith unto them, My meat is to do the will of him that sent me, and to finish his work.” (4:34); “I have glorified thee on the earth: I have finished the work which thou gavest me to do.” (17:4). Nothing is left for the sinner to add: “For I delivered unto you first of all that which I also received, how that Christ died for our sins according to the scriptures; And that he was buried, and that he rose again the third day according to the scriptures:” (1 Cor. 15:3-4).

John 20 — My Lord and My God

Setting: Early on the first day of the week, Mary Magdalene finds the stone taken away. Peter and John run to the empty tomb. Jesus appears to Mary, then to the disciples behind shut doors. Thomas is absent; eight days later, he is there.

  • Literal demand, Thomas called Didymus (20:24-25): “But Thomas, one of the twelve, called Didymus, was not with them when Jesus came. The other disciples therefore said unto him, We have seen the LORD. But he said unto them, Except I shall see in his hands the print of the nails, and put my finger into the print of the nails, and thrust my hand into his side, I will not believe.”
  • Jesus appears and offers Thomas the literal (20:26-28): “And after eight days again his disciples were within, and Thomas with them: then came Jesus, the doors being shut, and stood in the midst, and said, Peace be unto you. Then saith he to Thomas, Reach hither thy finger, and behold my hands; and reach hither thy hand, and thrust it into my side: and be not faithless, but believing. And Thomas answered and said unto him, My LORD and my God.”
  • Said again, for us (20:29): “Jesus saith unto him, Thomas, because thou hast seen me, thou hast believed: blessed are they that have not seen, and yet have believed.”
And many other signs truly did Jesus in the presence of his disciples, which are not written in this book: But these are written, that ye might believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God; and that believing ye might have life through his name. — 20:30–31

Note: Thomas’s confession matches 1:1: “In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.” Verse 31 states the purpose of the whole book.

John 21 — What Is That to Thee?

Setting: Overwhelmed, Peter leaves the ministry for his old life: “Simon Peter saith unto them, I go a fishing. They say unto him, We also go with thee” (21:3). Six others join him at the sea of Tiberias, and they fish all night and catch nothing. Jesus appears on the shore, fills the net with 153 fish, and serves them breakfast. Then three times He asks Peter, “lovest thou me?”, giving him the chance to answer each of his three denials. He foretells Peter’s martyrdom, Peter asks about John, and the book closes with one final misunderstanding.

  • His words (21:22): “Jesus saith unto him, If I will that he tarry till I come, what is that to thee? follow thou me.”
  • Literal hearers (21:23): “Then went this saying abroad among the brethren, that that disciple should not die.”

Note: John corrects the rumor by repeating exactly what Jesus said. Read what the Lord said, not read something into it.
