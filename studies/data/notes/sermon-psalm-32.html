Psalm 32

A Psalm of David · The blessedness of forgiveness

Wednesday Night Service

Psalm 32 (King James Version)

1 Blessed is he whose transgression is forgiven, whose sin is covered. 2 Blessed is the man unto whom the LORD imputeth not iniquity, and in whose spirit there is no guile.

3 When I kept silence, my bones waxed old through my roaring all the day long. 4 For day and night thy hand was heavy upon me: my moisture is turned into the drought of summer. Selah.

5 I acknowledged my sin unto thee, and mine iniquity have I not hid. I said, I will confess my transgressions unto the LORD; and thou forgavest the iniquity of my sin. Selah.

6 For this shall every one that is godly pray unto thee in a time when thou mayest be found: surely in the floods of great waters they shall not come nigh unto him.

7 Thou art my hiding place; thou shalt preserve me from trouble; thou shalt compass me about with songs of deliverance. Selah.

8 I will instruct thee and teach thee in the way which thou shalt go: I will guide thee with mine eye. 9 Be ye not as the horse, or as the mule, which have no understanding: whose mouth must be held in with bit and bridle, lest they come near unto thee.

10 Many sorrows shall be to the wicked: but he that trusteth in the LORD, mercy shall compass him about. 11 Be glad in the LORD, and rejoice, ye righteous: and shout for joy, all ye that are upright in heart.

The Blessedness of Forgiveness — Psalm 32:1–2

Blessed is he whose transgression is forgiven, whose sin is covered. Blessed is the man unto whom the LORD imputeth not iniquity, and in whose spirit there is no guile.

Psalm 32 opens with a familiar and important doctrine: the understanding of God's forgiveness for sin in the saved believer's life. This foundational fact is repeated by Paul in Romans:

Romans 4:6–8 — Even as David also describeth the blessedness of the man, unto whom God imputeth righteousness without works, saying, Blessed are they whose iniquities are forgiven, and whose sins are covered. Blessed is the man to whom the Lord will not impute sin.

The truth is that, in Christ, whether in David's day, Paul's, or our own, God forgives believers' past transgressions, covers our current sin, and will not hold our future sins and iniquity against us. Knowing that we are forgiven of all sin, not by works but by faith — the same good news today as in David's day. This Psalm serves as an evangelical appeal.

This positional standing of righteousness before God, despite our sins, through His imputed righteousness — the righteousness of Christ — is made possible through the atonement of Jesus' blood, and by the Holy Spirit, by the renewal, the regeneration, the quickening of our spirit, the new man within us.

Blessed is the man in whose spirit there is no guile.

John 16:13 — Howbeit when he, the Spirit of truth, is come, he will guide you into all truth: for he shall not speak of himself; but whatsoever he shall hear, that shall he speak: and he will shew you things to come.

We are given the Spirit of truth to teach us all things, the Bible says — to know truth from error, to recognize the teaching of God, to recognize heresy:

1 John 4:6 — We are of God: he that knoweth God heareth us; he that is not of God heareth not us. Hereby know we the spirit of truth, and the spirit of error.

We are also given that spirit, the love of truth, that we may truthfully acknowledge our sin. The lack of guile — cunning duplicity and dishonesty — carries with it an understanding that makes lying to God and ourselves about our own sin very difficult. We are convicted by that same spirit, which opens us up to the blessings that come from forgiveness. Confession is good for the soul.

The Weight of Silence — Psalm 32:3–5

When I kept silence, my bones waxed old through my roaring all the day long. For day and night thy hand was heavy upon me: my moisture is turned into the drought of summer. Selah. I acknowledged my sin unto thee, and mine iniquity have I not hid. I said, I will confess my transgressions unto the LORD; and thou forgavest the iniquity of my sin. Selah.

David writes of a man who kept his silence, who hadn't called upon the name of the Lord, who suffered in his sin — bones waxing old and dried up by his roaring in the daytime, the heaviness of his iniquity weighing on him at night. This applies to the lost, in that their conscience, if it has not been seared by reprobation, should eat at them, convicting them to bring them to make a decision about Christ. But it also applies to the saved, who are withholding confession to God and deceiving themselves about the wrong we may do.

The honesty and humility of acknowledging sin, not hiding anything from God, not remaining silent, confessing our transgressions unto the Lord, and knowing we are forgiven, is such a blessing.

1 John 1:6–9 — If we say that we have fellowship with him, and walk in darkness, we lie, and do not the truth: But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin. If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us. If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.

Sinning throughout the day and being kept up at night by our conscience, being convicted in the Spirit, gives way to the peace that passes understanding through the forgiveness that is given to us by God's mercy and grace when we own up to what we have done.

Seek the Lord While He May Be Found — Psalm 32:6

For this shall every one that is godly pray unto thee in a time when thou mayest be found: surely in the floods of great waters they shall not come nigh unto him.

David reminds us that there is a time when God may be sought, before it is too late. When it feels like we are going to be overwhelmed, like a flood, the great waters shall not come nigh unto those that trust in Him, who seek the Lord while He may be found.

Isaiah 55:6 — Seek ye the LORD while he may be found, call ye upon him while he is near.

God's Spirit will not always strive with man. There comes a time when those that put off or reject the truth, not acknowledging that they are sinners, lying to God and themselves, are no longer able to come to Him, where He no longer extends His hand, because they have hardened their hearts and will not seek Him, even as they face destruction.

Hebrews 4:7 — Again, he limiteth a certain day, saying in David, To day, after so long a time; as it is said, To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts.

We have the example of those who, in the days of Noah, mocked him for his faith in preparing the ark, and those disobedient, to whom Christ preached in Hell, were destroyed by the flood, never coming nigh unto God. Just as those left to God's wrath in the time to come will not repent, despite the horrors of His judgment.

Revelation 9:20–21 — And the rest of the men which were not killed by these plagues yet repented not of the works of their hands, that they should not worship devils, and idols of gold, and silver, and brass, and stone, and of wood: which neither can see, nor hear, nor walk: Neither repented they of their murders, nor of their sorceries, nor of their fornication, nor of their thefts.

For the lost, there is a time where God no longer extends His hand, where He gives them up and gives them over, to hold the truth in unrighteousness and believe lies, condemning themselves.

But Christians can also put off acknowledging and turning from the sin in their lives as well. We can lie to ourselves, rationalize it, and ignore the chastening of God.

Hebrews 10:26–31 — For if we sin wilfully after that we have received the knowledge of the truth, there remaineth no more sacrifice for sins, But a certain fearful looking for of judgment and fiery indignation, which shall devour the adversaries… It is a fearful thing to fall into the hands of the living God.

Now Is the Day of Salvation — The Evangelical Appeal

There is a time, and it is today. There is a day, and it is the present. If you haven't accepted the truth that we are sinners in need of salvation, and that it is offered as a free gift to all who believe, through the grace of Almighty God — now is the time.

2 Corinthians 6:2 — (For he saith, I have heard thee in a time accepted, and in the day of salvation have I succoured thee: behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation.)

Those who have heard and responded to the truth, who in the appointed day prayed unto Him, can rest assured that we are saved. But understand that God will chasten us, and can even call us home if we continue in sin.

The promises of God to His children who trust in Him and walk in His ways are a blessing. Getting right with God is its own reward.

Thou Art My Hiding Place — Psalm 32:7–9

Thou art my hiding place; thou shalt preserve me from trouble; thou shalt compass me about with songs of deliverance. Selah. I will instruct thee and teach thee in the way which thou shalt go: I will guide thee with mine eye. Be ye not as the horse, or as the mule, which have no understanding: whose mouth must be held in with bit and bridle, lest they come near unto thee.

This is the hope we have — the same hope the congregations of the children of Israel sang of in the temple of Solomon, with the songs of his father David, being compassed about by these songs of deliverance, all echoing this blessed promise, centuries before the first coming of Christ Jesus.

And God responds to His children, in this inspired Psalm, promising to teach and guide us in His ways. He asks that we come willingly, that we have the understanding to walk with Him, and not be like the horse or mule, needing a bit and bridle to pull us along. His yoke is light. The burden is easy.

Be Glad and Rejoice — Psalm 32:10–11

Many sorrows shall be to the wicked: but he that trusteth in the LORD, mercy shall compass him about. Be glad in the LORD, and rejoice, ye righteous: and shout for joy, all ye that are upright in heart.

The wicked may suffer sorrows of their own making, but we who are forgiven by His mercy — because we trust in the Lord — are preserved. What an amazing relief to know we are already forgiven; that we may come boldly before God in confession, comforted by His mercy, and sing His praises, glorifying Him.

Amen!

Deeper Study: Transgression, Sin & Iniquity

While it isn't entirely wrong to take the words transgression, sin, and iniquity and use them interchangeably, they do carry subtle differences in meaning.

Transgression

Transgression is the wilful rebellion against the truth of God and His commands. The transgression of Adam, for example — having one law to obey, and choosing of his own volition (even as Eve was deceived, Adam most assuredly knew better) — to be disobedient.

Sin

Sin, which the Bible says is the transgression of the Law, also carries with it the idea of missing the mark, falling short.

Romans 3:23 — For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God.

We aren't able, in the flesh, to hit the mark — the target being the perfect holiness of God. Only Jesus Christ did.

2 Corinthians 5:21 — For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.

Iniquity

Iniquity comes from the Hebrew word avon, meaning to twist, bend, or distort — the perverted and crooked nature of fallen man. The bent toward evil, the perverted character that drives the outward actions. It is the guile or deceit mentioned in verse 2, coupled with reflexive actions to do evil to others — the propensity to lie, to steal, to lust, to covet.

Our natural tendencies toward willful rebellion, to sin, and to lie to ourselves and others — including God — about the evil that we do.
