John 7:24 Judge not according to the appearance, but judge righteous judgment.

  • We as believers are called to judge (judge angels)
  • Not hypocritical judgement, but righteous according to God’s word

Luke 13:1-5; There were present at that season some that told him of the Galilaeans, whose blood Pilate had mingled with their sacrifices. And Jesus answering said unto them, Suppose ye that these Galilaeans were sinners above all the Galilaeans, because they suffered such things? I tell you, Nay: but, except ye repent, ye shall all likewise perish. Or those eighteen, upon whom the tower in Siloam fell, and slew them, think ye that they were sinners above all men that dwelt in Jerusalem? I tell you, Nay: but, except ye repent, ye shall all likewise perish.

  • Our nature to see all hardship of others as “karma” - Deserved
  • Sometimes we may even comfort ourselves, rationalizing our own sin because we aren’t as bad off as others are
  • This is the leaven of the Pharisees; Hypocricy

Don’t have to turn there but the story of Job (breifly summarized; Job, a righteous man, suffers immense loss—his wealth, death of his children, and his health, Job was afflicted with painful pusey sores, boils from the soles of his feet to the crown of his head— All due to Satan’s challenge to God that Job’s faith would falter under affliction. That it’s easy to be a believer when things are good, but what about in tribulation?)

  • Job's three friends; Eliphaz the Temanite, Bildad the Shuhite, and Zophar the Naamathite visit Job to comfort him
  • They sit mourning with Job for 7 days, but then one by one, end up critiquing him, asserting that his suffering must be due to grave sin; that he is experiencing divine retribution.
  • Eliphaz "Is not thy wickedness great? and thine iniquities infinite?" (Job 22:5)
  • Bildad "If thou wert pure and upright; surely now He would awake for thee, and make the habitation of thy righteousness prosperous." (Job 8:6)
  • Zophar doubles down - "Know therefore that God exacteth of thee less than thine iniquity deserveth." (Job 11:6)

With friends like these…

Finally God appears to correct the record, and God rebukes Job’s “friends”, declaring they judged wrongly, by appearances: "My wrath is kindled against thee” God says, and He instructs them to make a burnt offering and seek Job’s intercession to avoid punishment for their faulty judgment.

So we should be careful to assume anything by appearances

Don’t be a respecter of persons:

James 2:1; My brethren, have not the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Lord of glory, with respect of persons.

In other words, don’t judge people differently based on appearances.Just because someone looks rich and well off, don’t give them more respect than the poor in vile raiment.

… Hath not God chosen the poor of this world rich in faith, and heirs of the kingdom which he hath promised to them that love him? …if ye have respect to persons, ye commit sin

- Acts 10:34 Peter says God is not a respecter of persons

God commands us to be like Him, to uphold justice and compassion for the vulnerable—specifically the fatherless, widows, brokenhearted, and poor…Those who would appear the least among us.

  • Psalm 82:3-4, "Defend the poor and fatherless: do justice to the afflicted and needy. Deliver the poor and needy: rid them out of the hand of the wicked,"
  • James 1:27; “Pure religion and undefiled before God and the Father is this, To visit the fatherless and widows in their affliction, and to keep himself unspotted from the world.”

It is true that God chasten backslidden believers, He scourges every son he receives, the Bible says. Sometimes a believer in terrible straits is being punished. But there but for the grace of God go I. How merciful is the Lord when considering our own sin, that we be merciful to others when we judge their situation.

God’s grace is sufficient.

When Paul says in 2 Corinthians 12:7-10 that he was plagued by a thorn in his flesh, pleading with God three times to take it from him, to heal him, God says “My grace is sufficient for thee.” The apostle finally recognized that humbling truth.

  • We do not get all of what we truly deserve, because God is merciful and His grace abounds. We are blessed regardless of these troubles.
  • Whatever temptation or troubles we do have, that tribulation builds our faith, and our patience, so we should glory in it:
  • Romans 5:3-4: "...but we glory in tribulations also: knowing that tribulation worketh patience; And patience, experience; and experience, hope."
  • Romans 8:28: "And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose."

And so, man’s suffering can simply be a means for God to work in us, to make us conform to His will for us and is ultimately going to work for good.

Troubles in our life may also be a means of glorifying God to His purpose:

John 9:1-3; And as Jesus passed by, he saw a man which was blind from his birth. And his disciples asked him, saying, Master, who did sin, this man, or his parents, that he was born blind? Jesus answered, Neither hath this man sinned, nor his parents: but that the works of God should be made manifest in him. When Jesus heals him it sets off a chain of events that both give glory to God among the people, and stirs up hatred among those who opposed Christ.

How many poor, lame, blind, mute, crippled, even the possessed and the dead! - were healed by the mercy of Christ and their faith or the faith of others, such as parents? How much hope has that given Christians over the millenia? Were those healed by Christ from their afflictions sinners more than others in Jerusalem? Or were they suffering that the glory of God could be made manifest as a sign? As hope to us today?

Another point to consider, as the bumper sticker says - Stuff Happens

Ecclesiastes 8:14

  • "There is a vanity which is done upon the earth; that there be just men, unto whom it happeneth according to the work of the wicked; again, there be wicked men, to whom it happeneth according to the work of the righteous"
  • Sometimes the righteous suffer as if they were wicked, and vice versa - Stuff happens

Ecclesiastes 9:11

  • "I returned, and saw under the sun, that the race is not to the swift, nor the battle to the strong, neither yet bread to the wise, nor yet riches to men of understanding, nor yet favour to men of skill; but time and chance happeneth to them all."
  • Outcomes are not always tied to merit; chance affects everyone - Stuff happens

Matthew 5:45

  • "...He maketh his sun to rise on the evil and on the good, and sendeth rain on the just and on the unjust."
  • God’s blessings and trials fall on all, regardless of righteousness. - Because Stuff happens

Psalm 73:3, 12-13

  • "For I was envious at the foolish, when I saw the prosperity of the wicked… Behold, these are the ungodly, who prosper in the world; they increase in riches..."
  • The psalmist Asaph is bothered by the fact that the wicked may prosper while the righteous suffer but learns to see it as God sees it, and Asaph realizes - Stuff happens

So, in review, the Bible clearly teaches..

  • That we are to judge with righteous judgment Not according to appearances
  • We shouldn’t assume the afflicted are sinners or under divine judgment
  • God doesn’t want us to be a respecter of persons - assuming wealthy and fortunate are good and the poor of the afflicted are bad
  • That some suffering is for God’s purpose. Giving us strength for hard battles. To grow in faith. OR that He be glorified by it. To heal us or humble us. That He may use us better.
  • And some suffering is just chance, bad luck... because Stuff Happens.

Last point

All kidding aside - Just as I don’t think we should judge Lazarus by his suffering, status and situation, we shouldn’t judge other Christians based on that, OR over good faith disagreements about secondary topics where opinions can differ, like Mark and myself.

Because Mark and I agree on Christ’s atonement, the blood and eternal life, we can differ on Lazarus with love.

In Romans 14 Paul writes Who art thou that judgest another man's servant? to his own master he standeth or falleth. Yea, he shall be holden up: for God is able to make him stand.

When we are dealing with other believers, our brothers and sisters in Christ, we should be careful to judge them over minor disagreements on opinion. We should remember that all of us will stand or fall by the judgment of God.

Of course the primary doctrines of the faith are undebatable. The reality of a holy God, and sin of man, the Trinity, Christ’s eternal Sonship and the resurrection, the gift of eternal life, sealed by the Spirit…death and hell for those that reject the gospel. Differences here shouldn’t be respected. We should reject doubtful disputations on primary doctrines and hate every false way.

But there is room in much of scripture for reasonable men to come to different conclusions and have differing opinions on what God is showing us. The entire Bible is there to teach us discernment, and often things people do in scripture is there for us to practice judgment.

Some churches and friendships have split over minor disagreements. Over semantics and differences of opinion on timings of prophecy, the meaning of terms, etc. We should remember Romans 14 THAT whether we live, we live unto the Lord; and whether we die, we die unto the Lord: Whether we live therefore, or die, we are the Lord's.

Brothers and sisters should be able to respectfully disagree on things that aren’t explicitly taught in scripture. There should be room for grace regarding opinions and tertiary doctrine. I love Mark as a brother (despite his lies!) and we don’t all have to agree on how Lazarus got to the point he was in the story, because we DO agree on how he came to be held in the bosom of Abraham, being comforted by the patriarch’s embrace in paradise, in Heaven – because of his faith in the Lord.

Let us be of this mind when you may hear an opinion from a saved brother or sister that you aren’t sure you agree with. Seek understanding. Search the scriptures to see if it’s so. But don’t be personally offended- love one another. Even when they are obviously wrong like Mark is.
