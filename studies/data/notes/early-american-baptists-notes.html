Early American Baptists: Disestablishmentarianism and Believer's Baptism

Outline:

New England core figures

Obadiah Holmes

Daniel Merrill

Roger Williams

Banished from Massachusetts Bay. Argued the magistrate had no authority over the soul.

John Clarke

Imprisoned in Boston (1651). Helped secure Rhode Island’s charter guaranteeing religious freedom.

Isaac Backus

Fought Massachusetts’ tax-supported Congregational system. Key intellectual force for disestablishment.

John Leland

Pushed hard for the First Amendment. Famous for backing religious liberty for all, not just Baptists.

Hezekiah Smith

Supported liberty causes both civil and religious.

🔥 Southern / Separate Baptist movement (often jailed & harassed)

Shubal Stearns

Faced hostility for unlicensed preaching.

Daniel Marshall

Arrested in Virginia for preaching without Anglican approval.

Lewis Craig

Imprisoned for preaching; part of the “Jailed Baptist Preachers” tradition.

Elijah Craig

Also jailed in Virginia; tied to early frontier Baptist expansion.

John Waller

Arrested multiple times—famously preached through jail windows.

⚖️ What they were fighting

Tax-funded churches (especially Congregational in New England, Anglican in the South)

Licensing laws for preaching

Infant baptism as a civil/religious expectation

State enforcement of religious conformity

They argued for:

Soul liberty

Voluntary church membership

No state coercion in religion

🏛️ The “Antidisestablishmentarian” Side

These weren’t always villains—they genuinely believed religion needed state support for social order—but they were on the opposite side of the Baptist fight.

🔹 New England (Congregational establishment)

John Cotton

Defended a close church–state relationship (earlier generation, but foundational).

Increase Mather

Supported established church structures and authority.

Cotton Mather

Upheld the idea of a godly commonwealth with enforced orthodoxy.

🔹 Southern colonies (Anglican establishment)

Backed by the Church of England

Local magistrates and clergy enforced:

Preaching licenses

Parish taxes

Legal penalties for dissenters

Not always tied to one famous name, but a system of establishment:

County courts

Parish vestries

Crown-appointed governors

🔹 Political defenders of establishment (debated Baptists)

Patrick Henry

Proposed general religious tax support in Virginia (opposed by Baptists like Leland).

John Jay

Favored a more publicly Christian state structure (though not coercive like earlier systems).

⚔️ The Turning Point

The clash really peaks around:

Virginia disestablishment (1780s)

Influence on First Amendment to the United States Constitution

Baptists (especially through Leland and allies) helped ensure:

No national church, and no federal interference in religion.

🧭 Big Picture

Your guys—Holmes, Merrill, Backus, Leland—represent a consistent thread:

Willing to suffer for believer’s baptism

Refusal to submit to state church authority

Strong push for religious liberty for everyone (even those they disagreed with)

And their opponents weren’t just “bad actors”—they were defending:

Social cohesion

Moral order

A historically assumed union of church and state
