Terms and Conditions (Week 6)

Grace: The unmerited, unearned, undeserved favor of God. Mercy extended to someone by unconditional love of another. Favorable influence of God on persons, situations. (Romans 11:6 And if by grace, then is it no more of works: otherwise grace is no more grace. But if it be of works, then is it no more grace: otherwise work is no more work.)

Faith: Faith is the persuasion of mind that something is true, trustworthy, and able to be counted on, without having seen direct evidence. (Hebrews 11:1 Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen.) Belief and trust in someone’s word are the characteristics of faith. (Ephesians 1:12-14 That we should be to the praise of his glory, who first trusted in Christ. In whom ye also trusted, after that ye heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation: in whom also after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise, Which is the earnest of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased possession, unto the praise of his glory.) Every man is given a measure of faith by God.(Romans 12:3)

Repentance: A change of mind/heart when presented with new information. In the New Testament, the Greek word most commonly translated as "repent" is "metanoeo" (μετανοέω), which means "to change one's mind". The related noun, metanoia (μετάνοια), means "change of mind" or "repentance". The meaning emphasizes a shift in thinking and perspective, rather than feeling remorse or regret. God repents 14 times in the Bible. (Jonah 3:10 And God saw their works, that they turned from their evil way; and God repented of the evil, that he had said that he would do unto them; and he did it not. Acts 19:4 Then said Paul, John verily baptized with the baptism of repentance, saying unto the people, that they should believe on him which should come after him, that is, on Christ Jesus.) Sorrow at wrongdoing being pointed out can lead to repentance, but is not repentance. (2 Corinthians 7:10 For godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation not to be repented of: but the sorrow of the world worketh death.)

Trinity: Theological name for the concept of the Godhead, the three persons of God; The Father, Son and holy Spirit. (1 John 5:7 For there are three that bear record in heaven, the Father, the Word, and the Holy Ghost: and these three are one.)

Godhead: the essential being or the nature of God. All persons of the Trinity are of the Godhead. (Colossians 2:9 - For in [Jesus] dwelleth all the fulness of the Godhead bodily.)

Salvation: The deliverance from sin and its consequences, by grace through faith in Jesus Christ. It is a gift of God, not something earned by works. (Ephesians 2:8-9 For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.) It is a one-time event that, once secured, cannot be lost. (John 10:28-29 And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand. My Father, which gave them me, is greater than all; and no man is able to pluck them out of my Father's hand.) Save, saved and salvation are used for physical rescue as well, depending on the context.(Jude 1:5 I will therefore put you in remembrance, though ye once knew this, how that the Lord, having saved the people out of the land of Egypt, afterward destroyed them that believed not.)

Elect: Chosen. The saved. The sons and daughters of God. God determined that all who believe on Christ Jesus would be the elect.

Predestination: The act of God determining something to be so before it occurs. Jesus was predestined to be the lamb slain to take away the sins of the world. (Revelation 13:8 And all that dwell upon the earth shall worship him, whose names are not written in the book of life of the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world.) In terms of salvation, God predestined the means and results of salvation, not who would be saved (Romans 8:29-31 For whom he did foreknow, he also did predestinate to be conformed to the image of his Son, that he might be the firstborn among many brethren. Moreover whom he did predestinate, them he also called: and whom he called, them he also justified: and whom he justified, them he also glorified. What shall we then say to these things? If God be for us, who can be against us?, Ephesians 1:4-11 According as he hath chosen us in him before the foundation of the world, that we should be holy and without blame before him in love: Having predestinated us unto the adoption of children by Jesus Christ to himself, according to the good pleasure of his will, To the praise of the glory of his grace, wherein he hath made us accepted in the beloved. In whom we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of sins, according to the riches of his grace;

Wherein he hath abounded toward us in all wisdom and prudence; Having made known unto us the mystery of his will, according to his good pleasure which he hath purposed in himself: That in the dispensation of the fulness of times he might gather together in one all things in Christ, both which are in heaven, and which are on earth; even in him: In whom also we have obtained an inheritance, being predestinated according to the purpose of him who worketh all things after the counsel of his own will.)

Reprobate: Rejected. In the context of salvation, it refers to those who have been given over to a reprobate mind because they rejected the truth of God, and held it in unrighteousness. Conscience seared as with a hot iron, psychopaths. Filled with all unrighteousness. Sons of Belial. Twice-dead. Unable to come to the knowledge of the truth. (Romans 1:28 And even as they did not like to retain God in their knowledge, God gave them over to a reprobate mind, to do those things which are not convenient.)

Justification: The act of God by which He declares a sinner to be righteous and free from the guilt of sin. This is a legal declaration based on the righteousness of Christ imputed to the believer through faith. “Just as I’ve never sinned” (Romans 5:1 Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ: Romans 4:5 But to him that worketh not, but believeth on him that justifieth the ungodly, his faith is counted for righteousness)

Propitiation: The act of appeasing or satisfying God's holy wrath against sin. Jesus Christ, through His death on the cross, is the propitiation for our sins. (1 John 2:2 And he is the propitiation for our sins: and not for ours only, but also for the sins of the whole world.)

Sanctification: The process by which a believer is set apart from sin and made holy. This is both a lifelong process that begins at salvation and continues until the believer is redeemed at the Rapture, AND the work of the Spirit on our spirit, made clean by the blood of Christ. Our sanctification will never be perfect. The sanctification of the Spirit is perfect. (1 Thessalonians 4:3 For this is the will of God, even your sanctification, that ye should abstain from fornication: 1 Thessalonians 5:23 And the very God of peace sanctify you wholly; and I pray God your whole spirit and soul and body be preserved blameless unto the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ.)

Separation: A biblical doctrine that commands believers to be separate from sin, the world, and false doctrine. This includes personal separation in conduct and character, as well as separation from churches and organizations that do not hold to sound doctrine, and people who compel us towards sin. (2 Corinthians 6:17 Wherefore come out from among them, and be ye separate, saith the Lord, and touch not the unclean thing; and I will receive you.) Holding biblical standards in behavior, dress and attitudes. We are separated not only from the world, but separated towards the doctrines of God. (1 Peter 2:11-12 Dearly beloved, I beseech you as strangers and pilgrims, abstain from fleshly lusts, which war against the soul; Having your conversation honest among the Gentiles: that, whereas they speak against you as evildoers, they may by your good works, which they shall behold, glorify God in the day of visitation.)

Holy: Hallowed; consecrated or set apart to a sacred use, or to the service or worship of God; frequently in Scripture; such as the holy sabbath; holy oil; holy vessels; a holy nation; the holy temple; a holy priesthood. God Himself is holy, being perfect and separated from all things that aren’t holy.

Omnipotent: All powerful. A trait only found in the Godhead. (Revelation 19:6

And I heard as it were the voice of a great multitude, and as the voice of many waters, and as the voice of mighty thunderings, saying, Alleluia: for the Lord God omnipotent reigneth.)

Omniscient: All knowing. A trait only found in the Godhead. (1 John 3:20

For if our heart condemn us, God is greater than our heart, and knoweth all things.)

Disciple: A follower of someone or something. In Christianity, it is following Christ not just as a saved believer, but walking in Him, in the Spirit, and denying yourself to serve others. It is not a requirement for salvation, but a calling we all have. Not all believers are disciples. We must choose to pick up our cross and follow Him (Matthew 16:24 Then said Jesus unto his disciples, If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me.) Being a disciple has a cost associated with it (Luke 14:28-30 For which of you, intending to build a tower, sitteth not down first, and counteth the cost, whether he have sufficient to finish it? Lest haply, after he hath laid the foundation, and is not able to finish it, all that behold it begin to mock him, Saying, This man began to build, and was not able to finish.)

Apostle: Literally, a person sent by another; a messenger; envoy. A special follower of Christ, used as witnesses to His life and to spread the Gospel; generally applied to the 12 chosen for this purpose. (Mark 3:14 And he ordained twelve, that they should be with him, and that he might send them forth to preach.)

Rapture: Latin for “caught up,” a theological term for the resurrection of believers on the day of redemption, gathering of the elect. The exact timing of this event is disputed by differing interpretations of scripture surrounding the event, but the reality of it is indisputable. (Matt 24:31 And he shall send his angels with a great sound of a trumpet, and they shall gather together his elect from the four winds, from one end of heaven to the other., 1 Thessalonians 4:13-18 But I would not have you to be ignorant, brethren, concerning them which are asleep, that ye sorrow not, even as others which have no hope. For if we believe that Jesus died and rose again, even so them also which sleep in Jesus will God bring with him. For this we say unto you by the word of the Lord, that we which are alive and remain unto the coming of the Lord shall not prevent them which are asleep. For the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout, with the voice of the archangel, and with the trump of God: and the dead in Christ shall rise first: Then we which are alive and remain shall be caught up together with them in the clouds, to meet the Lord in the air: and so shall we ever be with the Lord. Wherefore comfort one another with these words.)

Eschatology: The doctrines and teachings of the End Times

Millennium: The period of time between the end of the wrath, where Satan is bound for 1,000 years in Hell, and his release to deceive the nations for a “little season.” (Revelation 20:1-3 And I saw an angel come down from heaven, having the key of the bottomless pit and a great chain in his hand. And he laid hold on the dragon, that old serpent, which is the Devil, and Satan, and bound him a thousand years. And cast him into the bottomless pit, and shut him up, and set a seal upon him, that he should deceive the nations no more, till the thousand years should be fulfilled: and after that he must be loosed a little season.) Called Premillennialism by those holding a “postmillennialism,” or “amillennialism” position.

Soteriology: The doctrines and teachings of Salvation

Exegesis: Receiving doctrine from interpretation or understanding of scripture, “Bible says it, I believe it.”

Eisegesis: Using the text to justify existing traditions or opinions, confirm biases.
