The Doctrine of the Holy Spirit - Week 9

John 16:7-12 Nevertheless I tell you the truth; It is expedient for you that I go away: for if I go not away, the Comforter will not come unto you; but if I depart, I will send him unto you. And when he is come, he will reprove the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment: Of sin, because they believe not on me; Of righteousness, because I go to my Father, and ye see me no more; Of judgment, because the prince of this world is judged. I have yet many things to say unto you, but ye cannot bear them now.

The Spirit of God, Who was in the beginning, Who inspired the prophets, fell upon the men of God and those He used throughout the Old Testament, has other roles and responsibilities in the New Testament we are under. He reproves, convicting the world of sin. That is saved or unsaved people. That little voice, our conscience. He reproves the world of righteousness, silently pointing the lost towards faith in Christ. And of judgment, showing us that the prince of this world, that is Satan, is wrong and will be destroyed.

He is the Spirit of truth. When we hear the truth, especially from the word of God, the thing that catches our attention and makes us listen is the Spirit.

He moves saved believers into the path of the unsaved that they may hear the gospel. Divine appointments. He softens hearts and opens ears. He helps lead people to salvation, if they respond.

Once we believe, He indwells us, He quickens our spirit so that we can know Him. This is regeneration. He sanctifies that new man inside us, sealing us unto redemption. The Bible says He is the earnest of our inheritance, the downpayment of God upon us, His purchased possession. He is the Spirit of adoption that lets us know we are the children of God, crying “Abba!” or “Father.” The Spirit testifies within us that we have eternal life. He guides us to all truth and lets us know truth from error. He brings to memory the word of God when we need it. He intercedes in our prayers, even when we don’t know what to pray for. He opens up knowledge to us and understanding when we read the Bible.

When we walk in the Spirit, the Bible says, we don’t fulfill the lusts of the flesh. He keeps us honest. We are His temple.

The Bible uses Spirit and Ghost interchangeably.

The nature and attributes of the Spirit The Holy Spirit is a distinct person of the Godhead, possessing all the attributes of God. He is eternal, omnipotent, omniscient, and omnipresent.

The gifts of the Spirit and the fruit of the Spirit are often discussed together, but they are different in purpose. The gifts are supernatural abilities given by God to a believer for the benefit of others and the church, such as teaching, helps, or prophecy. They are given as the Spirit wills, and not every believer has the same gifts. The fruit, on the other hand, is the character that is produced by the Spirit living in a believer's life. It is not an ability but a reflection of the Spirit's work, and all believers are commanded to cultivate these qualities. While a person may have a powerful gift, the true mark of spiritual maturity is the presence of the fruit in their life.

Fruit of the Spirit Galatians 5:22-23: But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, Meekness, temperance: against such there is no law. (The character produced by the Spirit in a believer's life)

Blasphemy of the Spirit - Matthew 12:31-32: Wherefore I say unto you, All manner of sin and blasphemy shall be forgiven unto men: but the blasphemy against the Holy Ghost shall not be forgiven unto men. And whosoever speaketh a word against the Son of man, it shall be forgiven him: but whosoever speaketh against the Holy Ghost, it shall not be forgiven him, neither in this world, neither in the world to come. (A specific sin that is unforgivable, often interpreted as the hardened rejection of the Spirit's testimony about Christ)
