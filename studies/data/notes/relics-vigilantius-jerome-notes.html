It is a heretical tradition originating with vipers.The debate between Vigilantius (c. 370–after 406 AD) and Jerome (c. 347–420 AD) is one of the clearest early flashpoints over creeping paganism and carnal traditions like relic veneration, ascetic practices, and other emerging customs in the church...Jerome’s Against Vigilantius was a defense of the practices (written in 406 AD in a single night) is the main surviving source of this debate. Vigilantius’s own writings are lost (likely destroyed by the Roman Church).Jerome, who had hosted Vigilantius in Bethlehem, wrote in reaction to an epistle by the presbyter from Gaul, which criticized the growing trends of veneration of relics, the bones and ashes of martyrs, night vigils at gravesites that had pagan cultish overtones, as well as the growing gnostic aestheticism, doctrines of devils, demanding celibacy, dietary prohibition, praying to and invoking the saints intercession, and the collection of alms by churches to send to the church at Jerusalem, which was growing in wealth while local poor were neglected.Similar to modern debates defending these then-new practices as "traditions," it came down to biblical fundamentalism being opposed by people with a financial stake in making merchandise of gullible people.Vigilantius called relic-venerators “ashmongers” (cinerarii) and idolaters for worshiping “a bit of powder wrapped up in a costly cloth,” seeing it as pagan-like under a Christian cloak. Saints’ souls are with God in heaven and have no special ongoing link to their earthly remains.Jerome's response is exactly the sort of rationalizing cope we are used to today. He said it was "honoring, not worship," similar to the "veneration!" semantics. He claimed supernatural occurrences were reported at the death vigils.
