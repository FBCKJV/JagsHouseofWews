1 Corinthians 12

Now concerning spiritual gifts, brethren, I would not have you ignorant. Ye know that ye were Gentiles, carried away unto these dumb idols, even as ye were led. Wherefore I give you to understand, that no man speaking by the Spirit of God calleth Jesus accursed: and that no man can say that Jesus is the Lord, but by the Holy Ghost. Now there are diversities of gifts, but the same Spirit. And there are differences of administrations, but the same Lord. And there are diversities of operations, but it is the same God which worketh all in all. But the manifestation of the Spirit is given to every man to profit withal. For to one is given by the Spirit the word of wisdom; to another the word of knowledge by the same Spirit; To another faith by the same Spirit; to another the gifts of healing by the same Spirit; To another the working of miracles; to another prophecy; to another discerning of spirits; to another divers kinds of tongues; to another the interpretation of tongues: But all these worketh that one and the selfsame Spirit, dividing to every man severally as he will. For as the body is one, and hath many members, and all the members of that one body, being many, are one body: so also is Christ.

At the church in Corinth, there were many disputes among the brethren that Paul needed to address for their edification, and for ours. One cause of contention among them was the distribution of the gifts of the Holy Spirit, with some envying the more flashy gifts and others lording over others for having more comely, or attractive talents given them by God.

Verse 19; And if they were all one member, where were the body? But now are they many members, yet but one body. And the eye cannot say unto the hand, I have no need of thee: nor again the head to the feet, I have no need of you. Nay, much more those members of the body, which seem to be more feeble, are necessary: And those members of the body, which we think to be less honourable, upon these we bestow more abundant honour; and our uncomely parts have more abundant comeliness. For our comely parts have no need: but God hath tempered the body together, having given more abundant honour to that part which lacked...

Paul's point; The importance of each gift being equal to another in the proper execution of the body as a whole. He goes on;

Verse 27 Now ye are the body of Christ, and members in particular. And God hath set some in the church, first apostles, secondarily prophets, thirdly teachers, after that miracles, then gifts of healings, helps, governments, diversities of tongues....

My sermon tonight is on the gift of helps. The gift of being a helper to the cause of Christ, and to the body of the local church, of which Christ is our head.

Not as flashy as some of the others mentioned. Wouldn't we all like to be healers? To speak diverse languages with ease (or miraculously through the Spirit?)

During the time of the New Testament churches, where some of the more extraordinary gifts were given for a sign, the natural human desire to be "special" must have weighed heavily on those not given to miraculous gifts.

But even now, there can be envy at those that are prophets, preachers, who are the center of attention, teachers or those who have the gift of governments, overseeing some aspect of the church's leadership.

But Paul tells us there's a more excellent way. That if we should desire and seek gifts of God, the first should be charity; to do all things for the profit of others, in love. A voluntary giving of help to others.

1 Corinthians 13:13-14:1,12

And now abideth faith, hope, charity, these three; but the greatest of these is charity....Follow after charity, and desire spiritual gifts..,Even so ye, forasmuch as ye are zealous of spiritual gifts, seek that ye may excel to the edifying of the church.

There are many early Christians who are immortalized in Paul's Epistles for their ministry in the church.

Here in 1 Corinthians 16, the Apostle calls out Stephanas (1 Corinthians 1:16; 16:15-17) – whose household was baptized by Paul, and who had addicted themselves to the ministry of the saints. What a beautiful example for us.

The list of helpers in Romans 16 is perhaps the most well known; Phebe, Priscilla, Aquila, Epaenetus, Mary, Andronicus, Junia, Amplias, Urbane, Stachys, Apelles, Tryphena, Tryphosa, Persis, Rufus. All given the gift of the Spirit of being helpers, ministering to all the saints. An example for us to follow.

As Paul writes in Ephesians 4:12, these gifts are “For the perfecting of the saints, for the work of the ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ…”

Unlike some of the gifts given as signs in the first century, the gift of helps is still given by the Spirit today. We all have it in some measure, as saved believers. The ability to put others before ourselves, and ask "Who needs help? What can I do to pitch in?" To humble ourselves, and put others needs, especially the needs of the church as a whole, before our own.

Our role model is Christ Jesus Himself. He made Himself of no reputation, and took upon Him the form of a servant, and was made in the likeness of men: And being found in fashion as a man, he humbled himself, and became obedient unto death, even the death of the cross. (Philippians 2:7-8)

The ultimate servant, who without contradiction is the Lord of lords.

When the Apostles disputed among themselves over who would be first in the Kingdom of God, Jesus told them If any man desire to be first, the same shall be last of all, and servant of all. (Mark 9:35)

Jesus gave himself as the perfect example, Mark 10:45 For even the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a ransom for many.

After washing the disciples' feet, Jesus says “If I then, your Lord and Master, have washed your feet; ye also ought to wash one another's feet. For I have given you an example, that ye should do as I have done to you.” (John 13:14-15)

Again and again, Jesus calls us to do unto the least of our brothers, and that by doing so, we are doing it unto Him.

That when we help a stranger, thereby some have entertained angels unawares. (Hebrews 13:2)

Let no man seek his own, but every man another's wealth. (1 Corinthians 10:24)

Charity, the edifying of others, caring for the poor, and the poor in spirit, serves the Lord, is pleasing to God and will be rewarded. Blessings now, and riches stored up in Heaven.

Remembering this, we should desire the gift of helps, to pray that God gives us opportunities throughout our day to be a blessing to others. To guide us in our life to better serve the Lord. To go out of our way to bring His love to someone who needs it. To be ready always to help out at church, that the pastor knows he can call on you to do something, and it will get done, cheerfully. Or that a brother or sister in need can know they can count on you in times of trouble.

And not just at home, or at church, but in the world. To let our light so shine. To be a servant to others. The Bible says seek not our own profit, but the profit of others, that they may be saved. That we, by our example, may lead them to Christ.

Acts 20:35

I have shewed you all things, how that so labouring ye ought to support the weak, and to remember the words of the Lord Jesus, how he said, It is more blessed to give than to receive.

There is no greater blessing than to be a help to others. So seek after the gift of helps.
