Haggai 1

In the second year of Darius the king, in the sixth month, in the first day of the month, came the word of the Lord by Haggai the prophet unto Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel, governor of Judah, and to Joshua the son of Josedech, the high priest, saying, Thus speaketh the Lord of hosts, saying, This people say, The time is not come, the time that the Lord's house should be built. Then came the word of the Lord by Haggai the prophet, saying, Is it time for you, O ye, to dwell in your cieled houses, and this house lie waste?

Haggai is considered one of the minor prophets, contrasted with the Major prophets by the length of their work, Haggai only consisting of 2 brief chapters. His ministry here is equally short, and takes place over just a 3 and a half month period at the end of the Babylonian exile and resulting in what we will cover tonight. He was a contemporary of Zecharaiah, and preached about a century before Malachi, the last of the OT prophets.

The pre-exile prophets, including Isaiah and Jeremiah, were given the word of the Lord to warn of the impending destruction of the Northern Kingdom of Israel, and the eventual invasion of the Southern Kingdom of Judah by Babylon.

The prophet Jeremiah had foretold that the Babylonian captivity would last 70 years, while Daniel, during the captivity recognized and reinforced this timeline, that God would eventually allow a remnant to return to Jerusalem, to keep His promise to David that a King would come from his lineage, the Messiah, and that the city where He chose to place His name, Jerusalem, would stand, setting the stage for Jesus.

Haggai the prophet brings the word of the Lord of hosts to Zerubbabel, the governor of the province, Zerubbabel, a direct descendant of David, the grandson of King Jehoiachin, the next-to-last King of Judah before him, and Joshua son of Josedak, A Levite from the line of Zadok, Solomon’s high priest.

The first message is given in the second year of the Persian King, Darius, on the first day of the sixth month. This was roughly 520 BC, again in the final year of the Babylonian captivity.

During this time, Haggai and his peer, Zechariah, were given the word of the Lord to encourage the returned Jews, getting the nation to begin the work of rebuilding the Temple that had been destroyed during Nebbuchedezer’s conquest decades earlier.

Over in Ezra 1:1-4, we would find Cyrus the Great's proclamation, declaring that the Lord God of heaven had given him all the kingdoms of the earth and that God had instructed Cyrus to rebuild the Temple in Jerusalem. Cyrus called for the Jews to go up to Jerusalem and encouraged those Jews remaining behind throughout the diaspora to support the cause financially. However after Cyrus's death, few had returned, most Jews remained scattered, and the work on the Second Temple did not progress. Only a foundation had been laid.

Then, after the passing of Cyrus the Great, and a brief period of political instability, his predecessor Darius came to power. He also supported the Jews return to Jerusalem and the rebuilding of the temple, but even with the support of these kings, the work had stalled.

Jerusalem itself had begun being rebuilt and the people there lived in “ceiled houses,” that is paneled and permanent structures. But the Temple reconstruction languished, only a foundation had been laid. and the people said, “The time is not come, the time that the Lord's house should be built” And so the prophet preached:

5 Now therefore thus saith the Lord of hosts; Consider your ways. Ye have sown much, and bring in little; ye eat, but ye have not enough; ye drink, but ye are not filled with drink; ye clothe you, but there is none warm; and he that earneth wages earneth wages to put it into a bag with holes.

The Lord tells them through the prophet, in the Spirit, that they are in famine and their efforts amount to nothing because they have not prioritized God and His Temple. They sow much and bring in little. They eat and drink, but aren’t full, they have clothing, but not enough to warm themselves. They make money and it's gone faster than they can earn it.

This can seem pretty relatable to many of us today. A nation's economy and our financial well-being can be a judgment of God and our walk with Him. And so God says to the people, Consider your ways.

Verse 7 Thus saith the Lord of hosts; Consider your ways. Go up to the mountain, and bring wood, and build the house; and I will take pleasure in it, and I will be glorified, saith the Lord.

As God says in Isaiah 1:18, Come now, and let us reason together. He tells the people their efforts come to naught because of their lack of work building His house, and their not taking advantage of the mercy He has shown them. The grace of bringing them back to Judah and Zion, to serve Him after all the tribulation, trials and travails that Judah's past sins brought on them. He calls on the people to build up His house, that He will take pleasure in it, and be glorified.

An application in the Christian life can be seen here, where many after hearing the gospel and believing on Christ, laying that foundation that only He can, and then they languish spiritually. They see little growth, maintain living in the world, continuing under the sins they’ve been forgiven of, not taking advantage of the mercy God has shown them. He tells us to consider our ways. That we sow much and bring in little, but if we go forth and build up the temple, our spiritual lives, It will please the Lord.

Haggai 2

In the seventh month, in the one and twentieth day of the month, came the word of the Lord by the prophet Haggai…

It is interesting to note that Haggai records four messages delivered over roughly 3.5 months in 520 BC, timestamped by God. The first message (1:1) came on the 1st of Elul (around August 29, coincidently enough), followed by another on the 24th of Elul (September 21), (1:15), a gap of 24 days. This third message in chapter 2 verse 1 comes on the 21st of Tishrei (October 17), about 27 days later. The final messages we will see were given on the 24th of Kislev (December 18) (2:10, 20), roughly 62 days after the third. From start to finish, these events span approximately 3 and a half months.

And so the word of the Lord came to Haggai saying, Speak now to Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel, governor of Judah, and to Joshua the son of Josedech, the high priest, and to the residue of the people, saying, Who is left among you that saw this house in her first glory? and how do ye see it now? is it not in your eyes in comparison of it as nothing?

While the people’s hearts were stirred, the work on the temple was still insufficient for the Lord, and here, God appeals to the older generation that had seen Solomon’s temple in its glory, and asking how do ye see it now? is it not in your eyes in comparison of it as nothing?

After 70 years of captivity and the destruction of the Temple, there would only be very elderly Jews who could remember the former glory of that Temple. Senior saints of the day. The work has begun, but clearly not enough was being done. He shames the elders among them, to get them to push the young to get it done.

Again, how many churches in America today have a core of senior saints who remember the glory days of their congregation, and serve as an example, pushing the younger generation to help return their church to that glory? This church is blessed to have such active elders, prayer warriors and soul-winners, serving as examples to the young who come and stay due to the infectious faith and devotion by the elders in a church that has such a great history.

He continues in verse 4 Yet now be strong, O Zerubbabel, saith the Lord; and be strong, O Joshua, son of Josedech, the high priest; and be strong, all ye people of the land, saith the Lord, and work: for I am with you, saith the Lord of hosts: According to the word that I covenanted with you when ye came out of Egypt, so my spirit remaineth among you: fear ye not. For thus saith the Lord of hosts; Yet once, it is a little while, and I will shake the heavens, and the earth, and the sea, and the dry land; And I will shake all nations, and the desire of all nations shall come: and I will fill this house with glory, saith the Lord of hosts. The silver is mine, and the gold is mine, saith the Lord of hosts.

What an encouragement to be reminded of all those things God has done for us! He says to be strong, and know that He will deliver for us. Have faith, do as He commands, and He will provide in due time, increasing our faith.

Many Christians stumble in their walk, establishing the foundation which is Christ, maybe even beginning to do what He has called them to, but then just trail off. Some worry about not having what they need to succeed in their minds, being discouraged and giving up. But it is God that began that work in you, and it is God Who will provide the materials and circumstances that we need to see it through. Be strong, all ye people of the land, saith the Lord, and work: for I am with you, saith the Lord of hosts.

He says in verse 9 The glory of this latter house shall be greater than of the former, saith the Lord of hosts: and in this place will I give peace, saith the Lord of hosts. A promise by God, Who cannot lie…applicable to our Christian lives, our nation and to the local New Testament Church.

10 In the four and twentieth day of the ninth month, in the second year of Darius, came the word of the Lord by Haggai the prophet, saying, Thus saith the Lord of hosts; Ask now the priests concerning the law, saying, If one bear holy flesh in the skirt of his garment, and with his skirt do touch bread, or pottage, or wine, or oil, or any meat, shall it be holy? And the priests answered and said, No. Then said Haggai, If one that is unclean by a dead body touch any of these, shall it be unclean? And the priests answered and said, It shall be unclean.

So we see, it is now a couple months later, Haggai in the Spirit inquires of the priests, does that which is holy touching that which is not holy, make it holy? The priests rightly answer, no.

And does that which is unclean touch that which is hole, shall it be unclean? Yes. Of course. So then that which is holy doesn't make that which is not holy clean, and that which is unclean makes even that which is holy unclean. You can't get something clean and holy out of that which is unholy and unclean by contact. A little dirt in water makes all the water dirty.

Haggai says this is the state of the people, they have no means of sanctification not having the Temple and the ordinances, to do for them what the Spirit does for believers today. And so all the work they offer is unclean.

And due to this, the Lord has made their works less profitable than they expect, less oil pressed, their crops covered with mold and disease. But God promises that even as the foundation is built upon, He will bless their efforts again.

This also has applications in the Christian life. When we are in sin, and under reproach, feeling like we are never getting ahead. We need to consider our ways. We need to cleanse ourselves, and make ourselves holy, separated unto His work. Then He will bless our efforts.

Then answered Haggai, and said, So is this people, and so is this nation before me, saith the Lord; and so is every work of their hands; and that which they offer there is unclean. And now, I pray you, consider from this day and upward, from before a stone was laid upon a stone in the temple of the Lord: Since those days were, when one came to an heap of twenty measures, there were but ten: when one came to the pressfat for to draw out fifty vessels out of the press, there were but twenty. I smote you with blasting and with mildew and with hail in all the labours of your hands; yet ye turned not to me, saith the Lord.

If you’re a gardener like me, you know that this is the season where plants start getting the blasting and mildew, mold and rot. Hail destroyed many people’s gardens up around Weymouth a few weeks ago. These things are devastating after a season of work, let alone when your very people’s survival depends on a harvest. But God promises to bless their efforts going forward.

Consider now from this day and upward, from the four and twentieth day of the ninth month, even from the day that the foundation of the Lord's temple was laid, consider it. Is the seed yet in the barn? yea, as yet the vine, and the fig tree, and the pomegranate, and the olive tree, hath not brought forth: from this day will I bless you.

God is a rewarder of those that diligently seek him.

Finally, in verse 20 And again the word of the Lord came unto Haggai in the four and twentieth day of the month, saying, Speak to Zerubbabel, governor of Judah, saying, I will shake the heavens and the earth; And I will overthrow the throne of kingdoms, and I will destroy the strength of the kingdoms of the heathen; and I will overthrow the chariots, and those that ride in them; and the horses and their riders shall come down, every one by the sword of his brother. In that day, saith the Lord of hosts, will I take thee, O Zerubbabel, my servant, the son of Shealtiel, saith the Lord, and will make thee as a signet: for I have chosen thee, saith the Lord of hosts.

The final word comes to Haggai, letting Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel, of the line of David know he is chosen, anointed to be king and of his line comes Christ Jesus.
