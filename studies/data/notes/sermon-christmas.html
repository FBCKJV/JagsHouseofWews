15 They are vanity, and the work of errors: in the time of their visitation they shall perish.

We are there in Jeremiah chapter 10, and the Lord, speaking through the prophet by the Spirit, is casting judgments upon Judah, the remaining children of Israel after the destruction of the Northern Kingdom, this time focusing on their adopting the heathen practices of idol worshiping of graven images. Wood carvings plated with molten metals, hammered into place and carried about for feasts, bowed and served, used for religious practices.

He begins by warning them not to be caught up in the signs of the sky. Astrology and the movement of the lights in the firmament, given as we read in Genesis as a way to mark time, but celestial disturbances were being used by those who worship idols, to scare people into thinking catastrophe was nigh.

Babylonians especially had a very advanced system of astrology for prophesying, and God says this is vanity. Just as the workman and the founder, cutting down trees to make idols to Baal or the Queen of Heaven are vain.

6 Those same Scrooges and Grinches will say that Christians are being tricked into worshiping pagan Gods because they choose to celebrate the birth of Christ on December 25th. I have heard some claim to know it was in the Spring, others around September, because of the shepherds being near Bethlehem. These are “educated guesses” and no more valid than late December. They will say that since the Bible doesn’t give us a day of Jesus’ birth, and because some pagan traditions held feasts during the first days of Winter, Christmas is pagan.

The winter solstice (the shortest day of the year in the Northern Hemisphere) usually falls on 21 December, and unsurprisingly, agrarian cultures would mark the time when days began to get longer again, the first promise of Spring, with a feast.

It is true that before the birth of Christ, December 25 was often a pagan holiday. The Encyclopedia Britannica says this date was a “Mithraic feast day”. We also know several other ancient cultures had some recognition of the times and season.

Evidently many new converts in pagan Europe during the first centuries of Christianity were tempted to keep the pagan feast celebrated on December 25. Possibly it was to counteract this pagan influence that Christian leaders decided to observe this day as “Christmas”, which means “Christ-sent,” hoping this would help new converts resist the temptation of partaking in the pagan feast.

Celebration of the birth of Jesus during this time isn’t wicked, we celebrate the Spring feast of Passover, which for Christians begins with the death of Christ, and His glorious resurrection 3 days later. Even though the pagans marked the same time with Spring feasts, it doesn’t turn our celebration into paganism. Or “stolen from the pagans,” as some would have it.

No one can be tricked into sin. And If the heart is blessing the day in Christ, then it is of Christ. Period.

These same people who claim that Christmas is “paganism” use the "pagan" names for days of the week and months of the year, and think nothing of it. “Thor’s day?” - really? Yet they sit in judgment against believers who seek to honor the Lord, and dictate to us that we aren't "real Christians."

7 That objection seems a little foolish in this context. Nearly all the names we have, we inherited from the heathen. Many cities, towns, counties and rivers in America have Indian names. But when we drive through Monponsett, Titicut, Manomet, or see the Nemasket or Wankinco River, we are not thinking about the Indians. When you say “I’m going fishing on Monponsett Pond” or “Meet me at the lights in Manomet,” you are speaking the same Wampanoag and Massachusett words that were used around pagan campfires 400 years ago. But nobody thinks you’re conducting a pow-wow or offering tobacco to the spirits when you say those names. The word has lost every trace of its original meaning and simply names a place or a time.
