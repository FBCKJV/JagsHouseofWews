Outline: The Biblical Doctrine of Reconciliation and Atonement

Luke 2:9-14 And, lo, the angel of the Lord came upon them, and the glory of the Lord shone round about them: and they were sore afraid. And the angel said unto them, Fear not: for, behold, I bring you good tidings of great joy, which shall be to all people. For unto you is born this day in the city of David a Saviour, which is Christ the Lord. And this shall be a sign unto you; Ye shall find the babe wrapped in swaddling clothes, lying in a manger. And suddenly there was with the angel a multitude of the heavenly host praising God, and saying, Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace, good will toward men.

I. Introduction: The Heart of the Gospel - Reconciliation Through Christ's Blood

  • The Bible presents the cross as God's great plan to make peace and restore what sin broke.
  • Atonement (covering, satisfaction for sin) is the means God uses to accomplish reconciliation (restoring harmony, ending enmity).
  • Jesus’ death and blood is the propitiation (the price needed to satisfy God)
  • 1 John 2:2 And he is the propitiation for our sins: and not for ours only, but also for the sins of the whole world.
  • Christ's death on the cross is sufficient and powerful enough to address the entire curse of sin on all creation Yet personal salvation comes by faith in His resurrection, with eternal security for the believer.
  • 1 Timothy 2:5-6 For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus; Who gave himself a ransom for all, to be testified in due time.
  • Key principle: Reconciliation is objective and cosmic in its scope and accomplishment (through His death); personal salvation/justification is applied and secured through baptism into Christ, by faith in His risen life. These are two separate concepts.
  • Without the atonement, justification would not be possible. But without justification of sinners, the atonement still has been paid.

Romans 5:10 For if, when we were enemies, we were reconciled to God by the death of his Son, much more, being reconciled, we shall be saved by his life.

II. The Scope of Reconciliation: All Things Reconciled to God

  • Christ's blood makes peace for the whole creation disrupted by sin (the Fall affected everything mankind, nature, heavenly powers).
  • Romans 5:12 Wherefore, as by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned
  • Romans 8:22-23 For we know that the whole creation groaneth and travaileth in pain together until now. And not only they, but ourselves also, which have the firstfruits of the Spirit, even we ourselves groan within ourselves, waiting for the adoption, to wit, the redemption of our body.
  • This is not a Universalist idea (the false doctrine that everyone is saved, with or without faith)
  • Atonement/Reconciliation is not synonymous with personal salvation
  • Despite reconciliation hell remains for the unsaved; and is reserved for the fallen angels.
  • It is the triumphant, objective victory where all things are brought under Christ's lordship, where He has overcome the world.
  • The cross subdues enmity, defeats powers, and prepares for the new heavens and new earth.

Colossians 1:20 And, having made peace through the blood of his cross, by him to reconcile all things unto himself; by him, I say, whether they be things in earth, or things in heaven.

Ephesians 1:10 That in the dispensation of the fulness of times he might gather together in one all things in Christ, both which are in heaven, and which are on earth; even in him.

  • Supporting cosmic impact: Creation groans under the curse but awaits deliverance (Romans 8:19-23 ); Christ triumphs over principalities and powers (Colossians 2:15 ).
  • All things are not yet subjected under Christ
  • Hebrews 2:8 Thou hast put all things in subjection under his feet. For in that he put all in subjection under him, he left nothing that is not put under him. But now we see not yet all things put under him.
  • The total subjection purchased with the atonement, takes place in the End Times, after the resurrection, and His millennial reign.
  • And once Jesus, the Alpha and Omega, the LORD of Lords and King of kings has subjected all under Him, Jesus gives it to the Father:
  • 1 Corinthians 15:28 And when all things shall be subdued unto him, then shall the Son also himself be subject unto him that put all things under him, that God may be all in all.

III. The Means of Reconciliation: By the Death of God's Son

  • God Himself initiates and accomplishes reconciliation while we were still sinners and enemies no human merit involved.
  • The atonement is substitutionary: Christ died for sins, bearing the penalty, making peace through His shed blood.
  • This objective work is finished It is finished (John 19:30 ) sufficient to reconcile the world with God from its fallen state.

Romans 5:8-10 But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us. Much more then, being now justified by his blood, we shall be saved from wrath through him. For if, when we were enemies, we were reconciled to God by the death of his Son...

2 Corinthians 5:21 For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.

IV. The Application to Believers: Personal Salvation by His Life

  • While reconciliation is accomplished by His death (objective, for enemies), believers receive personal justification, peace with God, and eternal life through union with the risen Christ.
  • 1 Timothy 4:10 For therefore we both labour and suffer reproach, because we trust in the living God, who is the Saviour of all men, specially of those that believe.
  • Salvation is by grace through faith alone once received, it is secure forever (OSAS), because Christ ever lives to make intercession, so believers are assured final salvation for those reconciled by faith.

Romans 5:1, 9-11 Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ... Much more then, being now justified by his blood, we shall be saved from wrath through him... And not only so, but we also joy in God through our Lord Jesus Christ, by whom we have now received the atonement [reconciliation].

Romans 5:10 ...much more, being reconciled, we shall be saved by his life.

Hebrews 7:25 Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.

V. The Ministry We Have Today: Proclaim the Reconciliation

  • Believers are ambassadors with the message: Be ye reconciled to God!
  • The gospel invites sinners to receive by faith what Christ has already accomplished.

2 Corinthians 5:17-20 Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new. And all things are of God, who hath reconciled us to himself by Jesus Christ, and hath given to us the ministry of reconciliation; To wit, that God was in Christ, reconciling the world unto himself, not imputing their trespasses unto them; and hath committed unto us the word of reconciliation. Now then we are ambassadors for Christ, as though God did beseech you by us: we pray you in Christ's stead, be ye reconciled to God.

VI. Conclusion: The Biblical Doctrine of Atonement Destroys The False Dichotomy of Limited/Unlimited Atonement

  • Christ's atonement is mighty: sufficient to reconcile all things through His blood (all creation’s victory over sin's curse), yet personally applied to save believers eternally by faith in His risen life.
  • Calvinism (Particular Redemption/Limited Atonement): Christ died efficiently and intentionally only for the elect (those unconditionally chosen by God before creation), securing salvation infallibly for them alone; His atonement is limited in scope and design, though sufficient in power for more if God had so decreed.
  • Arminianism (Unlimited Atonement with Conditional Application): Christ died provisionally for all mankind without exception, making salvation genuinely available to every person; the atonement is unlimited in scope but limited in efficacy—applied only to those whom God foreknew would believe—thus election is conditional upon foreseen faith rather than sovereign decree alone.
  • Both Calvinism and Arminianism misunderstand the atonement by confining it primarily to the question of individual salvation ("for whom did Christ die?").
  • – Calvinism limits its scope and power to the arbitrarily elect.
  • – Arminianism, while claiming unlimited provision of the atonement, still ties its efficacy to foreseen individual faith, making election conditional rather than sovereign decree.
  • Both systems reduce the cross to an individualistic framework and miss its grand biblical purpose: the cosmic, triumphant reconciliation of all things—ending enmity, defeating powers, and restoring creation through the blood of the cross
  • Two sides of a heretical coin - Arminianism essentially keeps the same Calvinistic framework (unconditional election based on God's decree) but swaps "arbitrary decree" for "foreseen faith"—so the atonement still ends up functionally limited in scope to the foreseen elect, just determined differently.
  • Scripture presents a finished, objective victory already accomplished, rendering the debate moot.
  • 2 Peter 3:9 The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.
  • Hebrews 2:9 But we see Jesus, who was made a little lower than the angels for the suffering of death, crowned with glory and honour; that he by the grace of God should taste death for every man.
  • Scripture shows a finished, powerful work that glorifies God and is the power to secure the saved.
  • Christ died for all sinners, rose for our justification, and keeps us forever by the power of God.
  • John 3:16 For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.
