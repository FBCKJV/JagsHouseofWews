Beyond TULIP: A Biblical Rejection of Calvinist/Arminian Premises

Series Overview (4–6 lessons)

Goal: Show that the entire TULIP debate rests on faulty premises that Scripture does not require us to accept. The Bible presents a straightforward gospel of grace through faith alone, with a finished atonement sufficient for all, applied to whosoever believes, and eternally secured by the power of God.

1. Lesson 1: The Wrong Question – Why the Limited vs. Unlimited Debate Misses the Point

- Both Calvinism and Arminianism confine the atonement to the question of individual election/salvation.

- Scripture presents a cosmic, triumphant reconciliation already accomplished (Col. 1:20; 2 Cor. 5:19), rendering the debate moot.

- Key: Christ’s work is bigger than either system allows.

2. Lesson 2: Total Depravity – Biblical Truth Without Calvinistic Inability

- Affirm: Man is dead in sins, none seek God, totally affected by sin (Rom. 3:10-23; Eph. 2:1).

- Reject the premise: This does not mean moral inability to believe the gospel when convicted by the Spirit through the Word (John 16:8; Rom. 10:17; Heb. 4:12; John 1:9; Rom. 1:16).

- The gospel is the power of God unto salvation—the Word quickens, the Spirit convicts all who hear, and whosoever will may come.

3. Lesson 3: Unconditional Election – The Biblical Doctrine of Election is Not About Individual Salvation

- Election in Scripture is primarily corporate (in Christ), to service, holiness, and inheritance (Eph. 1:4-5; Rom. 8:29-30 understood as conformity to Christ).

- Reject the premise that God arbitrarily chooses some for salvation and passes over others before creation.

- God desires all to be saved (1 Tim. 2:4; 2 Pet. 3:9); the gospel is a genuine offer to all.

4. Lesson 4: Irresistible Grace vs. the Resistible Drawing of the Spirit Through the Word

- The Spirit convicts the world (John 16:8), but men can resist (Acts 7:51; Rom. 1:18-28).

- Grace is not an internal overpowering but the external provision of Christ and the gospel call—effective when believed, resisted when rejected.

- Faith comes by hearing; the power is in the message, not a secret inward work only on the elect.

5. Lesson 5: Perseverance of the Saints – The Biblical Truth vs. the Calvinist/Arminian Counterfeit

- Calvinist "P" teaches that true believers will persevere in holiness/works as evidence of election—often leading to doubt and introspection.

- Arminians accept the same premise (real saints must endure in faith/works) but conclude salvation can be lost—also breeding fear.

- Biblical truth: Once a person believes, God keeps them eternally secure by His power (John 10:28-29; Heb. 7:25; Rom. 8:38-39; Eph. 1:13-14).

- Passages about "enduring to the end" (Matt. 24:13; Rev. 2–3) refer to physical deliverance through tribulation/persecution for kingdom inheritance, not eternal salvation.

- True believers are sealed and kept forever; fruit follows naturally but is not the root of assurance. No back-door works salvation.

6. Conclusion Lesson: Once Saved, Always Saved – The Simple Biblical Gospel

- Salvation is by grace through faith alone, not of works (Eph. 2:8-9).

- Eternal life is a present possession the moment one believes (John 5:24; 6:47).

- God finishes what He starts (Phil. 1:6); Christ ever lives to intercede.

- Call to rest in the finished work, rejoice in assurance, and proclaim the gospel freely to all.
