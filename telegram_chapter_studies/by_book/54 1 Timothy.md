Today we begin the series called "the Pastoral Epistles," Paul's letters to Timothy and Titus, respectively, giving them doctrine on planting churches and the only two biblical church offices; Bishop and Deacon. Bishop being the pastor, or head of a local independent NT church, and decon being an assistive role, aiding the pastor in the administration of the church. 

Here Paul writes to Timothy, his "son" in Christ, whom the last Apostle had won to the Lord, and who he is asking to remain in Ephesus, planting churches. 

The doctrines he teaches are instrumental in how God wishes to see our local churches established and run. In their roles as evangelist, Timothy and Titus are instrumental in passing on this instruction to those called to the offices. 

Most denominations ignore this teaching, entirely. The very notion of denomination, central control, unqualified priests, unbiblical hierarchies and the like, spreading bad doctrine by decree. 

Even OIFB churches, who as Baptists recognize the two offices doctrine, have abandoned the traditional training of future pastors, evangelists and church planting and embraced the unbiblical Bible College racket, siphoning off potential men of God and other young people, to feed them into a collegiate system that does more to promote and spread heresy than anything else. 

God's plan for churches is to train up future leaders, be pastor-led, and to go out soul winning, evangelizing a receptive community, and eventually planting a church and sending off a pastor, who then repeats the process. 

Some pastors grew so far and fast, they took pride in establishment of their Christian schools and Bible colleges, and for many, it was their eventual downfall in their service to the Lord.

So, as we wade into the doctrines laid out here, we see that Timothy is charged with instructing those he finds called to the office of bishop, or pastor as we call it in modern times (terms that come from overseeing a flock of sheep), that they give no heed to fables and genealogies that lead to foolish questions, and don't edify the congregation. This is similarly given to Titus, avoiding Jewish fables and endless genealogies to instruct the future leaders of these churches. 

At the time of this writing, many of the apocryphal books had been in circulation, non-inspired and many heretical concepts, being used by Jews and similarly creeping in to the new Christian churches. 

The ramifications of rejecting the teachings of the pastoral epistles can still be felt today, with apostate false church of Rome, which uses unqualified men in unbiblical positions and uses the apocrypha, Jewish fables, to create doctrines. Not just the fables of the already existing books during Paul's day, such as using Maccabees to create the lie of purgatory,  but false gospels of Thomas and Andrew, for example, which they've adopted their Mary-worship from. 

Of course we have the genealogies attempting to paint "seeds of Abraham" as special, continued in dispensationalist heresy today. 

Paul continues to say that, in instruction of these leaders to also stress the importance of *charity out of a pure heart, and of a good conscience, and of faith unfeigned.* The gracious love for others that comes from having a good conscience before God, being blameless and regenerate in the faith of Christ. Unfeigned, sincere. While all saved have reconciliation with God regarding their sin, it is a spiritual maturity that brings one to this gracious and merciful charitable love for others. 

There are some, Paul informs us, that having neglected this trait, and professing themselves wise, have become fools. Vain jangling. Just pontificating on the matters of the law, and works, and neglecting the faith, and forgiveness and grace. Many such cases, even today. 

Paul does caution that the law of God *is good,* in itself, for its purpose, which he lays out here. His admonition is against the legalistic works-based false teachers, not the law itself, which we who are saved have liberty from, but that the law continues to serve as a schoolmaster, and is *for the lawless and disobedient, for the ungodly and for sinners, for unholy and profane, for murderers of fathers and murderers of mothers, for manslayers, For whoremongers, for them that defile themselves with mankind, for menstealers, for liars, for perjured persons, and if there be any other thing that is contrary to sound doctrine.* To bring them to the knowledge of the Gospel, or at the very least, to punish wrongdoing on earth and to keep an orderly society, understanding that the majority of people are not saved. 

To many people, both believers and naturally, unbelievers, see no benefit or roles in God's law being used today. "That was for those times. Not for today." But, as we see from this list of sins, there is a clear need to maintain God's law regardless of what current year is. 

Whoremongers and whores, who defile themselves with mankind, murderers, liars and every other thing that is contrary to good teaching from scripture. It's a broad list, and we understand why there remains a need to enforce these things. 

There is an element of libertarian Christian that doesn't just reject the Levitical laws and carnal ordinances, done away with in Christ, but see no need to enforce the laws of God, written in our hearts and proclaimed from Adam on by God. They take their liberty for license to sin, and will not support the role of government and the law to protect people from evil and bring people to the understanding that there is sin and punishment, which is key to coming to repentance and accepting salvation.

Paul uses himself as an example of this, that being a chief of sinners, who did evil against believers, was so changed by God and the gospel of reconciliation in Jesus, that has made him effective in the ministry he was appointed to, and the glory of God it has allowed him. 

He encourages Timothy with this, and warns against those who, having been put away from the faith, who are reprobate, two of whom Paul names, infamous for their attempts to shipwreck, to lead to ruin, the faith of others. *Hymenaeus and Alexander; whom I have delivered unto Satan, that they may learn not to blaspheme.*

Paul, having laid out his instruction to Timothy, and his role in establishment of churches of the Ephesians, and giving some basic commands in that regard, and a warning against reprobate false prophets, moves therefore to spell out a broader doctrine that applies to all Christians. It is how to develop the men to have the temperament that will produce those fit for the office and called to that ministry, and how to edify the church, bringing in more believers. 

The first point, praying for all men, supplications, or asking of God for favor, thanksgiving for the blessings He bestows, intercessions that the Spirit may work to bring about outcomes that give glory to God. 

This is our instruction for the rulers of this world as much as the beggars, that we may live peaceably. Not given to fear or wrath. This causes many to bristle, but as much as possible we are to live peaceably and without offense, with all men, so we may in all godliness and honesty, bring others to the truth. 

*For this is good and acceptable in the sight of God our Saviour; Who will have all men to be saved, and to come unto the knowledge of the truth. For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus;*

We are to be a testimony to the truth of this. That our prayers and faith be lived out in a way that brings others to this truth. Doubt and wrath show a lack of faith and offends others from the truth. Simple as. 


The spiralling people do when it comes to the cares of this world, or their fearful outbursts show a lack of faith in the same God who they are supposed to be magnifying and giving glory to, as witness to others. 

Note that Jesus is our sole mediator for intercession with God. Not spirits of men. Not idols or preists. When we pray for others it is asking Jesus to intercede, God in His name to bless, the holy Spirit to give comfort. 

Also, the Bible says the will of God our savior that ALL MEN come to believe. It isn't a select few as Calvinism would claim, and it is our calling to help make God's will manifest. 

So men should be holy, in prayer, without fear, peaceable, without doubt and wrath. Praying for all men to come to the truth, even for the government that God may grant us peace, allowing more to be saved. 

In a similar manner, women have a role as well. Just as men who are unruly and fearful offer a bad testimony so to do women who dress immodestly, who are ostentatious in their appearance. Not only because of the obvious temptations, but as speaking to their character as Godly women. The two admonitions, one for men and one for women, have the same root context. That we show outward signs of the faith that is saved us to better bring others to salvation. 

It's often the men who are most fearful and wrathful, who have the loudest voice in pointing out to women not to dress shamefully. In this regard they are hypocrites. Women should do that which is pleasing to God, but so should men, we have an equal role in winning souls to Christ. 

Wear those rolls differ, the Bible says women aren't to teach, because, like eve, they are prone to being deceived regarding God's word. This is just the truth of it, and it is a sentiment found throughout the Epistles, and borne out by experience. This is not to excuse Adam and condemn eve, but rather just clarifying rules and natural tendencies to better demonstrate God's will. 

Paul goes on to say that it is in bearing children, and raising them up in a Godly way, that women have the greater influence than men leading the church. That they are saved, preserved and protected, by the generations of children that they bring into the world and give witness to God to, that they may be His. 

Too many cherry pick these verses and create an artificial men versus women divide. It is about roles and responsibilities, understanding there are differences between men and women. And we have different deficiencies and negative tendencies that can trip us up in our walk with God.

Now, having laid a baseline for general behavior that should be encouraged in the newly planted churches, to better bring about the type of men to be trusted with keeping watch over the flock, Paul moves on to describe the basic qualities required for the two offices the Bible provides us; Bishop and Deacon. 

Note these aren't preists, or a honorific for high preists, the way the false denominations use the term "bishop," as we know all saints are part of the royal preisthood of believers. Rather, this is specifically about pastors, leading the flock and deacons who help him minister. 

If you recall, deacon was the position created by the Apostles at the churches in Jerusalem back in Acts 6:

*Then the twelve called the multitude of the disciples unto them, and said, It is not reason that we should leave the word of God, and serve tables. Wherefore, brethren, look ye out among you seven men of honest report, full of the Holy Ghost and wisdom, whom we may appoint over this business. But we will give ourselves continually to prayer, and to the ministry of the word. And the saying pleased the whole multitude: and they chose Stephen, a man full of faith and of the Holy Ghost, and Philip, and Prochorus, and Nicanor, and Timon, and Parmenas, and Nicolas a proselyte of Antioch: whom they set before the apostles: and when they had prayed, they laid their hands on them. And the word of God increased; and the number of the disciples multiplied in Jerusalem greatly;*

We see in this that the churches are to be led by those ministering the Gospel, and the day to day management left to the deacons who work for them. 

Many modern churches are deacon-led, and this has caused innumerable problems, politicizing a church. They should be Christ-led, pastor in charge, deacons serving the pastor, as the Bible lays out. 

And here we come to the qualifications for each office, and see they are basically the same. Married, to one wife. With orderly children and household. In all gravity, meaning he takes this first role as husband and father  seriously. Many churches have failed because their pastor, who may be a godly man, with a good wife and seemingly orderly house, allowed sin and disorder into their homes, which in turn affected the church, brining many to ruin. 

Not this isn't saying he, his wife or kids must be perfect, but that the pastors and deacons be in control at home. This could mean a child act up, and being sufficiently punished, or even put out. Rather than their sins covered up and allowed due to the pastor or deacon's position. Many such cases. 

Sober men. Not given to wine, but full of hospitality for others. Not greedy. Not a striker, not a brawler. Not greedy for filthy lucre, money earned by dishonest means, and selling of favor from his position. Not covetous. Not a novice, who may be filled with pride at his newfound position of power.

So elders that serve the congregation faithfully should receive double honor, double payment for their efforts. Rewarded financially for their time. 

And accusations against them should be handled with respect, the same manner of which Jesus laid out, requiring witnesses and reproached privately, but publicly shamed and cast out if they don't repent. 

Far too many churches only do the first half, and then often will defer to the elder and their profession of innocence. It is sadly rare that they be openly shamed and cast out for their indiscretions and sin. Especially sexual immorality. The topic can be embarrassing for all involved, and the older man's excuses taken with more veracity than the younger girls (or boys) accusations. There is often fear of the entire congregation being smeared, and excuses and rationalizations made. This is the downfall to many churches, especially those that have drifted from biblical fundamentalism, and grown lukewarm. 

By not being respecters of persons in these matters, and worrying less about our own appearances, and more about doing what's right, an active lively church can avoid these pitfalls.

If your church has an elder or any member, for that matter, who engages in sinful activities, especially fornication, adultery, and taking advantage of their position against young ones, they need to be put out, and put out publicly, and police involved if necessary. 

There is no excuse. Jesus will remove the candlestick of a church that does this. The hedge of protection will be gone.

Paul charges these things to Timothy before God. This is a command as to how he should behave and what he must teach to the churches he is planting. It's a sacred duty. We have the same duty to our own churches, to maintain these standards and practices. To exercise pure religion, undefiled. 

He orders Timothy to not lay hands suddenly on no man. This may be an admonition against brawling, but more likely in this context, he means ordination with the Holy Ghost.

The final chapter in 1st Timothy, and it is building on the theme of being able to give a good testimony for Christ to others by living like you have the gift of eternal life, not stuck in the cares of this world but gowing in spirit.

The admonition to obey earthly authority, to please God and make His glory seen of man that you may give a good witness is repeated throughout the New Testament, from Jesus rendering unto Caesar, to Romans 13 and 1 Peter 2, and elsewhere. This is one of the hardest doctrines for most American Christians today, spoiled by the supposed promises of "Liberty" from the Constitution, and missing the liberty we have in Christ. 

We bristle with pride at the idea of being good servants at work, living agreeably with all men as much as possible, paying taxes, tribute to whom tribute is due, custom to whom custom. Honor to parents and elders. This doctrine is seen as being a "Christ cuck." Seen as weak by those lacking understanding. Missing that while we defer to authority, we have roles of authority in God's hierarchy ourselves, and that we are instructed to always place God and His word as our final authority.  

Often those who cry loudest about supposed persecution and calls for riot and seditions, have abandoned their own roles, or are derelict in their own responsibilities. They care more for their comfort than for their testimony. They live in fear rather than faith. They see wrath as theirs, not God's. 

They, not being satisfied with what God has given them, and impatient with His longsuffering, are unable to bring others to believe and so, shoot themselves in the foot and worsen their own condition. 

We have one means to lead a nation back from wickedness, and that is the Gospel. Soul winning. Revival. And that requires our being good witnesses to the power of the Spirit and faith that God, who is holy and just, and will keep His promise and restore us.

Paul is writing to Timothy who, living in Macedonia, a slave/caste society, will have gotten both masters and slaves saved, and in the same congregation. We are blessed not to be living in a society that openly enslaves people. But the same admonition applies to us as wage-slaves.
