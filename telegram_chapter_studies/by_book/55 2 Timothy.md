**2 Timothy 1**

*Paul, an apostle of Jesus Christ by the will of God, according to the promise of life which is in Christ Jesus,*

*2 To Timothy, my dearly beloved son: Grace, mercy, and peace, from God the Father and Christ Jesus our Lord.*

*3 I thank God, whom I serve from my forefathers with pure conscience, that without ceasing I have remembrance of thee in my prayers night and day;*

*4 Greatly desiring to see thee, being mindful of thy tears, that I may be filled with joy;*

*5 When I call to remembrance the unfeigned faith that is in thee, which dwelt first in thy grandmother Lois, and thy mother Eunice; and I am persuaded that in thee also.*

*6 Wherefore I put thee in remembrance that thou stir up the gift of God, which is in thee by the putting on of my hands.*

*7 For God hath not given us the spirit of fear; but of power, and of love, and of a sound mind.*

*8 Be not thou therefore ashamed of the testimony of our Lord, nor of me his prisoner: but be thou partaker of the afflictions of the gospel according to the power of God;*

*9 Who hath saved us, and called us with an holy calling, not according to our works, but according to his own purpose and grace, which was given us in Christ Jesus before the world began,*

*10 But is now made manifest by the appearing of our Saviour Jesus Christ, who hath abolished death, and hath brought life and immortality to light through the gospel:*

*11 Whereunto I am appointed a preacher, and an apostle, and a teacher of the Gentiles.*

*12 For the which cause I also suffer these things: nevertheless I am not ashamed: for I know whom I have believed, and am persuaded that he is able to keep that which I have committed unto him against that day.*

*13 Hold fast the form of sound words, which thou hast heard of me, in faith and love which is in Christ Jesus.*

*14 That good thing which was committed unto thee keep by the Holy Ghost which dwelleth in us.*

*15 This thou knowest, that all they which are in Asia be turned away from me; of whom are Phygellus and Hermogenes.*

*16 The Lord give mercy unto the house of Onesiphorus; for he oft refreshed me, and was not ashamed of my chain:*

*17 But, when he was in Rome, he sought me out very diligently, and found me.*

*18 The Lord grant unto him that he may find mercy of the Lord in that day: and in how many things he ministered unto me at Ephesus, thou knowest very well.*

The introduction to this letter to Timothy, which Paul is writing from Rome, under house arrest, chained to a centurion day and night. We learn that Timothy got saved following his grandmother and mother to Christ, and was given the gifts of the Holy Ghost by the laying on of hands by Paul, an Apostle indeed. 

In his bonds, Paul shares that it is memories of things like Timothy's accepting of the Gospel that bring joy to him while in his predicament, and to encourage Timothy and others who may read this epistle to be of good cheer, hopeful in Christ and not ashamed of Paul's status. 

*For God hath not given us the spirit of fear; but of power, and of love, and of a sound mind.*

He goes on to embolden Timothy, not to be ashamed of the Gospel, to lean on the power of the Holy Ghost upon him. To be a partaker of the persecution Paul suffers, willingly, because it is to the glory of God and the furtherance of the Gospel. 

Paul says to Timothy that God has called them, *called us with an holy calling, not according to our works, but according to his own purpose and grace, which was given us in Christ Jesus before the world began,* *But is now made manifest by the appearing of our Saviour Jesus Christ*.

Note it is the predestination of Jesus Christ, not of the believer, that brings us to be used by God for His purpose, now that Christ was made manifest in Jesus, who abolished death and revealed immortality through the good news. The Gospel. 

This is how predestination works. Foreknowledge by God, predestinated in Christ, fufifiled in our belief, conformed to His use. 

*For the which cause I also suffer these things: nevertheless I am not ashamed: for I know whom I have believed, and am persuaded that he is able to keep that which I have committed unto him against that day.*

Blessed assurance. We hold the ernest of His promise in our quickened spirit, the Holy Ghost indwelling within. Even in his persecution and humiliation, Paul knows the victory already won. He will not be ashamed for having placed his faith in the living God. 

**Romans 10:11**
*For the scripture saith, Whosoever believeth on him shall not be ashamed*.

Paul needs to write this encouraging word because of the betrayal of some in the faith, who having regarded his predicament, abandoned Paul. Not speaking out for him, and in fact turned away from the Apostle. Phygellus and Hermogenes are named in scripture, to their everlasting shame. 

But, not being a downer, Paul also gives praise to Onesiphorus for seeking him out and refreshing him in Paul's afflictions. Mercy and honor from God for being unashamed of the faith during tribulation. 

We should pray we can be like Paul, or at the very least Onesiphorus, when tribulations come.

Paul continues his impassioned appeal to his young charge Timothy, written near the end of his life, from chains. Under house arrest in Rome. Unsure of his future but confident in his fate, Paul seeks to put his son in Christ firmly on the rock of the gospel, and in chapter 1 had recounted that many in Macedonia that he'd won to Christ had turned their backs on him in his tribulation, and turned from the truth of the gospel. 

Paul charges Timothy to be stedfast in the faith that won him, unashamed of Christ and the promise of God. Holding fast to the scriptures.

He begs Timothy to be strong in the faith that Paul had won him by, with many witnesses, that is others who believed and were born again, those saved by the word, so as to be able to teach it to others. 

To endure hardness like a solider, as he's been called by God to be. Not entangled with any other cause or cares, but the one he's been drafted to. 

To strive for mastery, like an athlete. Earning crowns for his victories, without cheating, but with excellence. 

A husbandman, enjoying the fruits of his labor, souls won to Christ. 

He then impresses upon Timothy these doctrines that he feels are most important, beginning with the truth; *Jesus Christ of the seed of David was raised from the dead.* For preaching this is what brought Paul to be in bonds. Scourged and stoned. Now facing the end of his race, he says he's endured all these things for the elects' sake. For the souls won by the same Gospel he's been persecuted for. That it was worth it all for their sake, that they may obtain salvation in Jesus Christ. 

(Note that he is saying they believed the preaching to be elect, and that he endured that they may have believed. Not that they were going to no matter what. Not that they were picked to believe, but having believed, become elected to the promise. This is not calvinism, but how the truth of the good news and the light of the Holy Spirit works in men to bring them to repentance and towards faith in God. That we must endure tribulation to share, that all men might be saved.)

Paul continues. This is a faithful saying, that he charges Timothy to teach others;

*For if we be dead with him, we shall also live with him: If we suffer, we shall also reign with him: if we deny him, he also will deny us: If we believe not, yet he abideth faithful: he cannot deny himself.*

He writes that the vain babblings and striving, arguments about the law and doctrines not of scripture serve no other purpose but to subvert those who hear them. To keep people from the faith and salvation. It continues the theme of his previous letter to Timothy, about instructing church leaders at Ephesus to be first and foremost good witnesses to the word that men might be saved. Here too, he is seeking to reinforce this idea, especially where there are those like Hymenaeus and Philetus, teaching false doctrines and damnable heresies such as the resurrection having already been past. (Hymenaeus is the same heretic who Paul said he had given over to Satan for his blasphemy in 1 Timothy 1:20)

Like Phygellus and Hermogenes, who ashamed of Paul's bonds, abandoned him and the calling, weakening the power of the Gospel for those who witnessed their shame and fear. 

These false teachers and weak leaders, subverting and deserting, are like cankers. Their striving and arguments like sores that fester and eat away at the word, leading men to damnation. 

Instead he charges Timothy to study to be approved by God rightly dividing the word that he may be able to stablish the faith of many, where these heretics overthrow the faith of some. 

*Nevertheless the foundation of God standeth sure, having this seal, The Lord knoweth them that are his. And, let every one that nameth the name of Christ depart from iniquity.*

Again, God knows who are His, but he lets, allows, every one that calls upon the name of Christ be saved.

And being His, we are as vessels in His great house. Some earthen and wood. Carnal Christians. Who, while believing and part of the Kingdom, in the great house so to speak, but not being sanctified, are less suited to His purpose. He says not to be like they are, but to sanctify yourself that you become as the vessels made of gold and silver, meet for His use. For His good works. 

This dispels the false doctrine and damnable heresy of Lordship/perseverance of the saints. We are all saved who believe. And some will sanctify themselves to honor, becoming more useful to God. Others won't be more than earthen pots, and dishonored. 

But all are saved. 

We should strive for gold and silver. For crowns. For mastery. To be a soldier for God. Enduring hardship for the sake of those who may believe and be saved.

And how does one sanctify themselves? How can we as believers be more than a wooden or clay pot, but become tools for God's glory?

*Flee also youthful lusts: but follow righteousness, faith, charity, peace, with them that call on the Lord out of a pure heart.* 

*But foolish and unlearned questions avoid, knowing that they do gender strifes.*

*And the servant of the Lord must not strive; but be gentle unto all men, apt to teach, patient, In meekness instructing those that oppose themselves; if God peradventure will give them repentance to the acknowledging of the truth;*
*And that they may recover themselves out of the snare of the devil, who are taken captive by him at his will*.

We study His word. We know the answers to good questions that we may give meek and ready reason for the hope we have. Being patient. Charitable, loving people with the same grace shown us by God. Not striving, or argumentive, but gentle that those who are in sin come to the truth, and be saved. 

This is the meat of the word. It is next level stuff, not easily obtained, but that we must seek to be further transformed for the master's use. Praying for the wisdom and faith to grow in discipleship. Letting go of the cares of this world, that we may live in honor and glory in the next.

I'm not crying you are crying. 

Paul, having come close to the end of his life, having written to Timothy, his son in Christ whom he led to salvation, grew in the faith, laid hands on for the gifts of the Holy Ghost and sent out into Asia Minor to evangelize and spread the gospel, planting churches, charging his protege with teaching the fundamentallist doctrines of God, reaches his summation. A poignant and inspiring end.

Paul charges Timothy on God, in the name of Jesus, who will judge the quick and the dead, the living and the damned at His coming, to please be faithful to the doctrines he's left him. 

*Preach the word; be instant in season, out of season; reprove, rebuke, exhort with all long suffering and doctrine.*

The word of God must be preached, we must be ready with scripture, when it's popular and when it isn't, especially. For we must be, as Timothy, ready to use all scripture to teach, strengthen and correct those seeking truth. To whom God has called to us in the Holy Spirit. 

And this cause is great because, as Paul warns, people will wax cold to the truth, and turn towards fables and false doctrines that satisfy their itching ears, that justify and rationalize their sin, rather than lead them to the truth. So, as a stalwart in the faith, armed with the word, we, like Timothy must endure, for the sake of the elect that they may know the grace of God and His salvation. 

And we understand Paul's plea, that he facing uncertainty, and his impending death in this life, seeks to ensure the tradition of the Gospel lives on in his "son."

*For I am now ready to be offered, and the time of my departure is at hand. I have fought a good fight, I have finished my course, I have kept the faith:*

*Henceforth there is laid up for me a crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the righteous judge, shall give me at that day: and not to me only, but unto all them also that love his appearing.*

This is the crown we should all strive for, the reward of those who work towards His coming. 

The Bible says; *Lay not up for yourselves treasures upon earth, where moth. and rust doth corrupt, and where thieves break through and steal: But lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven.*

For Jesus will judge the quick, as well as the dead. We who believe and are given the gift of eternal life will see our works judged;

*Every man's work shall be made manifest: for the day shall declare it, because it shall be revealed by fire; and the fire shall try every man's work of what sort it is.*  *If any man's work abide which he hath built thereupon, he shall receive a reward. If any man's work shall be burned, he shall suffer loss: but he himself shall be saved; yet so as by fire.*

As Paul says, *Every man that striveth for the mastery is temperate in all things. Now they do it to obtain a corruptible crown; but we an incorruptible.*

And in Paul's striving for mastery, his running a good race, having fought the good fight, he knows with certainty as his end approaches, with that blessed assurance of the Spirit testifying within him, by the word that he has his hope in, that he will be rewarded by God as promised. For God is not slack regarding His promises. He is faithful in all things. Amen

In the final verses, we see Paul is with Luke, author of that Gospel and Acts. He asks Timothy to make haste to come see him in Rome, asking him to bring Mark, who also wrote one of the four canonical Gospels. 

(it's interesting that he states Mark is useful for it was Mark ducking out of the first missionary trip with Paul and his uncle Barnabas, that caused the split between them later. Mark has redeemed himself in Paul's eyes, clearly)

Prisca and Aquila, still doing great works for the Lord. Others, like Alexander the coppersmith, not so much. Paul lays it out there, good and bad, of those Timothy should salute, and those he cannot trust, having been shown what manner of men they are. Paul's warnings and salutations, his unwavering faith in God, and his personal requests for his cloak, his books, his parchments (possibly sources for Luke and Mark to draw on as they wrote their Gospels), are all touching in the context of his plight.  

Paul never stopped trusting God; *the Lord shall deliver me from every evil work, and will preserve me unto his heavenly kingdom: to whom be glory for ever and ever. Amen.*
