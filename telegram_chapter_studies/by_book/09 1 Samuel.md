Beginning the book of **1 Samuel**, we are given the origin of the prophet. His father, a Levite originally from Bethlehem (also known as an Ephrathite), named Elkanah is introduced. Having two wives, Hannah, who he loved and who was without child, and Peninnah, who had children by him, and who was the adversary of Hannah, who provoked her for being barren. 

Being devout, Elkanah took his family to the Tabernacle in Shiloh to offer sacrifices, during the time of Eli as High Priest, which was during the life of Samson the judge. Eli had two sons serving as priests under him, Hophni and Phinehas. 

And so, setting herself before the Lord during this pilgrimage, Hannah weeping asks the Lord to open her womb and bear her a son, vowing to dedicate him to the Lord from birth, never to see a razor taken to his head. 

Eli witnessing her silent prayers, seeing her lips move, presumed she was drunk. But she says;

*No, my lord, I am a woman of a sorrowful spirit: I have drunk neither wine nor strong drink, but have poured out my soul before the Lord. Count not thine handmaid for a daughter of Belial: for out of the abundance of my complaint and grief have I spoken hitherto.*

Eli answers that she may go in peace and the God of Israel may grant her her petition. 

They returned home, and Elkanah knew his wife, and the Lord remembered her and opened her womb, giving her a son. She names him Samuel, because he was asked of the Lord. 

And she kept him at home until Samuel had weaned, and then took him to Shiloh and made an offering to the Lord in thanksgiving, bringing the child to Eli. There she reminds the High Priest of her prayer and it being answered in her son, and her vow that he shall be lent to the Lord all his life.

Chapter 4 of **1st Samuel** picks up immediately from chapter 3, and Samuel was known throughout all Israel. 

But this is during the time of the Philistines, who had come up the coasts from Egypt to plague the Israelites. Samson had dealt them a blow and delivered Israel for a season, but here at the end of Eli's term as High Priest, God had left Israel without relief. 

The Philistines had taken parts of Israel and made them servants, the Israelites went to war with them and were beaten badly, and they decided that, rather turn to the Lord and ask for deliverance, they would force the issue by taking the Ark of the Covenant from the Tabernacle at Shiloh and bring it to the battle, along with the sons of Eli, Hophni and Phineas. The Israelites lose, the ark is captured and the sons of the High Priest are slain, just as the Lord had spoken to Samuel. 

When a survivor, a Benjamite of all people (see **Judges 19-21**), comes and tells Eli of all that had transpired, the elderly man falls back off his seat, and being fat from the sacrifices meant for the Lord, breaks his brittle neck. Worse, the pregnant wife of Phineas, hearing the news, goes into premature labor and dies in childbirth, bringing forth a son, who is named Ichabod, meaning the glory had gone out of Israel, for the ark of God is taken.

It is one of the most well known stories in the world, a feat everyone has heard of and a byword for overcoming impossible odds. *The Lord that delivered me out of the paw of the lion, and out of the paw of the bear, he will deliver me out of the hand of this Philistine*...

...*Then said David to the Philistine, Thou comest to me with a sword, and with a spear, and with a shield: but I come to thee in the name of the Lord of hosts, the God of the armies of Israel, whom thou hast defied. And so we come to the end of Saul, a Benjamite, son of Cis, or Kish as it is in the Old Testament. A man head and shoulders above the other men of Israel. Anointed by God to be their first king. 

And while Saul was most assuredly saved, his life as king was not the example of a Christian walk that we aspire too. 

We see him developing from a loyal son, seeking the lost donkey of his father, to an unsteady and uncertain leader, plucked from the least tribe of Israel, just a generation removed from their decimation for harboring sodomites. Every bit the part of king, but with none of the heart to lead. 

Saul spends his time as King worrying about what others think of him, what they may do or say. Caring about the world over the word of God. Indecisive. Prone to bad choices. And ultimately paranoid and wrathful, knowing the Lord had repented of his kingship and had chosen another to take his place. David, the apple of God's eye. A man after the Lord's own heart. 

Now to Saul and his sons would have been the promises given to David. But his transgressions against the Lord were too great. And even with warning after warning, Saul back slid further and further. 

Had this Saul repented, and confessed his sin and asked for forgiveness, he may have been restored. But he was blinded by the evil spirit given him by God for his disobedience. The two Sauls of Benjamin couldn't have been more different. But both were the anointed of God.
