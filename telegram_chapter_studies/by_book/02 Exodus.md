Wasting very little time, we are given Moses life story and how he came to be saved from the order of the Pharaoh to kill the newborn sons of Israel, raised as an Egyptian in the house of Pharaoh by his daughter. As an adult, committing murder against an Egyptian for his mistreatment of a Hebrew, fleeing to Midian, meeting and marrying Zipporah and living as a herdsman for Reuel. Bearing a son named Gershom. Being a stranger in a strange land. 

Moses, under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit, wrote his own biography and kept it short and to the point. 

We see in his story how he was saved by water in an ark, a picture of the salvation of Noah from the flood, and a picture of Christ who was similarly saved out of the hands of Herod as an infant, when Joseph fled to Egypt, just as the patriarchs Joseph had done.  

Parallels and pictures. 

All creation and history serves as parables for God's word.

And so Moses became a prophet of God. Jehovah. I AM THAT I AM. The God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. The living God.

Moses was neither Egyptian nor suffering as a Hebrew, living as a shepherd. Doubtful that his brethren would believe him, given they questioned him; *Who made thee a ruler and a judge over us?* 

And yet it was him who the Lord chose to confound Pharaoh and free His people from bondage. A picture of Christ. 

God's promise is that not only would they become free, but spoil Egypt as they left. Taking the treasure of the Egyptians and the land of the Canaanites, overflowing with milk and honey. 

And so Moses entered into this covenant with God.

Note that like the patriarchs before him, when Moses is called by God his reply is "here am I." 

The difference between here am I and here I am is that of being called as a servant and appearing before the Lord ready to do His will, vs giving your location, your current circumstance, where you've ended up. 

It's this distinction we should keep in mind when God calls us to His service. When God's will is to be done. That we answer "Here am I."

Just a few weeks ago, we did a [study](https://t.me/HouseOfWews2/9752)on **Exodus 12** and **Luke 22**, the ordinance of Passover the Lord's supper which replaced it. The foreshadowing of the feast, the burnt offering, the blood of the lamb without blemish. I recommend checking it out. Everything in the Old Testament , but especially the Passover and Exodus of the people from Egypt , point directly to Christ. Unbelief is sin and death. But faith brings salvation from the bonds of sin and the promise of a better country.

<!-- post 10080 · 2023-04-23 05:40 · study -->

I find it interesting that in spoiling Egypt, where the people gave the Israelites their gold and treasure, would ultimately become grievous sin in the wilderness, melted down to form the image of the calf the Hebrews worshipped while Moses was on Mt Sinai with God. 

Is that a picture of the blessings we have as saved Christians, which by not staying with the word of the Lord, not walking in the new man with the remembrance of the bondage God freed us from in Christ, that we risk turning to sin; into idols?

How often we forget our deserved fate. How often we forget what should have happened to us based on our choices, and spared the worst of the consequences by God in His mercy through Christ Jesus. 

I don't know a saved Christian, no matter how backslidden and carnal, who when pressed won't admit that God saved them physically from the most grave of consequences they rightly deserved. Moving things in our life in such a way that we were delivered from the fruit of our sin, as we gained faith. But many forget when it's not present in their mind. Many take their blessings for granted and turn their comfort into an excuse to sin.

There are people who have relatively easy lives and yet never come to peace with their life and the choices they made. And so they are miserable. 

We who are saved are blessed, that we can come to terms with it and understand what a blessing things actually are when you consider how bad it could be given the choices we've made. 

The humility and perspective we gain by understanding just how pitiful our position was that we trusted in God and called upon the name of the Lord, should allow us to see the bondage He freed us from and appreciate the wilderness He's led us through on our way to the promised land. 

God spares us by saving us from ourselves. His hand is constantly upon us. Like the Hebrews. His light, guiding us in the darkness. His bread, His word, come down from Heaven to feed and nourish us like manna. 

And like the Hebrews, we often complain at the hardships we must endure to get ourselves back to the place God has intended for us had we not sojourned in Egypt. Had we not chased after sin. 

We forget where we were. We forget how we escaped. We forget how the blood of the lamb upon our hearts, as it was upon the doorposts, spared us from destruction. 

We melt down the treasure God provided, and, *as it is written, The people sat down to eat and drink, and rose up to play.*

<!-- post 10081 · 2023-04-23 05:43 · study -->

We require an ordinance. A memorial. That our children's children can ask, why do we observe this? That we never forget or grow comfortable in our own conceits. To walk with God, feeding the new man, because the old man remains, calling us back to Egypt. To sin. 

We join with an assembly of believers, all who have been set at liberty from the same bondage, spared the same fate. We break bread and give thanks, in memory of Jesus and His sacrifice for us. We share the cup, of His blood spilled out for us. 

We need to be reminded that we not take our salvation for granted.

<!-- post 10082 · 2023-04-23 05:55 · study -->

A lot of new Christians, and many not-so-new, will become frustrated and despair that when they got saved, and began "turning their life around," that they still had to endure the pain and hard work of getting up out of the hole their old man had dug for them with their sin. 

But that hole was a grave. And in it was death. And God has given us the rope to climb out of it, and the light above to guide us from the darkness. 

That while the worst consequences are mitigated, and miraculous opportunities to escape are granted them, there is still a process of shedding sin in their life. There is still lost opportunity and easier paths lost to them by their choices. There's still a price to pay in this world. 

People sometimes can only see the walls of the hole they are in, and the light above seems small and distant. And the climb difficult, to where they take occasion to despise the rope they've been given. And they backslide. 

Sometimes God shakes the rope to draw their attention and get them back focused on the light.  

As we will see with the children of Israel, that sometimes we see falling back into sin, going back to servitude in Egypt, as easier than getting right with God. And God will remind us the foolishness of this. Harshly. 

*My son, despise not the chastening of the Lord; neither be weary of his correction: For whom the Lord loveth he correcteth; even as a father the son in whom he delighteth*

And so commanded God to all of Israel. To commemorate the day He freed them from bondage and brought them out to the land He promised their fathers, and gave ordnances regarding the feast of unleavened bread, the removal of all yeast and leavening from the home for 7 days, and the consecration of the firstborn males, in remembrance of the slaying of the firstborn of Egypt and as a picture of the Christ to come. 

Leaven represents sin in the Bible. Egypt, bondage to sin. This is done in remembrance of God, just as the Lord's supper took the place of Passover, and Jesus took the place of the lamb without blemish. And so going forward, Isralites were to offer sacrifice of the first born, and in place of their first born, to sacrifice them. 

Jews today claim the bar mitzvah, the purchase of sanctification by use of silver shekels, is the equivalent of the blood sacrifice required by God, because they don't worship Him or follow the Old Testament at all. They worship moloch, and follow the traditions of men. 

The idea of parents instilling in their firstborn male the knowledge of Him, and what He has done for Israel is important. God needed them to retain knowledge of Him and His Promises in mind throughout generations, that in the days of Jesus, a remnant may be saved. 

God, with a firey pillar by night and a pillar of smoke as a cloud, by day, took the Israelites and the mixed multitudes our, towards the Red Sea, that they not pass through the land of the Philistines, *Lest peradventure the people repent when they see war, and they return to Egypt*.

*Some fell upon stony places, where they had not much earth: and forthwith they sprung up, because they had no deepness of earth: And when the sun was up, they were scorched; and because they had no root, they withered away. And some fell among thorns; and the thorns sprung up, and choked them.*

Not everyone who is saved is fertile ground. Many fall away or become offended by and by. That is why the Apostles were so intent on edification. On walking in the new man.

In **Exodus 16** we find the recently freed Isralites, a month now out from bondage in Egypt, shown signs and wonders of God by Moses, murmuring that they were better off in chains. Having run low on supplies, their flesh in need afflicted their spirit. 

But God hears their murmurs, and tells Moses and Aaron that he will send them bread to eat in the morning, and the flesh of quails at night. God sets the rules of His heavenly bread that each person takes the measure allotted, and uses it that day, and all would be filled. If they tried to store it up for the next day, it would rot, except for that manna gathered on the day before the sabbath, which would feed them both days. 

And like the entire story of Exodus, we have a picture of salvation in Christ. 

Jesus, who is the bread of life. Jesus, who is the sabbath. Our rest. Jesus who was sent by God the Father that all who believe on Him shall never hunger. Jesus is the rock from which the water springs as well, as we will soon see. And those that believe on Him shall never thirst. 

In **John 6**, Jesus is preaching to the crowd that had followed Him to Capernaum, He had just fed the multitudes with a few loaves and fishes, that thousands were full and there was more to spare. Like in the wilderness of Sin, He fed them whether they followed God's laws or not, to prove them, that they may know He was of God. 

But Jesus first chastises the multitude, saying they didn't come to Him for spiritual reasons, but because their belly was full. 

And just as the children of Israel in the wilderness murmured against Moses, against God, here too the Bible says they murmured against Christ;

*The Jews then murmured at him, because he said, I am the bread which came down from heaven.*

And Jesus, being God, hears their murmurs and knows their hearts, and says; *Murmur not among yourselves. No man can come to me, except the Father which hath sent me draw him: and I will raise him up at the last day. It is written in the prophets, And they shall be all taught of God. Every man therefore that hath heard, and hath learned of the Father, cometh unto me. Not that any man hath seen the Father, save he which is of God, he hath seen the Father. Verily, verily, I say unto you, He that believeth on me hath everlasting life.*
 *I am that bread of life.*

*Your fathers did eat manna in the wilderness, and are dead. This is the bread which cometh down from heaven, that a man may eat thereof, and not die. I am the living bread which came down from heaven: if any man eat of this bread, he shall live for ever: and the bread that I will give is my flesh, which I will give for the life of the world.*

He is giving a spiritual lesson using the allegory of bread from Heaven, the manna in the wilderness, and applying it to His own body, which in death would take on the sins of the world that whoever believes on Him shall have eternal life.
