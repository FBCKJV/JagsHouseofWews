We are in **Nehemiah 1**, which continues the narrative of the rebuilding of Jerusalem, and it picks up roughly 13 years after the end of Ezra. Nearly 100yrs after Haggai and Zechariah. 

And we are told that the city's walls and gate remain in ruin, still fire scorched from the original invasion by Nebuchadnezzar and the Babylonian empire. 

Nehemiah, the cup bearer to the Persian king Artaxerxes I, in the 10th year of his reign, laments the languished reconstruction. That now, after many divine starts and human stops, Jerusalem still is not walled. 

He recounts the instructions of Moses, that if the people who call themselves the children of Israel failed to keep God's commandments, and chase after other gods, they would be scattered to the ends of the earth. 

But if they turn back again to Him, repenting towards the God of their fathers, He will bless them and gather them again. 

Nehemiah's prayer is often used by dispensationalists, saying "See, this proves that God will always gather His people!" 

But, alas, this is the final time. The city God chose to place His name needs to be ready for the coming of the Messiah. The people have been unable and unwilling to repent. Only a remnant remain faithful. 

In a few centuries, Jerusalem will be under Roman control which will act as a means to protect and preserve the city until Jesus' birth. Doing what the Jews failed to do all this time. 

Also, we see Nehemiah lay out the condition for this gathering, that the people turn back to the Lord and obey Him. That includes being ready for the coming of Christ, with hearts ready. That if they believed Moses and the prophets, they would be prepared for Him. 

Modern Israel was not the result of Jews coming to repentance, and turning towards God the Father by embracing His Son. It was the result of pride, and satanic forces. They are cut off in unbelief, their covenant faded away. 

As Jesus cried;  *O Jerusalem, Jerusalem, thou that killest the prophets, and stonest them which are sent unto thee, how often would I have gathered thy children together, even as a hen gathereth her chickens under her wings, and ye would not!*

*Behold, your house is left unto you desolate. For I say unto you, Ye shall not see me henceforth, till ye shall say, Blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord.*

<!-- post 16736 · 2025-09-19 06:23 · study -->

Nehemiah recounts how he came to be in the king of Persia's presence, as cupbearer. Bringing wine to the king. And his sad countenance at the condition of his fathers' home, Jerusalem, still not finished rebuilding. 

The king inquires about the matter, and he agrees to give Nehemiah a time to go an do the work, letters of passage and of support to show the governors beyond the river, that they prepare timber for beams and provide materials. 

Unlike Ezra, Nehemiah travels with captains and horsemen.

*When Sanballat the Horonite, and Tobiah the servant, the Ammonite, heard of it, it grieved them exceedingly that there was come a man to seek the welfare of the children of Israel.*

But Nehemiah continues to the city, and the gates are consumed with fire, collapsed to the point he has to enter on foot. He goes out and examines the gates and the walls without announcing himself, and afterwards he goes to the Jews and says *let us build up the wall of Jerusalem that we be no more a reproach.* 

Hearing his encouragement and that the hand of God was upon him and this goal, they rose up to join him. 

Sanballat the Horonite, and Tobiah the servant, the Ammonite and Geshem the Arabian scoff at Nehemiah's mission. But the cupbearer tells them they have no portion in Jerusalem. 

*The God of heaven, he will prosper us; therefore we his servants will arise and build.*

Christians today have a mandate from the King of kings, Christ Jesus, to build up heavenly Jerusalem. And He will prosper us when we do. 

But as Peter writes, *Knowing this first, that there shall come in the last days scoffers, walking after their own lusts.*..

When we seek to do the works of the Lord, we will face acorn and ridicule, often from those close to us. But they have no portion or memorial in Jerusalem. We should be ready laborers and remind ourselves of this. That we please God and not men. And do the work He has commanded us to do.

Many hands make light work. Nehemiah has spured men from all over Judah to help repair sections of the walls and fix the gates. Some working on sections near their homes, others helping out who live outside the district being surrounded. 

Priests from Jerusalem led repairs near the Sheep Gate and temple area; goldsmiths and apothecaries from the city handled sections by the Broad Wall; merchants from Keilah and Beth-haccerem worked on districts around the Valley Gate and Dung Gate; rulers from half the district of Jerusalem and the other half oversaw portions near the Refuse Gate; Levites from Mizpah and Gibeon, along with singers from the house of Gilgal, mended spots opposite the ascent to the armory; Tekoites from the village of Tekoa built near the great tower projecting to the wall of Ophel; and men from Jericho, Zanoah, Beth-zur, and other rural locales contributed to gates and walls in their home territories. All pitching in gladly, except some of the rulers who are mentioned derisively.

Nehemiah as governor over Judah, hears the cry of the people that they have been taxed so heavily during lean times that they had to turn to the rulers and the rich men, and borrow against their homes and property, and their sons and daughters. That their fellow tribesmen were changing brothers usury. 

Nehemiah makes a plea to the lenders and bankmen, that God is not pleased that they have done this to their people. And those holding the loans forgive the people their debts. 

*And forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors.* 

Then Nehemiah proceeds to say that in his time, he hasn't demanded the honor or tribute of the governor's bread. Only sufficient food to feed those at his table. This is a tax on all the people, and he is entitled to it, but refused it to avoid offending God. That they did not acquire land or get rich off the position Nehemiah and his house were in. Instead he labored on the wall with them 

It's similar to Paul's epistles.

**2 Thessalonians 3:8**
*Neither did we eat any man's bread for nought; but wrought with labour and travail night and day, that we might not be chargeable to any of you*.

**2 Corinthians 11:9**
*And when I was present with you, and wanted, I was chargeable to no man: for that which was lacking to me the brethren which came from Macedonia supplied*

**Acts 20:34**
*Yea, ye yourselves know, that these hands have ministered unto my necessities, and to them that were with me.*

Note Nehemiah did receive payment of food and lodging and was not left to starve. His house was supplied with the Ox and sheep and fouls. 

Similarly, a pastor is entitled to some payment for their services. 

**1 Timothy 5:18**
*For the scripture saith, thou shalt not muzzle the ox that treadeth out the corn. And, The labourer is worthy of his reward.*

It's a pretty clear line between being paid for the ministry with enough to live on, and taking advantage of the position to become rich from it. 

Because some have used filthy lucre to get mansions and private jets, some Christians begrudge any tithing or professional full time ministry. 

But the excesses of some doesn't mean that all are guilty. Tithes and supporting a pastor are a good thing. Pastors are to not take advantage of their flock.

The final chapter of Nehemiah's account of the restoration of the walls in Jerusalem, and the continued efforts to cleanse the priesthood and the people of outside influence. 

Nehemiah writes of how, when the law of Moses was read and the truth that Ammonites and Moabites were forbidden from joining the congregation forever, by God, that the people removed them, wives and children, from the priesthood and the church. 

But now, years later it's still a problem for a holy people, separated unto God, but having mixed multitudes in their midst. Unequally yoked to non-believers. 

Tobiah was a prominent Ammonite official who vehemently opposed Nehemiah's efforts to rebuild the walls of Jerusalem and was a bitter adversary of the Jewish people. Despite this, the high priest Eliashib, who was related to Tobiah by marriage, inappropriately prepared a large room in the Temple courts for Tobiah's use. The room was storage for sacrifice and holy things, so to give it to an Ammonite, especially one who opposed the man of God, was especially galling to Nehemiah. 

He threw the items out, cast out the Ammonite, and had the temple priests cleanse the space. 

He notes that intermarriage was rampant. Mixed children unable to speak the Jewish tongue. Merchants of foreign people came and sold on the sabbath. And so the Jews worked themselves on the day of rest. 

Outside influence was leading the people to sin. 

Comparing it to Solomon, the wisest and greatest king of Israel, who was undone and led to sin by foreign wives, Nehemiah not only preaches against mixed marriages, but assaults men into agreeing to not give their daughters to foreigners. He shuts the city gates on the sabbath and appoints his own sons to keep guard. 

When the merchants set up shop outside the city walls, he threatens them until they never come back. 

Nehemiah cleans the city and people of outside influence, driving the ungodly from among them. And he prays God to remember this good he did. 

It is shoveling against the tide sometimes, to hold standards and expectations of God, among a stubborn and rebellious people. As a pastor or a father in a Christian home, it can seem endless, the number of times the people go out into sin always seems greater than the rebukes and restoration done to undo the last. 

A constant struggle against the world and the god of this world. And the lust of the eyes, the pride of life. To keep the people who you have charge over pure and separated unto God. 

Let alone ourselves. 

But it is important to be constantly diligent. 

Just as Moses saw the Israelites rise up to play, and to worship an idol, being gone just 40 days, and so soon removed from bondage in Egypt, Nehemiah is gone a short time and the city of Jerusalem has descended into sin. 

People forget the bondage and cruelty of their unregenerate pre-Christian life. They quickly forget chastisements. They soon return like a dog to its vomit. Like a pig to the mire. 

It takes constant vigilance, preaching and hard leadership to maintain even a bare minimum of standards. 

But it is a good work. And something Christian men should commit themselves to.
