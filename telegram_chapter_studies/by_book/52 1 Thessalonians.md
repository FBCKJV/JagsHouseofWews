**1 Thessalonians 1**

*Paul, and Silvanus, and Timotheus, unto the church of the Thessalonians which is in God the Father and in the Lord Jesus Christ: Grace be unto you, and peace, from God our Father, and the Lord Jesus Christ.*

*2 We give thanks to God always for you all, making mention of you in our prayers;*

*3 Remembering without ceasing your work of faith, and labour of love, and patience of hope in our Lord Jesus Christ, in the sight of God and our Father;*

*4 Knowing, brethren beloved, your election of God.*

*5 For our gospel came not unto you in word only, but also in power, and in the Holy Ghost, and in much assurance; as ye know what manner of men we were among you for your sake.*

*6 And ye became followers of us, and of the Lord, having received the word in much affliction, with joy of the Holy Ghost.*

*7 So that ye were ensamples to all that believe in Macedonia and Achaia.*

*8 For from you sounded out the word of the Lord not only in Macedonia and Achaia, but also in every place your faith to God-ward is spread abroad; so that we need not to speak any thing.*

*9 For they themselves shew of us what manner of entering in we had unto you, and how ye turned to God from idols to serve the living and true God;*

*10 And to wait for his Son from heaven, whom he raised from the dead, even Jesus, which delivered us from the wrath to come.*

<!-- post 5629 · 2022-10-03 05:42 · study -->

From the risen Christ in **Matthew 28**, giving the Great Commission to His Apostles;

*All power is given unto me in heaven and in earth. Go ye therefore, and teach all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost: Teaching them to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you: and, lo, I am with you always, even unto the end of the world. Amen.*

Here, in **1 Thessalonians 1**, we see the fruit of this command to give the Gospel to every creature play out.

Paul, Silvanus and Timothy, writing to the church in Thessalonica, hailing them "*in God the Father and in the Lord Jesus Christ,*" write about their work for the Lord in spreading the same Gospel that they received from Paul and the other evangelists, throughout Asia Minor.

*For our gospel came not unto you in word only, but also in power, and in the Holy Ghost, and in much assurance; as ye know what manner of men we were among you for your sake.*

*Ye became followers of us, and of the Lord, having received the word in much affliction, with joy of the Holy Ghost. So that ye were ensamples to all that believe in Macedonia and Achaia. For from you sounded out the word of the Lord not only in Macedonia and Achaia, but also in every place your faith to God-ward is spread abroad; so that we need not to speak any thing.*

It is amazing that within a few years of Christ's resurrection and ascension, churches had sprung up throughout the world, dedicated to the same Gospel as He preached, and the prophets gave witness too. A light shining out into the world leading all men to repent towards *the living and true God;*

*And to wait for his Son from heaven, whom he raised from the dead, even Jesus, which delivered us from the wrath to come.* 

To understand the power of the word as it is given, and that power a testimony of its truth, is to understand how it would be possible to convince so many disparate peoples to come to the saving faith in Christ, not from firsthand account or witness, but by the Holy Ghost moving in those spreading the good news *in every place your faith to God-ward is spread abroad; so that we need not to speak any thing.*

Living faith to faith. Building the body of Christ. Amen

Continuing to exhort the church at Thessalonica, Paul reminds them of their conversion, repenting towards the Gospel, and of how Paul and his helpers behaved among them, just as they preached the truth of Christ Jesus in Philippi, a city a few hours west, in northern Greece.

It was there in Acts 16, where Paul and Silas are accused of causing "exceeding trouble" by those who protested the gospel being preached, and brought them before magistrates, had them scourged, and finally jailed Paul and Silas. All of which came to serve the glory of God even the more, getting their jailer and his family saved, and planting a church.

And we are told here, that it is this time in Philippi that the members of the church of Thessalonica would recognize how shamefully treated they were for their mission, and understand that they came to them boldly with the word of God, through much tribulation, without guile or flattery, but with the stripes of suffering and still proclaiming the truth. That they were willing to give them not just the Gospel, but give up their own lives to proclaim it, because it is truth, and out of the love they feel towards them that received it.

Paul then reminds them of how, like his own children, he gave them not just the good news of Christ that brought to them salvation and the Spirit, but further exhortation to live blamelessly, as is befitting a saved Christian. To walk worthy of the call they answered, worthy of the God that saved them. 

A reminder that it is not just saving souls that we should do, but then building upon that salvation, encouraging and edifying babes in Christ, as we would our own children, to walk a Christian walk. Preaching against sin and towards the path of righteousness. Even in tribulation and contentions by man, but pleasing to God.

Paul compares them and their tribulation and persecution they are suffering to the churches in Judea, and their persecution by the Jews. Following after the churches in Christ, to be likewise afflicted, persecuted by the Macedonians, their fellow countrymen.

Continuing the topic at hand from Chapter 2 regarding the persecution and tribulations the church at had been reportedly going through, Paul says He, Timothy and Silas were concerned for them and their faith, that they in their troubles have been believing in vain, and turned from practicing the faith. Unable to wait any longer, they dispatched Timothy, and he returned with good news that they were just as strong and stedfast, unmoving, as ever. 

Establish and comfort means to to strengthen, to edify and be a bulwark. In modern terms, comfort is seen as weakening. Coddling, but it's meaning in the Bible is to strengthen in tribulation. This is why the Holy Spirit is called the comforter by Jesus. The evangelists sought to be a comforting encouragement to the saints in Thessalonica, but their stedfast unmoving faith, he says, actually brought comfort to him. 

He expounds on the idea that all Christians will see tribulations, and persecution for His name's sake. He reminds them of that, and is warning us against being discouraged. 

Believers can be like the seed from Jesus' parable of the sower, that;

...*fell upon stony places, where they had not much earth: and forthwith they sprung up, because they had no deepness of earth: And when the sun was up, they were scorched; and because they had no root, they withered away.*

Jesus explained;

...*he that received the seed into stony places, the same is he that heareth the word, and anon with joy receiveth it;*
*Yet hath he not root in himself, but dureth for a while: for when tribulation or persecution ariseth because of the word, by and by he is offended.*

These are saved Christians, but who aren't firmly established. Who aren't stedfast. Who, having shallow roots and not grown deep enough in faith, are moved by troubles. Wilting and producing no fruit when the heat is turned up. This is why it is important for us to stay in the word, in prayer, and exercising our faith to the benefit of others, that our roots are strong and we be immovable, stedfast and not offended by the inevitable persecution and troubles that come our way. 

But Paul's fears of this are assuaged by Timothy's report, the Thessalonians were engaged and their faith alive, despite the persecution by their countrymen for that faith. 

Paul sends prayers of blessings and restates his fervent desire to come and visit them, and reiterates that they take hope in the returning of Jesus Christ and the gathering of believers unto Him. This is a point made at the end of chapter 1 and again in 2, now fleshed out even moreso in chapter 3;

*And the Lord make you to increase and abound in love one toward another, and toward all men, even as we do toward you: To the end he may stablish your hearts unblameable in holiness before God, even our Father, at the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ with all his saints.*  

This hope, the thing that we are convinced of by the evidence of faith, should remain a comfort. That the Christ who saved us is returning, and in that day, our resurrection. The gathering of the elect, of all saints. In the worst of tribulation, even unto death, this blessed assurance of eternal life in Jesus name establishes us firmly, so that we may not be moved. A rock unmoving.
