<!-- post 4736 · 2022-08-24 05:54 · study -->

Paul writes to the churches at Philippi from his bonds in Rome, chained 24/7to a guard, in house arrest for Christ. He gives greeting to the believers there, wishing grace and peace, assuring them of his confidence in their salvation and prayers that they grow even more stedfast with the news of his affliction, rather than be troubled or discouraged. 

Even in chains, hopeful and joyous. Even with threat of death, to which Paul writes would be better, to be with Christ than to live in the flesh, but that there are needs to be met while still here, and accepting God's will either way. 

What an example to the early churches, who all suffered some tribulation. 


Paul notes that there are some preachers using his bonds to inspire more to partake in the Gospel, and some, who out of envy for his renown, preach out of spite for Paul, and yet Paul sees even the good in that, because the preaching of the good news of salvation is all we should care about. Not ourselves or our reputation.

Here, Paul beautifully uses Jesus as our perfect example of service to others, love and complete obedience to the will of the Father.  It chokes me up, and fills me with that blessed hope of the day every knee bows to the greatest name in and under Heaven. 

And we should obey this will, working outwardly our salvation that is inward. Not having dead faith but doing the work of His will and good pleasure, just as Jesus showed and as Paul strives for. 

*That ye may be blameless and harmless, the sons of God, without rebuke, in the midst of a crooked and perverse nation, among whom ye shine as lights in the world.*

Paul then gives assurance of Timothy and Epaphroditus who he is sending in his stead, being in bondage.

<!-- post 4767 · 2022-08-25 06:08 · study -->

Philippians is one of my favorite epistles, for it's clear meaning and beautiful prose. It is easily understood and straightforward doctrine on sanctification and doing works of the Lord. 

However many works-based false teachers use the "work out your own salvation with fear and trembling" line to teach that you cannot be assured salvation, and that we must do works to be saved. This out of context understanding is contrary to scripture, even scripture within the same epistle. In chapter 1 Paul says: 

*For your fellowship in the gospel from the first day until now;*
*Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ: Even as it is meet for me to think this of you all, because I have you in my heart; inasmuch as both in my bonds, and in the defence and confirmation of the gospel, ye all are partakers of my grace.*

He has confidence in them, that they partake in the same grace that saved him. This is only conditioned on their having fellowship in the same Gospel he preached to them, not on their works, and with not doubt that God works in them. 

It is another case of reading the whole of scripture and comparing it with itself, saving us from bad doctrine.

Paul turns to the matter of adhering to the law and circumcision, a theme of epistles stemming from the decision made in Acts to warn against adding to the gospel of faith by Judaizers. 

Paul argues to these Gentiles, that if birth, circumcision and zeal for the law saved anyone, it would be Paul, but he counts all his own "righteousness" as dung, so that he may gain Christ. 

He warns to beware the dogs, reprobates sneaking in and judaizing churches, subverting houses, crept in unawares. Evil workers. They of the circumcision. Paul says 
*they are the enemies of the cross of Christ.*

Because believers are not circumcised of the flesh, but of the heart. Saved by faith, *not having mine own righteousness, which is of the law, but that which is through the faith of Christ, the righteousness which is of God by faith: That I may know him, and the power of his resurrection.*

Paul does remind us that despite our having the imputed righteousness of Jesus Christ, we should strive for sanctification, to be more perfect, more complete in our walk. Striving for the mark. 

*Nevertheless, whereto we have already attained, let us walk by the same rule, let us mind the same thing.*

Obeying the law, not for our own righteousness but for the love of God. Not walking as the reprobates do, glorying in the sins of the flesh. Their end is destruction, but ours is in Heaven and eternal life.

Paul ends what is one of the most beautifully written thank you notes of all time. Full of exhortation and doctrine, blessings and thanks. Lessons for how to live for God and be at peace in Christ. 

*Rejoice in the Lord always: and I say again rejoice.Let your moderation be known unto all men. The Lord is at hand. Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God. And the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus.*

This should be the default state of your Christian life. Joy, not worrying and fearful for the cares of this world but trusting, in prayer and supplication to the Lord, in thanksgiving for the blessings and the tribulation by which the Spirit strengthens us. Having the peace with God that comes from being Christ's. 

If your walk isn't this, if troubles of the world weigh you down to bitterness, anger, resentment, envy and strife, then know it doesn't have to be this way. Walk less in the flesh and more in the spirit. In daily prayer, scripture reading, singing songs of praise and the psalms. Knowing with confidence your blessed assurance, that *God shall supply all your need according to his riches in glory by Christ Jesus.*

Think upon things of virtue, honesty, pure, true. Meditate on the law of God, and you will learn to praise Him and be content with whatever state you find yourself in. Abased or abounding. Satisfied with what you have, thankful to God that you have it. Knowing for certain in faith that you can do all things through Christ which strengthens us. Living faith to faith. 

And be fruitful, not as the world measures in wealth and status, but in souls won by your faithful service to God, reached by His word, a path lit by the light of your example.
