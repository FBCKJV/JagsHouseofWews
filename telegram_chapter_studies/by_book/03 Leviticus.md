The first several chapters of Leviticus, which lays out the Levitical priesthood and its ordinances and practices, deal with the subject of sacrifice for atonement and oblations, gifts given to the Lord of meats, which in Biblical terms is foodstuffs, not just flesh of an animal. 

As we see in chapter 1, this is the holocaust or burnt offering of the lamb without blemish. The blood carrying the atonement. The sweet savour of the fire roasted fats and meats being pleasing to the Lord. 

And this is a picture of Christ. Just as are the meat offerings in chapter 2. Everything in the Old Testament does. 

Here we see the gift offering of fine flour, anointing oil and frankincense. The bread, representing Jesus, made without leaven, leaven representing sin.  Note that it was both raw ingredients baked by Aaron and his sons. Or finished cakes and wafers. Cooked or baked. And the priests eat thereof what is left over. The frankincense consumed completely, all anointed with oil, as befitting a King. 

*And the priest shall take from the meat offering a memorial thereof, and shall burn it upon the altar: it is an offering made by fire, of a sweet savour unto the Lord.*

Showing the Israelites the truth of their savior in these ordinances. As a memorial to Him that was to come. All burnt upon the fire. 

But the oblations of the firstfruits shall not be burnt in the altar. And all the meat offerings of the first fruits are to be salted generously. To season it completely as it is the salt of the covenant with God. 

This represents the saved, the firstfruits of the harvest. The salt, the holy Spirit. 

*Ye are the salt of the earth: but if the salt have lost his savour, wherewith shall it be salted? it is thenceforth good for nothing, but to be cast out, and to be trodden under foot of men*.

Salt is a seasoning, increasing the savour of the food, enhancing it. But it is also a preservative, keeping food fresh, longer. In this way, the Spirit acts in the believer. It isn't burned in the fire, just as believers are spared Hell. 

God makes a provision for the corn given in a meat offering to be as the flour or unleavened bread, anointed with oil and frankincense, to be burned as the other flours, as a cornmeal, that there be no confusion.

Now a peace offering given to the Lord is one of the inner fat, or suet of the animal presented, and its blood. The blood sprinkled about the altar and the fat rendered in fire to God. 

The people were not to eat the fat or the blood. The ban on blood predates the Levitical law and goes back to Adam being taught of God how to sacrifice, and the laws given to Noah after the flood. 

The inner fats being God's, offered in thanksgiving or at the completion of a vow, and burned unto Him, the sweet savour His as propitiation. The fats being where the flavor is, the best parts, especially around the organs.

But the meat of the offering is returned to the man, and in that way they are blessed again by God. 

The best is given to God, Who in return gives to the people seeking peace with Him, sharing in the sacrifice.

Running behind a little bit but luckily for us, the New Testament is the best study guide for the Old. 

We see that they sent offerings are given not only by the people but by the priest. And provides an opportunity for those in good conscience who may through ignorance violate the law. But later come to know that they did and seek forgiveness. 

This is just as a child is born sinless and until the knowledge of the law comes, until they know right from wrong, send isn't held against them by God. It is only the knowledge of the law that kills us in sin. And it's only from that knowledge that we come to understand we need a savior. As Paul writes in **Romans 7**:

*For I was alive without the law once: but when the commandment came, sin revived, and I died. And the commandment, which was ordained to life, I found to be unto death. For sin, taking occasion by the commandment, deceived me, and by it slew me.*

Now again the sacrifices are being given by God to the Hebrews as a sign of the Christ to come. He will fulfill all of the different oblations and propitiations. But we should note how bloody this is. How gory. That the man seeking forgiveness for himself or his immediate family is laying his hand on each sacrifice at the door and slaying it before the Lord. 

This is a gruesome reminder of what Christ will go through. We have the entire account of his passion from four gospels and still it's difficult for us to comprehend. Now imagine a people before Christ. Looking forward to the cross. And we see in this way God is showing us exactly how the cost for our sin is death and fire and blood.

The heave and wave offerings were a form of free will offering given in peace to God, in thanksgiving, and in supplication or to a vow, with the understanding that a portion went to feed the Levite priests. 

Heave and wave, the motions done with the portion that was given the priests. Literally lifting up or waving above their heads, signifying that portion that was theirs. 

Some interpretations hold that the wave offerings signify the resurrection of Christ, while the heave as either His being lifted up on the cross or, more commonly His assent into Heaven on Pentecost. I don't know, or really have a strong opinion on it. It is sufficient to know that Christ is the propitiation, the sacrifice, by which we make peace with God and Whom we give thanks through. Blessing all we do in Jesus' name.

I have covered the symbolism and importance of the law of leprosy, and the atonement offered at the cleansing of one who is cured in another post [here](https://t.me/HouseOfWews2/9168).

It is fascinating to me that an incurable disease is given a process by God for ritual cleansing in the case of a leper who is cured.   

There were cases in the OT of leprosy being "cured." In **Numbers 12**, after Aaron and Miriam gainsay Moses for marrying an Ethiopian, God curses Miriam with leprosy for 7 days. 

The other instance is in **2 Kings** 5, where a captain of the Syrian guard, Naaman contracts the disease, and the Syrian King sends him to the king of Israel asking him to heal his servant. 

*And it came to pass, when the king of Israel had read the letter, that he rent his clothes, and said, Am I God, to kill and to make alive, that this man doth send unto me to recover a man of his leprosy? wherefore consider, I pray you, and see how he seeketh a quarrel against me.*

As we see, it was as if he had asked him to raise the dead. An impossible task and the king worried that it was a provocation for war with Syria. But the prophet Elisha heard of it and said;

*And it was so, when Elisha the man of God had heard that the king of Israel had rent his clothes, that he sent to the king, saying, Wherefore hast thou rent thy clothes? let him come now to me, and he shall know that there is a prophet in Israel.*

It's a wonderful story of faith and shows the power of God, highly recommend reading it. 

But we can see that the idea that one could be healed from leprosy would have been something that the Israelites would have thought impossible save by God. 

And yet an entire chapter is devoted to this unlikely thing, filled with imagery of the sacrifice of Christ. A picture of Jesus saving sinners from death. And the purpose of the practical application, as we see Elisha say, is to let people know there is a prophet of God among them. 

Jesus heals the leper and sends him to the temple to perform this ritual, as a testimony unto the priests, that the Son of God was here.

The Levitical feasts are also important to understand for our study of Revelation going forward. They are a picture of Christ. Last Easter I gave a rundown of the ways Jesus fufifiled the spring feasts, Passover, the feast of unleavened bread and Pentecost. These were a picture of His first coming. Likewise, the fall feasts God commands in **Leviticus 23** represent Christ at His second coming. The timing, the harvest, the gathering. The blowing of trumpets. The dwelling in houses made by God, while singing His praises. All point to end times Bible prophecy. ("Booths" were tabernacles, tents, made from branches and boughs, rough cut and gathered from nature). The timing of things mirrors events in Revelation, as we will see. 

One incredibly important point of the feasts, these Sabbaths; NO WORK was to be done by the people. This is the reinforcement by God of His grace in giving us a free gift of salvation in Jesus, who is the Sabbath. Three days of no works, 3 Sabbaths in a row, from His crucifixion to His resurrection, Jesus doing all the work. And like the Spring feasts, the convocations or gathering of the congregation/church, there is no work allowed, for Jesus did it all. 

*For whatsoever soul it be that shall not be afflicted in that same day, he shall be cut off from among his people.*
*And whatsoever soul it be that doeth any work in that same day, the same soul will I destroy from among his people.*

These are those unsaved who are appointed to wrath. Notice it is both the unbelieving, those not afflicted by the atonement made by the sacrifice, and those who do any work who are cast out and destroyed. So too will it be in the second coming.

**Leviticus 24** begins with the command to have the lamps of the Tabernacle burn continually, dusk to dawn. A statute for ever. That there is light in the place of God. 

And God commands that there be bread baked as a memorial, continually, as an offering before the Lord, and eaten by the Levitical priests. This is called the shewbread, concentrated by the Lord. 

The light symbolizes the truth of God throughout the Bible, and so it is never dark before Him. The bread is both the life given the people by God's grace and His word. 

This shewbread becomes a means of feeding David and his men as they flee Saul, who was in a murderous rage against the future king. Even as it was offered to God and holy, but to the Lord's purpose, it fed His favored in a time of need. 

Likewise, as Jesus rebukes the Pharisees when He and His disciples gleaned corn on the Sabbath to eat, Jesus points to how these things are the Lord's, and not just blind tradition and meaningless ritual. That He is the Lord of the sabbath.

Often religion becomes rote practices, that forget the meaning behind them as the Pharisees had. Using these things to condemn. Jesus says had they understood who He was, that He was present for mercy in His death and resurrection to come, that the sacrifices of the temple were a picture of Him who had come not to condemn but to save, that they'd understand why it was acceptable for them to eat of their work, as the Levites were allowed on the sabbath. Because they did the work of God.

The second half of the chapter begins with a narrative of a child of Egyptian heritage born into the Tribe of Dan, blaspheming God, and God's instructions to take him without the camp and those who heard the blasphemy to stone him to death. 

God reiterates that even strangers among them are under His law, both to their good, and to their punishment for violating it. 

He says His law is justice, that a murderer be put to death. That blemish, or physical harm, be answered in a like manner; breach for breach, eye for eye, tooth for tooth.

This concept of God's law has been understood for millennia, preserved for all time, but in 1901 a Babylonian relic was found that contained what is called Hammurabi's Code, a list of laws credited to the ancient king by archeologists, which included the eye for an eye form of justice. 

During the 20th century, what was part of God's perfect law (repeated from **Exodus 21:24-25**
*Eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot*, *Burning for burning, wound for wound, stripe for stripe*) began to be credited to a man, and after it had permeated culture that the concept of an eye for an eye actually originated from Hammurabi's Code, people began to mistakenly think it wasn't God's perfect law. And once they'd managed to mislead people with that, they then began to discredit the concept as barbaric, a remnant of ancient Babylon and not the commandments of God. 

And so by the close of the 20th Century, most people had no idea that this was found in scripture, and were taught to think of it as a savage injustice.

If the concept was raised by Christians at all, it was in the context of **Matthew 5:38-39**; *Ye have heard that it hath been said, An eye for an eye, and a tooth for a tooth: But I say unto you, That ye resist not evil: but whosoever shall smite thee on thy right cheek, turn to him the other also.* And this was taught as somehow Jesus contradicting the concept, as if He wasn't the author of it. When Jesus says love thy neighbor, The same people pretend it was a new commandment when it comes from Leviticus as well.

But Jesus calling those who are saved by faith in Him to love even our enemies, to pray for them, to not answer violence with violence, but to show mercy and love to them, isn't saying that the law itself is unjust, for it will be the law in the Millennial Kingdom. He's talking about our hearts storing up wrath, vengeance, and hatred. 

Before a magistrate, the eye taken for an eye would still be justice. 

**1 Timothy 1:9-11**
*Knowing this, that the law is not made for a righteous man, but for the lawless and disobedient, for the ungodly and for sinners, for unholy and profane, for murderers of fathers and murderers of mothers, for manslayers, For whoremongers, for them that defile themselves with mankind, for menstealers, for liars, for perjured persons, and if there be any other thing that is contrary to sound doctrine; According to the glorious gospel of the blessed God, which was committed to my trust.*

But we who are righteous should forgive those who wronged us and show mercy to those in need. These two concepts aren't contradictory but complimentary. 

Our mercy doesn't upend the law. Our being at liberty from the law spiritually means we should be more circumspect in how we view our enemies. Our trust in God for vengeance taking priority over our executing our own wrath. 

But if the law demands restitution, so be it. The sword is not wielded in vain.

What those who seek to undermine confidence in scripture do is trust that Christians won't be able to rightly divide the word of God, and this higher level concept would escape us, and lead us to showing perverse judgment. 

If you notice, by abandoning the law for the unrighteous, instead of mercy and love and limited restitution to a like-for-like code, we have a "justice system" that either punishes the wrong doer far exceeding what is right in the eyes of God, or not doing anything to rightly punish the wicked and protect the innocent. Those that deserve death still live, often freed to offend again.
