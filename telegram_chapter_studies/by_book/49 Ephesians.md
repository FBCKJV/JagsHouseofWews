What an introduction. Paul lays so much doctrine in what is only the greeting of his letter to believers at Ephesus. It's fantastic, and dense and due to the extensive use of prepositional clauses, causes many believers and all natural men to struggle in understanding.

One aspect of understanding the Word is understanding God's use of conditional logic, if-then statements, often complex with multiple factors. This method of reasoning together with God is evident in this epistle. 

Paul is an Apostle, *by* the will of God. He's writing to the saints, the faithful *in* Jesus Christ. He wishes grace and peace to the reader *from* God and Jesus. God has blessed us *with* all spiritual blessings.

Keeping this in mind, consider verse 4-6:

According as he hath chosen us *in him* (Jesus) before the foundation of the world, that we should be holy and without blame before him in love: Having predestinated us unto the adoption of children **by** Jesus Christ **t**o himself, *according to the good pleasure of his will, To the praise of the glory of his grace, wherein he* (God) *hath made us accepted* in the beloved *(*Jesus*).* 

The error of Calvinism is to misapply the focus of predestination to those benefiting from it, rather than *by* whom, Jesus Christ. 

God has chosen us *in Jesus*. Predestinated *by Christ.* Accepted *in the beloved,* His Son. 

Jesus is the Son of God, beloved of the Father, the lamb slain before the foundation of the world. Jesus was predestinated to be the means *by which* we received grace. 

We weren't chosen. He was. We are only adopted sons, because of Jesus *In whom we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of sins, according to the riches of his grace.*

Paul is lauding God the Father and the Son, Christ Jesus, he is not indicating that the believers are special boys, predestined, but that in Christ through God's grace, we are. 

*In whom also we have obtained an inheritance, being predestinated according to the purpose of him who worketh all things after the counsel of his own will.* 

We obtain the inheritance in Jesus who was predestinated according to the will of God. 

And this isn't a question of perspective and interpretation, because Paul then makes our role in this process abundantly clear:

That we should be to the praise of his glory, who first trusted in Christ.

*In whom ye also trusted, after that ye heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation: in whom also after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise, Which is the earnest of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased possession, unto the praise of his glory.*

Follow that? We gain this inheritance IN Christ who we trusted when we heard the Gospel, and after hearing and believing, being sealed by the Holy Spirit. These are the conditional terms of our obtaining the inheritance, our salvation. Not God picking and choosing us, but God having predestinated Christ, that those *who first trusted* in Him are included in the inheritance. 

It's an order of operations that is focused on God and His Son. Our part is in believing it to be true.

The final chapter of Ephesians, Paul continues to build on the idea that we are not to despise government, the hierarchies put in place by God, but to honor and respect them, even when they are cruel to us. For we aren't theirs, but God's and what we do in our labors is His. 

Be it a husband, a mother, a child, a master or a servant, a king or a subject. If they aren't demanding we sin, truly sin in the despising of God, worship of idols or the blasphemy of the Spirit, and the like, we should in all good conscience work that which we can do, upright. This bothers many. 

"How can I obey and serve a cruel and ungodly master? A wicked husband? Dishonorable parents? A beast-system state?" The Bible tells us with faith, meekness, serving God and by our actions, convert the lost. 

I find that those that bristle the most at this were raised without proper fear and respect for their parents, due to whatever reason, although mostly having the rod spared or worse, used in anger, bringing their children to wrath. And as the relationship with the father goes, so to becomes their relationship with all superiors, and in the end, their relationship with God.

We should, as Christians, seek to be blameless. Be the hardest worker at your job. Be the most honest. Take the least advantage and support and uplift others, not being a respecter of persons. Diligent and happy. Having faith in God who rewards us, Paul says, *Knowing that whatsoever good thing any man doeth, the same shall he receive of the Lord, whether he be bond or free.*

This is not weakness, but strength. For the powers that be have authority from God, be they wicked or just, those who bear the sword do it to the fear of the unrighteous.

Here Daniel and the other believers don't wish to defile themselves with eating the kings sacrifices, but they don't seek to offend the head of the servants they serve.
