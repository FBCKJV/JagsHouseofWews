Continuing the prophets, Micah is preaching in the time before the destruction of Israel and the desolation and captivity of Judah, *in the days of Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah, kings of Judah*.

His burden of the Lord, the word come upon him, is very specific and clear. He lists places in Samaria and Judah, their sins and the destruction that's due them. 

It is amazing how the Lord sent so many prophets, even those not mentioned in scripture, with the same warnings against their idols, graven images, false religion and worship of foreign gods, fornication, murder...and it largely went unheeded. Just like modern times. 

And as with modern times, the prophets also brought the hope of salvation in the heir of God, the glory of Israel, the Messiah, Christ Jesus. 

Then, as now we are to preach the whole word of God, rebuking sin, spelling out the wrath that is to come, while showing the redemption possible for individuals who believe on the Lord. The promise of the Father to deliver us through His Son. The Word of God made flesh. 

All the prophets gave witness, speaking by the word of God. That word became flesh. The promise kept just as surely as the promise to destroy  Israel and the high places and groves and walled cities came true. Amen.

The Bible is not only about the grace and mercy God extends towards sinners in Christ, and the promises of forgiveness and redemption, but it is a book of judgments against evildoers. It is evidence of God's wrath against the workers of iniquity, and His promise to those who are meek and humble that their oppressors will surely be punished by an almighty Lord.

Micah preaches against Israel, those children of Jacob who have done wickedly. Woe to them that devise iniquity! They shall have their reward. 

Those who covet and steal, by force and violence. Who take what isn't theirs and even taking a man's inheritance. God says He has devised evil against them that He will not allow them to escape. That what they've spoiled will be taken from them. That what they own will be stripped away. They won't have even the inheritance cast by lots as sons of Jacob, because they shall be made desolate by His judgement against them. 

These wicked people who try to silence preaching of the word against them, because the word of God shames them.

**Micah 6** sees the prophet giving to the people of Israel the testimony of the Lord, Who is making His case to the world against those who had been His people. 

And even as Balaam could not but speak the words God gave him to speak, the prophet Micah rings out the charges against his countrymen. 

The Lord reminds them of His grace towards them, leading them out of Egypt, delivering them through the wilderness into the land they now possess (though not for long).

He reminds them of Balaam being unable to curse them, at Balak the Moabite's hire. The righteousness of God is evident in the righteousness of the people. *How shall I curse, whom God hath not cursed? or how shall I defy, whom the Lord hath not defied?* 

But even so, the council of Balaam was that if you wanted to curse the children of Israel, Balak had to make them sin against the Lord. And so Moab sent her daughters to commit whoredoms with fornication and bringing idols and false gods among them, until God sent a plague to smites His people, they had brought a curse on themselves. 

The Lord reiterates His often stated position that it isn't the blood of rams and goats that pleases Him, but faith and mercy and judgement. To do justly and walk humbly with God. 

The blood offerings were a picture of Christ. Not the means of salvation themselves. What good are dead works if they aren't given in faith? Trappings of religion rather than pure religion, undefiled;

**James 1:27**
*Pure religion and undefiled before God and the Father is this, To visit the fatherless and widows in their affliction, and to keep himself unspotted from the world.*

As Jesus rebuked the Pharisees;

**Matthew 23:23**
*Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! for ye pay tithe of mint and anise and cummin, and have omitted the weightier matters of the law, judgment, mercy, and faith: these ought ye to have done, and not to leave the other undone.*

In His displeasure, God tells them that His longsuffering mercy is over. That those who deal violently, dishonestly and with lies are now going to be punished by His wrath, and none shall stand. 

*Therefore also will I make thee sick in smiting thee, in making thee desolate because of thy sins. Thou shalt eat, but not be satisfied; and thy casting down shall be in the midst of thee; and thou shalt take hold, but shalt not deliver; and that which thou deliverest will I give up to the sword. Thou shalt sow, but thou shalt not reap; thou shalt tread the olives, but thou shalt not anoint thee with oil; and sweet wine, but shalt not drink wine.*

They walk in the statutes of Ahab and his wicked father, Omri. Worshiping false gods while doing wickedly. And now they will be made a desolation. A reproach. A hissing. A warning and parable among the nations. God revealed by His wrath upon those who were His people, and who chose to go the way of Balaam instead.

**Micah 7,** the final chapter of this book of prophecy. Micah was a contemporary of Isaiah and Hosea, and prophesied during time before the tragic fall of the Northern Kingdom of Israel to the Assyrian Empire. Yet, as we have seen, much of his preaching is of Christ. Of the Lord as a redeemer, full of mercy. 

Micah laments that the fruit of the vine is picked clean. There are none that do good, all are evil. The good men perished off the earth. Princes and judges are corrupt, seeking bribes. The mighty men, dishonest and cruel. 

He advises that as their destruction is nigh, trust not a friend, or a guide, or even your lover. Better to keep silent than be betrayed. There is dual prophecy here, both of the prophets near future, and the time of reformation in Christ. 

Verse 6 is quoted by Jesus, explaining what the division and distrust will be from; following Him. Unbelieving relatives and friends will betray us, yet He is faithful. The enemies are in our own house. 

If we can't trust leaders, or the walls and watchmen set to protect us. If we can't trust our friends or relatives because of their iniquity, what shall we do?

Look unto the Lord and wait for the God of our salvation. God will hear and deliver us. Our enemies will be made ashamed by His glory.
