**Habakkuk 1**
*The burden which Habakkuk the prophet did see.*

*2 O Lord, how long shall I cry, and thou wilt not hear! even cry out unto thee of violence, and thou wilt not save!*

*3 Why dost thou shew me iniquity, and cause me to behold grievance? for spoiling and violence are before me: and there are that raise up strife and contention.*

*4 Therefore the law is slacked, and judgment doth never go forth: for the wicked doth compass about the righteous; therefore wrong judgment proceedeth.*

*5 Behold ye among the heathen, and regard, and wonder marvelously: for I will work a work in your days which ye will not believe, though it be told you.*

*6 For, lo, I raise up the Chaldeans, that bitter and hasty nation, which shall march through the breadth of the land, to possess the dwellingplaces that are not their's.*

*7 They are terrible and dreadful: their judgment and their dignity shall proceed of themselves.*

*8 Their horses also are swifter than the leopards, and are more fierce than the evening wolves: and their horsemen shall spread themselves, and their horsemen shall come from far; they shall fly as the eagle that hasteth to eat.*

*9 They shall come all for violence: their faces shall sup up as the east wind, and they shall gather the captivity as the sand.*

*10 And they shall scoff at the kings, and the princes shall be a scorn unto them: they shall deride every strong hold; for they shall heap dust, and take it.*

*11 Then shall his mind change, and he shall pass over, and offend, imputing this his power unto his god.*

*12 Art thou not from everlasting, O Lord my God, mine Holy One? we shall not die. O Lord, thou hast ordained them for judgment; and, O mighty God, thou hast established them for correction.*

*13 Thou art of purer eyes than to behold evil, and canst not look on iniquity: wherefore lookest thou upon them that deal treacherously, and holdest thy tongue when the wicked devoureth the man that is more righteous than he?*

*14 And makest men as the fishes of the sea, as the creeping things, that have no ruler over them?*

*15 They take up all of them with the angle, they catch them in their net, and gather them in their drag: therefore they rejoice and are glad.*

*16 Therefore they sacrifice unto their net, and burn incense unto their drag; because by them their portion is fat, and their meat plenteous.*

*17 Shall they therefore empty their net, and not spare continually to slay the nations?*

Habakkuk was a prophet and contemporary of Jeremiah, preaching during the time leading up to and during the Babylonian captivity. He begins by saying the *burden* which he did see. Just as in Jeremiah we see that the prophets called being given the word of God *the burden of the Lord.* 

**Jeremiah 23:36-40** mocks the false prophets who continually claim the burden of the Lord. 

But Habakkuk sincerely carries this burden, beginning with his  crying out to the Lord in anger and frustration at the state of Judah in his day. 

There is violence, strife, contention and spoiling or thefts, the law isn't being enforced and corruption keeps right judgment from being made. The wicked are prospering and are protected by the government while the righteous are victimized. 

Sounds familiar.

In verse 5, God begins to respond. The Lord says He will work a wonder in the prophet's days, rising up the Chaldeans, the Babylonian empire, and they will invade Judah and possess it. 

God says they are terrible and mighty, even their horses like leopards fiercer than wolves. That their armies will gather up Judah to captivity, scoff at their kings, and the cities will be destroyed by them. 

God also gives Habakkuk the prophecy that the king of Babylon, Nebuchadnezzar, shall take credit for this wonder God wrought against Judah. 

*Then shall his mind change, and he shall pass over, and offend, imputing this his power unto his god.*

(This is fulfilled in **Daniel 4**, another study for another day)

Habakkuk replies, respectfully but a bit incredulous. 

*Art thou not from everlasting, O Lord my God, mine Holy One? we shall not die. O Lord, thou hast ordained them for judgment; and, O mighty God, thou hast established them for correction. Thou art of purer eyes than to behold evil, and canst not look on iniquity: wherefore lookest thou upon them that deal treacherously, and holdest thy tongue when the wicked devoureth the man that is more righteous than he?*

He asks how God could use the wicked Nebuchadnezzar and the heathen Chaldeans to destroy Judah, that as bad as it is, they are still more righteous than Babylon. They make men no more than fish to be caught in nets, slaying nations, and then worship the net rather than the Almighty God who established them. Worshiping the creature more than the Creator. 

*Therefore they sacrifice unto their net, and burn incense unto their drag; because by them their portion is fat, and their meat plenteous.Shall they therefore empty their net, and not spare continually to slay the nations?*

He asks, what about their judgment? 

It is so familiar to us, today, and likely this story resonated with believers throughout Biblical history. We decry our own nation falling from grace to judgment. We call out to God to deliver us, to punish the wicked, and set right the sins in the land. A Christian nation that has fallen away. 

But when God rises up the heathen, the wicked, the Muslim and the Jew and the pagans of India and the godless of China, and we are beset from all sides by the worst, whom God has raised up, we ask how can this be that *the wicked devoureth the man that is more righteous than he?*

But God will explain that while He may use the wicked to judge the more righteous, they too will face His judgement.

**Habakkuk 2** continues the prophet's dialogue with God and the burden places on him by the Lord. Habakkuk compares his waiting for the word as a watchman on a tower. 

Having asked God why, as evil as Judah is, that wicked Babylon would be used to destroy them. He awaits his reproval, and the Lord answered him, telling him to record what He said, in plain words that they may be read and understood, to be preached and affect the hearer. 

The Lord says the vision He is granting Habakkuk may take a while to come to fruition, but it surely will. 

The Lord begins describing Nebuchadnezzar, ruler of the Babylonian empire, both specifically and generally as a nation. This is a very common device used by God throughout the prophets. The empire can be the King, the capital city, the empire itself, or its people. 

He begins, *Behold, his soul which is lifted up is not upright in him: but the just shall live by his faith*.

Contrasting Nebuchadnezzar and his pride with the just, who live by faith places an understanding that for all his power and standing, he is not as the just, the righteousness of believers, humbled to receive God by faith. 

He drinks, and is proud, and never satisfied, constantly expanding his empire as Hell enlarges, and like death, can't be satisfied. The picture of Nebuchadnezzar's unquenchable desire for more lands to conquer and spoil being like Hell, in that it is continually growing to fit more and more taken into captivity by it, or as consistent and relentless as death, never being satisfied, never being full, is both poetic and insightful to his character, and doctrine. Death and Hell are as an empire ruining nations. But Jesus is victorious over it and delivers us from the captivity. 

And the people, as the saints in Heaven cry out for God's wrath, seek justice and judgment against Babylon, saying *Woe to him that increaseth that which is not his! how long?*

God promises their vengeance will come from Him; *Because thou hast spoiled many nations, all the remnant of the people shall spoil thee; because of men's blood, and for the violence of the land, of the city, and of all that dwell therein*.

*For the earth shall be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the Lord, as the waters cover the sea.*

Again, as dual fulfillment, this is a prophecy against Babylon and its king, and of the end times. God will repay. Vengeance is His. The whole world will know His glory and righteousness, and woe be unto them that face His wrath. Amen

*Woe unto him that giveth his neighbour drink, that puttest thy bottle to him, and makest him drunken also, that thou mayest look on their nakedness! Thou art filled with shame for glory: drink thou also, and let thy foreskin be uncovered: the cup of the Lord's right hand shall be turned unto thee, and shameful spewing shall be on thy glory*

This is an allegory for the sins of Babylon, and sound doctrine on drunkenness and the warped and perverse results of it.

Its neighbors, the nations it takes over and the abominations of false gods they introduce to the captives are compared to alcohol being given to take advantage of another. 

Similar to the Great Whore, Mystery Babylon; 

*And the woman was arrayed in purple and scarlet colour, and decked with gold and precious stones and pearls, having a golden cup in her hand full of abominations and filthiness of her fornication...with whom the kings of the earth have committed fornication, and the inhabitants of the earth have been made drunk with the wine of her fornication.*

But, it also is a stark reminder of what alcohol does to people, and the disgusting things that come about from it. 

God promises that for this sin, or leading others to drunkenness that they may be taken advantage of, Nebuchadnezzar's own nakedness will be revealed instead. His own shame and humiliation will be exposed. 

When we read **Daniel 4**, we will see this.

Finally, Habakkuk receives this gem;

*What profiteth the graven image that the maker thereof hath graven it; the molten image, and a teacher of lies, that the maker of his work trusteth therein, to make dumb idols? Woe unto him that saith to the wood, Awake; to the dumb stone, Arise, it shall teach! Behold, it is laid over with gold and silver, and there is no breath at all in the midst of it. But the Lord is in his holy temple: let all the earth keep silence before him.*

The truth of idolatry is its vanity. The idea that man can make a god of his own hands. That there is even a fraction of divinity and power in them is foolishness. 

And the people who today bow and serve idols, kneeling and offering prayers to them, lighting candles and incense and telling themselves that these lifeless statutes and carvings and molten images are a representation of the holy or divine are just as foolish as the maker of heaven images described here by the Lord. 

God is in His temple, sitting on His throne. Our God lives, and is not found in anything created by man's hands or in temples made of stone. The earth is His footstool. All creation is a testament to His power.

**Habakkuk 3**
*A prayer of Habakkuk the prophet upon Shigionoth.*

*2 O Lord, I have heard thy speech, and was afraid: O Lord, revive thy work in the midst of the years, in the midst of the years make known; in wrath remember mercy.*

*3 God came from Teman, and the Holy One from mount Paran. Selah. His glory covered the heavens, and the earth was full of his praise.*

*4 And his brightness was as the light; he had horns coming out of his hand: and there was the hiding of his power.*

*5 Before him went the pestilence, and burning coals went forth at his feet.*

*6 He stood, and measured the earth: he beheld, and drove asunder the nations; and the everlasting mountains were scattered, the perpetual hills did bow: his ways are everlasting.*

*7 I saw the tents of Cushan in affliction: and the curtains of the land of Midian did tremble.*

*8 Was the Lord displeased against the rivers? was thine anger against the rivers? was thy wrath against the sea, that thou didst ride upon thine horses and thy chariots of salvation?*

*9 Thy bow was made quite naked, according to the oaths of the tribes, even thy word. Selah. Thou didst cleave the earth with rivers.*

*10 The mountains saw thee, and they trembled: the overflowing of the water passed by: the deep uttered his voice, and lifted up his hands on high.*

*11 The sun and moon stood still in their habitation: at the light of thine arrows they went, and at the shining of thy glittering spear.*

*12 Thou didst march through the land in indignation, thou didst thresh the heathen in anger.*

*13 Thou wentest forth for the salvation of thy people, even for salvation with thine anointed; thou woundedst the head out of the house of the wicked, by discovering the foundation unto the neck. Selah.*

*14 Thou didst strike through with his staves the head of his villages: they came out as a whirlwind to scatter me: their rejoicing was as to devour the poor secretly.*

*15 Thou didst walk through the sea with thine horses, through the heap of great waters.*

*16 When I heard, my belly trembled; my lips quivered at the voice: rottenness entered into my bones, and I trembled in myself, that I might rest in the day of trouble: when he cometh up unto the people, he will invade them with his troops.*

*17 Although the fig tree shall not blossom, neither shall fruit be in the vines; the labour of the olive shall fail, and the fields shall yield no meat; the flock shall be cut off from the fold, and there shall be no herd in the stalls:*

*18 Yet I will rejoice in the Lord, I will joy in the God of my salvation.*

*19 The Lord God is my strength, and he will make my feet like hinds' feet, and he will make me to walk upon mine high places. To the chief singer on my stringed instruments.*

The final chapter of the prophet Habakkuk is a song, written in the Spirit, apparently upon a stringed instrument for the chief singer to sing. A *Shigionoth* may be the name of that instrument. It is also thought to be a style of prayerful song, but the Bible defines itself, and the closing verse indicates it was an instrument used to write this prayerful song.

Habakkuk writes of his fear of the word of the Lord he received, of the judgment against wicked Judah by the hands of the Chaldeans, and the judgment that will come upon Nebuchadnezzar and Babylon after they are used by the Lord for this purpose. 

The prophet asks that in the midst of the years, during this time, that in His wrath God remembers His mercy. 

He recounts that God came up from the South, from Tenant and mount Paran, in the wilderness where the children of Israel came, up towards the promised land. That His glory filled the heavens and the earth full of His praise, His brightness as the light. This is the Lord that was with them, the fathers in the wilderness who entered into the promised land as His people, and He their God. 

*He had horns coming out of His hand*...horns being leaders in the Bible, God sets up captains and kings over people and takes them down. He sent pestilence and fire before them, and drove the nations of Canaanites asunder. The mountains scattered and hills did bow, the great nations that were there before Israel. 

He asks, *Was the Lord displeased against the rivers? was thine anger against the rivers? was thy wrath against the sea, that thou didst ride upon thine horses and thy chariots of salvation?* 

We know that His wrath was against the people and their abominations. But the prophet is poetically saying that even the mountains and rivers were affected by the fury of God's wrath. Even the oceans deep, and the sun and moon stood still;

Joshua 10:13
*And the sun stood still, and the moon stayed, Until the people had avenged themselves upon their enemies. Is not this written in the book of Jasher? So the sun stood still in the midst of heaven, and hasted not to go down about a whole day.*

The prophet sings that God marched through the land in indignation and thresh the heathen in anger, while being the salvation and deliverer for His children, and *even for salvation with thine anointed*, a mention of Christ to whom all prophets gave witness. 

It is important to remember this, that the promise of salvation in His anointed, (Christ means anointed one, Messiah) was the faith of the Israelites in the wilderness, and of the saved throughout the Old Testament. 

Habakkuk describes how the Lord destroyed the rulers and delivered the land to them in violence and wrath. And then he says his lips quivered and he trembled and rottenness entered his bones knowing that the same wrathful vengeance was coming against Judah in his day. That the Lord would be doing to them what He did to the people before them.

*Although the fig tree shall not blossom, neither shall fruit be in the vines; the labour of the olive shall fail, and the fields shall yield no meat; the flock shall be cut off from the fold, and there shall be no herd in the stalls: Yet I will rejoice in the Lord, I will joy in the God of my salvation.*

Even so, in this time of tribulation and even in the desolation he describes, Habakkuk says he will find joy in the God of his salvation. He will rest in the day if trouble. 

This is a great illustration of the way we, as saved believers, should approach the Great Tribulation should it come in our day, or even individual troubles we may have. In the midst of days, the Lord remembers His mercy. We have rest. We find joy in the God of our salvation. 

We would do well to remember this. 

*The Lord God is my strength*. Amen.
