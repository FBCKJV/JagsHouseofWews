**2 Kings 1**
*Then Moab rebelled against Israel after the death of Ahab.*

*2 And Ahaziah fell down through a lattice in his upper chamber that was in Samaria, and was sick: and he sent messengers, and said unto them, Go, enquire of Baalzebub the god of Ekron whether I shall recover of this disease.*

*3 But the angel of the Lord said to Elijah the Tishbite, Arise, go up to meet the messengers of the king of Samaria, and say unto them, Is it not because there is not a God in Israel, that ye go to enquire of Baalzebub the god of Ekron?*

*4 Now therefore thus saith the Lord, Thou shalt not come down from that bed on which thou art gone up, but shalt surely die. And Elijah departed.*

*5 And when the messengers turned back unto him, he said unto them, Why are ye now turned back?*

*6 And they said unto him, There came a man up to meet us, and said unto us, Go, turn again unto the king that sent you, and say unto him, Thus saith the Lord, Is it not because there is not a God in Israel, that thou sendest to enquire of Baalzebub the god of Ekron? therefore thou shalt not come down from that bed on which thou art gone up, but shalt surely die.*

*7 And he said unto them, What manner of man was he which came up to meet you, and told you these words?*

*8 And they answered him, He was an hairy man, and girt with a girdle of leather about his loins. And he said, It is Elijah the Tishbite.*

*9 Then the king sent unto him a captain of fifty with his fifty. And he went up to him: and, behold, he sat on the top of an hill. And he spake unto him, Thou man of God, the king hath said, Come down.*

*10 And Elijah answered and said to the captain of fifty, If I be a man of God, then let fire come down from heaven, and consume thee and thy fifty. And there came down fire from heaven, and consumed him and his fifty.*

*11 Again also he sent unto him another captain of fifty with his fifty. And he answered and said unto him, O man of God, thus hath the king said, Come down quickly.*

*12 And Elijah answered and said unto them, If I be a man of God, let fire come down from heaven, and consume thee and thy fifty. And the fire of God came down from heaven, and consumed him and his fifty.*

*13 And he sent again a captain of the third fifty with his fifty. And the third captain of fifty went up, and came and fell on his knees before Elijah, and besought him, and said unto him, O man of God, I pray thee, let my life, and the life of these fifty thy servants, be precious in thy sight.*

*14 Behold, there came fire down from heaven, and burnt up the two captains of the former fifties with their fifties: therefore let my life now be precious in thy sight.*

*15 And the angel of the Lord said unto Elijah, Go down with him: be not afraid of him. And he arose, and went down with him unto the king.*

*16 And he said unto him, Thus saith the Lord, Forasmuch as thou hast sent messengers to enquire of Baalzebub the god of Ekron, is it not because there is no God in Israel to enquire of his word? therefore thou shalt not come down off that bed on which thou art gone up, but shalt surely die.*

*17 So he died according to the word of the Lord which Elijah had spoken. And Jehoram reigned in his stead in the second year of Jehoram the son of Jehoshaphat king of Judah; because he had no son.*

*18 Now the rest of the acts of Ahaziah which he did, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?*

*Is it not because there is not a God in Israel, that thou sendest to enquire of Baalzebub the god of Ekron?*

We see a King of Israel, Ahaziah son of Ahab, wounded and dying, sending his servants to inquire of Baalzebub, the false god of Ekron, to see what his fate would be. 

Imagine, an injured leader of a group of people called after His name, going after false gods, to seek their counsel and blessing. 

But the angel of the Lord comes to Elijah, and tells him to meet the servants of the king and to tell them that because of this evil of going after strange gods, he will surely die. 

Hearing this ill omen of the word of God angers the king, and he sends out a band of 50 and their captains tell Elijah to come with them, oh man of God. 
Elijah says, *If I be a man of God, then let fire come down from heaven, and consume thee and thy fifty*, and fire rains down consuming them. 

And again, another band comes for him, and again Elijah calls down fire from Heaven and kills them all. 

The third time, the captain of the men falls to his knees, begging the prophet to not kill them. And the angel of the Lord tells Elijah to go with them, no harm would come to him. 

And so King Ahaziah dies as the word of the Lord said, and with no heir. The line of Ahab cut off.

Reading this chapter, it should stand out how similar in form and content it is to how the Gospels are written. The anointed one of God going forth and having average people come to him seeking something, and miraculously receiving it by faith. 

Parallels with Christ are there; Elijah as John the Baptist, a voice in the wilderness, Elisha as he who came after him, with twice the spirit of the Lord upon him. The sons of the prophets as disciples. Bringing the word of God to the people. 

In the first story, we find a certain woman, wife of a man of God, a believer in the Lord. Her husband's death, leaving them in debt. Her and her sons, about to be sold into bondage to pay it. 

Elisha asks what does she have, and she replies only a little oil. He tells her to go out to her neighbors and ask for empty containers, and not a few. Here in **2 Kings 5**, we find the Syrian captain, Naaman, stricken with the incurable disease, leprosy. And a servant girl, captive out of Israel, tells Namaan's wife of the prophet of the Lord in Samaria, that is Israel.

With permission of his king and loaded with treasure, Naaman and servants go into the King of Israel, asking he be recovered of his leprosy. 

The king becomes upset, thinking this is a trap. How can he heal a man of something that is untreatable? The King of Syria would hold it against him, he rends his clothes in distress. 

Elisha heard of this and sends for Naaman, and when the Syrian comes to him, the prophet's servant comes to him with a message; Wash yourself in the Jordan seven times and be healed. 

At first, Naaman is offended. He expected the man of God to come and lay hands and make a show and heal him there in the spot. He questions why he just go to the Jordon, when Syrian rivers are superior in his mind, and more convenient. 

But his servants convince him to try, and he does as the prophet said, and is healed. Cleaned of the leprosy.

He returns to Elisha, and confesses that now he believes there is no other God in all the earth, but in Israel. And he offers the treasure he has brought to the prophet. 

Elisha refuses, in the name of the Lord. 

Namaan asks then for two burdens of earth, soil from Israel, that he would make sacrifices of burnt offerings only to the Lord. As he had previously believed the waters of the rivers were different in Syria, now he assumes the mounds of Israeli dirt would matter in the sacrifice to God. 

He also asks for pardon, for Elisha's blessing, that when he returned to Syria, that when he went in to the house of Rimmon, a temple to idols, and bow himself down, that the Lord forgive him. He is indicating that while he believes that the Lord is the only true God, he would still go before idols and bow down before them, because of custom and what others might think. 

Asking for permission, and the Lord's blessing to be unfaithful to the Lord he proclaimed, Elisha offers nothing, but says "Go in peace." 

Whether Naaman actually believed on the Lord is doubtful. He seemed caught up in the moment and the miracle that had occurred, and yet still carnal. Seeing the water as important, rather than following the word of God. Seeing the soil as different. Pledging with one hand to worship only the Lord, and at the other admit he would continue in the false religion of his people. 

In many ways, this is the experience many have when given the Gospel. They are resistant to the truth, show doubt and disdain when told there is only one way to salvation, faith in Christ Jesus. They may taste of the Heavenly gifts as the Spirit works in them to help bring them to the truth. They may even confess that this IS the truth, and they promise they will do whatever it takes to convince God, the person who gave them the gospel, other Christians. 

But in the end, they are already thinking about the ways they will back out of their promise. They are already back to their old beliefs before the words of the promise had come out of their mouths. They never accepted the truth in their hearts. 

They are like the seed sown on the pathway in the parables of the Sower;

*Behold, a sower went forth to sow; And when he sowed, some seeds fell by the way side, and the fowls came and devoured them up*.

*When any one heareth the word of the kingdom, and understandeth it not, then cometh the wicked one, and catcheth away that which was sown in his heart. This is he which received seed by the way side.*

An all too common occurrence.

Some get confused by Elisha's telling him to go in peace, that by this he was blessing Namaan's request to pardon his sin of returning to the worship of his false gods. But really, he doesn't acknowledge it, and politely sends him on his way. 

It may also be why he refused any of the rewards. Not just for keeping himself from filthy lucre, but that it wasn't a sincere conversion and Naaman may well have taken the wrong idea, that he could buy God's favor. 

But we see covetousness and greed strike Elisha's servant Gehazi, who rides after and makes up a story to get some of that silver and clothing for himself. He then lies to Elisha when he returns, but the Lord reveals to the prophet the truth. And so, fittingly, Gehazi is stricken with leprosy, and cursed to his entire lineage.

There's also a lesson here for saved Christians. Those who are the rocky soil, who receive the truth of the word with gladness and hope, but because they have no root in themselves, with her and die without producing fruit. 

It is possible to receive the gospel and believe unto salvation and yet return to the same old life. Healed, washed, clean and reborn the way the Syrian Captain was, and yet living in the old flesh doing the same old things. Seeking pardon for not living the way you should, knowing that you've been forgiven. 

Of course, if good works and sanctification came automatically with salvation, the New Testament wouldn't be filled with encouragement from the apostles to do good works. To pursue sanctification by walking in the spirit rather than in the flesh. To feed the new man rather than the old. 

It's also why we are to disciple those that we help lead to Christ. Or to be edifying and supportive in the lives of those who we come across. Encouraging them to walk in the Spirit. 

There are quite a few professing Christians and even entire denominations that teach that works come automatically, and without evidence of a changed life, we should doubt the salvation of someone. 

While faith without works is dead, not being profitable to others, and we are justified before men by our good works, we should be careful to be understanding rather than judgmental. To encourage rather than condemn. 

We have all been as Naaman, excited about the Lord and turning back towards the things that we were trusting in before. We should have some Grace and understanding for people who aren't where we are in our spiritual walk. Not to condone sin. But to not be confrontationally judgmental. Elijah could have lectured Naaman. But he told him to go in peace. 

I think there's a lot that can be taken from that.

We cannot imagine such tribulation and hunger. We are blessed beyond measure. And still we complain. 

We have food in abundance, clothing, shelter. Relative peace and security. 

We are free to worship God, to attend church, to read the Bible, free to give the gospel to the lost...All things denied Christians for hundreds of years. Things that could be a death sentence in the past. Yet how many people claiming Christ do them? How many exercise the liberty we have?

One of the reasons Israel was so often afflicted was, that when they knew God, *they glorified him not as God, neither were thankful; but became vain in their imaginations, and their foolish hearts were darkened.* 

They turned to the worship of idols and worse. They held the truth of God in unrighteousness. 

But first, they took for granted the blessings of the liberty they had from the Lord.

Itching ears, taking every rationalization they are fed, while rejecting the word preached, and complaining all the while.

Here in **2 Kings 14**, we are given more history of the Kings of Israel and Judah, and see the first open war between the kingdoms, when Amaziah of Judah and Jehoash of Israel met in battle, Judah losing to their brothers, and Jerusalem sacked. 

The Bible says that Amaziah did right in the eyes of the Lord, but did not have the heart of David. He kept Baal worship out of Judah as his father did, but allowed the high places and sacrifices there to continue. 

He did right by not slaying the sons of the servants who had murdered his father, killing only the assassins themselves. 

*The fathers shall not be put to death for the children, nor the children be put to death for the fathers; but every man shall be put to death for his own sin.*

A reminder that the concept of "original sin," the false idea that we are born condemned by the son of Adam, rather than having his same sinful nature, is unbiblical and goes against the teachings of God. 

Amaziah went against Edom and had success. This prompted his challenge to Jehoash in Israel, who taunted his challenge saying that he was a thistle compared to his mighty cedar. 

Jehoash did evil in the sight of the Lord, and so too his son Jeroboam who was crowned after his death. But the prophet Jonah, son of Amittai spoke to him the word of God, that he expand and shore up the territory of Israel due to the affliction He saw in the land. He used the wicked king to be a deliverer for the people, while the lukewarm king of Judah, who did that which was right but wasn't sold out for the Lord, was not as successful in ruling. He was overthrown by a seditious plot, as was his father. And like him, his son was placed on the throne.

A key element to the stories of the kings of Judah is their unwillingness to drive all the sin from the land. They may break down the house of Baal and destroy the images and slay the priests, but they never turn to the high places. They leave sin in the land. 

While Israel continues its slide towards destruction and promoting a false religion, they have varying success and failures, afflictions and victories, as the will of God found necessary. 

But Judah, who are as a saved Christian, trusting the Lord and getting right on major sin, never fully commit. And God chastens them for it. 

We can take that lesson and apply it to our own lives as saved believers. We should seek to have a heart like David's, and not be lukewarm in our walk, but strive to remove the high places in our hearts.

Here in **2 Kings 20**, the final tales of good King Hezekiah of Judah. First, we find the King near death. The prophet Isaiah comes and tells him to get his things in order, for the word of the Lord says he will surely die. 

Now we know from his introduction in chapter 18 that Hezekiah did that which was right in the eyes of the Lord, and walked in His ways. So hearing of his pending untimely death, he turned in prayer to the Lord. 

He reminded God of his faithfulness and his living according to God's precepts, and asks to be spared this fate, in tears. 

And before Isaiah had gone out of the courtyard, God repents and grants Hezekiah 15 more years. That after 3 days, when he is clean, he shall go up to the house of the Lord. (This seems like a picture of Christ, and the healing power of the Spirit to me).

And Hezekiah asks for a sign, to know that what Isaiah speaks is true, and he offers that the Lord can make the shadow of the sundial move forward 10 degrees, or backwards 10. Hezekiah asks for the sub to move backwards in the sky, being a more sure sign of God's might, and it does. 

And the Lord promises;

*I will deliver thee and this city out of the hand of the king of Assyria; and I will defend this city for mine own sake, and for my servant David's sake.*

Note again, it isn't for the Jews and it isn't for Hezekiah that the Lord preserved Jerusalem. But for His own sake and the covenant He made with David, that is Christ.

Hezekiah asked for a sign that the word of God spoken of Isaiah was true. Paul writes in **1 Corinthians 1**:**22-24**;

*For the Jews require a sign, and the Greeks seek after wisdom: but we preach Christ crucified, unto the Jews a stumblingblock, and unto the Greeks foolishness; but unto them which are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ the power of God, and the wisdom of God.*

In the Old Testament, the Hebrews often asked for a sign, and were given them. Sometimes, God offered a sign that they may know His word was true. But we have the Spirit testifying within us, and the more sure words of prophecy of the Bible. 

In our life, when we are praying to God to deliver us from bad things, illness, family members in trouble, whatever, we are reassured by the Spirit confirming the word of God and His promises to us. That according to His will, He is faithful to deliver us. This is sign enough. 

But often we need more. As the tearful father said to Jesus, asking Him to bring back his daughter unto him, *Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief.* Or as the apostles said to Christ, *Lord, Increase our faith.*

We can ask God for reassurance. We can ask for things we've hoped for, and see the work of the Spirit bringing it about. Increasing our faith. Helping our unbelief. It doesn't mean we doubt our salvation, or that God can't deliver. It just means we are human, and the flesh fears. 

This is why it is important to be in good standing with the Father, as Hezekiah was. Able to go before God to ask of Him what we need. By walking 3the Spirit and being right with the things of God, our faith is made stronger, and our trust in him when we are weak will come more naturally. 

But when we are in sin, and ashamed, and walk in the flesh, our doubts and fears increase. Our faith that God will save us falters, knowing we don't deserve His mercy. 

Thankfully, He is faithful to us, that if we turn to Him, and confess our sins, He will forgive us. **1 John 1:5-9** 

*This then is the message which we have heard of him, and declare unto you, that God is light, and in him is no darkness at all. If we say that we have fellowship with him, and walk in darkness, we lie, and do not the truth: But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin. If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us. If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.*

**1 John 5:13-15** 
*These things have I written unto you that believe on the name of the Son of God; that ye may know that ye have eternal life, and that ye may believe on the name of the Son of God. And this is the confidence that we have in him, that, if we ask any thing according to his will, he heareth us: And if we know that he hear us, whatsoever we ask, we know that we have the petitions that we desired of him.*

So, like Hezekiah, let us walk in the light that we may have confidence in the Lord to deliver us from our troubles.

<!-- post 14705 · 2024-08-07 06:44 · study -->

But a story of an Old Testament saint wouldn't be complete without an example of their sin. For all have sinned and come short of the glory of God. And Hezekiah was no different. 

The king of Babylon sends his regards, having heard of hezekiah's illness. And the recovered Hezekiah entertains the servants of the king. The Bible doesn't tell us that Hezekiah praised the Lord for his recovery, but rather showed them all of the riches of his kingdom, the city, his household and in the Temple.  

His sin is pride. Boasting of his riches while just a short while before he was on his deathbed. 

This is possibly the most common thing that I have experienced myself and witnessed in others; a great triumph of the Lord acting miraculously in our lives, followed by a low point where we forget the trouble we were in, and grow comfortable, taking it for granted. 

It is so easy for sin to creep in when this happens. 

One of the ways to avoid it is to not only go to God in our troubles and with supplications, but to praise the Lord for when He acts mightily in our lives. Bragging on him rather than ourselves. Staying humble. 

Because of Hezekiah's sin, Isaiah comes to him and tells him that God has judged and that all of the treasures he had shown off to Babylon would be carried away there. And that his son s will be eunuchs to their King.

Hezekiah accepts the rebuke and the judgment. And is thankful that it won't be in his day. But that small comfort isn't worth the moment of sin. 

Judah will soon learn a lesson in humility. And while God won't destroy them yet, because of his own interests and his promise to David, they will be made desolate for 70 years.

Josiah becomes King of Judah, and does that which is right in the eyes of the Lord. He seeks the truth and is given it, when the scribe and priest find a copy of the Law, the word of God, and deliver it to him. The young king reads it and rends his clothes knowing the sin of their fathers, and the people not harkening to all that which was written. 

He seeks to know God's will, and enquires of a prophetess who tells them that God will judge Judah harshly, with desolation, but that because of the tender heart of the King, and Josiah's humble and faithful reaction to hearing the word of God and believing it, judgment is spared for the length of his reign. 

The story of King Josiah is a picture of the coming of Christ. Where some were seeking truth, hearts meet for repentance, and the Word of God came among them, and they believed, and because of that faith, are spared the wrath of God. 

This applies to the 1st century Jews who accepted Christ, and the desolation of Jerusalem in 70AD. 

It also applies to believers in general, who hear the word of God with tender hearts and humble spirits, and are delivered from the wrath that will pour out in the world at Christ's return.

We are here in **2 Kings 23**, and the reign of Josiah, King of Judah. A godly man, who sought the Lord and, when finding the law of God and reading the covenant that had been ignored for generations, uses his power as king to set things right; to honor the covenant of the Lord. 

He orders that all, great and small, come to the temple and he reads the entire book of the law to the people, and standing by the King's pillar, makes a covenant with God to walk in His commandments, keeping His statutes with all their heart and soul. He leads a revival. 

Now the pillar he stood by, the same as that which other kings had, I believe is a picture of Christ. The promise of Him, shown to us as that unmoving pillar of the king whereby Josiah stands firmly in the word of God. 

And Josiah's zeal for the Lord is excellent. 

He destroys all of the groves, altars, high places, images, idols, and other abominations to false gods, not only throughout Judah, but even into the territory of Israel, whereby Jeroboam had led Israel to sin. 

He destroys that altar and the image where the prophet of old had come and preached against the first king of divided Israel, foretelling of this day, which we covered [here](https://t.me/HouseOfWews2/14487).

He destroys the houses of the sodomites, kills the evil priests and tears down the places where children were sacrificed by passing them through the fire. And at each place, the king not only grinds them to dust, but scatters their ashes and desecrated the places with the ashes of dead men's bones. 

When we read the list of places, and see that not only evil kings, but "good kings," such as Solomon, had put up these abominations. That the worship of false gods had permeated nearly everything, including the temple and the gates of the city. 

A little leaven had leavened the whole lump. But Josiah purges it with fire.

The city of Jerusalem is besieged for a little more than 2 years, and starved the people. And the King sought to flee, and was captured and his sons slain in his sight and then his eyes plucked out. And all Jerusalem was destroyed, the temple looted and destroyed. The walls burned. Absolute desolation came upon them. 

But the poor of the land were left to be vinedressers and husbandmen. 

This has echos of the end times. The period of great tribulation. The abomination of desolation in Jerusalem. The destruction of the temple. The king and the richmen taken away into captivity into Babylon. The poor left to tend the vineyard for the king, to bring in the harvest. 

And halfway through the 70yr captivity, Jehoiachin, now king of Israel, was lifted up, caught up with the King, and taken out of prison. 

*And he spake kindly to him, and set his throne above the throne of the kings that were with him in Babylon; And changed his prison garments: and he did eat bread continually before him all the days of his life. And his allowance was a continual allowance given him of the king, a daily rate for every day, all the days of his life.*

An image of the saved being raptured out of great tribulation, halfway through the 7 year period, and given new garments of white and made to sit at the table of Christ, having a continual allowance of the king all the days of our eternal life.
