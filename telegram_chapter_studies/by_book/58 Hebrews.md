*God, who at sundry times and in divers manners spake in time past unto the fathers by the prophets, Hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son.*..

In the letter to the Hebrews, Paul seeks to create a definitive case for Jesus Christ as the fulfilment of scripture, the Son spoken of to the fathers by the prophets and the other diverse manners of God's revelation. He is writing to the saved, but greater is his appeal to those among the Hebrews, who, like him had rejected the truth of Jesus until coming to the truth at Damascus. Paul uses his mastery of scripture and the inspired word of God to show that at all times, in all ways, God told them of His promise. And that now, His promise has come.

<!-- post 8488 · 2023-02-13 06:46 · study -->

Continuing his thesis, and having established Jesus is the Son, by whom all things were done, and now sat at the right hand of the Father, Paul begins to break down what this means, from scripture. 

Using angels, who are considered holy and close to God, Paul writes that at no time have they been called sons of God. That they have not the glory or the power or the inheritance that Christ Jesus has by His very nature. The angels are created, ministering spirits, but the Lord Jesus is through whom all things were created. They are not the same.

<!-- post 8489 · 2023-02-13 06:50 · study -->

Some dispute the authorship of this epistle, claiming that it was not Paul who wrote it. And in a way they're correct. I firmly believe Paul is the human author of the book of Hebrews, a shout out to Timothy later on essentially removes any doubt. But of all the books and epistles, Hebrews to me reads as the most inspired, the closest to straight dictation from the Holy Spirit. That's of course my subjective opinion, but the beauty of the prose in the text and the doctrine found in Hebrews is so elegant and yet simple that regardless of the human author, once again, God is speaking to us. And it is beautiful.

<!-- post 8490 · 2023-02-13 06:59 · study -->

One of the establishing doctrines in the first three verses of Hebrews chapter 1 is the understanding that while there is one God, the Father and the Son are distinct persons within the godhead, That makes up the Trinity, along with the Holy Spirit. 

The Bible says that jesus is a separate being but the express likeness of God the Father's  person, They are the same and yet *different* persons. Throughout the Bible we are told that Jesus is God. But having His own will, and obeying the will of He who sent Him, The basis and understanding of the Trinity is confirmed here, once again.

Having established Jesus as Christ, the redeemer, higher than the angels, Creator and the Son of God, our salvation to whom all the prophets gave witness, Paul builds upon this truth with the implications of our faith. 

Since Jesus is, therefore we should give greater heed, pay closer attention to the things spoken of by God, in diverse manners by the prophets to the fathers and by Lord Jesus Himself. That every transgression of the law (for sin is the transgression of the law) shall receive a "just recompense." Death and Hell. Just as the angels who rebelled will be all brought in subjection and cast into that lake of fire promised in Revelation, so too will man be if they neglect the good news of salvation, spoken of by the Lord and confirmed by those who witnessed Jesus ministry, and by signs and wonders of God, and through the gifts of the Holy Ghost given the disciples and Apostles. 

He's saying, "Look, we know who Jesus is not only by reputation but by scripture, the prophets, His followers who got it directly from Him, and we know about the fate of those angels who rebelled, Whom Jesus is greater than as He has them under subjection, so we need to listen and believe." 

But Jesus was also a man. Citing scripture, Paul notes how God made Jesus a little lower than the angels for the suffering of death, that He may taste death for all men. That the Creator became as the created that he may bring us to glory. That He, as the captain of our salvation was made perfect, complete, by the suffering of the cross and death as a sin offering. 

For in Christ, we believers are His brethren, His brothers. Sanctified in Jesus we become as one with Him, sons adopted into the promise of His glory by the Father. Who Jesus is not ashamed to call brethren. 

Again and again, citing scripture that his audience would know, primarily the Psalms of David which teach so much doctrine, he lays out the case for his argument. By taking heed we are the children of God by the partaking of Jesus flesh and blood through His death. And by His resurrection, steal the power of death from the devil. 

Satan was a murderer from the beginning. His goal is to ensure every man faces death and Hell like him. To sin, and in sin, be condemned. This is the power of death the devil possesses. 

But Christ's triumph granting salvation to all who believe, and eternal life to never taste death, delivered us. Set free from the bondage of the law, which by fear of death we try and fail to save ourselves. But in Christ, liberated from that fear knowing we won't die for our sins, as they are forgiven in Him. 

And so, to provide this salvation Jesus took on the form of man. And in all things He was like us, tempted of the flesh that He may know our struggles and understand our pain, that He may serve as the propitiation for our sin and succour, comfort us, in our temptations and afflictions. 

This is so important to understand. 

That God so loved us, that He so regarded man, that He allowed the express image of His person, His only begotten Son, through Whom all things were made and under Whom all things will be brought under subjection, to be made lower than the angels, to be made flesh and blood, to be tempted and suffer and made sin for all, that we may have eternal life. Liberated from the fear of death found in transgression of the law by our redemption in His perfect work. Just as the prophets and scriptures promised. Just as those who came before believed on for salvation. 

This is the good news. This is the Gospel.

Building the case laid out in the previous chapters, Paul makes an appeal to the faithfulness of Christ Jesus to God the Father, likening Him to Moses, and through this, linking the faith they share to enter into rest, and brought out of sin to the promised land to the Hebrews fleeing Egypt towards Canaan. 

He reminds them to be steadfast in the faith, to genuinely believe their salvation is as assured as the Hebrews was, least they like that first generation, be left in the wilderness for their sin and disbelief. 

Again, Paul uses the Psalms, credited to the true author, the Holy Spirit, rather than the man, David. 

And so too, like the Hebrews, if you hear His voice, heed it. Harden not your hearts with sin and doubt, but have full confidence in the promise made by the living God.

Chapter 4 again builds on the previous; **Hebrews 3**
*For some, when they had heard, did provoke: howbeit not all that came out of Egypt by Moses. But with whom was he grieved forty years? was it not with them that had sinned, whose carcases fell in the wilderness? And to whom sware he that they should not enter into his rest, but to them that believed not? So we see that they could not enter in because of unbelief*.

*Let us therefore fear, least, a promise being left us if entering into His rest, any of you come short of it.*

Therefore, because, their unbelief in the desert prevented them from entering the promised land, so too fear your unbelief keeping you from entering into the promised land, the rest in Jesus, the better country; the Kingdom of Heaven.  

By drawing the comparison to the Hebrews fleeing Egypt with those called coming out of bondage of the law and sin, Paul shows God's grace and His judgment parallels with Exodus spiritually. That it was a picture of Christ that so many Hebrews failed to believe and so died in the wilderness. 

For we which have believed do enter into rest. Note the tense. Salvation comes by belief at the moment we call upon His name. At that time we are freed of bondage and given rest, and it is done by the work God finished from the foundation of the world. 

**Genesis 2:2**
*And on the seventh day God ended his work which he had made; and he rested on the seventh day from all his work which he had made.*

Paul argues that **if** God says (again quoting **Psalm 95**) *If they shall enter into my rest,* that we can reckon that this is a possible thing, offered by God, **and** those whom it was first preached didn't enter their promised land due to unbelief, **then** we can know we will enter into His rest by faith, and be kept out without it. 

And further, as the Spirit moved Paul to write in **Psalm 95**; *Today if ye will hear His voice*, the offer is limited. We have but a short life to believe. A window of His calling, that can pass by death or prolonged neglect to hear His voice. To ignore it. To not retain God in their knowledge. Harden not your hearts when being called. 

 *(For he saith, I have heard thee in a time accepted, and in the day of salvation have I succoured thee: behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation.)*

God is reasoning with man, giving us the good news and His terms. Providing examples of what He means, and the if-then logic of it all that none have excuse. Harden not your hearts, hear His voice, NOW is the day of salvation. Believe or be shut out of the promise; never enter into His rest.

Continuing to reason with man, Paul writes that if it were only pertaining to the Exodus, and the offer of the promised land, if that was His rest given by a Jesus, then why would he say through David (again acknowledging who the real author of scripture is), that He would mention another day. And so, concludes there remains another day of rest for the people of God. Those who believe. That the picture of Exodus wasn't the fulfilment of Genesis. Jesus Christ is. 

And entering in to God's rest, we end our own work, because it is God's rest, not our own, and His labor, not our own, and His work has been finished since the foundation of the world. 

*For he that is entered into his rest, he also hath ceased from his own works, as God did from his. Let us labour therefore to enter into that rest, lest any man fall after the same example of unbelief.*

Using our own works to enter into His rest is a sign of unbelief that He did it all. Like Moses striking the rock twice for water. Like the Hebrews and mixed multitudes turning to the golden calf. Unbelief in God's word kept them from the promise. 

But we are saved by the blood of the Lamb slain before the foundation of the world. We rest in His work, not our own. We enter into rest by faith. Belief. And having entered into the promise by faith, not work, we are in the Sabbath of Christ. Again a picture of salvation without works.

*For the word of God is quick, and powerful, and sharper than any twoedged sword, piercing even to the dividing asunder of soul and spirit, and of the joints and marrow, and is a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart. This is the argument being made. This is God reasoning with man. 

*Come now, and let us reason together, saith the Lord: though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow; though they be red like crimson, they shall be as wool.*

God isn't the author of confusion. He has inspired and preserved His word for all time that we may be thoroughly furnished, to know that He loves us, and to give us the offer of His gift of salvation in Jesus.

Now having established that Jesus Christ, the Son of God seated at the right hand of the Father, is greater than the angels, greater than Moses, Abraham, David, in all ways, better than the Levites and the descendants of Aaron as the High Priest after the order of Melchisedek, Paul continues to show the superiority of Christ to the Mosaic law itself, which is a picture of His glory. That faith in Him that saves and the promises made of God for that faith, a better covenant. A new and better testament. 

Better than the commandments which condemn us, transgressions of the law being sin, better than the dead works which cannot save us and Jesus as our redeemer is a better sacrifice than all the sacrifices offered under the law. 

The entire epistle is an appeal to the superiority and primacy of Christ Jesus as our Lord, High Priest, advocate, and sole means of the cleansing of our sins, in either dispensation. Man has always been saved by grace through faith in Christ. The blood of bulls and goats never took away sins. It was not possible, the Bible says. No sacrifice other than Christ cleans the conscience. And so those who yearly sought forgiveness by sin offering rather than as a picture of their faith, fruitlessly returned year after year seeking to clean their conscience. 

A conscience, or "with knowledge," offended by sin, knowingly transgressing the law, with understanding, can only be made clean with the blood of Christ. The vail only removed by His flesh, offered for us. A better sacrifice, done once and forever. Taking on the sins of the whole world. 

Burnt offerings and sacrifice for sin gave God no pleasure, but Jesus doing the will of the Father, to become sin and die for us that all may be called and those that believe be saved, sanctified once and for all in the offering of Jesus Christ's body. In this the LORD delights. 

For by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified.

Practical application of this, the implications of this, that we are forgiven of all sin by this perfect sanctification, total remission, forever, is that we be freed from the burden of guilt and the fear of judgment that we may serve the living God. 

Faith in Christ is to be born again, our spirit without sin. The Holy Ghost as witness that we can let go of the sins of the past and have full assurance of forgiveness for sins of the flesh going forward. The knowledge that God so loved us, He offered His only begotten Son. The rest that is found in Christ. The true Sabbath. 

*This is the covenant that I will make with them after those days, saith the Lord, I will put my laws into their hearts, and in their minds will I write them; And their sins and iniquities will I remember no more.*

*Now where remission of these is, there is no more offering for sin....*

Most of our troubles as man come from the carrying of that burden. The heavy yoke of the conscience. The fear of judgment and death. The pangs of guilt are the Spirit, bringing us reproof of sin that we may understand the need for salvation, and pricked by the hearing of His word, come to understanding and in that knowledge, repent unto faith. 

But for some, who taste that heavenly knowledge, partake in the rebuke of the Holy Ghost, reject it. Resenting being judged. Clinging to the trauma, reveling in their sin. And they willfully sin knowing the judgment of God, rejecting Christ and being made reprobate by God. Having their conscience seared as with hot iron. 

These are the three spiritual states of man. Unsaved, saved and reprobate. And the underlying condition of fallen man comes in our conscience; be it guilty, cleansed by the blood, or seared by God for our hardened heart.

<!-- post 8691 · 2023-02-23 08:00 · study -->

And so to those who live by faith, the burden of our sin borne on the cross by Jesus, quickened and sealed by the Spirit, made temples of God, adopted heirs, part of His priesthood, an holy people, we should have full confidence in the covenant and patience towards the day of redemption. 

We shouldn't be miserable. We shouldn't cling to guilt for sins and inequities God has forgiven and remembers no more. 

*If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us. If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.*

This is the liberty which is found in Christ. 

And so, by implication Paul encourages us;

*Let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience, and our bodies washed with pure water. Let us hold fast the profession of our faith without wavering; (for he is faithful that promised;) And let us consider one another to provoke unto love and to good works: Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as the manner of some is; but exhorting one another: and so much the more, as ye see the day approaching.*

If we truly believe all of these truths and have faith in our salvation, then let us as brethren encourage and edify each other, and do good works with a clean conscience. Not selfishly trying to appease an angry God, not in dead works and fruitless sacrifices of bulls and goats, but as servants to the Lord who saved us. Free from the bonds of the law. Free from death and Hell. Out of the same grace and mercy shown to us by our Father. 

It should be noted here that Paul encourages us, in the Spirit, to gather with other believers in an assembly for the purpose of building our faith and provoking each other to love and good works. Exhortation in the faith, especially as we see that day of Christ's return approaching. 

This is an appeal to go to church. Note, not for salvation, not just because it's sin not to, not because you can lose your salvation if you don't. But like all good works, to the benefit of others and a reflection of the love God has shown us in our salvation. 

Do not forsake the assembly, because by it we become more complete, more perfect and able to serve God better. And it will build your faith and patience, your knowledge of the Lord, bring blessings for your works as it brings more opportunities to serve others.

*Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen....But without faith it is impossible to please him.*

*So then faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.*

Faith is trusting someone or something promised. Relying on the hope of an offer or covenant. Of belief in the unseen based on faithfulness of the one making the promise. The testimony given. Their word. 

Faith in God relies on His word being true, His promises being kept. His end of the covenant being as a rock. God, who cannot lie tells us His word, and by hearing, we believe the report. 

That is faith. 

And the just live by faith. 

It isn't a mystical force. It isn't will-to-power sorcery. If faith moves mountains, it is by the promise of God to move it, not our will but His. 

The Hall of Faith saints written about here, the recitation of those people who the scriptures tell us believed God when He made promises to them, who were made righteous by their faith and did the will of God for a reward not seen. A promise to be kept. Blessings bestowed on future generations. But their faith held on His word allowed them the opportunity to do great things for the Lord. Because one of the many promises God makes unto us is that He is a rewarder of them that diligently seek Him. Beginning with salvation. 

*For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God.*

*That whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life. For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.*

*For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.*

This is the first promise. Faith in this is the rock upon which Christ built His church. The Kingdom of promise that we trust in. The better country; *that is, an heavenly: wherefore God is not ashamed to be called their God: for he hath prepared for them a city.*..*a city which hath foundations, whose builder and maker is God.*

We are dead in sin. But made righteous by faith, our spirit quickened. Given eternal life. For the just lives by faith. Literally. 

But more, once justified, we live by faith diligently seeking Him in His word. Rightly dividing His promises, and being told His will. And by faith we are blessed and rewarded.
