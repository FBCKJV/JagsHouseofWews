Zechariah begins with the prophet receiving the word of God, telling him to preach to the people that the Lord wants reconciliation with them. That if they turn to Him, He will restore them. To not be as their fathers, stiff-necked and rebellious, and who didn't harken to Him. 

Here, at the end of the 70 year exile and captivity, Zechariah's message mirrors Haggai. Zechariah and Haggai were contemporary prophets ministering around 520 BC, during the second year of Darius I of Persia, when the Jewish exiles had returned from Babylon to rebuild Jerusalem and the temple. Haggai’s primary message was to urge the people to resume work on the temple, which had stalled due to opposition and apathy. Zechariah’s visions, including those of the red horses and four carpenters, were similarly meant to encourage the people by affirming God’s presence, judgment on their enemies, and His commitment to restore Jerusalem.

The Lord reminds them that their fathers and their prophets are dead. But His word and statutes remain. That the Lord of hosts had done to them according to their ways. They got what they deserve.

A short time later, Zechariah is given a vision, a man riding a red horse, standing among myrtle trees, with other horses with him, red horses, red speckled with white and pure white. 

The angel tells the prophet that he will explain, and as he does, the man among the trees, the Lord of hosts Himself, says that these are they whom the Lord hath sent to walk the earth, to and fro. These answer to the Lord saying they have walked their routes and they found the earth to be still and at rest. 

There are a few interpretations of this, and what the horses represent. I will tell you mine. That the red are judgment and wrath, war. The speckled are wrath tempered with mercy. The white, the mercy of the Lord upon the people. Their role, to travel about ready to exact judgment on nations. Their report, everything at that moment was at peace. I could be wrong. 

The. The angel guiding Zechariah asks the man, the Lord of hosts, (pre-incarnate Christ Jesus? I think so. A theophany), he asks "How long wily though not have mercy on Jerusalem and the cities of Judah?" Noting the 70 years they've been desolate. 

*And the Lord answered the angel that talked with me with good words and comfortable words.*

And the angel instructs the prophet; *Cry thou, saying, Thus saith the Lord of hosts; I am jealous for Jerusalem and for Zion with a great jealousy.* 

That He is displeased with the heathen who are at peace, given their role in the desolation and captivity of Judah. That the Lord returns to Jerusalem with mercies, that His house will be rebuilt. That prosperity will spread out from Jerusalem, and the Lord shall comfort Zion, and shall choose Jerusalem. As earlier prophets spoke, the city where the Lord chose to place His name. 

Then Zechariah receives another vision, seeing four horns, those nations whose rulers scattered Judah, Israel and Jerusalem. Assyria, Babylonians, Persians and Egypt (in my opinion). And that there were 4 carpenters who come to fray these horns, to cause them terror and to be cast out. 

Again, there's speculation as to who these four are, or what it means that four builders come and restore Jerusalem. I think the simplest explanation are the 4 figures we are given who are responsible for the restoration of the Temple and the practices among the people. Zerubbable and Joshua the high priest, also contemporaries of the prophets, but also Ezra and Neamiah. Zerubbabel and Joshua (520 BC),  Ezra (458 BC) and Nehemiah (445 BC) came later.

This makes sense to me. But again, I could be wrong.

Here in **Zechariah 2**, we see the prophet again encouraging the people to rebuild and restore, knowing that the Lord is with them and will be their strength, if they only keep His ways. 

The angel that Zechariah sees in his vision, like those that appeared to Ezekiel and John, is measuring, this time the size of the city, Jerusalem. The angels speak, and the one saying to tell Zechariah that the city will be so large, with the multitude of men and cattle, that there isn't a physical wall to protect them, but a wall of fire, the glory of God in her midst. 

Similarly, we see in Isaiah this same concept, that God's Salvation is the wall. His salvation is the bulwark. 

The second major theme repeated from Isaiah is the understanding that all nations shall flow into the city, to Zion. The nation of the righteous who keeps the truth. 

This has been consistently the promise of God for Judah. And in the Old Testament times of Zerubbabel, and Ezra and Neamiah, it was a promise to the physical nation, as well as a prophecy of the end times Kingdom that would include all people who believed. That He shall choose Jerusalem again. 

And when Jesus came, the Messiah, they slew Him and hanged Him on a tree. So it becomes what could have been. 

So now, in the New and better Testament, there is a similar, but different Kingdom. A millennial Kingdom of believers from all nations, still. And the peace of swords becoming plowshares. But the children of the Kingdom shall be cast out for unbelief. 

Salvation is the wall. Without faith and trust in that, there is no part for them. But all those that believe are given the inheritance and blessings, the promises of God.

**Zechariah 5** begins a series of very symbolic visions for the prophet. The angelic guides explain their representation, though their ultimate meaning may be left open to interpretation.

The first is a vision of a roll, or scroll, a length of parchment flying in the sky, 20 cubits long and 10 wide. 

The angel says that this scroll contains a curse against those that steal and those that swear, falsely in God's name, that they shall be cut off. That their house shall be consumed and the timber of it. I believe that this means the house, as in the family and the structure, both.

This is gone out to the world, not confined to Israel or Judah. God promises there is a recompense for their error. A curse for their sin. Thieves never prosper, and those that swear falsely are eventually done in by their own lies. 

Then the angel shows Zechariah another vision; an ephah, or basically a measured container, according to Webster's 1832 "an ancient Hebrew dry measure equivalent to a bushel." And in the ephah, a woman representing wickedness, kept in by a lead lid weighing a talent. She is carried by two female angelic beings with wings like storks, to the land of Shinar, which is Babylon. 

This is cryptic and while we can see what it represents in the text, it is difficult to determine what it means. I think it is the spirit of the whore, Mystery Babylon, being delivered to its place, where that same spirit will rest on empires and kings, who become drunk in her fornication, from **Revelation 17**;

*Come hither; I will shew unto thee the judgment of the great whore that sitteth upon many waters: With whom the kings of the earth have committed fornication, and the inhabitants of the earth have been made drunk with the wine of her fornication.
