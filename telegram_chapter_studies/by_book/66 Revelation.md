The revelation of Jesus Christ as told to John, signified by His angel, written to Jesus' servants, and directly to the 7 churches which were in Asia, to tell us the things which John had seen, which are and things which shall be hereafter. 

The book of Jesus Christ's Revelation is not written to obscure. Its intent is literally the title. To reveal. Those of us saved by Christ are blessed by reading it. Hearing it. Keeping the prophecy of those things written therein, for the time is at hand. 

Written in the late first century, likely around 93AD, by the Apostle John, who  served Jesus in His earthly ministry with his brother James, who was martyred shortly after Christ's ascent. Called by Jesus "Boanerges," or the "sons of thunder," their mother asked Jesus for favor for her sons in **Matthew 20:20-28**. 

John was the apostle Jesus loved, who wrote the Gospel and epistles. 

Now, exiled to Patmos by Domitian, emperor of Rome from 81 to 96AD, for, as John writes here; "*for the word of God, and for the testimony of Jesus Christ."* We know the timing and details from John's deciple, Polycarp, and early texts by historians Clement of Alexandria, Victorinus, Eusebius and Jerome. 

The timing of its writing doesn't much matter for us, blessed readers. The earthly things are incidental to spiritual. But the heresy of preterism requires the authorship to be pre-70AD, because it relies on the early writing to make the desolation of Judah by Rome, the destruction of the temple and murder and scattering of the Jews, serve as fulfilment of the prophecy.  

Other modern "scholars" cast doubt on the author's identity, deny John wrote Revelation, often claiming different people wrote the Gospel, epistles and Revelation attributed to John, however the style and description of spiritual things by the Apostle, as well as the clearly inspired truth contained put that heresy to bed sufficiently for believers. That John's peers and ancient historians testify that it was his bolsters that, but a careful reading of scripture is all we really need.

Revelation chapters 2 and 3 deal with "what is," in the prophetic sayings of Christ Jesus, to the angels or messengers of the churches in Asia Minor, through John the Apostle. The letters He bids John write are addressed to the individual churches, from an aspect of description representing Jesus, have compliments and criticism and warning and rebuke for the church, followed by an encouragement for those with an ear, of what it means to be saved. This pattern can be split up, to better see the doctrine being given. 

First, we recall from chapter 1 that the 7 stars are the angels of the 7 churches. The 7 candlesticks, the churches themselves. Taking away their candle in this context means the church would fail. No longer having the blessing of God. And, they all eventually did as none remains. 

This is a lesson to churches today. Some or all of what these 7 model churches are engaged in can be found in even the best churches today. The Lord Jesus is warning each and warning us. 

The message of overcoming, as I posted a study on yesterday, is the promises of Christ to the saved who stay faithful, who repent to do the things He instructs. A reminder of what awaits us as saved sons of God that we may be true. 

Jesus singles out three groups whom He hates and will judge; those who say they are Jews but are not, who are the synagogue of Satan. The Nicolaitanes. Jezebel and those she's seduced.  

So, let's break it down, missive by missive to each church.

*Unto the angel of the church of Ephesus write; These things saith he that holdeth the seven stars in his right hand, who walketh in the midst of the seven golden candlesticks;*

Jesus begins using a descriptor of Himself, from the description given by John from His vision in chapter 1. Jesus is holding the angels of the 7 churches in His hand. He is walking in this midst of their candlesticks. His message is to the Ephesians, the church founded 30 odd years before the writing of Revelation, by Paul and the other evangelists. That had fornication in their midst back in Ephesians 1. Jesus goes on to praise their good qualities;

*I know thy works, and thy labour, and thy patience, and how thou canst not bear them which are evil: and thou hast tried them which say they are apostles, and are not, and hast found them liars: And hast borne, and hast patience, and for my name's sake hast laboured, and hast not fainted.*

All good things. 

*Nevertheless I have somewhat against thee, because thou hast left thy first love. Remember therefore from whence thou art fallen, and repent, and do the first works; or else I will come unto thee quickly, and will remove thy candlestick out of his place, except thou repent.*

The Ephesians have left their first love. This is generally accepted as the spreading of the Gospel. They are working and laboring and being persecuted in Christ's name, trying false prophets who crept in unawares and rejecting them. But the first love of any church is the winning of souls to Christ. The joy of our salvation when fresh leads to our telling everyone about the good news. Churches grow from giving the Gospel. But many successful churches plateau and settle in with who they have, and get wrapped up in feasts of charity, laboring in their own community of believers while neglecting this command of Christ to preach the word to every creature. Without that, Jesus says, He will remove their candlestick. The church will die.  

*But this thou hast, that thou hatest the deeds of the Nicolaitanes, which I also hate.*

Another compliment, that the church at Ephesus hates the deeds of the Nicolaitanes. We have to discern from this limited information that they were a group either led by or claiming to be followers of someone named Nicolas. Many speculate that it refers to Nicolas the proselyte from Antioch, who was elevated to decon along with Stephen and Philip back in Acts. 

The doctrines refered to here, according to early writings and histories is that Nicolas, a pagan convert to the faith of the Jews, then accepting Jesus as the promised Christ, had the doctrine of despising the flesh, hating the material world, that he could better focus on the spiritual. The idea is given he was a just believer, but those who came after him took his views and drifted heavily into gnosticism. We can't know directly from scripture, but this seems very likely. It's also possible that gnostics took the name as a way of gaining legitimacy in the early churches, that they may creep in unawares, with damnable heresy. 

*He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches; To him that overcometh will I give to eat of the tree of life, which is in the midst of the paradise of God.*

The first promise to those who overcome, who believe on the Lord Jesus Christ for salvation, is the gift of eternal life. The eating of the fruit of the tree of life, in the midst of the paradise of God. The location is going to come up later in Revelation, but the important thing is the knowledge that we who believe in He that has overcome will be given of it to eat. 

Recall in Genesis 3 that God banishes Adam and Eve from the Garden to prevent their eating of this tree themselves, having already eaten of the tree of knowledge of good and evil. Having sinned.
